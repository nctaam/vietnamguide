How to Get to Con Dao: Flight vs High-Speed Ferry (2026)

Updated September 26, 2026

Reaching the remote Con Dao archipelago requires a firm plan. Located 185 kilometers south of Vung Tau and 230 kilometers from Ho Chi Minh City, the islands are famous for coral reefs, sea turtle nesting grounds, and somber French colonial prison history. Because Con Dao sits far offshore in open waters, travelers must choose between short turboprop domestic flights or modern high-speed express catamarans departing from coastal mainland ports.

The Concierge Verdict

Fly directly from Ho Chi Minh City (SGN) or Can Tho (VCA) with Vietnam Airlines or VASCO if your budget permits. The 50-minute flight lands at Con Dao Airport (VCS) with zero sea sickness risk and bypasses open-ocean swell conditions for 1,800,000 to 2,600,000 VND ($72 – $104 USD) one way. If you are traveling on a backpacker budget or exploring the Mekong Delta, take the Phu Quoc Express high-speed catamaran from Tran De port in Soc Trang for 390,000 to 520,000 VND ($15.60 – $20.80 USD), which crosses in just 2.5 hours.

Travel Options Breakdown: Flights vs Fast Ferries

1. Flying to Con Dao: The Stress-Free Standard

Because Con Dao's Co Ong Airport (VCS) has a relatively short runway situated between coastal mountains, standard Airbus A321 and Boeing 737 passenger jets cannot land here. Vietnam Airlines and its regional subsidiary VASCO operate ATR-72 turboprops and Embraer 190 regional aircraft from Tan Son Nhat International Airport (SGN) in Ho Chi Minh City and Can Tho International Airport (VCA).

Flights take approximately 50 minutes from Saigon. Upon touch-down, resort shuttles and airport taxis meet incoming flights to drive passengers the 12 kilometers along scenic cliffside roads into Con Son town for roughly 150,000 to 200,000 VND ($6 – $8 USD). Ticket demand is high throughout the year, so reserve your flights at least three to four weeks in advance.

2. High-Speed Catamaran from Tran De (Soc Trang)

For budget-conscious adventurers and travelers who are already exploring the Mekong Delta, taking the ferry from Tran De port in Soc Trang province is the most efficient maritime route. Phu Quoc Express operates daily double-hulled red catamarans capable of cruising at 30 knots.

Because Tran De sits geographically closest to Con Dao, the open-ocean crossing takes only 2 hours to 2.5 hours. Standard tickets cost 390,000 VND ($15.60 USD) on weekdays and 520,000 VND ($20.80 USD) on weekends, docking at Ben Dam Port on the southern tip of Con Son Island.

3. High-Speed Catamaran from Vung Tau

Travelers based in Saigon who prefer maritime travel without trekking down to Soc Trang can board the Con Dao Express catamaran departing from Cau Da Pier in Vung Tau. The voyage takes between 3.5 and 4.5 hours depending on sea currents.

Standard tickets range from 790,000 to 950,000 VND ($31.60 – $38.00 USD). However, during the northeast monsoon from November through February, ocean swells in this sector can reach 2 to 3 meters, causing severe motion sickness for sensitive passengers.

Seasonal Weather & Rough Sea Advice

The best season for calm sea crossings to Con Dao runs from March to September, when southern ocean winds remain light and water visibility for scuba diving exceeds 15 meters. From October to February, strong winter monsoon winds frequently cause maritime authorities to suspend ferry sailings for multiple days. If traveling in winter, flying is strongly recommended to avoid being stranded on the island.

Terminal Boarding Protocol & Electronic Ticketing

Modern transit networks across Vietnam operate predominantly on paperless verification. Knowing the boarding routine prevents confusion at crowded transport hubs:

  • Digital QR Code Check-in: Vietnam Railways (dsvn.vn) and major intercity coach operators (FUTA Bus Lines, Interbus, Hai Van) issue digital boarding vouchers directly to your smartphone. You do not need to print physical boarding passes. Present the PDF confirmation or SMS reservation code alongside your original passport at platform ticket barriers 20 to 30 minutes before departure.
  • Luggage Allowances & Cargo Handling: Standard express limousine vans allocate space for one 20kg checked suitcase plus a small daypack per passenger inside rear cargo compartments. Vietnam Railways permits up to 20kg of personal luggage per ticket without surcharge. High-speed ferries to Con Dao and Phu Quoc enforce a 15kg carry-on limit, with oversized diving duffels requiring a nominal excess baggage fee of 30,000–50,000 VND at pier scales.
  • Highway Rest Stop Realities (Trạm Dừng Chân): Overland coach journeys exceeding 3 hours schedule mandatory 20-minute rest breaks at commercial highway complexes (such as those along Expressways CT01 and CT02). These facilities feature clean Western-style restrooms, hot food noodle stalls (phở, bánh mì), and convenience marts. Note your bus license plate number carefully, as dozens of identical green or red coaches park simultaneously in busy transit lots.
  • Arrival Station Scam Prevention: When disembarking at provincial railway terminals or bus plazas, bypass aggressive freelance drivers who crowd exit doors whispering flat rates. Proceed directly to marked taxi pickup bays to hire metered Mai Linh cabs, or book via ride-hailing applications (Grab, Be) where fares and routes remain digitally locked.

Transit Booking Windows & Holiday Peak Fares

Fare pricing and seat availability fluctuate significantly around national public holidays. Following these timing rules guarantees confirmed reservations:

  • Standard Advance Reservation Windows: Regular weekend departures should be reserved 3 to 7 days ahead through reputable digital platforms (such as 12Go Asia or Vexere). For standard mid-week journeys outside holiday cycles, reserving 24 to 48 hours in advance provides ample seat selection.
  • Tet Holiday & Peak National Festivities: During the Lunar New Year (Tet Nguyen Dan, occurring in late January or February) and Reunification Day week (April 30 – May 1), rail tickets open 60 days in advance and sell out within hours. Intercity coach companies apply legal holiday surcharges of 30% to 50% to cover empty return legs. Book your transportation before locking in non-refundable hotel stays during these peak dates.
  • Child Fares & Family Policies: Vietnam Railways offers free travel for children under 6 years old sharing a berth with an adult; children aged 6 to 10 receive a 25% discount on passenger tickets. On luxury limousine vans, children occupying their own leather seat pay full adult fare regardless of age. High-speed boat services provide discounted youth tickets for children measuring under 1.2 meters in height.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I fly directly from Hanoi to Con Dao?

Direct flights between Hanoi (HAN) and Con Dao are seasonal and limited. Most travelers from northern Vietnam fly into Ho Chi Minh City or Can Tho first, then take a connecting domestic flight to Con Dao.

How far is Ben Dam Port from Con Son town?

Ben Dam Port sits 12 kilometers southwest of central Con Son town. Shared passenger vans and metered taxis wait for incoming ferries, charging roughly 50,000 to 70,000 VND ($2 – $2.80 USD) per person.

Do ferries get canceled often due to rough seas?

During the windy winter months (November to January), ferry cancellations occur roughly 15 to 20 percent of the time. In the calm summer months (April to August), cancellations are extremely rare.
