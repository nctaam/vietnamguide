Rach Gia to Phu Quoc Ferry (2026): Speedboat Guide

Updated September 26, 2026

Taking the Rach Gia to Phu Quoc ferry is the most direct maritime route connecting the southern mainland of the Mekong Delta to Vietnam's premier tropical island. Departing from the commercial passenger piers of Rach Gia in Kien Giang province, high-speed catamarans and modern passenger ferries cruise 120 kilometers across the Gulf of Thailand to arrive at Bai Vong Port on the eastern shore of Phu Quoc. This maritime corridor operates multiple daily sailings, serving travelers journeying from Can Tho, Chau Doc, and Ho Chi Minh City.

The Concierge Verdict

Book Phu Quoc Express for the fastest, most stable sea passage. Their twin-hull red catamarans complete the 120-kilometer crossing in 2 hours and 15 minutes, cutting through open-water chop significantly better than single-hull vessels. Standard Eco tickets cost 340,000 VND ($13.60 USD), while VIP tickets with spacious leather reclining seats run 540,000 VND ($21.60 USD). If tickets are sold out or you travel with a motorcycle, Superdong operates dependable speedboats taking 2.5 hours for 330,000 VND ($13.20 USD). Arrive at Rach Gia passenger pier on Nguyen Cong Tru street at least 45 minutes prior to departure with your physical passport.

Terminal Logistics & Boarding Procedures

1. Rach Gia Departure Pier (Ben Tau Rach Gia)

The ferry terminal is located at the coastal end of Nguyen Cong Tru street in Vinh Thanh ward, Rach Gia City. The terminal building contains ticketing counters, air-conditioned waiting lounges, restrooms, and small coffee stalls. Taxis from Rach Soi long-distance bus station to the pier take 20 minutes and cost 90,000 to 130,000 VND ($3.60 – $5.20 USD). If arriving from Rach Gia Airport (VKG), a taxi to the terminal costs roughly 150,000 VND ($6.00 USD).

2. Bai Vong Arrival Port (Phu Quoc)

All speedboats from Rach Gia dock at Bai Vong Port in Ham Ninh commune on Phu Quoc's east coast. Upon exiting the pier, licensed transfer options connect to the island's main tourist hubs:

  • Air-Conditioned Shuttle Vans: Operated directly by ferry companies, shared shuttle vans run from Bai Vong to Duong Dong town center for 50,000 VND ($2.00 USD) per passenger.
  • Metered Taxis: Mai Linh and Vinasun taxis wait along the pier. A private ride to Duong Dong (14 km) costs 180,000 to 240,000 VND ($7.20 – $9.60 USD), while transfers to the southern resort strip in An Thoi cost 260,000 to 320,000 VND ($10.40 – $12.80 USD).
  • Ride-Hailing: Grab operates on Phu Quoc Island, with standard 4-seat car rides from Bai Vong to Duong Dong averaging 170,000 to 210,000 VND ($6.80 – $8.40 USD).

Transporting Motorbikes & Luggage Policies

Both ferry operators accommodate two-wheeled vehicles and standard baggage under clear guidelines:

  • Motorcycle Transport: Superdong and Phu Quoc Express carry motorbikes on designated lower decks. Loading costs 160,000 to 200,000 VND ($6.40 – $8.00 USD) per bike, with an additional 50,000 VND ($2.00 USD) port porter fee for ramp handling. Fuel tanks must be nearly empty for maritime safety regulations.
  • Luggage Limits: Passenger tickets include up to 20 kilograms of personal luggage. Oversized surfboards or dive gear incur excess baggage charges of 30,000 to 50,000 VND ($1.20 – $2.00 USD) per piece.
  • Documentation: Foreign travelers must present a valid passport matching the full name printed on the boat ticket. Vietnamese citizens may present national citizen ID cards (CCCD).

Weather & Sea Conditions in the Gulf of Thailand

Sea state strongly influences the crossing experience depending on the time of year:

  • Dry Season (November to April): The northeast monsoon creates flat, glass-like water conditions across the Gulf of Thailand. Crossings are exceptionally smooth with zero weather cancellations.
  • Monsoon Swells (May to October): The southwest monsoon produces occasional 1.5 to 2.5-meter open-ocean waves. Phu Quoc Express catamarans handle these conditions with minimal rolling, but motion-sickness tablets (anti-nausea pills, sold at port pharmacies for 15,000 VND / $0.60 USD per blister) are strongly recommended. When winds exceed Beaufort Force 7, maritime port authorities suspend sailings.

Terminal Boarding Protocol & Electronic Ticketing

Modern transit networks across Vietnam operate predominantly on paperless verification. Knowing the boarding routine prevents confusion at crowded transport hubs:

  • Digital QR Code Check-in: Vietnam Railways (dsvn.vn) and major intercity coach operators (FUTA Bus Lines, Interbus, Hai Van) issue digital boarding vouchers directly to your smartphone. You do not need to print physical boarding passes. Present the PDF confirmation or SMS reservation code alongside your original passport at platform ticket barriers 20 to 30 minutes before departure.
  • Luggage Allowances & Cargo Handling: Standard express limousine vans allocate space for one 20kg checked suitcase plus a small daypack per passenger inside rear cargo compartments. Vietnam Railways permits up to 20kg of personal luggage per ticket without surcharge. High-speed ferries to Con Dao and Phu Quoc enforce a 15kg carry-on limit, with oversized diving duffels requiring a nominal excess baggage fee of 30,000–50,000 VND at pier scales.
  • Highway Rest Stop Realities (Trạm Dừng Chân): Overland coach journeys exceeding 3 hours schedule mandatory 20-minute rest breaks at commercial highway complexes (such as those along Expressways CT01 and CT02). These facilities feature clean Western-style restrooms, hot food noodle stalls (phở, bánh mì), and convenience marts. Note your bus license plate number carefully, as dozens of identical green or red coaches park simultaneously in busy transit lots.
  • Arrival Station Scam Prevention: When disembarking at provincial railway terminals or bus plazas, bypass aggressive freelance drivers who crowd exit doors whispering flat rates. Proceed directly to marked taxi pickup bays to hire metered Mai Linh cabs, or book via ride-hailing applications (Grab, Be) where fares and routes remain digitally locked.

Transit Booking Windows & Holiday Peak Fares

Fare pricing and seat availability fluctuate significantly around national public holidays. Following these timing rules guarantees confirmed reservations:

  • Standard Advance Reservation Windows: Regular weekend departures should be reserved 3 to 7 days ahead through reputable digital platforms (such as 12Go Asia or Vexere). For standard mid-week journeys outside holiday cycles, reserving 24 to 48 hours in advance provides ample seat selection.
  • Tet Holiday & Peak National Festivities: During the Lunar New Year (Tet Nguyen Dan, occurring in late January or February) and Reunification Day week (April 30 – May 1), rail tickets open 60 days in advance and sell out within hours. Intercity coach companies apply legal holiday surcharges of 30% to 50% to cover empty return legs. Book your transportation before locking in non-refundable hotel stays during these peak dates.
  • Child Fares & Family Policies: Vietnam Railways offers free travel for children under 6 years old sharing a berth with an adult; children aged 6 to 10 receive a 25% discount on passenger tickets. On luxury limousine vans, children occupying their own leather seat pay full adult fare regardless of age. High-speed boat services provide discounted youth tickets for children measuring under 1.2 meters in height.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Rach Gia ferry better than Ha Tien ferry?

Rach Gia is significantly more convenient if you are traveling from Can Tho, Hau Giang, or southern Mekong Delta provinces, as it cuts 2 hours of road travel compared to driving all the way up to Ha Tien. However, the boat ride from Rach Gia is longer (2h 15m vs 1h 15m from Ha Tien).

How far in advance should I book ferry tickets?

Booking 2 to 4 days ahead is sufficient during regular weekdays. For weekends, lunar new year (Tet), and national holidays (April 30 – May 1), book at least 2 weeks in advance as popular morning sailings sell out completely.

Are there food and drinks sold on board?

Yes. Both ferry lines have small snack counters selling bottled water (10,000 VND / $0.40 USD), soft drinks (15,000 VND / $0.60 USD), canned coffee, and cup noodles (20,000 VND / $0.80 USD) with boiling water provided.
