Ho Chi Minh City to Da Lat: Bus, Flight & Car

Updated September 26, 2026

Traveling from Ho Chi Minh City to Da Lat covers roughly 300 km, trading tropical southern heat for pine forests and cool highland air at 1,500 meters altitude. While the route once involved grueling ten-hour journeys on winding mountain passes, expanded expressways and modern cabin coaches have made the passage significantly faster and more comfortable.

The Concierge Verdict

For convenience and value, overnight limousine sleeper buses are the superior choice. Reputable operators like Phuong Trang (FUTA Bus Lines) and Phong Phu provide private curtained cabins departing central Saigon late in the evening and arriving in Da Lat by dawn, saving both a hotel night and daylight sightseeing hours. Flying makes sense only if you have severe motion sickness on mountain curves or carry tight schedules, though remember that Lien Khuong Airport sits 30 km south of Da Lat town.

Transport Options At A Glance

Option 1: Limousine Sleeper Bus (Recommended)

Dozens of bus companies operate around the clock between Saigon and Da Lat along the Long Thanh-Dau Giay Expressway and National Highway 20. Modern “cabin buses” (xe giưậng phòng) feature single or double private pods equipped with privacy curtains, USB charging ports, personal reading lamps, and reclining orthopedic mattresses.

Top Bus Operators & Terminals

Phuong Trang (FUTA Bus Lines) runs departures every 30 to 60 minutes from their Le Hong Phong depot in District 5 and Mien Dong Bus Station. Standard 34-bed sleepers cost 280,000 VND, while premium royal cabin coaches cost 420,000 VND. Phong Phu VIP and Thanh Buoi offer luxurious 21-cabin luxury liners departing from Pham Ngu Lao in District 1, priced at 450,000 VND per ticket. Booking online via platforms like Vexere at least 48 hours in advance is strongly recommended for Friday night departures.

The Pass Experience: Bao Loc & Prenn

The journey climbs steadily through Dong Nai province before ascending the dramatic 10 km Bao Loc Pass, where hairpin turns require cautious driving. Closer to Da Lat, coaches navigate the newly widened Prenn Pass, offering sweeping vistas of pine-clad ravines. If you are sensitive to motion sickness, book lower-deck cabins located near the middle of the bus chassis, and take anti-motion medication 30 minutes before boarding.

Option 2: Direct Flights (SGN to DLI)

Flying represents the quickest physical transit. Vietnam Airlines, VietJet Air, and Bamboo Airways operate four to six daily direct flights from Tan Son Nhat International Airport (SGN) to Lien Khuong International Airport (DLI). Flight time is just 50 to 55 minutes. Fares fluctuate between 650,000 VND for early-morning VietJet budget fares and 1,500,000 VND for full-service Vietnam Airlines tickets including 23 kg checked baggage.

Lien Khuong Airport Transfer to Da Lat City

Lien Khuong Airport is located in Duc Trong district, 30 km south of downtown Da Lat. You have three transport choices upon arrival:

  • Airport Shuttle Bus: Operates outside the terminal, dropping passengers at Nguyen Cong Tru Street near Xuan Huong Lake. Ticket price is 50,000 VND per person, taking 45 minutes.
  • Metered Taxi: Mai Linh and Lado Taxi charge a flat airport transfer rate of approximately 250,000 VND to 300,000 VND for a 4-seater sedan.
  • Private Transfer: Pre-arranged hotel pickups typically cost 350,000 VND for comfortable SUVs.

Option 3: Private Car Hire

Hiring a private car with a dedicated driver provides unparalleled flexibility for families or photography enthusiasts wanting to stop at tea plantations in Bao Loc or roadside coffee roasters along Route 20. A one-way private transfer in an air-conditioned Toyota Fortuner costs between 3,200,000 VND and 4,200,000 VND, inclusive of expressway tolls, fuel, and driver fees. Travel time averages 6.5 hours including a lunch break.

Terminal Boarding Protocol & Electronic Ticketing

Modern transit networks across Vietnam operate predominantly on paperless verification. Knowing the boarding routine prevents confusion at crowded transport hubs:

  • Digital QR Code Check-in: Vietnam Railways (dsvn.vn) and major intercity coach operators (FUTA Bus Lines, Interbus, Hai Van) issue digital boarding vouchers directly to your smartphone. You do not need to print physical boarding passes. Present the PDF confirmation or SMS reservation code alongside your original passport at platform ticket barriers 20 to 30 minutes before departure.
  • Luggage Allowances & Cargo Handling: Standard express limousine vans allocate space for one 20kg checked suitcase plus a small daypack per passenger inside rear cargo compartments. Vietnam Railways permits up to 20kg of personal luggage per ticket without surcharge. High-speed ferries to Con Dao and Phu Quoc enforce a 15kg carry-on limit, with oversized diving duffels requiring a nominal excess baggage fee of 30,000–50,000 VND at pier scales.
  • Highway Rest Stop Realities (Trạm Dừng Chân): Overland coach journeys exceeding 3 hours schedule mandatory 20-minute rest breaks at commercial highway complexes (such as those along Expressways CT01 and CT02). These facilities feature clean Western-style restrooms, hot food noodle stalls (phở, bánh mì), and convenience marts. Note your bus license plate number carefully, as dozens of identical green or red coaches park simultaneously in busy transit lots.
  • Arrival Station Scam Prevention: When disembarking at provincial railway terminals or bus plazas, bypass aggressive freelance drivers who crowd exit doors whispering flat rates. Proceed directly to marked taxi pickup bays to hire metered Mai Linh cabs, or book via ride-hailing applications (Grab, Be) where fares and routes remain digitally locked.

Transit Booking Windows & Holiday Peak Fares

Fare pricing and seat availability fluctuate significantly around national public holidays. Following these timing rules guarantees confirmed reservations:

  • Standard Advance Reservation Windows: Regular weekend departures should be reserved 3 to 7 days ahead through reputable digital platforms (such as 12Go Asia or Vexere). For standard mid-week journeys outside holiday cycles, reserving 24 to 48 hours in advance provides ample seat selection.
  • Tet Holiday & Peak National Festivities: During the Lunar New Year (Tet Nguyen Dan, occurring in late January or February) and Reunification Day week (April 30 – May 1), rail tickets open 60 days in advance and sell out within hours. Intercity coach companies apply legal holiday surcharges of 30% to 50% to cover empty return legs. Book your transportation before locking in non-refundable hotel stays during these peak dates.
  • Child Fares & Family Policies: Vietnam Railways offers free travel for children under 6 years old sharing a berth with an adult; children aged 6 to 10 receive a 25% discount on passenger tickets. On luxury limousine vans, children occupying their own leather seat pay full adult fare regardless of age. High-speed boat services provide discounted youth tickets for children measuring under 1.2 meters in height.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a train from Ho Chi Minh City to Da Lat?

No direct train line connects Ho Chi Minh City to Da Lat. The historic cog railway between Thap Cham and Da Lat remains non-operational for long-distance transit, so buses, flights, and cars are your only options.

Are night buses safe on mountain passes?

Established carriers like FUTA and Thanh Buoi use speed limiters and professional dual-driver rotations. While mountain passes feature sharp bends, serious incidents on scheduled major bus lines are rare.

What time should I take the night bus?

Departing between 11:00 PM and midnight is ideal. This puts your arrival at Da Lat Bus Station around 6:00 AM, just as morning mist rolls across Xuan Huong Lake and local noodle shops open for breakfast.
