Best Vietnam Routes for First-Time Visitors: North, Central, South or Open-Jaw?

The best first Vietnam route is not the one that touches the most famous places. It is the one that gives each night a job: recover after the flight, protect a countryside morning, hold a bay night, keep a central heritage stretch calm, or finish the trip without backtracking across the country.

Use this guide before booking flights, hotels, or private transfers. It is a route-shape decision tool for first-time international travelers, not a maximum-coverage itinerary and not a checklist for forcing every region into one trip.

VietnamGuide verdict

For most first-time trips, a north-to-south open-jaw or a north-plus-central route is the strongest default. Shorter trips should stay inside one region or one tight spine. Ten to fourteen days unlock open-jaw logic, while twenty-one days can justify a fuller north-central-south route if you still protect transfer days and do not chase every famous stop.

Do not build the trip by adding all the pins that fit on the map. Route success comes from what you remove: duplicate overnights, forced backtracking, and long transfer days that erase the value of the place you just visited.

  • 7 days or less: one region only. Choose north, central, or south.
  • 8 to 10 days: north-plus-central or a very tight open-jaw if flights are clean.
  • 11 to 14 days: open-jaw becomes the premium default for most first-time travelers.
  • 15 to 21 days: a fuller north-central-south route can work if you still limit hotel moves.
  • Anything shorter than the route deserves: cut a stop, not sleep, food, or transfer margin.

Open-jaw flights are often the cleanest answer

Open-jaw means you arrive in one city and leave from another. For Vietnam, that often means Hanoi in and Ho Chi Minh City out, or Hanoi in and Da Nang out if the central route is the real priority. It is not a luxury trick; it is often the simplest way to stop backtracking.

Does the route move in one direction?If yes, open-jaw saves a return leg.North to south, or north to central, or a clear loop with one clean finish.You need to backtrack to recover a forgotten city.
Are baggage and booking rules simple?Separate tickets can break a pretty itinerary if connections are fragile.One ticket or a well-buffered connection with enough margin.Tight self-transfer with heavy luggage and a long first-day route.
Do the arrival and departure cities match the actual route job?Route shape should follow city function.Hanoi for the north, Ho Chi Minh City for the south, Da Nang for the central coast.Choosing a flight path that forces a city detour only because the fare looked lower at first glance.
Is the route long enough to feel the benefit?Short trips do not always justify complex ticket logic.Ten to fourteen days or more with a real multi-region sequence.A very short trip where a simple round-trip would be calmer.

Live checks before you lock the route

  • Check the flight map first: arrival city, departure city, and whether open-jaw saves a real transfer.
  • Check the weather and the month before you choose beach or bay priorities.
  • Verify whether the route depends on a train, domestic flight, or long road transfer that will need an early wake-up.
  • Review how many hotel transitions the route creates and whether each move solves a real problem.
  • Check if the route still feels good when you cut one stop from the draft. If it collapses, it was too full.
  • Check cancellation and baggage rules before using separate tickets or open-jaw flights.

FAQ

What is the best route for first-time visitors to Vietnam?

For most travelers, a north-to-south open-jaw or a north-plus-central route is the strongest answer. The best route is the one that gives each night a job and avoids needless backtracking.

Is 10 days enough for Vietnam?

Yes, but only for a north-plus-central route or a very tight open-jaw. Ten days is usually not enough to cross every region without rushing.

Should I start in the north or the south?

Start where your route shape and flight pricing make the most sense. North works well for scenery and a classic first-trip arc. South works well when the south is the point, or when a southern exit makes the route cleaner.

Is open-jaw worth it?

Usually yes if the trip is long enough. Open-jaw saves backtracking when the route naturally moves in one direction, especially on 10- to 14-day trips.

Should I add an island to every first trip?

No. An island is only worth it when the route has enough nights to let it feel like a rest chapter rather than a transfer detour.

Key Route Mileages, Transit Times & Costs

  • Hanoi to Ninh Binh (95 km): Daily regional express trains (SE5, SE7) take 2 hours 15 minutes (fare 85,000–140,000 VND); luxury limousine minivans depart every 30 minutes taking 90 minutes (fare 160,000–220,000 VND / $6.50–$9.00 USD).
  • Hanoi to Ha Long Bay (160 km): Highway 5B express limousine shuttles take 2 hours 30 minutes door-to-door (fare 280,000–350,000 VND / $11–$14 USD).
  • Da Nang to Hoi An (30 km): Coastal transit takes 40 minutes via meter taxi or Grab (fare 280,000–350,000 VND) or public bus line 1 (fare 30,000 VND).
  • Ho Chi Minh City to Can Tho (165 km): Expressway coach lines (Futa Bus Lines / Thanh Buoi) depart every hour taking 3.5 hours (fare 140,000–180,000 VND / $5.60–$7.20 USD).
