# 21 Days in Vietnam: The Definitive Three-Week Route Guide

A three-week journey across Vietnam marks the transition from casual sightseeing to genuine geographical and cultural discovery. With twenty-one full calendar days at your disposal, you no longer face the painful trade-offs inherent in one-week or two-week itineraries. You can experience the country's celebrated dynastic capitals and UNESCO maritime bays while incorporating remote frontier mountains, ancient subterranean cavern systems, and relaxed tropical island sanctuaries.

However, three weeks without disciplined planning often leads to travel fatigue. Attempting to move every single night or stitching together disconnected bus connections across sixteen different towns will leave you drained before reaching the central coast. 

The premier three-week itinerary anchors itself in eight regional headquarters, linked by modern expressways, scenic coastal railways, and three strategic domestic flights.

```
          21-DAY TRANS-VIETNAM ROUTE: HANOI TO PHU QUOC
========================================================================
Days 1-2:   Hanoi Old Quarter -> Dynastic Heritage, Street Food & Lakes
Days 3-5:   Ha Giang Karst Loop -> Dong Van Geopark & Ma Pi Leng Pass
Day 6:      Transit Day -> Return to Hanoi & Rest
Day 7:      Ninh Binh -> Trang An River Grottoes & Hang Mua Dragon Summit
Days 8-9:   Lan Ha Bay -> Luxury 2D1N Marine Karst Overnight Cruise
Days 10-11: Phong Nha Caves -> Paradise Cave & Son River Boat Exploration
Days 12-13: Imperial Hue -> Nguyen Dynasty Citadel & Royal Mausoleums
Days 14-16: Hoi An & Coast -> Lantern Alleys, Cham Valley & Beach Calm
Days 17-18: Ho Chi Minh City -> War Remnants, Cholon & French Boulevards
Day 19:     Mekong Delta -> Can Tho Riverside & Cai Rang Dawn Market
Days 20-21: Phu Quoc Island -> Tropical Beaches, Seafood & Departure
========================================================================
```

## Detailed 21-Day Field Itinerary

### Days 1–2: Hanoi Arrival, Street Culture, and Imperial Relics

Land at Noi Bai International Airport (HAN) and transfer 45 minutes into the historic Old Quarter.

- **Day 1**: Settle into a boutique heritage hotel along Hang Gai or Hang Be. Walk around Hoan Kiem Lake, visit the red-painted Huc Bridge and Ngoc Son Temple (30,000 VND). At 18:00, wander through the historic 36 Guild Streets. Dine on *pho bo tai lan* (60,000 VND) at Pho Thin (13 Lo Duc) or Pho Gia Truyen on Bat Dan. Finish with hot egg coffee (*ca phe trung*, 35,000 VND) at Cafe Giang.
- **Day 2**: Explore the 1070 **Temple of Literature** (*Van Mieu*, 70,000 VND) and its 82 doctoral turtle stelae. Tour **Hoa Lo Prison Historical Relic** (50,000 VND; audio guide 50,000 VND). Enjoy lunch at Bun Cha Ta (70,000 VND). Spend the afternoon at the **Vietnam Museum of Ethnology** (40,000 VND) to understand the northern highland ethnic groups you will encounter in Ha Giang. Watch an evening performance at Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre (100,000–200,000 VND).

### Days 3–5: The Northern Alpine Frontier: Ha Giang Loop

Board an evening VIP cabin sleeper bus (300,000–350,000 VND) or depart at 06:30 AM on Day 3 via luxury limousine van to Ha Giang City (300 km, 6 hours).

- **Day 3 (Ha Giang to Dong Van)**: Meet your experienced local driver ("Easy Rider") or mount your semi-automatic motorbike. Ride north along National Route 4C ("The Road to Happiness"). Ascend the winding Bac Sum Pass and marvel at Quan Ba Heaven Gate and the Twin Fairy Mountains. Pass through the pine forests of Yen Minh, conquer the 9 hairpin turns of Tham Ma Slope, and tour the 1902 **Vuong Family Palace** (*Dinh Vua Meo*, 30,000 VND) in Sa Phin. Sleep in the ancient stone quarter of Dong Van town.
- **Day 4 (Dong Van, Lung Cu & Ma Pi Leng Pass)**: Drive north to the **Lung Cu National Flag Tower** (40,000 VND) overlooking the Chinese border. In the afternoon, conquer the legendary **Ma Pi Leng Pass** (1,490 meters altitude), carved into vertical limestone walls above the jade-green Nho Que River. Descend by motorbike to the river wharf and take a wooden boat cruise through Tu San Canyon (120,000–150,000 VND), the deepest canyon in Southeast Asia. Sleep in Meo Vac or the pastoral valley of Du Gia.
- **Day 5 (Du Gia Waterfalls to Ha Giang City & Hanoi Return)**: Swim in the clear pools of Du Gia Waterfall. Complete the loop through scenic mountain terraces back to Ha Giang City. Board a comfortable limousine bus back to Hanoi, arriving around 22:00.

> [!WARNING]
> Self-driving motorbikes in Ha Giang requires a 1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit (IDP) with a Category A motorcycle endorsement. Vietnam does not recognize 1949 Geneva Convention IDPs (held by US, Australian, and Canadian citizens). Driving without a valid permit voids all international medical and evacuation insurance. Hiring an Easy Rider licensed driver ($180–$250 USD for 3 days including fuel and lodging) is the safest and most enjoyable strategy.

### Day 6: Rest, Laundry, and Contemporary Hanoi

Dedicate Day 6 to personal rest, laundry drop-off (25,000 VND/kg), and exploring Hanoi’s quieter corners:

- **10:00 – West Lake Stroll**: Visit the historic Tran Quoc Pagoda (6th century) on West Lake. Savor pour-over highland Arabica coffee at lakeside cafes along Quang Khanh.
- **14:00 – French Quarter Architecture Walk**: Wander south of the lake along Trang Tien Street. Photograph the Hanoi Opera House (1911) and browse art galleries along Trang Tien and Hang Bai.
- **19:00 – Northern Heritage Dinner**: Dine on grilled catfish with fresh dill and rice noodles (*cha ca*, 180,000 VND) at Cha Ca Thang Long (6B Duong Thanh).

### Day 7: The Inland Karst Valley: Ninh Binh

Depart Hanoi at 07:30 AM in a 9-seat limousine van (150,000–220,000 VND) along the CT01 Expressway to Ninh Binh (95 km, 90 minutes).

- **09:30 – Trang An Scenic Landscape Complex**: Board a 4-person wooden sampan rowed by local boatwomen along **Route 2** (250,000 VND per passenger). Drift through four limestone caves and visit the historic King Le royal guard temple.
- **13:00 – Local Mountain Goat Lunch**: Taste roasted goat meat with lemongrass and golden crispy burnt rice cakes (*com chay*, 150,000 VND).
- **15:30 – Hang Mua Summit Climb**: Climb the 486 carved limestone stairs of Ngoa Long Mountain (100,000 VND). Enjoy panoramic sunset views over the Ngo Dong River.
- **Evening Overnight**: Stay in a rustic eco-resort in Tam Coc or Trang An (600,000–1,200,000 VND).

### Days 8–9: Marine Limestone Karst: Lan Ha Bay Luxury Cruise

Transfer directly from Ninh Binh at 07:30 AM eastward to Got Pier or Tuan Chau Harbor (2.5 hours, 250,000 VND).

- **Day 8**: Board your 5-star cruise ship into **Lan Ha Bay** (3,500,000–7,500,000 VND per cabin for 2 days/1 night all-inclusive). Paddle sea kayaks through the tidal arch of Dark and Bright Cave into calm saltwater lagoons. Watch twilight settle over the limestone pillars from the top deck. Savor an elegant multi-course seafood dinner.
- **Day 9**: Practice morning Tai Chi at sunrise. Explore limestone grottos on Cat Ba Island or cycle through Viet Hai village. Enjoy brunch while cruising back to the harbor, disembarking at 11:30 AM. Transfer back to Hanoi.
- **Overnight Rail Transit**: Board the comfortable overnight sleeper train **SE1** departing Hanoi Station at 22:15, bound for Dong Hoi (arriving at 08:00 AM). Book a 4-berth air-conditioned soft sleeper compartment (700,000–900,000 VND).

### Days 10–11: The Subterranean Kingdom: Phong Nha - Kẻ Bàng

Arrive at Dong Hoi Railway Station at 08:00 AM. Meet your driver for the 45-minute transfer inland to Phong Nha village (400,000 VND taxi or 35,000 VND on public bus B4).

- **Day 10 (Paradise Cave & Dark Cave)**: Tour **Paradise Cave** (*Dong Thien Duong*, 270,000 VND entrance including electric golf buggy). Walk along 1 kilometer of illuminated wooden boardwalks beneath soaring 70-meter-high cathedral ceilings adorned with ancient calcite stalactites. In the afternoon, visit **Dark Cave** (*Hang Toi*, 450,000 VND all-inclusive package) for a 400-meter zipline, headlamp spelunking through therapeutic mineral mud pools, and river kayaking.
- **Day 11 (Phong Nha Cave & Botanic Trails)**: Board a motorized dragon boat from Son Trach tourism pier along the Son River to **Phong Nha Cave** (150,000 VND entrance + 550,000 VND boat fee shared among passengers). The boat cuts its motor to glide silently 600 meters through subterranean river grottos. Dine on grilled wild pepper chicken (*ga doi*) at the legendary Duck Stop. 

### Days 12–13: Imperial Dynasties: Hue

Take an early morning private car (4 hours, 1,800,000–2,200,000 VND) or regional train south from Dong Hoi across the 17th Parallel (historic DMZ and Hien Luong Bridge) to Hue.

- **Day 12**: Tour the sprawling **Imperial City** (*Dai Noi*, 200,000 VND), walking through the restored Kien Trung Palace and the secluded courtyards of the Forbidden Purple City. Stroll along the Perfume River at dusk. Dine on imperial steamed rice dishes (*banh beo, banh nam, banh loc*, 50,000–80,000 VND) at Quan Hanh.
- **Day 13**: Visit the monumental hillside **Tomb of Emperor Khai Dinh** (150,000 VND) with its dramatic porcelain mosaic murals. Continue to the serene pine-shaded **Tomb of Emperor Tu Duc** (150,000 VND). Admire the historic seven-story Thien Mu Pagoda (1601). In the evening, enjoy a bowl of authentic spicy beef noodle soup (*bun bo Hue*, 45,000 VND) at Bun Bo Ba Tuyet.

### Days 14–16: Maritime Heritage & Coastal Relaxation: Hoi An

Travel south from Hue to Da Nang aboard the dedicated **"Central Heritage Connection"** tourist train HD1 (110,000–180,000 VND). The train hugs the coastal cliffs of the Hai Van Pass, providing spectacular ocean vistas. Transfer 30 minutes south from Da Nang to Hoi An.

- **Day 14 (Ancient Port Discovery)**: Purchase the official five-monument heritage pass (120,000 VND). Visit the restored Japanese Covered Bridge (*Chua Cau*), the centuries-old Tan Ky family house, and the Fujian Assembly Hall. Commission custom tailored shirts or suits at Bebe Tailor or Yaly Couture. In the evening, watch silk lanterns illuminate the Thu Bon River.
- **Day 15 (My Son Hindu Sanctuary & Craft Villages)**: Take an early 07:30 AM excursion to the forest valley of **My Son Sanctuary** (150,000 VND), touring 4th-to-13th-century red-brick Cham towers honoring Shiva. In the afternoon, cycle through the organic herb gardens of Tra Que village. Dine on *cao lau* noodles and crispy white rose dumplings.
- **Day 16 (An Bang Beach Relaxation & Cooking School)**: Take a leisurely bicycle ride to An Bang Beach for sunbathing, fresh coconut juice, and grilled tiger prawns under palm-thatch umbrellas. Pick up your pressed, finished tailored garments in the late afternoon.

### Days 17–18: Southern Commercial Metropole: Ho Chi Minh City

Transfer to Da Nang Airport (DAD) for a swift 85-minute flight south to Ho Chi Minh City (SGN) (850,000–1,500,000 VND).

- **Day 17 (Colonial Boulevards & War Memorials)**: Check into your hotel in District 1 or District 3. Walk along Dong Khoi Street past the Saigon Opera House to the 1891 Central Post Office designed with wrought-iron arches. Visit the sobering **War Remnants Museum** (60,000 VND) on Vo Van Tan Street. Dine on charcoal-grilled pork chops with broken rice (*com tam suon*, 65,000 VND) at Com Tam Ba Ghiền.
- **Day 18 (Authentic Cu Chi Tunnels & Cholon)**: Take a morning trip to the **Ben Duoc Tunnels** (70 km northwest, 125,000 VND), avoiding the commercialized tourist buses of Ben Dinh to crawl through authentic subterranean command passages. In the afternoon, explore **Cholon (Chinatown)** in District 5, admiring the aromatic coils of incense inside the 1760 Ba Thien Hau Pagoda. Watch the sunset from the Saigon Waterbus (15,000 VND).

### Day 19: Deep Mekong Riverine Culture: Can Tho

Depart Ho Chi Minh City at 07:30 AM in a luxury limousine coach along the CT01 Expressway to Can Tho (170 km, 3.5 hours, 180,000–220,000 VND).

- **Afternoon**: Check into a tranquil riverside boutique resort along the Hau River. Cross by small passenger ferry to Son Islet to cycle through tranquil rambutan and pomelo orchards.
- **Evening**: Stroll along Ninh Kieu Wharf. Dine on grilled snakehead fish in rice paper (*ca loc nuong trui*, 180,000 VND) and sweet river fish hot pot with yellow sesbania flowers (*canh chua ca linh bong dien dien*).

### Days 20–21: Island Finale: Phu Quoc Beaches & Departure

Board an early morning wooden sampan at 05:30 AM from Ninh Kieu Wharf (400,000–500,000 VND private boat) to experience **Cai Rang Floating Market** at its vibrant dawn peak. Eat hot noodle soup and drink iced coffee directly on the water.

- **Midday Flight to Phu Quoc**: Return to the wharf, check out, and take a short 15-minute taxi to Can Tho Airport (VCA) for a 50-minute direct flight to Phu Quoc International Airport (PQC) (750,000–1,200,000 VND).
- **Day 20 (White Sands & Sunset Cocktails)**: Settle into a beachfront resort along Bai Truong (Long Beach) or Bai Khem. Swim in the calm, crystalline waters of the Gulf of Thailand. Dine on fresh grilled sea urchins (*nhum bien*) and herring salad (*goi ca trich*) at the sunset seaside night market.
- **Day 21 (Snorkeling or Rest & Departure)**: Spend your final morning swimming or taking an island canoe excursion to the An Thoi archipelago. Transfer to Phu Quoc International Airport (PQC) for your outbound international flight or connection back through Hanoi or Saigon.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is twenty-one days too long for a single trip to Vietnam?
Not at all. Given Vietnam's elongated 1,650-kilometer geography and immense regional variety, three weeks allows you to absorb the distinct rhythms of the country without physical exhaustion. You enjoy two- to three-night stays in each base, giving you time to do laundry, swim at the beach, and wander back alleys without a frantic schedule.

### Can I do this entire route by train instead of flying?
While the coastal railway between Hanoi, Hue, and Da Nang is highly recommended, taking the train across the entire length of Vietnam consumes over 34 hours of travel time. Using short domestic flights for the long hauls (Da Nang to Saigon, and Can Tho to Phu Quoc) saves two daylight days, which are much better spent kayaking in Lan Ha Bay or exploring caves in Phong Nha.

### What is the visa policy for a 21-day trip?
Citizens of many European and Asian countries qualify for a 45-day single or multi-entry visa exemption. Travelers from countries requiring visas (including the United States, Canada, and Australia) can easily obtain a 90-day electronic visa (e-Visa) through Vietnam's official immigration portal for $25 USD. Always apply at least two weeks before departure.
