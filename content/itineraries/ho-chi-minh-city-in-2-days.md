# Ho Chi Minh City in 2 Days (2026): 48-Hour Itinerary

Landing in Vietnam’s southern economic capital introduces an immediate shift in scale and velocity. Eight million motorbikes surge down wide tree-shaded avenues, glass skyscrapers tower above 19th-century French post offices, and incense smoke drifts through centuries-old Cantonese guild halls in Cholon. Because Greater Saigon sprawls across 2,095 square kilometers, unstructured sightseeing quickly devolves into hours lost inside air-conditioned taxis during tropical rush hours.

To experience the best of Ho Chi Minh City within 48 hours, you must organize your schedule around strict district clustering. Dedicating Day 1 to the walkable colonial and wartime monuments of District 1 and District 3, and reserving Day 2 for an early morning excursion to the authentic Ben Duoc underground tunnels followed by afternoon exploration in District 5’s Chinatown, eliminates cross-town backtracking while balancing solemn history with street-level culinary energy.

## Day 1: District 1 Colonial Monuments, Wartime History, and the Saigon River

### 08:00 – Independence Palace (Reunification Palace)

Begin at 08:00 AM at the **Independence Palace** (*Dinh Doc Lap*, 135 Nam Ky Khoi Nghia, entrance 40,000 VND for main palace or 65,000 VND including the historical exhibition). Designed by Paris-trained Vietnamese architect Ngo Viet Thu and completed in 1966 on the site of the former French Norodom Palace, this mid-century modernist landmark served as the residence and command center of the President of South Vietnam until April 30, 1975, when North Vietnamese Tank 390 crashed through its wrought-iron front gates.

Walk through the opulent Cabinet Room, the President's receiving chambers, and the rooftop helipad where two bomb impact circles from a 1975 air raid are marked in paint. Descend into the reinforced concrete **Underground Command Bunker**, where original map rooms, rotary military telephones, and shortwave radio transmitters remain preserved exactly as they stood in 1975.

### 10:30 – Saigon Central Post Office, Book Street, and Dong Khoi

Walk five minutes northeast through the shaded lawns of 30/4 Park to Paris Commune Square. While the red Marseille-brick **Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon** (1880) remains enclosed in exterior scaffolding for roof and bell-tower restoration through 2027, you can admire its twin 58-meter spires from the square before entering the **Saigon Central Post Office** (*Buu Dien Trung Tam*, 2 Cong Xa Paris, free admission).

Constructed between 1886 and 1891 under French architect Alfred Foulhoux, the post office’s grand concourse features green wrought-iron structural arches, original wooden telephone booths, and two hand-painted 1892 fresco maps above the entrance: *Saigon et ses environs* and *Lignes télégraphiques du Sud Vietnam et du Cambodge*. Stroll alongside the cathedral down **Nguyen Van Binh Book Street**, a car-free pedestrian lane lined with independent Vietnamese publishers and shaded outdoor book cafes.

### 12:30 – Charcoal-Grilled Broken Rice and Phin Coffee

Experience Saigon’s quintessential working-class lunch: *com tam* (broken rice). Take a 10-minute Grab ride to **Com Tam Ba Ghien** (84 Dang Van Ngu, Phu Nhuan—the only broken rice eatery in Vietnam awarded a Michelin Bib Gourmand) or dine centrally at **Com Tam Moc** (85 Ly Tu Trong, District 1). Order *com tam suon bi cha* (65,000–95,000 VND): fractured jasmine rice grains topped with a thick, caramelized pork chop grilled over glowing charcoal, shredded pork skin tossed in roasted rice powder (*bi*), steamed egg-and-pork meatloaf (*cha trung*), pickled radish, and sweet chili-garlic fish sauce.

After lunch, stop at **The Workshop** (27 Ngo Duc Ke, 3rd floor of a 1920s colonial corner building) for a specialty Da Lat Arabica pour-over (75,000 VND), or sit on a low stool in Tao Dan Park for traditional southern iced milk coffee (*ca phe sua da*, 25,000 VND).

### 14:00 – War Remnants Museum

Head to District 3 to visit the **War Remnants Museum** (*Bao Tang Chung Tich Chien Tranh*, 28 Vo Van Tan Street, entrance 60,000 VND, open daily 07:30–17:30 without a midday break). Allow two full hours to walk through the three-story facility.

The outdoor courtyard displays American military hardware left behind in 1975, including a Bell UH-1 "Huey" helicopter, an F-5A fighter jet, a Chinook CH-47 transport helicopter, and a 6,800-kilogram BLU-82 "Daisy Cutter" bomb. Inside, the second-floor gallery houses **Requiem**, an internationally acclaimed photojournalism exhibition curated by Horst Faas and Tim Page featuring photographs taken by 134 journalists from 11 countries who died covering the Indochina conflicts, including Larry Burrows and Robert Capa. Subsequent halls document the long-term ecological and human toll of dioxin defoliants (Agent Orange) and the French-era "Tiger Cages" (*Chuong Cop*) replicated from Con Dao Island prison.

### 17:00 – Saigon Waterbus Sunset and Banh Xeo Dinner

Avoid expensive tourist dinner boats by walking to **Bach Dang Wharf** (10B Ton Duc Thang) and boarding the yellow **Saigon Waterbus** (15,000 VND each way; book your return seat at the ticket kiosk before boarding). The 30-minute river commute glides past the Ba Son cable-stayed bridge and the 461-meter **Landmark 81** supertall tower as the tropical sky turns amber.

At 19:30, dine at **Banh Xeo 46A** (46A Dinh Cong Trang, District 3, 80,000–120,000 VND). Watch cooks ladle rice-flour and turmeric batter into flaring wok pans over wood fires, tossing in whole tiger prawns, fatty pork belly, and mung bean sprouts. Wrap wedges of the lacy, crisp pancake inside fresh mustard greens (*cai be xanh*) and lettuce with mint and perilla, dipping each roll into sweet-sour *nuoc mam*. End your night with a Passion Fruit Wheat Ale at **Pasteur Street Brewing Company** (144 Pasteur Street).

### 07:30 – Morning Excursion to Ben Duoc Underground Tunnels

Depart District 1 at 07:30 AM for the **Cu Chi Tunnels**—a 250-kilometer subterranean network of hand-dug command bunkers, field hospitals, smokeless Hoang Cam kitchens, and supply passages excavated out of iron-hard laterite clay between 1948 and 1968.

> [!WARNING]
> **Ben Duoc vs. Ben Dinh Crucial Choice**: Over 90 percent of budget tour buses go to **Ben Dinh** (50 km from Saigon), where the tunnels have been artificially widened with concrete for Western tourists and long queues form at every trapdoor. Insist on visiting **Ben Duoc** (70 km from Saigon, 125,000 VND entrance). Ben Duoc sits in dense secondary forest, receives a fraction of the crowds, preserves original multi-level clay tunnels, and features the monumental **Ben Duoc Memorial Temple**. You can reach Ben Duoc via private car (900,000–1,200,000 VND round-trip), small-group minivan tour, or public Bus 13 from 23/9 Park to Cu Chi Station (10,000 VND) followed by Bus 79 directly to Ben Duoc gate (7,000 VND).

Spend two hours walking the forest trails with a park guide. Inspect concealed punji-stake traps, test-fit the tiny wooden secret entrance doors covered in dried leaves, crawl a 20-to-50-meter section of original first-level tunnel, and taste steamed cassava root (*khoai mi*) dipped in crushed roasted peanuts and sesame salt.

### 12:30 – Cholon Arrival and Cantonese Roast Duck Lunch

Return from Cu Chi directly to **District 5 (Cholon)**, the historic Chinese quarter established in 1778 by ethnic Chinese merchants from Bien Hoa. Have your driver drop you along Nguyen Trai or Tran Hung Dao Street for lunch at a traditional Teochew or Cantonese roast house, sampling crispy-skin roast duck noodle soup (*mi vit tiem*, 75,000–95,000 VND) or freshly steamed shrimp har gow dumplings.

### 14:00 – Ba Thien Hau Pagoda and Herbal Medicine Alleys

Walk to **Ba Thien Hau Pagoda** (710 Nguyen Trai Street, free admission, open 06:00–17:30). Built around 1760 by Cantonese maritime merchants, the temple honors Mazu (*Thien Hau*), the Taoist protector of sailors. Look up at the open central courtyard roof rimmed with intricate 19th-century ceramic dioramas from Foshan kilns, while dozens of meter-wide conical incense coils burn slowly overhead, filtering shafts of afternoon sunlight through fragrant smoke.

From the temple, walk south down **Hai Thuong Lan Ong Street**, the historic center of traditional Eastern medicine (*Thuoc Bac*). Shopfronts are lined from floor to ceiling with wooden apothecary drawers and glass jars holding dried ginseng roots, star anise, cinnamon bark from Quang Nam, and dried lotus seeds.

### 15:30 – Binh Tay Wholesale Market and Rooftop Farewell

Continue to **Binh Tay Market** (57A Thap Muoi Street, free admission). Financed in 1928 by Cantonese philanthropist Quach Dam, this grand yellow market hall merges French structural engineering with upturned Chinese dragon-tile roofs and a central clock tower surrounding an airy garden courtyard. Unlike tourist-oriented Ben Thanh Market, Binh Tay serves local wholesale merchants trading dried seafood, candied ginger, cookware, and textiles at fixed wholesale prices.

Take a Grab car back to District 1 (65,000 VND) or ride **Metro Line 1** into Ben Thanh Station. At 17:30, ascend to the 9th-floor **Saigon Saigon Rooftop Bar** at the historic Caravelle Hotel (19 Lam Son Square, opened Christmas Eve 1959)—where foreign war correspondents watched flares over the river during the 1960s—for a sunset drink overlooking the Opera House before an upscale farewell dinner at **Anan Saigon** (Vietnam’s first Michelin-starred restaurant, located inside the vibrant Ton That Dam wet market).

## Step-by-Step Airport & Street Security Protocols

1. **Tan Son Nhat Airport (SGN) to District 1 Transfers**: SGN sits just 7 kilometers northwest of District 1. For budget transit, walk out to the bus lane in front of International Terminal 2 and board **Airport Bus 109** (15,000–20,000 VND, runs 05:30–23:45 directly to 23/9 Park near Ben Thanh Market) or local **Bus 152** (5,000 VND + 5,000 VND per large suitcase). If booking a **GrabCar** (120,000–180,000 VND), follow the app instructions to the designated rideshare pickup columns (Columns B18–B20 at the International Terminal or the 3rd-floor deck of the TCP parking garage at the Domestic Terminal); never accept rides from unbadged drivers soliciting inside the arrivals hall, who frequently rig meters or demand 500,000 VND flat fares.
2. **Avoiding Street Scams Around Ben Thanh Market**: Around Ben Thanh Market and 23/9 Park, two classic tourist traps persist: aggressive **shoe-shiners** who point at your sneakers and begin gluing soles without permission before demanding 300,000 VND, and **roving coconut vendors** who place their bamboo carrying pole on your shoulder for a photo and then demand 150,000 VND per coconut. Politely wave your hand and keep walking without breaking stride.
3. **Phone Snatch Prevention**: While violent crime in Ho Chi Minh City is exceptionally rare, opportunistic motorbike drive-by phone snatching occurs along busy curbs in District 1 (especially Pham Ngu Lao, Bui Vien, and Dong Khoi). Always step two meters back from the roadway curb toward a building wall when checking Google Maps or ordering a Grab ride.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I do a Cu Chi Tunnels half-day trip or a Mekong Delta day trip if I only have two days in Ho Chi Minh City?
With only 48 hours in the city, choose the **morning half-day trip to Ben Duoc (Cu Chi Tunnels)**. You depart at 07:30 AM and return to Cholon by 12:30 PM, leaving your entire second afternoon free to explore District 5's temples and markets. A Mekong Delta day trip to My Tho or Ben Tre consumes 8.5 to 9.5 hours (07:30–17:00), wiping out your entire second day in the city.

### Is Bui Vien Walking Street worth visiting at night?
Bui Vien Street in Pham Ngu Lao (District 1) is Saigon’s backpacker strip—a neon-lit corridor of loud bass-heavy bars, cheap plastic-stool beer stalls, and street performers. While some travelers enjoy a quick 15-minute walk through the chaos out of curiosity, those seeking refined cocktails, craft beer, or authentic Vietnamese night dining will have a far better evening along Pasteur Street, Ngo Duc Ke, or in District 3.

### What is the best time of year to visit Ho Chi Minh City?
Ho Chi Minh City maintains warm tropical temperatures (28°C–34°C) year-round. The **dry season runs from December to April**, offering sunny skies and lower humidity—with December and January providing the most comfortable walking weather (24°C–31°C). During the wet season (May to November), carry a compact umbrella for predictable 45-minute tropical downpours between 15:00 and 17:00, and schedule indoor visits (like the War Remnants Museum or Independence Palace) during the mid-afternoon hours.
