# 10 Days in Vietnam: The Strategic First-Timer Itinerary

Ten days represents the golden threshold for international travel in Vietnam. Unlike a rushed seven-day journey, a ten-day window provides sufficient breathing room to connect Vietnam's three distinct geographic regions—the dynastic North, the lantern-lit Central heritage coast, and the tropical Southern delta—without reducing your vacation to an endurance trial.

To succeed across ten days, you must maintain ruthless discipline regarding domestic transfers. Trying to include more than three geographic base camps will compromise your trip. By basing yourself in Hanoi, Hoi An, and Ho Chi Minh City, linked by two efficient internal flights, you experience Vietnam’s complete cultural spectrum while keeping your transit footprint below 12 percent of total waking hours.

```
       10-DAY HIGHLIGHTS CORRIDOR: HANOI -> HOI AN -> HO CHI MINH CITY
========================================================================
Days 1-2: Northern Capital -> Hanoi Old Quarter & Dynastic Shrines
Days 3-4: Marine Karst -> Lan Ha / Ha Long Bay 2D1N Overnight Cruise
Day 5:    Central Transit -> Morning Flight HAN to DAD -> Hoi An Lanterns
Days 6-7: Heritage & Coast -> My Son Sanctuary, An Bang Beach & Tailoring
Day 8:    Southern Transit -> Flight DAD to SGN -> War Relics & French Core
Day 9:    Delta or Tunnels -> Mekong Riverine Excursion or Cu Chi Complex
Day 10:   Cholon Heritage -> Binh Tay Market, Coffee Tasting & Departure
========================================================================
```

## Strategic Route Architecture: The North-to-South Logic

Travelers frequently debate whether to travel from north to south (Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City) or from south to north. For first-time visitors, the **North-to-South direction** delivers a significantly more coherent narrative arc:

1. **Chronological cultural progression**: Starting in Hanoi immerses you in Vietnam’s 1,000-year dynastic foundations, Confucian scholarly traditions, and French colonial architecture. Central Vietnam transitions into the 18th-century mercantile history of the Cham and maritime trade routes in Hoi An. The journey culminates in Ho Chi Minh City, revealing 20th-century geopolitical conflict, post-war transformation, and contemporary economic ambition.
2. **Climatic comfort and energy management**: Northern Vietnam requires substantial walking through narrow, dense pavements and historic sites. Tackling the physical demands of Hanoi and limestone kayaking early in the trip—when energy levels are highest—allows you to unwind along Hoi An's beaches at the midway point before experiencing the high-octane pace of Saigon.
3. **Culinary evolution**: Your palate starts with the subtle, savory, and herb-forward broths of northern cooking, shifts to the fiery chilies and lemongrass pastes of central cuisine, and finishes with the sweet coconut curries and abundant tropical produce of the southern delta.

> [!TIP]
> When booking internal flights between Hanoi, Da Nang, and Ho Chi Minh City, schedule departures between 10:00 AM and 13:00 PM. This avoids peak morning rush-hour traffic on airport access expressways and aligns seamlessly with standard hotel checkout (11:00 or 12:00) and check-in (14:00) policies, eliminating dead luggage-holding time.

## Detailed 10-Day Day-by-Day Itinerary

### Day 1: Arrival in Hanoi and the Historic 36 Guild Streets

Touch down at Noi Bai International Airport (HAN). Collect local currency and activate cellular connectivity. Take a pre-booked airport car (300,000–350,000 VND) across the Red River into the Old Quarter.

Check into your boutique hotel on Hang Gai, Hang Be, or Ma May. Spend your first afternoon strolling around Hoan Kiem Lake. Cross the scarlet Huc Bridge to visit Ngoc Son Temple (30,000 VND entrance). 

By 17:30, wander through the historic 36 Guild Streets. Notice how streets still cluster around specialized trades: Hang Bac (Silverware), Hang Ma (Paper Offerings), and Hang Chieu (Mats). Dine at Bun Cha Ta (21 Nguyen Huu Huan) for grilled pork patties in fish sauce broth with fresh rice vermicelli (70,000 VND). Finish the evening with an authentic hot egg coffee (35,000 VND) at Cafe Giang, created in 1946 during wartime dairy shortages.

### Day 2: Hanoi’s Intellectual Roots and Colonial Relics

Dedicate your morning to Vietnam's dynastic scholarship and modern revolutionary history:

- **08:30 – Temple of Literature (*Van Mieu*)**: Walk through the five tranquil courtyards of Vietnam's first national university, founded in 1070. Pay respects at the Well of Heavenly Clarity and inspect the 82 stone turtle stelae engraved with doctoral names dating back to 1442 (entrance 70,000 VND).
- **11:00 – Hoa Lo Prison Memorial**: Explore the preserved cellblocks of this 1896 French penitentiary, where anticolonial leaders were held in irons and American aviators were later imprisoned (entrance 50,000 VND; audio guide strongly recommended at 50,000 VND).
- **13:00 – Authentic Northern Noodle Lunch**: Stop for *pho bo* at Pho Thin (13 Lo Duc) for stir-fried beef with green scallions in rich bone broth (75,000 VND).
- **15:00 – West Lake & Tran Quoc Pagoda**: Catch a short Grab taxi to West Lake (*Tay Ho*) and visit Tran Quoc Pagoda, the oldest Buddhist shrine in Hanoi, established in the 6th century during the reign of Emperor Ly Nam De (free entry, modest dress required covering shoulders and knees).
- **19:00 – Thang Long Water Puppet Performance**: Conclude your evening at the theater beside Hoan Kiem Lake (tickets 100,000–200,000 VND).

### Day 3: Hai Phong Expressway to Lan Ha Bay Marine Karst

Check out of your Hanoi hotel, leaving bulky heavy bags with the concierge if returning north, or bringing all luggage aboard your vessel. Board a comfortable shuttle van at 08:00 AM heading east along the CT04 Expressway to the Gulf of Tonkin (2 hours).

- **12:00 – Marine Embarkation**: Board your 4-star or 5-star cruise ship at Got Pier or Tuan Chau Harbor (typical cost: 3,400,000–7,000,000 VND per cabin for 2 days/1 night all-inclusive). Sail past colossal limestone towers rising straight from emerald waters.
- **14:30 – Kayaking Dark and Bright Cave**: Take a two-person sea kayak through sea-eroded arches into secluded tidal lagoons inhabited by golden-headed langurs.
- **17:30 – Twilight on the Top Deck**: Enjoy sunset drinks while anchored in tranquil waters far from commercial freighter lanes.
- **19:30 – Fresh Marine Seafood Dinner**: Dine on grilled tiger prawns, steamed squid, and local oysters, followed by evening squid fishing from the boat's stern.

### Day 4: Morning Mist, Disembarkation, and Flight to Central Vietnam

Wake early at 06:15 AM for Tai Chi on the sun deck as morning light breaks through the karst towers.

- **07:30 – Morning Inlet Exploration**: Visit Cat Ba Island's limestone caverns (such as Trung Trang Cave) or cycle along Viet Hai village trails.
- **10:00 – Early Lunch & Harbor Return**: Enjoy an early buffet lunch while sailing back toward port. Disembark by 11:30 AM.
- **12:00 – Direct Transit to Noi Bai Airport (HAN)**: Have your transfer van take you directly to Hanoi Airport (2 hours via the express ring road), bypassing central city traffic.
- **16:00 – Flight to Da Nang (DAD)**: Board an 80-minute domestic flight (Vietnam Airlines or Vietjet, 900,000–1,500,000 VND).
- **18:30 – Arrival in Hoi An**: Collect your bags and take a 45-minute private car transfer (300,000–350,000 VND) south to Hoi An Ancient Town. Check into a riverside boutique hotel in Cam Chau or along the Thu Bon River. Dine on *cao lau* noodles made with lye water from ancient Cham wells (40,000–55,000 VND).

### Day 5: Hoi An Ancient Town and Lantern-Lit Alleys

Spend a full day exploring this exceptionally preserved 16th-to-18th-century international trading port:

- **09:00 – Heritage Walking Tour**: Purchase an official entrance ticket (120,000 VND), which grants entry to five heritage monuments. Tour the Japanese Covered Bridge (*Chua Cau*, fully restored in late 2024), the 200-year-old wooden Tan Ky Merchant House, and the elaborately ornamented Fujian Assembly Hall.
- **12:00 – Custom Tailoring Fittings**: Hoi An is internationally famous for rapid bespoke tailoring. Visit reputable establishments such as Bebe Tailor or Yaly Couture on Tran Phu Street to choose fabrics and be measured for custom suits, linen shirts, or dresses (jackets delivered within 24 to 36 hours).
- **13:00 – Banh Mi Pilgrimage**: Grab lunch at Banh Mi Phuong or Madam Khanh "The Banh Mi Queen" (35,000–45,000 VND) for crusty baguettes layered with pate, braised pork, pickles, cilantro, and chili sauce.
- **15:30 – Bicycle Ride to An Bang Beach**: Rent a bicycle from your hotel (20,000–40,000 VND or complimentary) and pedal 4.5 kilometers through green rice paddies to An Bang Beach. Relax at a beachfront lounge under thatched umbrellas.
- **19:30 – Thu Bon River Wooden Boat Ride**: Return to the old town after dark to see thousands of silk lanterns glowing. Board a licensed wooden boat at Hoai River wharf (150,000 VND for 1–3 passengers for a 20-minute cruise).

### Day 6: Cham Ruins of My Son and Craft Villages

Venture into the surrounding countryside to understand Central Vietnam's deep history:

- **07:30 – My Son Sanctuary Early Tour**: Drive 45 kilometers inland to the sacred valley of My Son (150,000 VND entrance). Built between the 4th and 14th centuries by the kings of Champa, these red-brick Hindu tower-temples honoring Shiva stand in dramatic jungle clearings beneath Cat's Tooth Mountain. Arriving early avoids midday heat and coach tours.
- **12:30 – Local Lunch in Nam Phuoc**: Dine on *mi Quang* noodles (40,000 VND), featuring wide rice noodles stained with turmeric, shrimp, pork, crushed peanuts, and toasted rice crackers in a rich broth.
- **14:30 – Tra Que Organic Herb Village**: Cycle to Tra Que village, where local farming families have cultivated aromatic herbs for three centuries using organic algae from the lagoon instead of chemical fertilizers.
- **17:00 – Final Tailoring Fitting**: Return to your tailor for final adjustments and pick up your pressed garments.
- **20:00 – Hoi An Memories Show (Optional)**: Watch this spectacular outdoor theatrical production staged on an island in the Thu Bon River with over 500 performers (tickets 600,000–1,200,000 VND).

### Day 7: Transit to Ho Chi Minh City and the French Colonial Core

Check out of Hoi An and transfer back to Da Nang International Airport (DAD) for your domestic flight to Ho Chi Minh City (SGN).

- **10:00 – Flight to Ho Chi Minh City**: Board an 85-minute direct flight to Tan Son Nhat Airport.
- **12:30 – Arrival in District 1**: Take a verified Grab car or Vinasun taxi (140,000–180,000 VND) to District 1 or District 3. The contrast with quiet Hoi An is immediate: Saigon pulses with eight million motorbikes, towering glass high-rises, and energetic commercial activity.
- **14:00 – Colonial Heritage Walking Circuit**: Walk along Dong Khoi Street. Admire the exterior of Notre-Dame Cathedral (under scaffolded restoration through 2027) and explore the 1891 Saigon Central Post Office, designed with wrought-iron vaulted ceilings engineered by Gustave Eiffel's company (free entry).
- **16:00 – War Remnants Museum**: Visit this sobering museum on Vo Van Tan Street (60,000 VND entrance). The graphic photojournalism galleries—including the "Requiem" collection dedicated to photographers killed on both sides—provide essential context for modern Vietnam's historical resilience.
- **19:30 – Broken Rice Dinner**: Feast on *com tam suon nuong* (broken rice with charcoal-marinated pork chops, steamed egg meatloaf, and crispy pork skin, 50,000–85,000 VND) at Com Tam Ba Ghiền or Com Tam Moc.

### Day 8: Cu Chi Tunnels Historical Exploration

Dedicate your eighth morning to the underground battlefield systems northwest of the city:

- **07:30 – Departure to Ben Duoc Tunnels**: Book a small-group tour or private vehicle to **Ben Duoc Tunnels** (70 km northwest, 1.5 to 2 hours). While most tour buses visit the widened, crowded Ben Dinh site, Ben Duoc offers a vastly more authentic and less commercialized perspective with original multi-level tunnel networks (125,000 VND entrance).
- **09:30 – Crawling the Underground Network**: Descend into the claustrophobic earthen passages used by National Liberation Front soldiers during the American War. Learn about camouflage trapdoors, ventilation systems disguised as termite mounds, and hospital bunkers. Sample boiled tapioca root dipped in salted peanut sugar, the staple wartime diet.
- **13:30 – Return to Saigon**: Arrive back in District 1 in early afternoon.
- **16:00 – Bitexco Skydeck or Saigon Waterbus**: Watch the sunset over the winding Saigon River either from the Bitexco Financial Tower observation deck (240,000 VND) or by riding the public Saigon Waterbus from Bach Dang Wharf to Binh An and back (15,000 VND each way).
- **20:00 – Craft Beer Exploration**: Discover Vietnam’s thriving craft brewing movement at Pasteur Street Brewing (144 Pasteur) or Heart of Darkness on Ly Tu Trong.

### Day 9: Mekong Delta Riverine Excursion

Experience the fertile waterways and agricultural heartland of the lower Mekong Basin:

- **07:30 – Day Trip to Ben Tre**: Travel 85 kilometers southwest along the CT01 Expressway into Ben Tre province, the coconut capital of the Mekong Delta (1.5 to 2 hours).
- **09:30 – River Cruising & Narrow Canals**: Board a private motorized wooden boat along the Ham Luong River. Transition into traditional two-paddle wooden sampans (*xuong ba la*) to glide through tranquil canals shaded by overhanging water coconut palms (*dua nuoc*).
- **12:00 – Delta Countryside Lunch**: Savor classic southern river cuisine at a local orchard: deep-fried elephant ear fish (*ca tai tuong chien xu*) served upright on wooden stands, rolled tableside in rice paper with fresh herbs and pineapple dipping sauce.
- **14:00 – Cottage Industries**: Visit small family workshops producing warm handmade coconut candy, woven sedge sleeping mats, and wild honey tea.
- **17:00 – Return to Ho Chi Minh City**: Arrive back in the city for an evening at leisure.

### Day 10: Cholon Chinese Heritage and Departure

Spend your final morning discovering Saigon's cultural layers before heading to the airport:

- **08:30 – Cholon (Chinatown) Heritage**: Head west to District 5. Visit the 1760 **Ba Thien Hau Pagoda**, dedicated to Mazu, goddess of the sea. Admire the massive coils of hanging incense suspended from the carved porcelain roof beams (free entry).
- **10:30 – Binh Tây Wholesale Market**: Walk through this grand 1928 French-Chinese market hall designed by businessman Quach Dam, browsing stalls piled high with dried sea cucumbers, mountain mushrooms, and southern spices.
- **12:30 – Specialty Vietnamese Coffee**: Experience modern third-wave Vietnamese Robusta and Arabica pour-overs at The Workshop on Ngo Duc Ke.
- **14:00 – Airport Transfer**: Check out and take a taxi to Tan Son Nhat International Airport (SGN) at least 3.5 hours before international flights to navigate notoriously congested airport access corridors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is ten days enough to see Vietnam properly?
Ten days is enough to experience Vietnam's three core cultural regions without exhaustion, provided you restrict your bases to Hanoi, Hoi An, and Ho Chi Minh City. Trying to add Sapa, Phong Nha, or Phu Quoc into this same ten-day window will turn your vacation into an exhausting series of baggage checks and airport departures.

### Should I take overnight sleeper trains instead of flying?
While sleeper trains like the Reunification Express (SE1/SE3) are legendary cultural experiences, they require 15 to 17 hours to cover Hanoi to Da Nang, or 17 to 20 hours from Da Nang to Saigon. On a ten-day holiday, losing an entire daylight day to train travel or arriving exhausted from overnight rail noise is an inefficient use of limited vacation time. Flying 80 minutes between hubs is strongly recommended.

### How far in advance should domestic flights be booked?
Book domestic flights at least four to six weeks in advance, especially for travel between November and April. Booking early secures preferred mid-morning departure slots and includes checked baggage allowances (standard on Vietnam Airlines, but an extra fee on budget carriers like Vietjet).
