# Vietnam Train Travel: 2026 Reunification Express, Sleeper Cabins & Scenic Routes

Stretching 1,726 kilometers from Hanoi Railway Station (120 Le Duan) to Saigon Railway Station (01 Nguyen Thong, District 3), Vietnam's single-track meter-gauge North–South Railway (Đường sắt Thống Nhất)—universally known in English as the Reunification Express—is the most atmospheric overland spine in Southeast Asia. While domestic Airbus A321 jets connect Hanoi, Da Nang, and Ho Chi Minh City in eighty minutes, Vietnam Railways (Đường sắt Việt Nam) carries you straight along cliffside ocean ledges on the Hai Van Pass, past karst monoliths in Ninh Binh, and right into city-center stations while saving a night of hotel accommodation on overnight legs.

Riding the rails in Vietnam requires understanding one critical operational reality: "Reunification Express" is not a single luxury train, but a corridor shared by multiple train numbers with vastly different carriage ages, speeds, and privately chartered sleeper cars. This 2026 guide breaks down every train code, bunk layout, coastal seating side, and official booking channel.

## Train Numbering Hierarchy: Why `SE1/SE3` Beat `SE5/SE7`

Vietnam Railways assigns odd train numbers (**`SE1`, `SE3`, `SE5`, `SE7`, `SE19`, `HD1`, `SP3`**) to southbound departures from Hanoi, and even train numbers (**`SE2`, `SE4`, `SE6`, `SE8`, `SE20`, `HD2`, `SP4`**) to northbound return runs from Ho Chi Minh City or Da Nang. Not all `SE` ("Super Express") trains are created equal:

1. **`SE1 / SE2` and `SE3 / SE4` (Top-Priority National Express)**: These two pairs are the flagship trains on the 1,726 km Hanoi–Saigon main line, completing the full end-to-end journey in **32 to 34 hours**. Because Vietnam's railway is primarily single-track, slower freight and regional trains pull onto passing sidings to let `SE1/SE2` and `SE3/SE4` pass without delay. They feature the newest third-generation soundproofed composite carriages, low-noise bogies, vacuum-flush airplane-style toilets, and evening departure times from Hanoi (19:30 for `SE3`, 20:55 for `SE1`) that put you into Hue (08:30–09:45) and Da Nang (11:15–12:30) the next morning.
2. **`SE5 / SE6` and `SE7 / SE8` (Daytime Coastal Visibility Express)**: Running slightly slower (35 to 36 hours end-to-end) with additional provincial stops, `SE5/SE6` and `SE7/SE8` are scheduled for daytime scenic visibility along the central coast and morning departures out of Hanoi (06:10 for `SE7`, 08:50 for `SE5`) toward Ninh Binh.
3. **`SE19 / SE20` (Hanoi–Da Nang Refurbished Tourist Express)**: Specifically upgraded for the Northern-to-Central heritage corridor, `SE19` departs Hanoi at 19:40, stops at Ninh Binh (21:55) and Dong Hoi (05:25), and rolls over the Hai Van Pass in full morning daylight between 09:30 (Hue) and 12:15 (Da Nang).
4. **`HD1 / HD2` & `HD3 / HD4` ("Connecting Central Heritage" / *Kết nối Di sản Miền Trung*)**: Launched specifically for sightseers on the **103 km Hue–Da Nang coastal stretch**, these dedicated tourist trains depart **Hue at 07:45 (`HD1`) and 14:25 (`HD3`)** and depart **Da Nang at 07:50 (`HD2`) and 15:00 (`HD4`)**. Tickets cost **150,000 VND to 210,000 VND** ($6.00–$8.40 USD) and grant access to a central cultural carriage featuring live traditional **`Ca Huế` chamber music**, Hue royal cakes (`Bánh Ít`, `Bánh Lọc`), panoramic windows, and a scheduled **10-minute platform stop at Lang Co Station (`Ga Lăng Cô`)** for lagoon photos.
5. **Regional Overnight & Coastal Specialists (`SP3/SP4`, `SNT1/SNT2`, `SPT1/SPT2`)**:
   - **`SP3 / SP4` (Hanoi to Lao Cai for Sapa)**: Covers the 294 km northern mountain line in 8 hours overnight (22:00 departure from Hanoi Gate B on Tran Quy Cap Street, arriving Lao Cai at 05:55), where 55,000 VND public buses or 350,000 VND shared limousines climb the final 35 km (50 minutes) up to Sapa town.
   - **`SNT1 / SNT2` (Ho Chi Minh City to Nha Trang)**: The premier southern overnight sleeper (411 km, 8.5 hours), departing Saigon Station at 20:30 (`SNT2`) and arriving in central Nha Trang at 05:00.
   - **`SPT1 / SPT2` (Ho Chi Minh City to Phan Thiet / Mui Ne)**: Convenient daytime coastal train (185 km, 3 hours 50 minutes) departing Saigon at 06:40 (`SPT2`, 185,000 to 265,000 VND).

## Cabin Classes Decoded: 4-Berth vs 6-Berth & Lower vs Upper Bunk

* **Why 4-Berth Soft Sleeper (`AnL`) Is the Sweet Spot**: Each 4-berth carriage contains 7 lockable compartments (28 beds total) with two lower bunks (`Tầng 1`) and two upper bunks (`Tầng 2`), foam mattresses, white duvets, reading lights, and 220V / USB sockets.
* **Lower Berth (`Tầng 1`) vs Upper Berth (`Tầng 2`) Trade-Off**:
  - **Lower Berth (`Tầng 1`)**: Costs **50,000 VND to 90,000 VND** ($2.00–$3.60 USD) more than an upper bunk. It delivers the widest eye-level window view, zero ladder climbing, and direct access to under-bed storage (32 cm tall, fitting 24–26 inch suitcases laid flat).
  - **Upper Berth (`Tầng 2`)**: Cheaper and more private—climb the fold-out step and sleep undisturbed at 19:00 while lower-bunk passengers chat below. Large bags store in the overhead cubby above the corridor door.
* **The 6-Berth (`BnL`) Ceiling AC Warning**: In a 6-berth cabin (3 tiers), middle (`Tầng 2`) and upper (`Tầng 3`) bunks lack sitting headroom. Moreover, the top bunk (`Tầng 3`) sits just **50 cm beneath the ceiling AC vent**, which blasts 18°C air all night—pack a hoodie and socks.
* **VIP Private Charter Carriages (`Lotus Train`, `Chapa Express`, `Violette`, `Livitrans`, `Victoria`, `The Vietage`)**: Private operators attach 2 to 3 boutique carriages to the rear of state `SE1/SE2`, `SE3/SE4`, `SE19/SE20`, and `SP3/SP4` trains. Paying **$75.00 to $160.00 USD** (1,950,000 to 4,000,000 VND) upgrades you to wood-paneled 4-berth or VIP 2-berth cabins with hotel mattresses, free Wi-Fi, snacks/beer, and dedicated attendants—or splurge **$420.00 USD** on **The Vietage by Anantara** between Da Nang and Quy Nhon (`Diêu Trì`).

## Top 5 Scenic & Practical Rail Segments (with 2026 Fares)

Rather than riding all 1,726 km from Hanoi to Saigon nonstop (33 hours), smart itineraries combine 2 or 3 high-impact rail segments:

1. **Hanoi to Ninh Binh (115 km | 2 hours 15 minutes)**
   - **Best Trains & Fare**: `SE7` (06:10), `SE5` (08:50), `SE3` (19:30), `SE19` (19:40); Air-Conditioned Soft Seat (`NML`) **130,000 VND to 190,000 VND** ($5.20–$7.60 USD). Drops at Ninh Binh Station (01 Hoang Hoa Tham), 6 km (10 minutes by 80,000 VND GrabCar) from Tam Coc and Trang An.
2. **Hanoi to Dong Hoi (Phong Nha), Hue, or Da Nang Overnight (522–791 km | 10 to 16 hours)**
   - **Best Trains & Fare**: `SE3` (19:30), `SE19` (19:40), `SE1` (20:55) from **Hanoi Station (120 Le Duan, Gate A)**. Vietnam Railways 4-Berth Soft Sleeper (`AnL`): **950,000 VND to 1,450,000 VND** ($38.00–$58.00 USD), or **1,950,000 VND to 2,400,000 VND** ($78.00–$96.00 USD) in a `Lotus Train` / `Violette` boutique 4-berth cabin. Wake up at 05:25 in **Dong Hoi** (for Phong Nha caves) or roll through **Hue (08:30–09:45)** and across the **Hai Van Pass into Da Nang (11:15–12:30)**.
3. **Hue to Da Nang via Hai Van Pass & Lang Co Bay (103 km | 2.5 to 3 hours)**
   - **Best Trains & Fare**: **`HD1` (07:45)**, **`SE7` (10:25)**, **`HD3` (14:25)** from Hue; or **`HD2` (07:50)**, **`HD4` (15:00)** from Da Nang. Standard `SE` Soft Seats cost **110,000 VND to 145,000 VND** ($4.40–$5.80 USD); the `HD1/HD2` Heritage Train costs **150,000 VND to 210,000 VND** ($6.00–$8.40 USD). While highway buses bypass the coast inside the 6.28 km road tunnel, the railway hugs 500-meter granite cliffs on the **Hai Van Pass (`Đèo Hải Vân`)**, curling around **Lap An Lagoon** and **Lang Co Bay (`Vịnh Lăng Cô`)** above the turquoise East Sea.
4. **Da Nang to Quy Nhon (`Diêu Trì Station`) or Nha Trang (300–524 km | 6 to 10.5 hours)**
   - **Best Trains & Fare**: `SE1`, `SE3`, `SE5`, `SE21`; **280,000 VND to 580,000 VND** ($11.20–$23.20 USD) to **Dieu Tri Station** (11 km west of Quy Nhon beach), or **620,000 VND to 950,000 VND** ($24.80–$38.00 USD) in a 4-berth sleeper to **Nha Trang Station (17 Thai Nguyen)**.
5. **Ho Chi Minh City (`Ga Sài Gòn`, 01 Nguyen Thong, District 3) to Phan Thiet (`SPT2`) or Nha Trang (`SNT2`)**
   - **Best Trains & Fare**: Morning **`SPT2` (06:40 departure, 3 hours 50 minutes)** to **Phan Thiet (185,000 to 265,000 VND / $7.40–$10.60 USD)** for Mui Ne beach resorts; or overnight **`SNT2` (20:30 departure, 8.5 hours)** to **Nha Trang (650,000 to 980,000 VND / $26.00–$39.20 USD)** in a 4-berth sleeper.

> [!TIP]
> **The Golden Window Rule for the Hai Van Pass (`Hue <-> Da Nang`):** To sit directly on the ocean cliff side as the train climbs the Hai Van Pass, always choose a seat or berth on the **LEFT side of the carriage when traveling South (from Hue to Da Nang)**, or on the **RIGHT side of the carriage when traveling North (from Da Nang to Hue)**. In Soft Sleeper (`AnL`) carriages, the standing corridor runs along the east/ocean side on most compositions—meaning you can stand by the wide corridor windows with your camera as the train slows to `30 km/h` around the horseshoe curves.

## How to Book Vietnam Train Tickets Without Agency Scams

1. **Avoid Copycat Agency Domains**: The **only official state website** of Vietnam Railways is **`dsvn.vn`** (`vetau.com.vn`). Clone domains like `vietnam-railway.com` or `vietnamrailways.net` copy the state logo and mark up tickets by 40% to 100%.
2. **How to Pay with a Foreign Visa or Mastercard (`12Go Asia`, `Baolau`, `Vexere`)**: Because `dsvn.vn` uses domestic `VNPAY` / `Napas` gateways that frequently decline overseas cards, book via official API partners **Baolau (`baolau.com`)**, **12Go Asia (`12go.asia`)**, or **Vexere (`vexere.com`)**. They let you pick exact lower/upper bunks (`Tầng 1` vs `Tầng 2`) from live seat maps, accept foreign cards and Apple Pay, issue official QR e-tickets, and charge only a **$2.00 to $4.00 USD** (50,000–100,000 VND) fee.
3. **Baggage Limits, Food Carts & Onboard Security**: Official rules allow **20 kg** of luggage per adult ticket (rarely weighed if bags fit under the 32 cm lower bunk). Onboard carts sell hot rice trays (`cơm tàu`, 50,000 VND) and every carriage has a free **95°C hot water dispenser** for instant noodles (15,000 VND), though packing fresh **`Bánh Mì` (30,000 VND), fruit, and bottled water** before boarding is far tastier. Overnight, lock the interior cabin bolt and keep your passport and phone in a pouch by your pillow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to print my Vietnam train ticket or is a phone QR code accepted?
**A digital QR code on your smartphone is 100% accepted** at all stations. Show the official PDF boarding pass (`Thẻ lên tàu hỏa`) and your passport to the conductor **15 to 30 minutes before departure**.

### Which Hanoi station gate do I use for Sapa (`SP3`) vs Da Nang/Saigon (`SE1/SE3`)?
**Main Gate A (120 Le Duan Street)** handles all southbound Reunification Express trains (**`SE1`, `SE3`, `SE5`, `SE7`, `SE19`**), while **Gate B (1 Tran Quy Cap Street)** serves the overnight **`SP3/SP4`** trains to Lao Cai (Sapa).

### Are the toilets on Vietnam Railways overnight trains clean?
Flagship **`SE1/SE2`, `SE3/SE4`, and `SE19/SE20`** carriages feature a western sit-down toilet and sink at the end of the hall. Pack **tissues, wet wipes, and hand sanitizer**, or upgrade to a private charter carriage (`Lotus Train`, `Violette`, `Chapa Express`) with an hourly steward.

### Is it better to take the overnight train or fly from Hanoi to Da Nang/Hue?
On a 7-day trip, a 1-hour 20-minute flight (1,100,000 to 1,800,000 VND) saves time. On a 10-to-14-day trip, the **`SE19` or `SE3` overnight 4-berth sleeper (950,000 to 1,450,000 VND)** saves a hotel night, drops you in central Hue (02 Bui Thi Xuan), and crosses the **Hai Van Pass and Lang Co Bay** in morning daylight.
