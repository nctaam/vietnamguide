# Vietnam to Cambodia Boat Guide (2026): Fast Mekong Ferry

Crossing from the Mekong Delta into Cambodia by fast ferry transforms an ordinary border transit into a `135 km` riverine voyage up one of Southeast Asia's primary shipping arteries. Every morning between `07:00` and `07:30`, high-speed passenger vessels cast off from the Bassac River (Hau River) waterfront in Chau Doc, An Giang Province, and arrive at the Phnom Penh Autonomous Port on Sisowath Quay between `12:30` and `13:30`. Total transit time runs `5 to 5.5 hours`. That schedule includes `60 to 75 minutes` of mandatory immigration clearance at the twin river checkpoints of Song Tien (Vinh Xuong) on the Vietnamese side and Kaam Samnor in Cambodia's Kandal Province.

River speedboats cost more than highway buses running through Moc Bai, with one-way boat tickets priced from `$35 to $65 USD` (`880,000 to 1,625,000 VND`) depending on vessel class. What you buy with that premium is speed, open-air river visibility, and a remarkably calm immigration sequence where your boat crew handles the paperwork queue for a single cabin of `18 to 40 passengers` rather than competing with `500` coach travelers at a dusty highway gate.

> **2026 River Border Cash Alert:** Neither the Song Tien Vietnamese river gate nor the Kaam Samnor Cambodian checkpoint has a working ATM or credit card terminal. Carry at least `$50 USD` per person in crisp, post-2013 US dollar bills (`$20`, `$10`, and `$5` denominations with zero tears or ink stamps) to cover the `$30 USD` Cambodian Visa on Arrival fee, `$2–$5 USD` port handling surcharge, and onward Phnom Penh tuk-tuk fare.

## Detailed Operator Breakdown: Choosing Your Hull

### 1. Hang Chau Tourist Express Boat
Hang Chau Tourist is the workhorse of the Chau Doc to Phnom Penh river corridor. Operating daily year-round, its fleet consists of elongated aluminum and fiberglass express launches powered by twin `200 to 250 HP` Yamaha outboard engines. One-way fares cost `$35 USD` (`880,000 VND`) when booked directly in Chau Doc or `$38 to $40 USD` (`950,000 to 1,000,000 VND`) through online aggregators and Ho Chi Minh City travel desks.

Check-in opens at `06:30` at the Hang Chau Tourist office (`10 Nguyen Cong Tru Street` or the adjacent riverfront pier near `01 Le Loi Street`, depending on seasonal water levels). The main cabin holds `28 to 42 seats` arranged in a `2-2` layout. Cabin air conditioning blows briskly once the engines reach cruising speed (`42 km/h`), while the narrow aft deck accommodates `4 to 6 standing passengers` who prefer fresh river breezes and unobstructed photography. One `20 kg` checked suitcase and one `7 kg` daypack ride free. Oversized bicycles cost an extra `$10 to $15 USD` (`250,000 to 375,000 VND`).

### 2. Victoria Speedboat
Originally built for guests of the four-star Victoria Chau Doc Hotel, the Victoria Speedboat sells remaining seats to non-resident travelers for `$48 to $65 USD` (`1,200,000 to 1,625,000 VND`) one way. Boarding happens right at the private floating jetty behind the Victoria Chau Doc Hotel (`01 Le Loi Street`, Chau Doc Ward) at `07:00` for a prompt `07:30` departure. Space is strictly capped.

The vessel is a custom-built steel cruiser outfitted with `18 to 26 high-back leatherette seats`, wide legroom (`85 cm` pitch), sliding panoramic side windows, and a clean marine lavatory at the stern. Ticket prices include chilled mineral water, canned soft drinks, fresh tropical fruit platters, and a morning pastry box. Because passenger headcount rarely exceeds `20 travelers`, passport stamping at both Song Tien and Kaam Samnor moves `15 to 20 minutes` faster than on high-capacity tour boats.

### 3. Mandarin Cruiser & Blue Cruiser
Mandarin Cruiser and Blue Cruiser fill the middle tier at `$38 to $45 USD` (`950,000 to 1,125,000 VND`). These vessels frequently coordinate shared departures during the low-water shoulder months (April through June) and operate independent daily boats during the peak European winter season (November through March). Both include hotel pickup within a `2 km` radius of Chau Doc Market between `06:15` and `06:40`, bottled water, and an English-speaking deckhand who collects passports and distributes Cambodian arrival forms before the boat reaches the Vinh Xuong canal junction.

## Route Geography: From the Bassac River to the Tien River

Many first-time passengers assume Chau Doc sits directly on the main stem of the Mekong River. In reality, Chau Doc fronts the Hau River (Bassac River), the western distributary of the Mekong system. Leaving Chau Doc pier at `07:30`, your speedboat does not follow the Bassac north into Cambodia (which crosses at the local Khánh Bình – Chrey Thom gate). Instead, the captain steers east-northeast through the `16 km` Vinh An and Vinh Xuong lateral canal system.

This `25-minute` canal transit is the most intimate segment of the journey. Wooden stilt houses, floating catfish (`cá tra`) cages, and local ferries line a waterway barely `60 meters` wide. Around `08:00`, the boat emerges onto the expansive Tien River—the main eastern channel of the Mekong River, over `1.2 km` wide—and turns north toward the international river boundary in Tan Chau District, An Giang Province.

## Step-by-Step River Border Clearance: Song Tien & Kaam Samnor

Immigration formalities on the Mekong River require two separate landings spaced roughly `1.5 km` apart. Keep your passport, printed visas, and US dollar bills in a waterproof pouch inside your carry-on bag; heavy backpacks and suitcases remain safely locked aboard the vessel under the captain's watch.

### Step 1: Boarding Handover in Chau Doc (`06:30–07:15`)
Arrive at your designated Chau Doc pier at least `30 minutes` before departure. During check-in, the boat crew inspects your passport validity (minimum `6 months` remaining with at least `2 blank pages`) and checks your Cambodian visa status.
- **If you hold a Cambodian e-Visa (`$36 USD`)**: Hand one printed paper copy of your e-Visa approval certificate to the crew member.
- **If you require a Cambodian Visa on Arrival (VoA)**: Hand the crew your passport, one passport-sized photo (`4x6 cm` or `2x2 inch`), and `$35 USD` in crisp, unmarked cash (`$30 USD` statutory T-class tourist visa fee plus a `$5 USD` river-port courier/processing fee charged by the boat crew and border police). If you lack a passport photo, border officers charge a `$2 USD` (`50,000 VND`) scan fee.

### Step 2: Vietnamese Exit Stamp at Song Tien / Vinh Xuong (`08:30–09:00`)
After `60 to 75 minutes` of cruising (`32 km` from Chau Doc), the speedboat docks at the **Song Tien International River Border Gate** (`Cửa khẩu đường sông Vĩnh Xương`), located on the west bank of the Tien River in Vinh Xuong Commune, Tan Chau Town, An Giang Province.

All passengers disembark and walk `80 meters` up the concrete ramp into the Vietnamese immigration pavilion. The boat crew submits the passenger manifest and passports to the Border Guard (`Bộ đội Biên phòng`) counter. While officers verify your Vietnamese entry record and stamp your exit permit (`20 to 30 minutes`), you can sit under the shaded riverside pergola, buy iced coffee (`20,000 to 30,000 VND` / `$0.80 to $1.20 USD`), or use the free restrooms. Once your name is called, verify that the red hexagonal Vietnamese exit stamp is inked clearly in your passport before walking back down to the jetty.

### Step 3: Cambodian Entry & Visa Issuance at Kaam Samnor (`09:05–09:45`)
Re-boarding the boat, you cruise `1.5 km` upstream across the invisible river demarcation line into Kandal Province, Cambodia. Within `5 minutes`, the captain ties up at the floating pontoon of the **Kaam Samnor International Border Checkpoint**.

In 2026, two Cambodian requirements apply here:
1. **Cambodia Visa (e-Visa or Visa on Arrival)**: Both pre-approved e-Visas (`evisa.gov.kh`, `$36 USD`) and Visa on Arrival (`$30 USD` statutory fee + `$2–$5 USD` handling) are fully accepted at Kaam Samnor.
2. **Cambodia e-Arrival Card**: While Cambodia rolled out the digital e-Arrival system (`arrival.gov.kh`) primarily for international airports (Phnom Penh, Siem Reap, Sihanoukville), river checkpoints at Kaam Samnor still frequently process paper arrival/departure cards handed out on the boat or use biometric tablets at the counter. Completing the free online `arrival.gov.kh` registration within `7 days` before travel—and saving the QR code offline on your phone—prevents delays if the river post enforces digital-only scanning on your travel date.

Passengers walk up the steep metal gangway to the Cambodian immigration shed. Each traveler steps up to the biometric window for a digital webcam photograph and ten-finger scan (`2 to 3 minutes` per person). Once the crew returns your passport containing the full-page Cambodian visa sticker (if using VoA) and entry stamp showing a `30-day` stay limit, everyone boards for the final leg.

## The Final Upstream Leg to Phnom Penh (`09:45–13:00`)

North of Kaam Samnor, the Mekong River widens to `1.5 to 2 km`. Few bridges cross this stretch apart from the soaring Neak Loeung (Tsubasa) cable-stayed bridge (`60 km` southeast of Phnom Penh), which your speedboat passes beneath around `11:00`. Along the clay riverbanks, golden Buddhist temple roofs, sand-dredging barges, and stilt fishing villages mark your progress through Kandal Province.

Between `12:30` and `13:30`, the high-rise skyline of Phnom Penh appears at the Chaktomuk confluence, where the Mekong Upper, Mekong Lower, Bassac, and Tonle Sap rivers meet. Boats dock at the **Phnom Penh Autonomous Port Passenger Terminal** on **Sisowath Quay** (near `Street 104` and the Night Market), right in the riverfront Daun Penh district.

At the gate outside `Street 104`, dozens of independent tuk-tuk drivers wait behind the port barrier, often quoting inflated fares of `$8 to $10 USD` for a `1.5 km` ride. Walk `50 meters` past the parking lot gate onto Sisowath Quay and book a PassApp or Grab rickshaw: fares to Riverside hotels cost `4,000 to 8,000 KHR` (`$1.00 to $2.00 USD`), while rides to BKK1 or Tuol Tom Poung (Russian Market) run `10,000 to 16,000 KHR` (`$2.50 to $4.00 USD`).

## Essential 2026 Cash, Luggage & Seasonal Rules

- **Strict US Dollar Note Condition**: Cambodian border officials and riverside vendors reject US dollar bills printed before `2013` or notes with even a `2 mm` edge tear, pinhole, ink stamp, or heavy crease. Withdraw VND in Chau Doc and exchange for crisp `$10`, `$20`, and `$5` USD notes at gold shops around Chau Doc Market (`Chợ Châu Đốc`) the afternoon before departure.
- **Reverse Direction (Phnom Penh to Chau Doc)**: Boats depart Sisowath Quay (`Street 104`) daily at `12:30` or `13:00`, arriving in Chau Doc around `17:00 to 17:30` (`4.5 hours` downstream with the current). Traveling downstream into Vietnam requires either a visa-exempt passport or a **pre-printed Vietnam 90-day e-Visa** (`$25 USD` single-entry / `$50 USD` multiple-entry via `evisa.gov.vn`) designating **Song Tien Landport/Riverport** as the entry gate. Vietnam does **not** issue Visa on Arrival at Song Tien.
- **Sun and Noise Protection**: Twin outboard motors generate `80 to 85 dB` of continuous hum at the stern, and glare off the Mekong water is intense between `10:00` and `12:30`. Pack earplugs, polarized sunglasses, SPF 50 sunscreen, and a light windbreaker for the air-conditioned cabin.
