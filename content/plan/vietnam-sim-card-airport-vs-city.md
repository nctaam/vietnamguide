# Vietnam SIM Card: Airport vs City Stores Guide (2026)

Landing at Noi Bai (`HAN`) or Tan Son Nhat (`SGN`) without mobile data creates an immediate logistical bottleneck. Free airport Wi-Fi frequently drops connection outside the glass arrival doors, exactly where you need to summon a Grab car or verify a hotel pin `28 km` away. Yet walking up to the first illuminated telecom booth in the terminal often results in paying `350,000 VND` (`$14.00 USD`) for a card that stops working after five days.

Since the Ministry of Information and Communications (MIC) tightened subscriber enforcement under Decree 49/ND-CP, Vietnam's mobile market has changed. Anonymous, pre-activated street SIM cards are aggressively purged from carrier networks. Whether you buy a physical nano-SIM at an airport counter, visit an official carrier store in town, or install a travel eSIM before departure, knowing how registration and pricing work will save you money and prevent mid-trip blackouts.

---

## 1. The 2026 Legal Reality: Decree 49/ND-CP and Subscriber Purges

Every active mobile number in Vietnam must link to a verified identity record. Under MIC regulations and Decree 49/ND-CP, telecom operators must collect a high-resolution scan of your original passport bio-page alongside a live biometric portrait photograph before activating a SIM or eSIM.

Do not buy pre-registered SIM cards from convenience stores, backpacker hostels, or street hawkers around Bui Vien and Ta Hien. Those cards are registered under bulk proxy identities. Carrier algorithms audit new activations continuously, and unverified street SIMs get disconnected within `3–7 days`. When the line is cut, you lose your data balance and cannot receive SMS one-time passwords (OTPs) for banking or transport apps.

Legitimate registration takes `10–15 minutes` at an official counter. The clerk photographs your passport, snaps a quick webcam portrait of your face, and asks you to sign a digital tablet screen. That single step guarantees your line remains active for the full `30 days` of your tourist data package.

## 3. Option 1: Official City Carrier Stores (`Cửa hàng giao dịch`)

Buying directly from a corporate telecom branch in central Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, or Da Nang is the gold standard. You pay transparent state-regulated tariffs, get your passport legally tied to the SIM serial number, and walk out with a genuine `+84` phone number.

### Exact Pricing Breakdown in the City

At an official counter, your bill splits into two clear components:
1. **Blank Physical SIM or eSIM Issuance Fee:** `50,000 VND` (`$2.00 USD`) for a physical nano-SIM, or `35,000–50,000 VND` (`$1.40–$2.00 USD`) to generate a digital eSIM QR code.
2. **30-Day Prepaid Data Plan:** `100,000–200,000 VND` (`$4.00–$8.00 USD`).

Your total outlay sits between `150,000 VND` and `250,000 VND` (`$6.00–$10.00 USD`). Compare that against the `350,000–450,000 VND` (`$14.00–$18.00 USD`) charged at airport baggage claim counters.

### Best 2026 Viettel and Vinaphone Data Packages

Viettel (owned by the Ministry of National Defence) operates the densest cellular tower grid in Vietnam. If your itinerary includes [Hanoi to Sapa Transport](/plan/hanoi-to-sapa-transport/), the Ha Giang Loop along Highway QL4C, Phong Nha limestone valleys, or offshore islands like Con Dao and Cat Ba, choose **Viettel**. In remote valleys `150 km` from provincial capitals, Viettel holds 4G signal where competitors drop to zero bars.

Ask the city store clerk for one of these specific 30-day Viettel packages:
* **SD120 (`120,000 VND` / `$4.80 USD`):** Provides `2 GB/day` of high-speed 4G/5G data (`60 GB` total over `30 days`). If you exhaust the daily `2 GB` allowance before midnight, traffic pauses until the next day's reset at `00:00`.
* **SD135 (`135,000 VND` / `$5.40 USD`):** Provides `5 GB/day` of high-speed 4G/5G data (`150 GB` total over `30 days`). This is the sweet spot for heavy navigation, video calls, and tethering a laptop for `2–3 hours` daily.
* **MXH100 (`100,000 VND` / `$4.00 USD`):** Gives `1 GB/day` (`30 GB` total) plus unlimited zero-rated bandwidth for YouTube, Facebook, Messenger, and TikTok.

**Vinaphone** (VNPT) and **MobiFone** offer fast 5G speeds in urban cores like District 1, Tay Ho, and My Khe Beach for `120,000–160,000 VND` (`$4.80–$6.40 USD`), though their mountain coverage trails Viettel by roughly `15–20%`.

### Verified Official Store Locations

Skip small phone repair stalls displaying faded carrier logos; those are unauthorized dealers (`Điểm bán lẻ`) that cannot process foreign passport registrations. Go only to corporate transaction centers (`Cửa hàng giao dịch`):
* **Hanoi (Hoan Kiem / Old Quarter):** Viettel Store at `26 Hang Dau` (just `250 meters` north of Hoan Kiem Lake, open `08:00–20:00`) or Vinaphone Transaction Center at `75 Dinh Tien Hoang` (directly facing the lake, `400 meters` from Ngoc Son Temple).
* **Ho Chi Minh City (District 1):** Viettel Flagship Store at `166C–166D–166E Tran Hung Dao` (`1.2 km` from Ben Thanh Market, open `08:00–21:00`), Vinaphone Main Office at `80 Nguyen Du` (`150 meters` from Notre Dame Cathedral), or MobiFone at `125 Hai Ba Trung`.
* **Da Nang (Hai Chau District):** Viettel Center at `95 Hung Vuong` (`600 meters` west of Han Market, open `07:30–20:30`).

## 4. Option 2: Airport Arrival Hall Kiosks (`HAN`, `SGN`, `DAD`)

Sometimes convenience wins. If your flight touches down at `00:45 AM`, city branches are shut, and paying an extra `150,000 VND` (`$6.00 USD`) at the terminal is worth getting into a taxi within `10 minutes`.

### How Airport Kiosks Work

After clearing immigration and passing customs x-ray scanners at Noi Bai International Terminal 2 (`HAN`), Tan Son Nhat Terminal 2 (`SGN`), or Da Nang Terminal 2 (`DAD`), you will face a row of `8 to 12` brightly lit kiosks selling SIM cards and currency exchange.

Prices range from `250,000 VND` to `450,000 VND` (`$10.00–$18.00 USD`) for packages marketed as "`6 GB/day` for `30 days`" or "Unlimited Tourist Data." The clerk pops your SIM tray, inserts the chip, and gets your phone online in `3 minutes`.

### The 3 Airport SIM Traps to Avoid

1. **Unregistered Pre-Activated Cards:** During peak arrival waves when `300 passengers` exit customs simultaneously, third-party kiosk staff often hand out pre-activated SIMs without scanning your passport. Three to five days later, while you are `300 km` away in Ha Long Bay or Da Lat, the carrier suspends the number for Decree 49 non-compliance.
2. **7-Day Packages Sold as 30-Day Plans:** A common reseller trick involves charging `350,000 VND` (`$14.00 USD`) for a 30-day tourist plan, but actually activating a `70,000 VND` (`$2.80 USD`) 7-day trial package (`ST70K`). By day eight, your data dies.
3. **MVNO Sub-Brands Masquerading as Major Networks:** Some booths sell SIM cards from virtual operators like **Wintel** (which rides on Vinaphone towers) or **Local** (riding on MobiFone) while telling buyers it is a "Viettel-equivalent unlimited card." These budget MVNOs suffer severe deprioritization inside thick concrete buildings and drop connections entirely on rural highways.

### Mandatory 60-Second Counter Verification Protocol

Never walk away from an airport SIM booth until you run this check on your own screen:
* **Check Carrier Name:** Look at the top status bar of your phone. It should display `VIETTEL`, `VINAPHONE`, or `MOBIFONE`.
* **Verify Plan Expiry via SMS (`191`):** On Viettel, send a free text message typing `KTTK` to `191`. Within `10 seconds`, an automated reply will state your exact package code (such as `SD135`), remaining daily gigabytes, and the exact expiration timestamp (for example, `30/11/2026 23:59:59`).
* **Check USSD Balance (`*098#` or `*101#`):** Dial `*101#` and press call to check main account credit, or dial `*098#` on Viettel to inspect active tariff bundles.
* **Install the Carrier App:** Download *My Viettel* or *My VNPT* while standing at the booth. Tap the profile icon to confirm your own name and passport number appear under subscriber info (`Thông tin thuê bao`). If a random Vietnamese name appears, demand that the clerk re-register the SIM under your passport immediately.

## 5. Option 3: International and Local Travel eSIMs

If your smartphone is unlocked and supports eSIM (iPhone XS or newer, Samsung Galaxy S21 or newer, Google Pixel 4 or newer), digital provisioning eliminates physical plastic swaps.

### International Roaming eSIMs (Airalo, Nomad, Holafly)

Global app-based eSIMs like **Airalo** (*Xin Chao* SIM), **Nomad**, and **Holafly** cost `$8.00–$28.00 USD` (`200,000–700,000 VND`) for `3 GB` to `20 GB` valid over `7 to 30 days`.
* **Pros:** Zero airport queue. You install the profile over home Wi-Fi `12 hours` before your flight, and data connects the moment the aircraft wheels touch the tarmac. Because international eSIMs operate via roaming agreements (routing traffic through Singapore or Hong Kong gateways), they bypass local MIC passport checks. Your primary home SIM stays active for iMessage, WhatsApp, and bank 2FA codes.
* **Cons:** Higher per-gigabyte cost (`$3.00–$4.50 USD` per GB versus `$0.10 USD` per GB at a Viettel shop), extra `60–110 ms` network ping latency due to foreign gateway routing, and **no local `+84` phone number**.

### Local Vietnam Travel eSIMs (Gigago, Viettel eSIM)

Vietnamese providers like **Gigago** bridge the gap by selling local Vinaphone and Viettel eSIM profiles online for `$8.00–$18.00 USD` (`200,000–450,000 VND`) delivering `4–7 GB/day`.

### Do You Need a Local `+84` Number for Vietnam Travel Apps?

Before buying a data-only eSIM, consider your app setup. Review our [Vietnam Travel Apps](/plan/vietnam-travel-apps/) guide for the full software stack. Here is how `+84` verification impacts your daily logistics:
* **Grab and Be:** You can register **Grab** using your home country phone number (`+1`, `+44`, `+61`, `+65`) before leaving home. Grab drivers communicate via built-in in-app chat and VoIP audio calls over data, so a data-only eSIM works fine.
* **Xanh SM (VinFast Electric Taxis) and Vexere:** Booking electric taxis via **Xanh SM** or reserving sleeper bus tickets on **Vexere** works smoother with a local `+84` number. More importantly, when you book a [Vietnam Sleeper Bus Guide](/plan/vietnam-sleeper-bus-guide/) departure or a Ha Giang limousine van, the driver or ticket dispatcher will call your local `+84` cell number via regular voice line `30 minutes` before pickup to confirm your hotel lobby address. They will not call an international number.

## 6. Step-by-Step Protocol by Traveler Profile

> **Field Note:** Always photograph your physical SIM plastic card frame before tossing it away. That card displays your 8-digit PUK unlock code and your printed `+84` subscriber number, which you will need if you accidentally lock your SIM PIN or top up data via a convenience store terminal.

1. **The Daytime Arrival (`07:00–19:00`) Staying `10+ Days`:** Pre-register **Grab** on your home phone number before flying. Land at `HAN` or `SGN`, connect to terminal Wi-Fi (or use a cheap `$4.50 USD` `1 GB` Airalo eSIM) to book your `45-minute` airport ride into the city (`180,000–300,000 VND` / `$7.20–$12.00 USD`). The next morning, walk `10 minutes` to an official **Viettel Store** (`26 Hang Dau` in Hanoi or `166C Tran Hung Dao` in HCMC) and pay `185,000 VND` (`$7.40 USD`) for an official passport-registered **SD135** (`5 GB/day`) SIM or eSIM.
2. **The Midnight Arrival (`22:00–05:00`) or Short `4-Day` Trip:** Buy a local eSIM online (`$10.00–$15.00 USD`) before boarding, or use an official carrier-branded counter inside the arrival hall (`250,000–350,000 VND`). Run the `KTTK` to `191` SMS verification before stepping outside to the taxi curb.
3. **The Remote Loop Rider (Ha Giang, Cao Bang, Mu Cang Chai):** Stick exclusively with **Viettel**. Even if your primary phone runs an international eSIM on Vinaphone or MobiFone towers, buy a secondary `170,000 VND` (`$6.80 USD`) physical Viettel **SD120** card for a backup handset or dual-SIM slot. When fog rolls across the Ma Pi Leng Pass `22 km` outside Meo Vac, that military-grade tower network is your lifeline for offline map refreshes and emergency roadside calls.
4. **Top-Up and Extension After `30 Days`:** Extending your stay on a `90-day` e-Visa? Do not buy a new SIM card. Open the *My Viettel* or *My VNPT* app, purchase a `100,000 VND` (`$4.00 USD`) top-up scratch card at any Circle K or WinMart convenience shop, and dial `*100*<PIN_CODE>#` to reload. Your existing `30-day` data bundle will automatically renew at midnight without another store visit.
