# Tet in Vietnam Travel Guide: Dates, Closures and Routes

**Quick Verdict**: Traveling through **Vietnamese Lunar New Year (Tết Nguyên Đán)** rewards planned itineraries while punishing spontaneous transit hops. Every year in late January or February, roughly **15 to 20 million domestic workers and students** migrate across Vietnam's **1,726 km** rail and aviation spine within a tight **14-day window**. Do not attempt cross-country train or flight jumps during the week before **Mùng 1 Tết** or the four days immediately afterward. Instead, book all domestic flights and **SE2/SE4** sleeper trains **60 to 90 days in advance**, withdraw **3,000,000 to 5,000,000 VND ($120.00 to $200.00 USD)** in crisp banknotes by **28 Tết**, and lock in one stationary **3 to 4 night base**—such as **Hanoi's Old Quarter**, **Hoi An and Da Nang**, **Phu Quoc Island**, or a **2D1N / 3D2N Ha Long and Lan Ha Bay cruise**—spanning **29 Tết through Mùng 3 Tết**.

## 1. Exact Official Tet Dates Calendar (2026, 2027, 2028) & Multi-Year Planning Matrix

Unlike the fixed Gregorian calendar, **Tết Nguyên Đán** tracks the lunisolar cycle, shifting between **January 21 and February 20** each year. While the core spiritual celebration spans three lunar days (**Mùng 1, Mùng 2, and Mùng 3 Tết**), Vietnam's Prime Minister ratifies an official **7 to 9 day national public holiday** by combining weekend rest days with statutory leave. However, the real-world logistical impact stretches across **18 to 21 days**, starting on **Kitchen Gods Day (Ông Công Ông Táo / 23 Chạp)** and running until **Mùng 10 Tết**.

## 2. The 3 Distinct Phases of Tet: What Opens, What Closes & Transport Pressure

Travelers often assume Vietnam shuts down for an entire month during Lunar New Year. That myth is false. Tet unfolds in three sharply contrasting operational phases, each carrying distinct street atmospheres, ticket prices, and closure patterns.

### Phase A: The Pre-Tet Rush (7 to 10 Days Before Mùng 1 / 23 to 29 Chạp)

This week delivers the highest visual energy of the calendar year. Cumulative festive atmosphere hits **10/10** in major cities, and **100% of museums, historical monuments, tailors, and street-food stalls remain open**. In **Hanoi**, motorbikes strap **1.5 m to 2.5 m** pink peach blossom branches (**Hoa đào**) and kumquat trees (**Cây quất**) onto rear racks at the **Quảng Bá Night Flower Market** (**236 Âu Cơ Street, Tây Hồ**, busiest **02:00 to 06:30**, **50,000 VND** Grab ride), while **Hàng Mã Street** and **Hàng Lược Flower Market** (**500 m** north of Hoan Kiem Lake) overflow with red lanterns costing **100,000 to 350,000 VND ($4.00 to $14.00 USD)**. In **Ho Chi Minh City**, wooden boats from Ben Tre and Sa Dec moor along a **1.8 km** canal at **Bến Bình Đông Floating Flower Market** in **District 8** (**6 km** or **20 minutes** by taxi for **90,000 VND**), and **Nguyen Hue Flower Street (Đường Hoa Nguyễn Huệ)** opens its **700 m** pedestrian floral avenue in **District 1** on **28 Chạp**.

Long-distance directional transit, however, hits maximum gridlock. Because millions of workers live in southern industrial zones around **Ho Chi Minh City** but hail from northern and central provinces, **South-to-North trains (`SE2/SE4`) and flights out of `SGN` to `HAN/DAD/VII/THD` are 95% to 100% sold out** and cost **2x to 3x normal fares**, reaching **3,200,000 to 4,800,000 VND ($128.00 to $192.00 USD)** one-way. Meanwhile, North-to-South Reunification Express trains (**SE1 and SE3** from Hanoi to Saigon) run with open berths at **950,000 to 1,450,000 VND ($38.00 to $58.00 USD)**, whereas northbound **SE2 and SE4** sleeper cabins sell out **60 days** ahead at **2,400,000 to 3,100,000 VND ($96.00 to $124.00 USD)**.

### Phase B: The Core 3 Days (30 Tết Afternoon through Mùng 1, Mùng 2 & Mùng 3 Tết)

Around **13:00 on Lunar New Year's Eve (29 or 30 Tết)**, metal shutters roll down across urban commercial districts as families gather for the ancestral meal (**Cúng Tất Niên**). At **00:00 midnight (Giao Thừa)**, **15-minute** municipal fireworks illuminate **Hoan Kiem Lake** in Hanoi, the **Dragon Bridge** in Da Nang, and the **Saigon River Tunnel (Thủ Thiêm)** in Ho Chi Minh City.

For the next **48 to 72 hours**, **Hanoi's Old Quarter** and **Ho Chi Minh City's District 1** turn remarkably car-free and quiet. During **Mùng 1 and Mùng 2 Tết**, roughly **60% to 75% of family-run street-food stalls, Hoi An tailors, and state museums** (such as the **Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum** and **War Remnants Museum**) close from **30 Tết** afternoon through **Mùng 2 or Mùng 3**. Outdoor heritage landmarks like **Hue's Imperial Citadel** (**200,000 VND** entry) and **Hanoi's Temple of Literature** (**70,000 VND** entry, **08:00 to 17:00**) remain open. Every **4-to-5-star hotel restaurant**, **24-hour Circle K** convenience store, major shopping mall (**Lotte, Takashimaya**), and **30% to 40% of tourist cafes** operate daily.

> **Mandatory Holiday Service Surcharge (Phụ thu Tết) Explained**: From **29 Tết through Mùng 3 Tết**, open cafes, restaurants, and Grab operators add a transparent **15% to 30% Holiday Service Surcharge (Phụ thu Tết)**—or **10,000 to 25,000 VND ($0.40 to $1.00 USD)** per noodle bowl—printed on menus. Under the Vietnam Labor Code, employers must pay staff working on official Tet days **300% legal holiday overtime wages**.

### Phase C: Post-Tet Reverse Migration & Pagodas (Mùng 4 to Mùng 8 Tết)

By **Mùng 4 Tết**, city restaurants and museums reopen to **85% capacity**, and domestic travelers flood coastal and highland resorts (**Phu Quoc, Nha Trang, Da Lat, Sapa, Ninh Binh, Ha Long**), pushing hotel rates up **30% to 50%** (**1,500,000 to 3,200,000 VND / $60.00 to $128.00 USD**). Avoid major pilgrimage pagodas between **Mùng 4 and Mùng 8 Tết**, where **40,000 to 80,000 worshippers** create **2-hour** cable-car and boat queues at **Perfume Pagoda (Chùa Hương, 60 km south of Hanoi, 480,000 VND combo)**, **Bái Đính (Ninh Binh, 95 km, 250,000 VND)**, **Yên Tử (Quảng Ninh, 130 km, 350,000 VND)**, and **Miếu Bà Chúa Xứ (Châu Đốc, 250 km)**. Simultaneously, **North-to-South flights (`HAN/DAD/VII -> SGN`)** and southbound **SE1/SE3** trains sell out completely.

## 3. Strategic Route Planning: Park Yourself in One Base from 29 Tết to Mùng 3

Eliminate intercity transit between **29 Tết and Mùng 3 Tết** by anchoring yourself in one stationary **3 to 4 night base**:

1. **Ha Long Bay or Lan Ha Bay Cruise (2D1N or 3D2N)**: Overnight ships departing **Tuần Châu Marina** (**150 km** or **2.5 hours** from Hanoi) operate **365 days a year** (**7,800,000 to 12,500,000 VND / $312.00 to $500.00 USD** per person all-inclusive), insulating you from city closures with full meals, kayaking, and hotel transfers.
2. **Hoi An & Da Nang**: Lantern-lit streets along the **Thu Bồn River** and **An Bang Beach** restaurants stay active at **70% capacity** across **Mùng 1 to Mùng 3** while cloth tailors pause for **3 days**.
3. **Phu Quoc Island**: Dry February sunshine (**28°C to 31°C**) where beachfront resorts run full dining and pool services (book **1,800,000 to 3,500,000 VND** flights **60 to 90 days** ahead).
4. **Hanoi Old Quarter**: Ideal for walking **8 km to 10 km** across a serene, traffic-free **Hoan Kiem District** in crisp **18°C** weather.

## 4. Step-by-Step Tet Cash, ATM & Cultural Etiquette Checklist

1. **Withdraw 3,000,000 to 5,000,000 VND Cash by 28 Tết**: Commercial banks close for **5 days**, and street ATMs run low on **50,000 to 200,000 VND** notes during the holiday. Visit a **Vietcombank, BIDV, Techcombank, or TPBank** branch ATM (**0 to 55,000 VND** fee) **48 hours** before New Year's Eve.
2. **Respect "Mở Hàng Đầu Năm" (First Morning Sale)**: Never haggle aggressively and walk away empty-handed from a vendor's first morning sale (**07:00 to 11:00**) on **Mùng 1 to Mùng 3**, which locals believe dictates their annual fortune.
3. **Wear Bright Red or Yellow & Greet with "Chúc Mừng Năm Mới"**: Avoid funeral-associated all-black or stark white outfits on **Mùng 1**, and greet staff with **"Chúc Mừng Năm Mới"** ("Happy New Year").
4. **Carry 20,000 to 50,000 VND Red Envelopes (Lì Xì)**: Handing crisp **20,000 to 50,000 VND ($0.80 to $2.00 USD)** notes in red envelopes (**Phong bao lì xì**, **15,000 VND** per pack) to hotel bellhops, housekeepers, and drivers is warmly appreciated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do Reunification Express trains and Grab cars still run on Mùng 1 Tết?
Yes. Both Vietnam Railways (**SE1–SE4** trains) and ride-hailing apps (**Grab and Xanh SM**) operate **24 hours a day** throughout Tet. Active driver supply drops by **40%** on **Mùng 1** morning, extending pickup waits from **3 minutes to 10 minutes** and adding a **15,000 to 30,000 VND** driver surcharge.

### Are Ha Long Bay cruises and Ninh Binh boat tours open during Tet?
Overnight cruises in **Ha Long Bay and Lan Ha Bay** sail every day of the year. In **Ninh Binh**, **Tràng An and Tam Cốc** boat piers (**250,000 VND** ticket) pause briefly on **30 Tết** afternoon and **Mùng 1** morning, resuming full operations by **13:00 on Mùng 1** (avoid **Mùng 4 to Mùng 8**, when queues hit **90 to 120 minutes**).

### Can I get a Vietnam e-Visa processed during the Tet holiday week?
No. The **Vietnam Immigration Department** suspends routine **90-day e-Visa** processing (`evisa.gov.vn`, **$25.00 USD** single-entry / **$50.00 USD** multiple-entry) during the **7 to 9 day official Tet holiday**. Submit your application at least **14 to 21 days** before Tet starts.
