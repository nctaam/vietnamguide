# Vietnam Motorbike License Laws (2026): IDP & Legal Rules

Renting a scooter in Vietnam is effortless. Riding one legally is not. Across Hanoi, Ha Giang, Da Nang, and Phu Quoc, guesthouses and beachfront shops will gladly hand you the keys to a `110cc` **Honda Wave** or `125cc` **Honda AirBlade** for **150,000 VND per day** (`$6.00 USD`) in exchange for a passport photocopy and zero questions about your driving credentials. That casual rental counter transaction creates a dangerous legal illusion.

Under Vietnam's **2024 Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety** (effective January 1, 2025) and **Decree 168/2024/ND-CP** (`Nghị định 168/2024/NĐ-CP`), traffic police (**Cảnh sát giao thông**, or **CSGT**) enforce strict new license tiers and sharply higher financial penalties. Worse yet, riding an over-`50cc` motorbike without both a valid home motorcycle license and a **1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit (`IDP`)** automatically voids your travel medical insurance. One patch of loose gravel on the **350 km Ha Giang Loop** can leave an unlicensed rider facing **`$50,000 to $120,000 USD`** in uncovered hospital and medical evacuation bills.

> **The 2026 Legal Bottom Line**: To legally ride any scooter or motorcycle over `50cc` (`> 50 cm³` or `> 4 kW`) in Vietnam on a tourist visa, you must physically carry **both** your original domestic driver's license bearing a full motorcycle endorsement (**Category A1 or A**) **and** a **1968 Vienna Convention IDP**. Because the **United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Ireland, Japan, and Singapore** only issue **1949 Geneva Convention IDPs**, tourists from those countries cannot legally self-ride `110cc to 150cc` motorbikes in Vietnam—and must use **sub-50cc petrol scooters**, **electric scooters under 4 kW**, or licensed **Easy Rider** drivers instead.

## 1. The Two-Document Legal Requirement (Home License + 1968 Vienna IDP)

Vietnamese traffic law does not recognize foreign national driver's licenses on their own. Showing a California, New South Wales, Ontario, or European Union plastic card by itself at a roadside checkpoint counts as riding completely unlicensed.

To pass a **CSGT** inspection on any motorbike above `50cc`, a foreign visitor must present two matching physical documents together:

1. **Physical National Driver's License with a Motorcycle Endorsement (`Category A / A1`)**: Your plastic card from home must explicitly cover motorcycles. A standard car-only license (`Category B`) is never valid for `110cc–150cc` scooters in Vietnam, even if domestic rules in Spain, France, or the United Kingdom allow car drivers to operate light `125cc` mopeds within their own borders.
2. **Official 1968 Vienna Convention IDP**: Your grey paper booklet must conform to the **1968 Convention on Road Traffic**, carry an official issuing stamp in the **Category A** (or **A1**) box, and remain within its `1- to 3-year` validity window. Digital PDFs on a phone screen are rejected.

### How the 2025/2026 Engine Size Tiers Work (50cc vs 125cc vs 150cc+)

When the **2024 Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety** took effect on January 1, 2025, Vietnam aligned its domestic motorcycle license categories with 1968 Vienna Convention thresholds, lowering the old `175cc` ceiling to `125cc`:

* **Sub-50cc Mopeds & Electric Scooters (`< 50 cm³` or `< 4 kW`)**: No driver's license or IDP is required for riders aged **16 or older**. Top speed is capped below **50 km/h**.
* **Category A1 (`50 cm³ to 125 cm³` or `4 kW to 11 kW` electric)**: Covers standard rental scooters including the `110cc` **Honda Wave**, `110cc` **Honda Blade**, `110cc` **Honda Vision**, `125cc` **Honda AirBlade**, and `125cc` **Honda Lead** (**150,000–220,000 VND/day** or `$6.00–$8.70 USD/day`). Requires a home **Category A1/A** license plus a **1968 IDP**.
* **Category A (`Over 125 cm³` or `> 11 kW` electric)**: Covers touring bikes such as the `150cc` **Honda XR150L**, `150cc` **Honda Winner X**, and `155cc` **Yamaha Exciter** (**300,000–650,000 VND/day** or `$12.00–$25.50 USD/day`). Riding a `150cc` **Honda XR150L** with only an `A1` (`<= 125cc`) stamp triggers a **6,000,000 to 8,000,000 VND** (`$240–$315 USD`) fine.

### Beware of the $49 USD Online "International Driver's Document" Scam

Search social media or Google Ads for a Vietnam motorbike permit, and dozens of slick websites (`International Automobile Association / IAA`, `International Driver's Document / IDD`, or `IDL-Vietnam`) promise to ship an "instant digital and booklet IDP valid in 190 countries, including Vietnam, in 15 minutes for **`$39 to $69 USD`**."

Every one of those commercial websites sells a worthless piece of laminated cardboard. Under international law, only the government-designated motoring authority in the country that issued your domestic license can issue a genuine IDP. Handing a **`$49 USD`** internet booklet to a **CSGT** officer at **Km 0 in Ha Giang** or along **Mui Ne's DT706B coastal road** results in an immediate **2,000,000 to 8,000,000 VND** (`$80 to $315 USD`) unlicensed-driving fine and a **7-day vehicle seizure**.

### Zero-Tolerance Alcohol Enforcement (`0.00% BAC`) and Key Checkpoints

Vietnam enforces a strict **0.00% Blood Alcohol Concentration (`Nồng độ cồn = 0`)** law. Drinking two cans of **Bia Hà Nội** or corn wine (`Rượu ngô` / happy water) at a `21:30` homestay dinner in Dong Van frequently leaves enough residual alcohol on your breath at `08:00` the next morning to trigger a **2,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND** (`$80 to $400 USD`) fine and immediate **7-day bike seizure**.

Expect daily **CSGT** checkpoints at four major tourist corridors in 2026:

* **Ha Giang Loop (`QL4C`)**: Permanent and mobile police teams station themselves at the **Km 0 Milestone** in Ha Giang City, the base of **Bac Sum Pass (`Km 12–15`)**, the exit of **Quan Ba Heaven Gate (`Km 46`)**, and the town entrances to **Dong Van** and **Meo Vac**.
* **Mui Ne (`Binh Thuan Province`)**: Officers patrol the wide **DT706B coastal road** and the **Hon Rom** desert highway (**25 km** northeast of Mui Ne beach) every morning between `07:00 and 11:00`, stopping nearly all foreign riders heading toward the **White Sand Dunes (`Bàu Trắng`)**.
* **Da Lat (`Lam Dong Province`)**: Regular license and breathalyzer checks operate at the foot of **Prenn Pass (`QL20`)**, **Mimosa Pass**, and the multi-lane roundabouts bordering **Xuan Huong Lake**.
* **Hai Van Pass (`Da Nang–Hue Border`)**: Checkpoints sit at the southern approach north of **Da Nang Bay** and the northern descent into **Lang Co Lagoon** across the **21 km** mountain pass.

## 4. The $50,000+ USD Travel Insurance Exclusion & 3 Legal Alternatives

A **2,000,000 to 4,000,000 VND** (`$80 to $160 USD`) traffic ticket is minor compared to the medical insurance trap. Every international travel insurance policy—including **SafetyWing**, **World Nomads**, **Allianz**, **AXA**, and **Cover-More**—contains a mandatory **"Motorbike & Scooter Licensing Clause."**

Read the fine print carefully. Coverage for motorbike accidents applies **only** if you hold both a valid motorcycle license in your home country for that specific engine displacement **and** a locally legal permit in the host country (**1968 IDP** in Vietnam). If an American, Australian, Canadian, or unlicensed European rider slides out on wet limestone pavement and suffers a compound fracture or head injury, the insurer requests your **Category A/A1 license and 1968 IDP** before approving hospital guarantees. Without both documents, the claim is denied. Surgical fixation at **FV Hospital in Ho Chi Minh City** or **Vinmec International Hospital in Hanoi** runs **`$12,000 to $35,000 USD`**, while an ICU air-ambulance jet evacuation to Bangkok or Singapore costs **`$50,000 to $120,000 USD`**.

### Three 100% Legal Ways to Ride or Explore Vietnam Without a 1968 IDP

Holders of a **1949 IDP** (`US, AU, CA, NZ, IE, JP, SG`) or a car-only license have three 100% legal options that preserve medical insurance coverage:

1. **Rent a Sub-50cc Petrol Scooter (`49cc`) or `< 4 kW` Electric Scooter**: Two-wheelers under `50cc`—such as the **49cc Honda Cub**, **SYM Elite 50**, or **VinFast Evo200 Lite** e-scooter (`< 50 km/h`, **150 km** range)—require **no driver's license** for riders aged `>= 16`. Rentals cost **120,000 to 180,000 VND/day** (`$4.75 to $7.10 USD/day`) in **Hoi An**, **Da Nang**, **Ninh Binh**, and **Phu Quoc**. Flat coastal roads and rice-paddy lanes suit them best, whereas steep Ha Giang mountain passes require larger engines.
2. **Ride Pillion with a Licensed Local Easy Rider (`Ha Giang Loop & Hai Van Pass`)**: Hiring an **Easy Rider** costs **1,200,000 to 1,600,000 VND/day** (`$48 to $64 USD/day`) on the **Ha Giang Loop** or **Hai Van Pass**. Your local driver holds a valid Vietnamese **Category A1/A** license. Your medical insurance stays 100% valid as a helmeted passenger (`người ngồi sau`).
3. **Combine GrabBike / Xanh SM Bike with Chartered Private Cars**: Short city hops on **GrabBike** or **Xanh SM Bike** cost **15,000 to 45,000 VND** (`$0.60 to $1.80 USD` for **2 to 6 km**). Chartering a **4-seat car with a private driver** for **80 to 150 km** countryside routes runs **1,100,000 to 1,600,000 VND/day** (`$43 to $64 USD/day`).

## 5. Step-by-Step Protocol If Stopped at a CSGT Police Checkpoint

Even fully legal riders carrying a **1968 Vienna IDP** and a home **Category A** license are waved into **Ha Giang**, **Mui Ne**, or **Da Lat** checkpoints for inspection. Follow four steps:

1. **Slow Down and Pull Inside the Cones**: Never swerve around a **CSGT** officer. Pull over smoothly, switch off the ignition (`tắt máy`), and flip up your visor.
2. **Pocket the Key and Show Papers**: Slip the ignition key into your pocket. Present your **1968 Vienna IDP**, **physical home motorcycle license**, laminated **Blue Card copy (`Giấy đăng ký xe`)**, and **Compulsory Insurance slip (`Bảo hiểm TNDS`)**.
3. **Verify Official Citations (`Biên bản vi phạm`)**: Legal riders clear checkpoints in **60 seconds**. Unlicensed riders receive a written citation under **Decree 168/2024/ND-CP** payable via `dichvucong.gov.vn` or a **State Treasury bank**, plus a **7-day bike impoundment**.
4. **Call Your Rental Shop**: Reputable shops (`QT Motorbikes`, `Jasmine`, `Style Motorbikes`, `Tigit`) print a 24/7 hotline on the keychain. Call them before signing any Vietnamese form.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I legally ride a 110cc or 125cc scooter in Vietnam with a US (AAA), Australian, or Canadian (CAA) International Driving Permit?
No. That permit is not valid in Vietnam. The **United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Ireland, Japan, and Singapore** only issue **1949 Geneva Convention IDPs**, whereas Vietnam exclusively recognizes **1968 Vienna Convention IDPs**. Even with a heavy-motorcycle endorsement on your US state or Australian license, a 1949 IDP carries zero legal standing here. Stick to **49cc petrol scooters**, **VinFast Evo200 Lite (`< 50 km/h`)** e-scooters, or a licensed **Easy Rider** driver.

### Do I need a driver's license to rent an electric scooter or a 50cc moped in Hoi An, Ninh Binh, or Phu Quoc?
No license is required. Under Vietnam's **2024 Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety**, any two-wheeler with a petrol engine **under 50cc (`49 cm³`)** or an electric motor **under 4 kW** capped below **50 km/h** is classified as a moped (`Xe gắn máy`). Riders aged **16 or older** can legally rent one for **120,000 to 180,000 VND/day** nationwide without a license or IDP. Always wear a buckled helmet and obey the **0.00% BAC** alcohol limit.

### How can long-term expats or digital nomads obtain a legal Vietnamese motorbike license?
Holders of a **Temporary Residence Card (`TRC`)** or visa valid for **at least 3 months (`90 days`)** have two legal paths. If you already hold a national motorcycle license from home, you can convert it directly into a Vietnamese **Category A1 or A** PET card at the **Department of Transport (`Sở GTVT`) or Traffic Police** for **135,000 VND** (`$5.30 USD`) with no riding test. If you only hold a foreign car license (`Category B`), you skip the written theory exam and only take the **15-minute figure-eight practical riding test (`Vòng số 8`)** at a local test center (`~1,200,000–1,800,000 VND` total).
