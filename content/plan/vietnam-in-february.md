February delivers some of the most stable and rewarding travel weather across Vietnam. Dry sunshine warms the southern beaches, while the central coast sheds its winter monsoon rains. In the northern mountains, warming days coax plum and peach orchards into bloom across limestone valleys.

However, February travel hinges completely on the timing of Tết Nguyên Đán (Lunar New Year). The country shifts across three distinct behavioral phases before, during, and after the holiday. Understanding this cultural rhythm ensures a seamless journey across transport arteries and historic quarters.

## February Regional Weather Dynamics

Weather patterns shift favorably across all three geographic zones throughout February. Increasing sunshine and falling precipitation indices create prime conditions for multi-region itineraries.

### Northern Provinces: Spring Warming, Blossom Season, and Gradual Clearing

Winter gradually relaxes its grip across the north by mid-February. Daytime temperatures in [Hanoi](/destinations/hanoi/) range between 15°C and 22°C, with the mercury occasionally touching 24°C during late afternoons. While overcast mornings and light humidity persist near 75% humidity, prolonged spells of clear spring sunshine emerge by late February.

Mountain highlands undergo a visual transformation. In Moc Chau, hillsides fill with white plum blossoms (*hoa mận*) under crisp 12–18°C skies. Meanwhile, pink peach blossoms (*hoa đào*) cover traditional villages across Sa Pa and Bac Ha. In Ha Giang, daytime temperatures along QL4C climb above 15°C. Farmers begin preparing mountain terraces for the spring planting season at 1,200m elevation. Over in Ninh Binh, limestone waterways average 16–21°C, while Ha Long Bay records 17–22°C with significantly fewer fog delays than in mid-winter.

### Central Provinces: Rain Tapering Off and Coastal Warmth

Central Vietnam experiences its most welcome climate shift of the winter during February. Rainfall drops drastically from January levels. Da Nang and [Hoi An](/destinations/hoi-an/) record comfortable 20–26°C warmth, receiving under 40mm rainfall across the entire month.

Ancient streets in Hoi An dry out completely, making cycling through surrounding rice paddies pleasant under mild sunshine. North of the Hai Van Pass, Hue warms to 19–24°C as persistent winter drizzle dissipates into periodic cloud breaks. Further south in Khanh Hoa province, Nha Trang basks in 23–29°C temperatures with calm blue seas and 20mm monthly precipitation. Up on the Lang Biang plateau, Da Lat maintains pleasant 14–24°C days, accompanied by cool 12°C evenings and blooming cherry blossom trees (*mai anh đào*).

### Southern Provinces: Peak Dry Season and Tropical Heat

Southern Vietnam experiences peak dry season conditions with consistent tropical warmth throughout February. Ho Chi Minh City reaches daily highs of 28–34°C with low humidity and virtually zero rainfall, registering less than 5mm across twenty-eight days. Mornings and evenings remain comfortable for walking through District 1 and Cho Lon.

Offshore islands and coastal stretches enjoy optimal conditions for outdoor recreation. Phu Quoc marks its warmest and driest weather of the calendar year, with temperatures steady at 28–32°C. Sea conditions remain calm and clear, offering 15 to 20 meters underwater visibility for snorkeling and scuba diving. Mui Ne records 25–31°C with reliable coastal breezes, while Con Dao registers 26–31°C as seasonal winter winds drop, calming southern boat passages.

## Tet Holiday Mechanics and Travel Phases

Tết Nguyên Đán follows the lunar calendar and dictates commercial life across the country. Key dates for upcoming lunar cycles include:
- **2026:** February 17 (Mùng 1 Tết Bính Ngọ)
- **2027:** February 6 (Mùng 1 Tết Đinh Mùi)
- **2028:** January 26 (Mùng 1 Tết Mậu Thân)

Traveling in February requires navigating the three distinct phases of the Tet festival cycle.

> **Editorial Strategy Callout:** Do not attempt multi-city transit during the peak three days of Tet. Instead, settle into a comfortable coastal or resort base before Mùng 1 to enjoy festive decorations without logistical stress.

### Phase 1: Tuần Trước Tết (The Pre-Holiday Surge)

During the week leading up to Tet, domestic transport experiences peak migration. Millions of workers journey from industrial centers in Ho Chi Minh City, Binh Duong, and Dong Nai toward northern and central hometowns. Domestic flights between SGN, HAN, and DAD sell out weeks in advance, with economy fares surging past 3,500,000 VND ($140 USD).

Commercial activities peak as families purchase peach blossom trees, kumquat bushes, and holiday provisions. By the 28th and 29th days of the lunar month (28–29 Tết), between 60% and 75% of private family businesses, local markets, and street food stalls begin shuttering for their annual holiday.

### Phase 2: Mùng 1 Đến Mùng 3 (The Core Holiday Days)

The first three days of the lunar year represent sacred family time. Major metropolitan areas like Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City transform into quiet, peaceful enclaves free of typical motorbike traffic. Streets are adorned with vibrant red banners, yellow chrysanthemums, and national flags.

Most independent restaurants and boutique shops remain closed during this window. However, international hotels, major chain convenience stores, and select tourist establishments continue operating. Open restaurants often apply a holiday surcharge (*phụ thu Tết*) of 15% to 30% to compensate staff working on national rest days. Travelers who observe the holiday witness quiet, ceremonial city life at its most authentic.

### Phase 3: Mùng 4 Đến Mùng 8 (The Domestic Tourism Surge)

Beginning on Mùng 4, millions of domestic holidaymakers embark on spring vacations and temple pilgrimages (*du xuân*). Tourist destinations experience major visitor surges. Phu Quoc, Sa Pa, Da Lat, Ninh Binh, and the Perfume Pagoda (Chùa Hương) see full hotel occupancy and crowded scenic spots through Mùng 8.

Interprovincial expressways and regional airports handle heavy inbound traffic. Transport operators resume normal timetables, street markets reopen, and everyday commercial life quickly returns to full pace across urban centers.

## Optimal February Itineraries

Aligning your travel itinerary with the Tet calendar guarantees smooth connections and great weather. Two strategic route concepts prove highly effective during February.

### The Static Holiday Base Itinerary

If your travel dates coincide directly with Mùng 1 through Mùng 3, avoid intercity travel by establishing a single base.

Book an extended 5-day stay in Hoi An or Da Nang. Spend the holiday cycling quiet heritage lanes, strolling along An Bang Beach in 24°C sunshine, and observing local family altars. Hotels organize special Tet banquets featuring traditional sticky rice cakes (*bánh chưng* and *bánh tét*). Alternatively, book a private resort on Phu Quoc to enjoy calm beaches and uninterrupted hospitality. Another popular option is an overnight cruise in Ha Long Bay, where marine itineraries operate without disruption.

### The Post-Tet Grand Circuit

Arriving immediately after Mùng 4 opens up exceptional nationwide travel conditions.

Begin in Hanoi as city businesses reopen, spending two days savoring local dishes and egg coffee at 19°C. Fly or take the Thống Nhất express train 790 km south to Da Nang, continuing by private car to Hoi An for two sunny days along the Thu Bon River.

Fly south to Ho Chi Minh City for two days of architectural tours in warm 32°C sunshine. Connect by express limousine 150 km southeast to Vung Tau or board a 50-minute domestic flight directly to Phu Quoc for three days of pristine island relaxation. From there, return to Hanoi or venture north to the Ha Giang Loop, where mountain temperatures exceed 15°C and blooming peach orchards line the limestone passes.

## Cultural Etiquette and Practical Logistics

Experiencing Tet requires thoughtful cultural awareness and practical financial preparation. Small gestures ensure smooth interactions with local communities during the festive season.

Cash management requires early preparation. Bank branches and money changers close for up to seven consecutive days during Tet. Automated teller machines in tourist districts frequently run out of currency due to heavy holiday withdrawals. Withdraw between 3,000,000 VND and 5,000,000 VND ($120–$200 USD) in cash before 28 Tết to cover incidental expenses, taxi fares, and food purchases. Consult our comprehensive guide to [Money, Cash, Cards & ATMs](/plan/money-cash-cards-atms/) for banking hours and foreign card policies.

Embrace the red envelope tradition (*lì xì*). Carrying crisp 20,000 VND, 50,000 VND, or 100,000 VND notes inside small red envelopes allows you to gift hotel housekeeping, drivers, or service staff during Mùng 1 through Mùng 3. On Mùng 1, avoid wearing all-black or all-white clothing, as these colors are associated with mourning. Instead, choose red, yellow, or bright colors symbolizing luck and prosperity. Dining options may be limited for plant-based eaters during holiday closures, so consult our [Vietnam Vegetarian Travel Guide](/plan/vietnam-vegetarian-travel-guide/) for temple dining and market stalls.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is February a good time to travel to Vietnam?
Yes, February is one of the best months to travel throughout Vietnam. Central and southern regions enjoy warm, dry sunshine, while northern provinces warm up and celebrate the spring blossom season.

### How do I handle meals when restaurants close for Tet?
International hotels and major western restaurants remain open during Tet. Convenience stores like Circle K and 7-Eleven stock hot meals and essentials throughout the holidays. Many vegetarian restaurants associated with Buddhist pagodas open specifically to serve temple pilgrims during the first lunar days.

### What are the exact dates for Tet in upcoming years?
Tet dates vary with the lunar calendar. Tet falls on February 17, 2026 (Year of the Horse), February 6, 2027 (Year of the Goat), and January 26, 2028 (Year of the Monkey).

### Do museums and tourist attractions close during Tet?
Major state museums, historic monuments, and government offices close from 29 Tết through Mùng 2 or Mùng 3. Outdoor sites, beaches, temples, and private theme parks generally remain accessible, reopening fully by Mùng 4.

### Are domestic trains and sleeper buses running during Tet?
Yes, Thống Nhất express trains and select interprovincial sleeper buses run on modified holiday schedules. However, ticket availability is extremely constrained. Book rail tickets via dsvn.vn at least 4 to 6 weeks before your journey to secure confirmed berths.
