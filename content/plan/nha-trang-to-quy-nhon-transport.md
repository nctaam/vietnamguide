The 215-kilometer journey along Vietnam's South-Central coast between Nha Trang and Quy Nhon traces one of the country's most scenic coastal corridors. Linking Khanh Hoa province with Binh Dinh province via coastal Phu Yen, this route navigates past granite headlands, blue lagoons, and coastal fishing coves framed by the foothills of the Truong Son mountain range.

Travelers can choose between the North-South Reunification Express train—which hugs the sea cliffs and cuts through Deo Ca Pass—and high-frequency VIP limousine buses that cruise along Highway 1A and National Highway 1D. Evaluating these options depends on whether you prefer panoramic daytime train views or direct downtown-to-downtown bus transfers without changing vehicles at the Dieu Tri junction.

### The Rail Route and Onboard Experience

Trains depart from **Nha Trang Railway Station** (*Ga Nha Trang*), located at 17 Thai Nguyen street near Long Son Pagoda. Built in 1936, the station's distinct French Art Deco facade opens onto a circular turnaround platform designed to reverse locomotives.

As the train rolls north, the tracks skirt Nha Trang Bay, traverse the green rice plains of Ninh Hoa, and pass the white sand beach of Dai Lanh. The engineering highlight of the route occurs around kilometer 100, where the railway hugs the cliffside above Vung Ro Bay and navigates through multiple stone tunnels piercing **Deo Ca Pass**. Passengers seated on the right side of the carriage enjoy unhindered panoramic views of turquoise water, offshore fishing vessels, and rocky outcrops.

Air-conditioned soft seats (*ngồi mềm điều hòa*) feature individual reading lights, electrical charging outlets, and reclining seatbacks. Tickets range from 150,000 to 230,000 VND ($6.00–$9.20 USD). For additional comfort, passengers can book a bed in a four-berth sleeper cabin (*giường nằm khoang 4*) for 250,000 to 350,000 VND ($10–$14 USD). Onboard food service carts push down the aisles offering hot black coffee (*cà phê đen*), boiled sweet corn, mineral water, and boxed lunches (*cơm hộp*) for 35,000 to 50,000 VND.

### The Dieu Tri Station Transfer

The primary main-line railway does not terminate in downtown Quy Nhon. Instead, long-distance express trains halt at **Dieu Tri Railway Station** (*Ga Diêu Trì*), situated in Tuy Phuoc district, roughly 11 kilometers west of Quy Nhon's beachfront.

Upon exiting Dieu Tri Station, travelers have two straightforward transfer options:

1. **Metered Taxi / Ride-Hailing**: Taxis operated by Mai Linh, Sun Taxi, or Xanh SM electric vehicles wait outside the station plaza. The 20-minute ride to central Quy Nhon hotels costs between 150,000 and 200,000 VND ($6.00–$8.00 USD).
2. **Local Public Bus T22**: Departing every 20 to 30 minutes from the main road 150 meters outside the station entrance, Bus T22 connects Dieu Tri with the Quy Nhon Central Bus Station on Tay Son street. The fare is 20,000 VND ($0.80 USD) per passenger.

> [!NOTE]
> Certain regional trains (such as the SQN branch line service) occasionally pull directly into Quy Nhon Station (*Ga Quy Nhơn*) on Le Hong Phong street in the city center. However, these branch trains operate on limited seasonal timetables. Always check your ticket destination code: **DTR** designates Dieu Tri, whereas **QNH** indicates central Quy Nhon.

### Highway Route and Comfort

Limousine coaches utilize National Highway 1A and bypass mountain passes via modern engineering projects:

- **Deo Ca Road Tunnel**: Opened in 2017, this 4.1-kilometer dual-bore tunnel cuts through the granite barrier between Khanh Hoa and Phu Yen, eliminating 20 minutes of steep winding mountain grades and sharp curves.
- **Cu Mong Road Tunnel**: Opened in 2019, this 2.6-kilometer tunnel bypasses Cu Mong Pass, expediting transit between Song Cau and Quy Nhon.
- **Highway 1D Coastal Link**: Rather than remaining on inland bypasses, passenger vans follow National Highway 1D, a cliffside four-lane road entering southern Quy Nhon with sweeping views across Xuan Dai Bay and Queen's Beach (*Bãi Trứng*).

Limousine vans provide wide leather captain chairs, USB device charging ports, air conditioning, and complimentary bottled water. The direct road journey takes 4.0 to 4.5 hours, including one brief 15-minute rest stop near Tuy Hoa for refreshments. Drivers will drop passengers off at centrally located hotels along Xuan Dieu or An Duong Vuong beachfront streets in Quy Nhon.

### Navigating the Old Deo Ca Pass

While commercial buses and container trucks must use the Deo Ca tunnel, motorcyclists can legally choose between the tunnel or the historic mountain pass road. The old pass spans twelve kilometers of winding asphalt climbing to an altitude of 333 meters above sea level. 

Because heavy logistics traffic has shifted almost entirely to the tunnel, the old pass is now quiet and relatively empty. Riders enjoy sweeping hairpin turns carved directly into granite bluffs, with panoramas extending over the deepwater expanse of Vung Ro Bay and aquaculture lobster cages below. Riders must maintain moderate speeds and watch for scattered gravel along roadside drainage shoulders.

### Highlights Along the Route

A self-drive motorbike journey allows riders to explore regional landmarks:

- **Dai Lanh Cape & Mui Dien Lighthouse (*Hải Đăng Mũi Điện*)**: Diverging east from the base of Deo Ca Pass onto coastal road DT657 leads to Bai Mon beach and Mui Dien. Erected by French colonial engineers in 1890, the 26-meter granite lighthouse perches 110 meters above the sea. Hiking the stone pathway to the summit rewards travelers with views of the easternmost headland of mainland Vietnam (admission: 20,000 VND; scooter parking: 10,000 VND).
- **Tuy Hoa City & Da Rang River**: Crossing the two-kilometer Da Rang bridge brings riders into Tuy Hoa, the capital of Phu Yen province. Stop at **Quán Bà Tám** (289 Le Duan street) for a bowl of *mắt cá ngừ đại dương* (ocean tuna eyes braised in Chinese medicinal herbs, 45,000 to 60,000 VND).
- **Gành Đá Đĩa (The Cliff of Stone Plates)**: Located 35 kilometers north of Tuy Hoa in An Ninh Dong commune, this UNESCO-recognized geological wonder consists of over 35,000 basalt columns formed by ancient volcanic lava flows cooling rapidly against ocean water. The polygonal dark stone pillars interlock like a giant honeycomb projecting into the sea (admission: 40,000 VND).
- **Highway 1D Coastal Approach**: Approaching Quy Nhon, the road tracks the edge of sea cliffs past quiet coves like Bai Xep, where boutique homestays and seafood restaurants sit steps from the surf.

### Railway Online Booking Mechanics

Vietnam Railways (*Đường sắt Việt Nam - ĐSVN*) manages ticket inventory through its official portal, `dsvn.vn`. While the site offers an interactive carriage map, foreign credit cards frequently fail 3D-Secure verification on domestic payment gateways. 

Travelers holding international credit cards often find it smoother to purchase tickets through verified regional booking platforms such as **Baolau** or **12Go Asia**. These platforms charge a small processing surcharge (typically $1.50 to $2.50 USD per ticket) and issue digital boarding vouchers with valid scannable QR codes that train conductors scan directly from smartphone screens.

Alternatively, travelers staying near central Nha Trang can visit the ticket counter at Nha Trang Railway Station (open 06:00 to 22:00 daily). Bring physical passports for all passengers, as full names and passport numbers are printed directly onto train tickets.

### Shipping a Motorbike on the Train

Travelers riding a rented or purchased motorcycle who want to skip the highway section can transport their two-wheeler aboard Vietnam Railways cargo cars:

1. Ride the motorcycle to the luggage freight office (*Phòng hành lý*) beside Nha Trang Railway Station at least two hours before train departure.
2. Railway staff will inspect vehicle registration papers, drain the petrol tank completely for fire safety, and wrap mirrors, headlamps, and body fairings in protective cardboard and plastic wrap.
3. Shipping a standard 125cc scooter between Nha Trang and Dieu Tri costs approximately 250,000 to 350,000 VND ($10–$14 USD), plus a 50,000 VND packaging fee.
4. Collect the vehicle at Dieu Tri Station freight depot by presenting your shipping receipt, and refill fuel at the petrol station located 200 meters outside the station gate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the train or bus better between Nha Trang and Quy Nhon?
Both modes take roughly 4 hours. The train provides scenic ocean vistas along Deo Ca Pass, wide legroom, and freedom to walk around, but arrives at Dieu Tri Station (11 km from central Quy Nhon). Limousine vans take slightly less time and drop passengers off directly in central Quy Nhon without requiring a second taxi connection.

### How much does the taxi cost from Dieu Tri Station to Quy Nhon city center?
Metered taxis (Mai Linh, Sun Taxi, or Xanh SM electric vehicles) charge between 150,000 and 200,000 VND ($6.00–$8.00 USD) for the 11-kilometer, 20-minute drive from Dieu Tri Station to downtown Quy Nhon hotels.

### Can I travel from Nha Trang to Quy Nhon by scooter?
Experienced riders can cover the 215-kilometer route in 5.5 to 6.5 hours via Highway 1A and Highway 1D. While coastal stretches through Dai Lanh and Song Cau are spectacular, heavy commercial container trucks on Highway 1A demand defensive riding, proper helmet gear, and caution.

### Which side of the train has the best ocean views from Nha Trang to Quy Nhon?
Reserve a seat on the **right-hand side** of the train relative to the forward direction of travel (facing north). This ensures unhindered views of the South China Sea, fishing lagoons, and rocky bays throughout the journey.

### Do I need to book Nha Trang to Quy Nhon transport tickets in advance?
During normal travel seasons, booking train or limousine tickets one to two days ahead is sufficient. During major Vietnamese holidays such as Tet (Lunar New Year) or National Day (late April / early May), rail tickets sell out weeks in advance.
