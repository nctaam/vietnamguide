# Vietnam Travel Insurance & Health Guide: What to Cover

Medical care in Vietnam splits sharply into two worlds. Walk into a public district hospital in a provincial town and you will face crowded corridors, cash-only payment desks, and minimal English signage. Step inside an accredited private international hospital in Hanoi, Da Nang, or Ho Chi Minh City—such as Hanoi French Hospital, FV Hospital, or Vinmec—and you will be treated by French-, Australian-, or US-trained physicians with 24/7 direct-billing desks and modern diagnostic imaging.

The catch is cost and policy fine print. An outpatient consultation at an international clinic runs 1,500,000 to 3,200,000 VND ($60 to $128 USD), overnight inpatient admission for severe dengue fever or acute gastroenteritis costs 12,000,000 to 25,000,000 VND ($480 to $1,000 USD) per night, and an emergency air-ambulance evacuation to Bangkok or Singapore reaches $15,000 to $80,000 USD. Worse, thousands of travelers discover after a crash on the Ha Giang Loop that riding a scooter without a 1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit automatically voids their insurance policy. This 2026 guide covers legal motorbike coverage rules, vetted hospitals, dengue and rabies protocols, and pharmacy survival across Vietnam.

## 1. The Motorbike & Ha Giang Loop Insurance Exclusion Clause

Road crashes involving 110cc to 150cc scooters account for the largest share of catastrophic tourist hospital bills in Vietnam. Every major travel insurer—including **World Nomads, SafetyWing, Allianz, AXA, Cover-More, and Insured Nomads**—inserts a strict **Legal Operation & Licensing Clause** into its policy wording. To claim medical or evacuation reimbursement after a motorbike accident as the rider, you must satisfy two legal conditions simultaneously:

1. **A Full Motorcycle License from Your Home Country**: A standard Class B/C passenger car driver's license does not qualify for two-wheelers above 50cc.
2. **A Valid 1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit (`IDP`)**: Because Vietnam is a signatory only to the **1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic**, it legally recognizes only the 1968-format IDP.

Here is the legal trap that catches citizens of the **United States, Australia, the United Kingdom (unless holding a specific 1968 UK permit), and Canada**: authorities in the US, Australia, and Canada issue **1949 Geneva Convention IDPs**, which are **not legally valid for driving in Vietnam**. While a traffic police checkpoint in Ha Giang or Mui Ne might only issue a 1,000,000 to 2,000,000 VND ($40 to $80 USD) roadside fine for riding without a valid 1968 IDP, your travel insurance underwriter will invoke the "driving illegally under local law" exclusion to deny 100% of a $25,000 USD orthopedic surgery or $65,000 USD medical evacuation claim.

> [!CAUTION]
> **How to Keep Your Insurance Valid on the Ha Giang Loop**: If you do not hold both a full home-country motorcycle license and a 1968 Vienna Convention IDP, **do not self-drive a motorbike above 50cc**. Book a licensed local **Easy Rider** and ride as a **pillion passenger** wearing a strapped helmet. Most major travel insurance policies cover medical injuries sustained as a passenger on a legally operated motorcycle taxi (`xe ôm`), GrabBike, or Easy Rider tour without requiring you to hold a motorcycle license yourself—always confirm "motorbike passenger coverage without CC cap" in your policy schedule before departure.

### Emergency Numbers vs GrabCar Hospital Transfers

Vietnam's national emergency telephone numbers are **115** for a medical ambulance, **113** for police, and **114** for fire and rescue. However, calling **115** connects you to a Vietnamese-speaking municipal dispatch center that routes patients to the nearest public hospital rather than an international facility. Unless the patient has a suspected spinal injury or requires in-transit life support (in which case you should call the private ambulance hotline of **Family Medical Practice `*9999`**, **FV Hospital `+84 28 5411 3500`**, or **Hanoi French Hospital `+84 24 3574 1111`**), jumping straight into a **GrabCar or metered Mai Linh / Vinasun taxi** to HFH, FV, or Vinmec is typically **20 to 30 minutes faster** in dense urban traffic.

## 3. Recommended Vaccines, Dengue Prevention & Rabies Protocol

Vietnam does not require mandatory vaccinations for travelers arriving directly from North America, Europe, Australia, or East Asia (a Yellow Fever certificate is required only if arriving from an endemic zone). Travel medicine clinics recommend verifying four core immunizations 4 to 6 weeks before your trip:

* **Routine & Foodborne Vaccines**: Ensure your **Tetanus-Diphtheria-Pertussis (`Tdap`)** booster is current within 10 years (vital for road scrapes), alongside **Hepatitis A** and **Typhoid** (both transmitted via contaminated water or food handling) and **Hepatitis B**. Travelers spending more than 30 days trekking or sleeping in rural agricultural valleys during the May to October rainy season should also discuss **Japanese Encephalitis**.
* **Dengue Fever Prevention & The NSAID Warning**: While malaria has been largely eliminated from Vietnam's tourist circuit, **Dengue Fever** is an active year-round risk inside major cities including Hanoi, Da Nang, Hoi An, and Ho Chi Minh City. Dengue is transmitted by the black-and-white striped *Aedes aegypti* mosquito, which bites aggressively **during daylight hours** (especially 06:00 to 09:00 and 15:00 to 18:00). There is no preventive pill for dengue; apply tropical mosquito repellent containing **15% to 30% DEET** (buy local **Soffell** or **Remos** spray at any convenience store or pharmacy for **35,000 to 55,000 VND**, or $1.40 to $2.20 USD). If you develop a sudden high fever (39°C to 40°C), severe retro-orbital headache behind the eyes, and joint pain, **never take Ibuprofen (Advil/Nurofen), Aspirin, or Naproxen (`NSAIDs`)**. Because dengue lowers blood platelet counts, NSAIDs can trigger dangerous internal bleeding. Take **Paracetamol / Acetaminophen (`Efferalgan` or `Panadol` `500mg`)** only and visit an international hospital after 24 hours of fever for a rapid NS1 antigen blood test (600,000 to 1,200,000 VND).
* **Rabies Post-Exposure Protocol (`VNVC` & `Pasteur Institute`)**: If you are bitten or scratched by a dog, cat, or monkey (such as on Monkey Island in Cat Ba or Son Tra Peninsula in Da Nang), scrub the wound immediately with soap and running water for 15 minutes, apply povidone-iodine (`Betadine`), and seek a post-exposure rabies shot within 24 hours. Vietnam has an exceptional network of modern **VNVC Vaccination Centers** across every major city, plus the **Pasteur Institute (`167 Pasteur, District 3, Ho Chi Minh City`)** and **NIHE (`1 Yersin, Hai Ba Trung, Hanoi`)**, stocking cell-culture rabies vaccines (`Verorab` or `Abhayrab`) at **350,000 to 650,000 VND ($14 to $26 USD) per dose** alongside Rabies Immunoglobulin (`RIG`).

## 4. Navigating Vietnamese Pharmacies & Water/Food Hygiene

Vietnam's retail pharmacy sector is dominated by three reliable, air-conditioned national chains: **Nhà Thuốc Long Châu (FPT Long Chau)**, **Pharmacity**, and **An Khang**. Always use these chains rather than un-air-conditioned street-corner stalls where heat can degrade medication potency. Pharmacists sell most everyday travel remedies over the counter at transparent, barcode-scanned prices:

* **Stomach & Traveler's Diarrhea Kit**: Stock up on **Oresol** (Oral Rehydration Salts, **5,000 VND** per packet—mix with exact bottled water volume), **Smecta** (diosmectite clay sachets that coat the gut lining and bind toxins, **4,000 to 6,000 VND** per sachet), **Berberin** (traditional herbal antimicrobial tablets, **25,000 VND**), and **Imodium (`Loperamide 2mg`)** for emergency bus or flight days. For severe bacterial traveler's diarrhea accompanied by fever, international clinics and major pharmacies dispense a 3-day course of **Azithromycin (`500mg`)** for **60,000 to 120,000 VND ($2.50 to $5 USD)**.
* **Tap Water & Street Ice Rules**: Municipal tap water in Vietnam is **not potable**. Drink only sealed bottled water (**LaVie, Aquafina, or Dasani** cost **6,000 to 10,000 VND** for 500ml and **12,000 to 15,000 VND** for 1.5L at Circle K or WinMart) or boiled water from your hotel kettle. However, you do not need to fear iced coffee (`cà phê sữa đá`) at busy cafés: look for **uniform cylindrical tube ice with a hollow hole through the center (`đá viên tinh khiết`)**, which is produced in UV-filtered commercial factories and delivered daily across Vietnam. Avoid only ragged, hand-chipped block ice at rural roadside stalls.

## 5. Step-by-Step Medical Claim & Direct-Billing Protocol

1. **Carry Physical & Digital Copies of Your Policy**: Save your insurance policy number and 24/7 emergency assistance phone number offline on your phone, and carry your physical passport to the hospital reception desk (Vietnamese hospitals require an original passport to register foreign patients).
2. **Call the Insurer's 24/7 Assistance Hotline Before Inpatient Admission**: For outpatient bills under $250 USD (6,250,000 VND), you typically pay upfront by Visa or Mastercard and submit itemized receipts (`hóa đơn đỏ` VAT invoice + signed medical report with ICD-10 diagnosis code) via the insurer's app. For overnight hospitalization or surgery, call your insurer immediately so they can fax a **Guarantee of Payment (`GOP`)** directly to Hanoi French Hospital, FV Hospital, or Vinmec.

## 6. Frequently Asked Questions

### What minimum coverage limits should my Vietnam travel insurance policy include?
Purchase a policy providing at least **$100,000 USD in emergency medical treatment coverage** and **$250,000 USD in emergency medical evacuation and repatriation**. Complex trauma care or intensive care admission in Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi, followed by an air-ambulance jet transfer to Bangkok or Singapore, easily exceeds $50,000 to $80,000 USD.

### Will SafetyWing or World Nomads cover me if I crash a rented scooter in Vietnam?
Only if you hold **both a valid full motorcycle license from your home country AND a 1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit (`IDP`)**, wear a helmet, and have zero blood-alcohol violation. If you hold a standard car license or a 1949-format IDP from the US, UK, Canada, or Australia, riding a 110cc+ scooter is illegal under Vietnamese law and voids your medical claim. Ride pillion behind a licensed Easy Rider instead.

### Is ice safe in Vietnamese street drinks and iced coffee?
Yes, in 95% of urban cafés and restaurants. Inspect the ice cubes inside your glass: **smooth, cylindrical tube ice with a hole down the middle (`đá bi` / `đá ống`)** is manufactured from purified, UV-treated water in regulated ice factories. Skip ice only if it looks like cloudy, chipped shards broken off a large block on the floor.

### Can I bring my own prescription medications into Vietnam?
Yes, travelers may bring up to a **30-day personal supply** of standard prescription medications (and up to 7 to 10 days of strictly controlled psychotropic or narcotic pain medications) provided every medication remains in its **original pharmacy-labeled packaging** accompanied by a printed English doctor's prescription matching the name on your passport.
