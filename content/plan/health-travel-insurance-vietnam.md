# Vietnam Travel Insurance & Health Guide: What to Cover

Medical care in Vietnam splits sharply into two worlds. Walk into a public district hospital in a provincial town 300 km from Hanoi and you will face crowded corridors, cash-only payment desks charging 300,000 VND to 800,000 VND ($12.00 to $32.00 USD), and minimal English signage. Step inside an accredited private international hospital in Hanoi, Da Nang, or Ho Chi Minh City (such as **Hanoi French Hospital**, **FV Hospital**, or **Vinmec**) and you will be treated by French-, Australian-, or US-trained physicians with 24/7 direct-billing desks and modern diagnostic imaging.

The catch is cost and policy fine print. An outpatient consultation at an international clinic runs 1,500,000 VND to 3,200,000 VND ($60.00 to $128.00 USD), while overnight inpatient admission for severe dengue fever or acute gastroenteritis costs 12,000,000 VND to 25,000,000 VND ($480.00 to $1,000.00 USD) per night. Emergency air-ambulance evacuation across 750 km to 1,100 km to Bangkok or Singapore reaches 375,000,000 VND to 2,000,000,000 VND ($15,000.00 to $80,000.00 USD).

Thousands of travelers also discover after a crash on the 350 km Ha Giang Loop that riding a 110cc scooter without a 1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit automatically voids their insurance policy. This 2026 empirical guide covers legal motorbike coverage rules, vetted hospitals, dengue and rabies protocols, and pharmacy survival across Vietnam.

## 1. The Motorbike & Ha Giang Loop Insurance Exclusion Clause

Road crashes involving 110cc to 150cc scooters account for the largest share of catastrophic tourist hospital bills in Vietnam. Every major travel insurer (including **World Nomads, SafetyWing, Allianz, AXA, Cover-More, and Insured Nomads**) inserts a strict **Legal Operation & Licensing Clause** into its policy wording. To claim medical or evacuation reimbursement after a motorbike accident as the rider, you must satisfy two legal conditions simultaneously:

1. **A Full Motorcycle License from Your Home Country:** A standard Class B or Class C passenger car driver's license does not qualify for two-wheelers above 50cc (or electric scooters exceeding 40 km/h).
2. **A Valid 1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit (IDP):** Because Vietnam is a signatory only to the **1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic**, it legally recognizes only the 1968-format IDP alongside your original home license.

This legal rule creates a serious trap for citizens of the **United States, Australia, Canada, and the United Kingdom** (unless holding a specific 1968 UK permit). Authorities in the US, Australia, and Canada issue **1949 Geneva Convention IDPs**, which are not legally valid for driving in Vietnam. Under updated traffic enforcement penalties, a police checkpoint in Ha Giang, Cat Ba (24 km Highway DT356), or Mui Ne can issue a roadside fine of 2,000,000 VND to 4,000,000 VND ($80.00 to $160.00 USD) for riding an under-175cc scooter without a valid license, or 6,000,000 VND to 8,000,000 VND ($240.00 to $320.00 USD) for motorcycles above 175cc.

Far worse than a 3,000,000 VND ($120.00 USD) traffic fine is the insurance consequence. Your travel insurance underwriter will invoke the "driving illegally under local law" exclusion to deny 100% of a 625,000,000 VND ($25,000.00 USD) orthopedic surgery claim or a 1,625,000,000 VND ($65,000.00 USD) medical evacuation flight.

> [!CAUTION]
> **How to Keep Your Insurance Valid on the Ha Giang Loop:** If you lack a full motorcycle license and a 1968 Vienna IDP, do not self-drive a motorbike above 50cc. Book a licensed **Easy Rider** (550,000 VND to 850,000 VND or $22.00 to $34.00 USD per day) and ride pillion wearing a strapped DOT helmet. Most travel insurers cover injuries sustained as a passenger on a motorcycle taxi (*xe ôm*), GrabBike, or Easy Rider tour without requiring a motorcycle license; verify "motorbike passenger coverage without CC cap" in your policy before departure.

### Emergency Numbers vs GrabCar Hospital Transfers

Vietnam's national emergency telephone numbers are **115** for a medical ambulance, **113** for police, and **114** for fire and rescue. Calling **115** connects you to a Vietnamese-speaking municipal dispatch center that routes patients to the nearest public hospital rather than an international facility.

Unless the patient has a suspected spinal fracture or requires in-transit cardiac support (in which case you should call the private ambulance hotline of **Family Medical Practice *9999**, **FV Hospital +84 28 5411 3500**, or **Hanoi French Hospital +84 24 3574 1111**, costing 1,500,000 VND to 3,500,000 VND or $60.00 to $140.00 USD within a 15 km city radius), taking a **GrabCar or metered Mai Linh taxi** (80,000 VND to 180,000 VND or $3.20 to $7.20 USD for a 5 km to 8 km urban transfer) reaches HFH, FV, or Vinmec 20 minutes to 30 minutes faster.

If an accident occurs in a remote highland province such as Ha Giang (300 km north of Hanoi) or Sa Pa (315 km northwest of Hanoi), patients receive initial wound splinting and X-rays at the provincial public hospital (500,000 VND to 2,500,000 VND, or $20.00 to $100.00 USD) before arranging a 5.5 hours to 6.5 hours private ambulance transfer down Expressway CT05 to Hanoi French Hospital or Vinmec Times City (8,500,000 VND to 15,000,000 VND, or $340.00 to $600.00 USD).

## 3. Recommended Vaccines, Dengue Prevention & Rabies Protocol

Vietnam does not require mandatory vaccinations for travelers arriving directly from North America, Europe, Australia, or East Asia (a Yellow Fever certificate is required only if arriving from an endemic zone). Travel medicine clinics recommend verifying four core immunizations 4 weeks to 6 weeks before departure:

### Routine & Foodborne Immunizations
Verify that your **Tetanus-Diphtheria-Pertussis (Tdap)** booster is current within 10 years (essential for road scrapes on gravel mountain passes), alongside **Hepatitis A** and **Typhoid** (both transmitted via contaminated water or uncooked food handling) and **Hepatitis B**. Travelers spending more than 30 days trekking or sleeping in rural agricultural valleys like Ba Be Lake (220 km north of Hanoi) or the Mekong Delta during the May to October monsoon season should also consider **Japanese Encephalitis**.

### Dengue Fever Prevention & The NSAID Warning
While malaria has been largely eliminated from Vietnam's tourist trail, **Dengue Fever** remains an active year-round risk inside major cities including Hanoi, Da Nang, Hoi An, and Ho Chi Minh City, peaking between June and November when monthly rainfall reaches 250 mm to 350 mm at 82% humidity. Dengue is transmitted by the black-and-white striped *Aedes aegypti* mosquito, which bites aggressively during daylight hours (especially 06:00 to 09:00 and 15:00 to 18:00).

Apply tropical mosquito repellent containing 15% to 30% DEET every 4.0 hours; local **Soffell** or **Remos** 60ml spray bottles cost 35,000 VND to 55,000 VND ($1.40 to $2.20 USD) at any pharmacy. If you develop a sudden high fever (39°C to 40°C), severe retro-orbital headache behind the eyes, and muscle pain, **never take Ibuprofen (Advil/Nurofen), Aspirin, or Naproxen (NSAIDs)**.

Because dengue lowers blood platelet counts, NSAIDs can trigger severe internal bleeding. Take **Paracetamol / Acetaminophen (Efferalgan or Panadol 500mg, 35,000 VND or $1.40 USD per box)** only and visit an international hospital after 24 hours of fever for a rapid NS1 antigen and CBC platelet blood panel (600,000 VND to 1,200,000 VND, or $24.00 to $48.00 USD).

### Rabies Post-Exposure Protocol (VNVC & Pasteur Institute)
If you are bitten or scratched by a dog, cat, or macaque monkey (such as on Monkey Island in Cat Ba or Son Tra Peninsula 10 km from Da Nang), scrub the wound immediately with soap and running water for 15 minutes, apply povidone-iodine (*Betadine*, 30,000 VND or $1.20 USD), and seek a post-exposure rabies shot within 24 hours.

Vietnam operates a nationwide network of modern **VNVC Vaccination Centers** in every major city (open 07:30 to 17:00), plus the **Pasteur Institute** (167 Pasteur, District 3, Ho Chi Minh City, 2.0 km from Ben Thanh Market) and **NIHE** (01 Yersin, Hai Ba Trung, Hanoi, 1.8 km south of Hoan Kiem Lake). These centers stock WHO-approved cell-culture rabies vaccines (*Verorab* or *Abhayrab*) at 350,000 VND to 650,000 VND ($14.00 to $26.00 USD) per dose alongside Equine or Human Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG, 1,800,000 VND to 3,500,000 VND or $72.00 to $140.00 USD).

## 4. Navigating Vietnamese Pharmacies & Water/Food Hygiene

Vietnam's retail pharmacy sector is anchored by three reliable, air-conditioned national chains kept at 24°C storage temperatures: **Nhà Thuốc Long Châu (FPT Long Chau)**, **Pharmacity**, and **An Khang**. Use these chains rather than open-air street stalls where 34°C heat degrades medication potency. Pharmacists dispense everyday travel remedies over the counter at barcode-scanned prices:

* **Stomach & Traveler's Diarrhea Kit:** Stock up on **Oresol** (Oral Rehydration Salts, 5,000 VND or $0.20 USD per packet; dissolve in 200ml or 1,000ml of bottled water as printed), **Smecta** (diosmectite clay sachets that coat the gut lining, 4,000 VND to 6,000 VND or $0.16 to $0.24 USD per sachet), **Berberin** (herbal antimicrobial tablets, 25,000 VND or $1.00 USD per bottle), and **Imodium (Loperamide 2mg, 30,000 VND or $1.20 USD)** for 5.0 hours bus days. For bacterial diarrhea with fever, clinics dispense a 3-day course of **Azithromycin (500mg)** for 60,000 VND to 120,000 VND ($2.40 to $4.80 USD) plus **Enterogermina** probiotics (160,000 VND or $6.40 USD per 20-vial box).

* **Seasonal Air Quality & Heat Protection:** Between December and March, thermal inversions in Hanoi and the Red River Delta can push PM2.5 Air Quality Index (AQI) readings between 150 and 220; Long Chau and Pharmacity sell 3M N95/KN95 particulate respirators for 15,000 VND to 35,000 VND ($0.60 to $1.40 USD) each, plus sterile saline eye drops (*Natri Clorid 0.9%*, 10,000 VND or $0.40 USD) and broad-spectrum SPF 50+ sunscreen (180,000 VND to 320,000 VND or $7.20 to $12.80 USD).

* **Tap Water & Street Ice Rules:** Municipal tap water in Vietnam is not potable. Drink only sealed bottled water (**LaVie, Aquafina, or Dasani** cost 6,000 VND to 10,000 VND or $0.24 to $0.40 USD for 500ml and 12,000 VND to 15,000 VND or $0.48 to $0.60 USD for 1.5L at Circle K or WinMart) or boiled water from your hotel kettle. Iced coffee (*cà phê sữa đá*, 25,000 VND to 45,000 VND or $1.00 to $1.80 USD) at urban cafés is safe when served with **uniform cylindrical tube ice with a hollow hole through the center (*đá viên tinh khiết*)** produced in UV-filtered plants; avoid hand-chipped block ice.

## 5. Step-by-Step Medical Claim & Direct-Billing Protocol

1. **Carry Physical Passport & Digital Policy Documents:** Save your insurance policy PDF and 24/7 emergency assistance phone number offline on your smartphone, and bring your physical passport to the hospital reception desk (Vietnamese regulations require an original passport to register foreign patients within 15 minutes of arrival).

2. **Pay Upfront for Outpatient Visits Under $250.00 USD (6,250,000 VND):** For routine consultations, blood panels, or minor stitches costing 1,800,000 VND to 5,500,000 VND ($72.00 to $220.00 USD), pay by Visa or Mastercard at the cashier and request three mandatory claim documents before leaving: the official red VAT invoice (*hóa đơn đỏ* / electronic VAT receipt), the itemized pharmacy breakdown, and the physician's signed medical report listing the exact **ICD-10 diagnosis code**.

3. **Activate Guarantee of Payment (GOP) for Inpatient Admission:** If a physician recommends overnight admission (14,000,000 VND to 25,000,000 VND or $560.00 to $1,000.00 USD per night) or surgery, call your insurer's 24/7 assistance hotline immediately and hand the hospital's insurance coordinator your policy number so the insurer can transmit a direct-billing GOP within 2.0 hours to 4.0 hours.

## 6. Frequently Asked Questions

### What minimum coverage limits should my Vietnam travel insurance policy include?
Purchase a policy providing at least **$100,000.00 USD (2,500,000,000 VND) in emergency medical treatment coverage** and **$250,000.00 USD (6,250,000,000 VND) in emergency medical evacuation and repatriation**. Complex trauma surgery or intensive care admission in Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi (25,000,000 VND to 45,000,000 VND or $1,000.00 to $1,800.00 USD per day), followed by a 2.5 hours air-ambulance jet transfer across 1,000 km to Bangkok or Singapore, routinely totals $50,000.00 to $80,000.00 USD.

### Will SafetyWing or World Nomads cover me if I crash a rented scooter in Vietnam?
Only if you hold **both a valid full motorcycle license from your home country AND a 1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit (IDP)**, wear a strapped helmet, and have zero blood-alcohol violation (Vietnam enforces a strict 0.00% blood-alcohol limit for all riders). If you hold a standard car license or a 1949-format IDP from the US, UK, Canada, or Australia, riding a 110cc scooter is illegal under Vietnamese law and voids your medical claim; hire a licensed Easy Rider (550,000 VND to 850,000 VND or $22.00 to $34.00 USD per day) and ride pillion instead.

### Is ice safe in Vietnamese street drinks and iced coffee?
Yes, in 95% of urban cafés and restaurants. Inspect the ice cubes inside your glass: **smooth, cylindrical tube ice with a hole down the middle (*đá bi* / *đá ống*)** is manufactured from purified, UV-treated water in regulated ice factories. Skip ice only if it appears as cloudy, uneven shards chipped off a large floor block.

### Can I bring my own prescription medications into Vietnam?
Yes, travelers may bring up to a **30-day personal supply** of standard prescription medications (and up to a 7-day to 10-day supply of strictly controlled psychotropic or narcotic pain medications) through customs at Noi Bai (28 km from Hanoi) or Tan Son Nhat (8 km from central HCMC), provided every medication remains in its **original pharmacy-labeled container** accompanied by a printed English doctor's prescription matching your passport name.
