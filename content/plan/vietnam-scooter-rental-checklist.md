# Vietnam Scooter Rental Checklist (2026): Bike Checks

Bad brakes ruin trips fast. Across coastal hubs like [Da Nang](/destinations/da-nang) and [Hoi An](/destinations/hoi-an) or mountain gateways like [Ha Giang](/destinations/ha-giang), sidewalk boards advertise scooters for **120,000 to 180,000 VND per day** ($4.80 to $7.20 USD), yet fleet upkeep varies wildly between professional mechanics and casual guesthouse owners. One uninspected machine can leave you stranded with a snapped drive belt on a 10 percent grade or facing a **2,000,000 VND** ($80.00 USD) dispute over pre-existing plastic scratches.

Never rush the handover. Spending 3 minutes running a structured mechanical, document, and deposit inspection before signing your rental slip protects both your safety and your wallet. Review our [Vietnam Motorbike License Laws](/plan/vietnam-motorbike-license-laws) guide first to confirm your 1968 International Driving Permit eligibility, then run every check below before turning the ignition key.

> **The 3-Minute Curbside Golden Rule**: Never surrender your original physical passport as collateral to an independent street kiosk, never accept a scooter without recording a 60-second 4K walkaround video showing the exhaust heat shield (`Ốp pô`), and never descend steep mountain passes on a 110cc automatic belt-drive scooter where continuous lever braking causes total brake fade.

### Sub-50cc Petrol & VinFast Electric Scooters (`120,000–180,000 VND/day`)
No motorcycle endorsement? Stay legal on low-power machines. Under Vietnamese traffic rules, riders aged 16 or older may legally operate a petrol scooter under `50cc` (49cc Honda Cub or Sym Elite 50) or an electric motorbike under `4 kW` without a driver's license. In flat towns like Hoi An, Tam Coc in Ninh Binh, coastal Da Nang, and Phu Quoc, renting a **VinFast Evo200 Lite** or **VinFast Feliz S** for **120,000 to 180,000 VND per day** ($4.80 to $7.20 USD) delivers quiet acceleration and **70 to 120 km** of real-world range per charge on a lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery. Overnight charging uses a standard 220V plug at your homestay and takes 6 to 8 hours.

### 110cc–125cc Automatic vs Semi-Automatic: The Mountain Brake-Fade Hazard
Automatics dominate city streets. Models like the lightweight **Honda Vision 110cc** (**130,000 to 160,000 VND/day**), the dual-shock **Honda AirBlade 125cc** (**150,000 to 200,000 VND/day**), the cargo-friendly **Honda Lead 125cc** with its cavernous **37-liter** under-seat trunk, and the sporty **Yamaha NVX 155cc** (**180,000 to 220,000 VND/day**) make flat urban and beach errands effortless. However, never take a single-shock 110cc belt-drive scooter down 10 percent mountain passes. When a CVT automatic scooter coasts downhill slowly, its centrifugal clutch disengages and freewheels, forcing continuous hand braking that glazes automatic brake shoes, boils hydraulic fluid, and causes total brake fade. That exact hazard is why Da Nang bans all automatic scooters past the InterContinental resort junction on the steep western loop of **Son Tra Monkey Mountain**. For Son Tra, Sapa, Pu Luong, Cao Bang, or [Ha Giang Easy Rider vs Self-Drive](/compare/ha-giang-easy-rider-vs-self-drive) routes, always rent a **110cc–125cc semi-automatic** (**Honda Wave Alpha**, **Honda Blade 110cc**, **Yamaha Sirius**, or **Honda Future 125cc**) for **150,000 to 250,000 VND per day** ($6.00 to $10.00 USD)—or a **150cc manual dual-sport** (**Honda XR150L**, **Honda Winner X 150cc**, **Yamaha WR155R**) for **350,000 to 650,000 VND per day** ($14.00 to $26.00 USD). Shifting a 4-speed rotary semi-automatic down with your left foot into **2nd gear (`Số 2`)** or **1st gear (`Số 1`)** provides true mechanical engine braking on steep descents without touching a clutch lever.

## 2. The 10-Point 3-Minute Mechanical Inspection Protocol Before Signing

Test everything on the kickstand first. Walk away immediately if a clerk dismisses bald rubber or spongy brakes as "normal."

### 1. Shoot a Timestamped 60-Second 4K Walkaround Video
Film before touching the helmet. Pan your camera across all four plastic fairings, the front fender, brake lever tips, mirrors, odometer mileage, fuel gauge, and especially the **exhaust heat shield (`Ốp pô`)** while the shop staff stands in frame. Because Vietnamese parking attendants pack bikes tightly together, the right-side exhaust shield is the number-one scratched part when parking and the favorite target for **500,000 to 1,500,000 VND** ($20.00 to $60.00 USD) return damage claims.

### 2. Test Front & Rear Brake Lever Reserve (`Phanh trước / Phanh sau`)
Squeeze both levers hard. Both front and rear brakes must hold firm with **at least 50 percent lever travel reserve** before touching the handlebar grip. Roll forward 5 meters and snap the front hydraulic disc brake to verify there is zero metal-on-metal grinding from worn pads.

### 3. Check Tire Tread Depth (`>= 2mm`) & Cold Pressure (`30–33 PSI`)
Inspect the rear center groove. Never accept bald slick rear tires in Vietnam's monsoon rain (`28°C to 34°C` wet asphalt) or gravel mountain bends; demand at least **2 mm of tread depth** inflated to **30 to 33 PSI**.

### 4. Verify Horn (`Còi`), High/Low Headlight Beam, Turn Signals & Brake Light
Thumb the horn button first. A loud, instant **horn (`Còi`)** is essential for alerting blind-spot trucks and merging alley traffic in Vietnam. Toggle low and high headlight beams, test left and right turn signals, and confirm the rear red brake light brightens when pulling either lever.

### 5. Confirm Dual Side Mirrors (`Gương chiếu hậu`) for Decree 168 Compliance
Check both mirror stalks. Under **Decree 168/2024/ND-CP**, riding without a functional **left mirror (`Gương bên trái`)** triggers an immediate **400,000 to 600,000 VND** ($16.00 to $24.00 USD) **CSGT** traffic police fine at roadside checkpoints.

### 6. Compress Steering Head Bearings & Front Fork Suspension
Hold the front brake and push down hard on the handlebars. Check for clunking steering bearings or black oil leaking past the front fork seals.

### 7. Inspect the Phone Mount (`Giá đỡ điện thoại`) & Protect Camera OIS
Tighten the handlebar clamp and verify the cradle locks securely over bumps. Better yet, keep your primary smartphone in your pocket and use one Bluetooth earbud for turn-by-turn navigation—high-frequency handlebar vibration frequently damages optical image stabilization (`OIS`) camera sensors on modern iPhones.

### 8. Verify Chain Tension, CVT Belt Engagement, Steering Lock & Kickstart
Start the engine cold. Check for **20 to 30 mm** of drive chain slack on a Honda Wave, smooth CVT belt engagement on an AirBlade, a working magnetic ignition shutter (`Khóa từ`), steering lock (`Khóa cổ`), and backup kickstart lever (`Cần đạp nổ`).

## 3. Helmet Safety (`CR` Stamp Rule) & The Under-Seat Document Packet

Reject "eggshell" novelty caps (`Mũ lưỡi trai nhựa mỏng`). Street shops routinely hand out thin **30,000 VND** ($1.20 USD) plastic caps that shatter on impact and offer zero protection against road dust or flying beetles.

Insist on a **three-quarter (`3/4`) or full-face helmet** from **Andes, Royal, Protec, Yohe, or Bulldog** bearing the official **`CR` (`QCVN 2:2021/BKHCN`) safety certification sticker**, a clear scratch-free visor (`Kính chắn gió`) against road grit, and a working chin strap (an unbuckled strap carries a **400,000 to 600,000 VND** / $16.00 to $24.00 USD fine).

Next, open the seat and verify the laminated waterproof pouch contains two mandatory documents:
1. **Certified Copy of the Blue Vehicle Registration Card (`Bản sao công chứng Cà vẹt xe / Giấy đăng ký xe`)** matching the rear license plate number.
2. **Valid Compulsory Civil Liability Insurance Certificate (`Bảo hiểm trách nhiệm dân sự bắt buộc xe máy`)**, which costs the shop **66,000 VND per year** ($2.65 USD); riding without a current certificate triggers a **200,000 to 300,000 VND** ($8.00 to $12.00 USD) CSGT fine.

## 4. The Passport Deposit Trap & Official Fueling Protocol

Protect your identity documents. As warned in our [Vietnam Safety and Scams Guide](/plan/safety-scams-vietnam), handing your original physical passport to a random street kiosk is a major logistical trap.

### Why You Must Never Leave Your Original Passport at a Street Kiosk
Vietnamese hotels legally require your physical passport at check-in to register your overnight stay (`Khai báo tạm trú`) with local police. Leaving your passport at a scooter shop locks you out of checking into your next hotel 30 km down the coast and gives shady shops leverage to demand **2,000,000 to 5,000,000 VND** ($80.00 to $200.00 USD) for pre-existing fairing scratches before returning your document. Rent directly through your hotel reception or reputable multi-city shops (**Tigit Motorbikes**, **Style Motorbikes**, **QT Motorbikes**, **Flamingo Travel**) that accept a **cash deposit of 1,000,000 to 3,000,000 VND** ($40.00 to $120.00 USD), a secondary ID card, or a passport photocopy.

### Fueling Protocol: Petrolimex `RON 95-III` vs Roadside Bottles
Most rentals are handed over with an almost empty tank (`100 ml` of fuel). Ride straight within 5 minutes to an official **Petrolimex** or **PVOIL** station and ask for **`RON 95-III`** (`~21,500 to 23,000 VND/liter`; a full **4.5-liter** scooter tank costs **90,000 to 100,000 VND** / $3.60 to $4.00 USD and covers **180 to 220 km**) rather than buying adulterated roadside **1.5-liter** plastic bottles (**30,000 to 35,000 VND** / $1.20 to $1.40 USD).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I take a 125cc Honda AirBlade or Vision up Son Tra Peninsula (Monkey Mountain) in Da Nang?
Only partway. While you can ride an automatic scooter 10 km along the coastal road to **Linh Ung Pagoda**, Da Nang authorities strictly ban all automatic belt-drive scooters (`Xe tay ga`) past the InterContinental junction on the steep western loop to **Ban Co Peak** because the **10% to 15% grades** glaze CVT brakes; rent a **110cc–125cc semi-automatic Honda Wave or Blade** (**150,000 VND/day**) to ride the full 35 km summit loop legally.

### What should I do if my rented scooter gets a flat tire on the road?
Pull over immediately so you do not bend the alloy rim, then wheel the bike to any roadside **`Vá xe`** or **`Sửa xe máy`** mechanic (spaced every 1 to 2 km across Vietnam). Patching a punctured inner tube costs **20,000 to 40,000 VND** ($0.80 to $1.60 USD) and takes 10 minutes, while replacing a split tube (`Thay săm`) costs **90,000 to 130,000 VND** ($3.60 to $5.20 USD).

### Where can I legally park a rented scooter in Vietnamese cities?
Never leave a scooter unattended on an unguarded curb where municipal trucks tow bikes. Always park inside attended **`Giữ xe`** lots or guarded cafe forecourts, collect your numbered claim ticket (`Vé xe`), keep the ticket in your wallet rather than under the seat, and pay the standard **5,000 to 10,000 VND** ($0.20 to $0.40 USD) daytime parking fee (or **15,000 to 20,000 VND** overnight) upon exit.
