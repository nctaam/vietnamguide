# Money in Vietnam (2026): Zero-Fee ATMs, Polymer Notes & Cash vs Card Guide

**Quick Verdict**: Vietnam operates a dual-speed economy in 2026: urban coffee chains (*Highlands*, *Katinat*), convenience stores (*Circle K*, *GS25*, *WinMart*), ride-hailing apps (*Grab*, *Xanh SM*), and hotels accept contactless **Apple Pay, Google Pay, Visa, and Mastercard** seamlessly, yet **street food vendors, wet markets, rural homestays (Ha Giang, Pu Luong, Ninh Binh, Mekong Delta), and many monument ticket booths remain 100% cash-only in Vietnamese Dong (`VND`)**. Never rely on outdated travel blogs claiming **`TPBank` (`purple LiveBank`)** is still free—TPBank ended its zero-fee policy for foreign cards and now levies a **`50,000–55,000 VND` (`~$2.00–$2.20 USD`)** surcharge per withdrawal. Instead, seek out green-and-white **`VPBank` (`Vietnam Prosperity Bank`)** ATMs, which remain **100% free (`0 VND` local operator fee)** for international Visa and Mastercard debit cards while dispensing up to **`10,000,000 VND` (`~$400 USD`) in a single transaction**. Pair a zero-foreign-transaction-fee debit card (*Charles Schwab*, *Wise*, *Revolut*, *Capital One 360*, or *Starling*) with `$200–$300 USD` in crisp, unmarked **`$100 USD` bills** for backup exchange at **Ha Trung Street (Hanoi)** or **Ben Thanh Market gold shops (Ho Chi Minh City)**.

## 2. Vietnamese Polymer Banknotes & The 500,000 vs 20,000 VND Trap

Vietnam circulates no coins. All everyday transactions use **waterproof polymer banknotes** ranging from `10,000 VND` (`$0.40 USD`) up to `500,000 VND` (`$20.00 USD`), alongside older cotton-paper notes for small change (`1,000`, `2,000`, and `5,000 VND`). Because everything is priced in thousands, locals routinely drop the final three zeros in speech and on menus—a menu listing `"45"` or `"45k"` means **`45,000 VND` (`~$1.80 USD`)**.

### Two Dangerous Color-Pair Traps to Memorize
1. **The Cyan-Blue Pair (`500,000 VND` vs `20,000 VND`)**:
   - **`500,000 VND` (`~$20.00 USD`)** is Vietnam's highest-value banknote, printed in **teal/cyan blue** (`152 mm` long).
   - **`20,000 VND` (`~$0.80 USD`)** is printed in a nearly identical **cobalt/cyan blue** (`136 mm` long).
   - **The Risk**: Under dim street lighting or inside the back seat of a taxi at night, handing over a `500,000 VND` bill when you meant to hand over a `20,000 VND` bill costs you `$19.20 USD` instantly. Conversely, unscrupulous street vendors or rogue meter-taxi drivers use the **"Drop-and-Switch" sleight of hand**: you hand them a `500,000 VND` note, they drop their hand below the dashboard for half a second, hold up a `20,000 VND` note, and say *"Excuse me, you only gave me twenty thousand!"*
2. **The Orange-Red Pair (`200,000 VND` vs `10,000 VND`)**:
   - **`200,000 VND` (`~$8.00 USD`)** has a warm terracotta orange-red hue, while **`10,000 VND` (`~$0.40 USD`)** is brownish-yellow with red undertones.

### How to Bulletproof Your Wallet
- **Use the Two-Pocket Separation System**: Keep all **`500,000 VND` and `200,000 VND`** notes inside a zippered inner travel wallet or separate compartment. Keep your daily spending stash (`10,000`, `20,000`, `50,000`, and `100,000 VND` notes) in an outer quick-access pocket.
- **Announce the Denomination Out Loud**: Whenever you hand a `200,000 VND` or `500,000 VND` note to a driver or cashier, hold the bill clearly at eye level under the cabin light and say **"Five hundred thousand"** (*Năm trăm nghìn*) before releasing your fingers.
- **Break `500,000 VND` Notes at Convenience Stores, Never on the Street**: ATMs almost always spit out crisp `500,000 VND` bricks. A morning *bánh mì* vendor (`25,000 VND`) or GrabBike rider (`30,000 VND`) genuinely will not have `470,000 VND` in small change at 07:30 AM. Step into any air-conditioned **Circle K, GS25, FamilyMart, 7-Eleven, WinMart+, or Pharmacity**, buy a `10,000 VND` bottle of LaVie mineral water, and use your `500,000 VND` bill to collect `490,000 VND` in crisp small denominations.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The "Crisp Post-2017 `$100 USD` Bill" Rule**: Vietnamese banks and gold shops inspect US Dollar banknotes with extreme scrutiny. To receive the highest Tier-1 exchange rate, bring **2017A-series or newer `$100 USD` notes (featuring the blue 3D security ribbon)** that are completely flat, crisp, and free of **any ink teller stamps, pen marks, staple pinholes, or edge nicks**. A `2 mm` margin tear or tiny ink stamp on a `$100` bill will either cause banks to reject the note outright or trigger a `50,000–100,000 VND` (`2%–4%`) damaged-note discount at gold shops. Smaller denominations (`$50`, `$20`, `$10`, `$5`, `$1`) are legally exchanged at a slightly lower tiered rate (`~100–300 VND less per USD`).

## 4. Credit Cards, Apple Pay, POS Surcharges & VietQR in 2026

Card infrastructure in Vietnam has expanded rapidly since 2023, driven by the rollout of **Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Samsung Pay** across domestic Vietnamese payment gateways (*VNPay*, *Payoo*, *MPOS*).

### Where Cards Work Seamlessly (`0%` Extra Fee)
- **Supermarkets, Pharmacies & Convenience Stores**: *WinMart*, *Lotte Mart*, *AEON Mall*, *Circle K*, *GS25*, *7-Eleven*, *Pharmacity*, *Long Chau*, and *Guardian*.
- **Chains, Cafes & Modern Dining**: *Highlands Coffee*, *Katinat*, *Phuc Long*, *The Coffee House*, *Pizza 4P's*, *Starbucks*, and Michelin-selected restaurants in Hanoi, Da Nang, and Ho Chi Minh City.
- **Transport & Official Booking Portals**: *Grab* (subject to Grab's `4%` foreign card surcharge), *Xanh SM* (`0%` surcharge), *Vietnam Airlines*, *VietJet Air*, and the official *Vietnam Railways (`dsvn.vn`)* portal (via international gateway).

### Watch Out for the 3% Merchant POS Surcharge & DCC
At many independent 3-star hotels, Hoi An custom tailor shops, Halong Bay tour desks, and spa boutiques, merchants pass their bank's credit card processing fee onto the customer by adding a **`2.5% to 3.5%` POS terminal surcharge** when you tap a foreign Visa, Mastercard, or Amex. Always ask *"Is there a card fee?"* before tapping; if they charge `3%`, paying cash from a zero-fee `VPBank` withdrawal saves you money.
Furthermore, whenever a handheld POS terminal or ATM asks whether you want to be charged in **your home currency (`USD / EUR / GBP / AUD`) or local currency (`VND`)**, **always choose `VND` ("Continue Without Conversion")**. Selecting USD/EUR activates **Dynamic Currency Conversion (`DCC`)**, allowing the local processor to bake an invisible **`4.5% to 7.0%` exchange rate markup** into your bill.

### Can Foreign Tourists Use VietQR Scan-to-Pay?
Across Vietnam, even tiny street-corner iced tea (`trà đá`) stalls display a printed **VietQR (`Napas 247`)** matrix code on a plastic stand. Standard Western banking apps (*Chase*, *Revolut*, *Wise*) cannot scan domestic Vietnamese bank QR codes directly. However, two exceptions exist in 2026:
1. Travelers from **Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, Malaysia, Singapore, and South Korea** using supported national cross-border QR banking apps can scan qualifying **VietQR Global** merchant codes directly.
2. International visitors staying longer can install the **Moreta Pay** or **LocalPay / VNPay Tourist** wallet apps, which link a foreign Visa or Mastercard to scan VietQR codes for a `~2.5%–3.5%` convenience top-up fee. Small polymer `10,000–50,000 VND` cash notes remain faster and completely free.

## 5. Step-by-Step Arrival Money Protocol (First 60 Minutes in Vietnam)

1. **Step 1 — Enable Travel Notices & Check Daily ATM Caps at Home**: Unlock international withdrawals before boarding. Open your banking app (*Schwab*, *Wise*, *Revolut*, *Monzo*, *Starling*, or *Chase*) to ensure magnetic-stripe and EMV chip transactions in Vietnam (`Asia`) are enabled, and raise your single-transaction withdrawal limit to at least **`$450 USD` (`~11,500,000 VND`)**.
2. **Step 2 — Clear Customs Within the `$5,000 USD` / `15,000,000 VND` Cash Limit**: Under **Circular 15/2011/TT-NHNN**, you may bring up to **`$5,000 USD`** (or equivalent in EUR/GBP/AUD/JPY) and **`15,000,000 VND`** into or out of Vietnam without declaring. Carrying more than `$5,000 USD` in cash is legal only if you declare it at the **Red Channel Customs Desk (`Hải Quan`)** upon arrival and keep the stamped declaration slip (`Tờ Khai Hải Quan`) to present if exiting with remaining cash.
3. **Step 3 — Hit a Bank ATM in the Outer Arrival Hall**: Walk past baggage claim first. After passing the customs X-ray at `HAN`, `SGN`, or `DAD`, locate the row of official bank ATMs (`VPBank`, `ACB`, `BIDV`, `Vietcombank`) along the outer arrival wall and withdraw an odd amount like **`2,900,000 VND` or `4,900,000 VND`** (instead of a round `3,000,000` or `5,000,000 VND`), which forces the dispenser to include at least five `100,000 VND` or `200,000 VND` bills instead of handing you exclusively `500,000 VND` notes!
4. **Step 4 — Convert Leftover VND Before Passing Exit Immigration**: Vietnamese Dong (`VND`) is a **closed, non-convertible currency outside Vietnam**—airports in London, Sydney, Frankfurt, or Los Angeles will either refuse your leftover VND or buy it back at a `30%` loss. Spend down your final `500,000–1,000,000 VND` on coffee beans, Marou chocolate, or airport dining, or convert excess VND back into USD/EUR at the bank kiosks in the departure check-in hall before going through security.

## 6. Frequently Asked Questions (Money, Cash & ATMs in Vietnam)

### How much cash in VND should I carry per day in Vietnam?
For a typical traveler who pays for hotels, flights, and *Grab* / *Xanh SM* rides by card, carrying **`500,000 to 1,000,000 VND` (`$20–$40 USD`) per person per day** in physical cash easily covers street food meals (`40,000–80,000 VND` per bowl), iced specialty coffees (`35,000–65,000 VND`), temple/museum admission tickets (`30,000–250,000 VND`), and small tips. When departing for multi-day rural trips (`Ha Giang Loop`, `Pu Luong`, `Mu Cang Chai`, `Ba Be Lake`, or `Phong Nha`), withdraw **`2,500,000 to 3,500,000 VND` (`$100–$140 USD`) per person** in advance before leaving the city.

### Do Wise and Revolut physical debit cards work well at Vietnam ATMs?
**Yes—physical Wise and Revolut Visa/Mastercard debit cards work at 99% of Vietnamese ATMs and contactless POS terminals.** Note that while **`VPBank`** charges `0 VND` local ATM fee, Wise and Revolut impose their own monthly free ATM withdrawal thresholds (typically `~$100–$250 USD` / `£200` per rolling month before a `1.75%–2.0%` home card fee kicks in). To maximize your Wise/Revolut allowance, pay directly via **Apple Pay / contactless tap** at supermarkets, cafes, and hotels (which is `100%` fee-free up to your currency conversion limit) and save physical ATM withdrawals strictly for cash-only street vendors.

### Are American Express (Amex) or Discover cards accepted in Vietnam?
**Rarely.** Outside of international 5-star hotel chains (*Sofitel Legend Metropole*, *Park Hyatt Saigon*, *InterContinental*, *JW Marriott*), *Vietnam Airlines*, and a handful of luxury fine-dining restaurants, **American Express and Discover are rejected by 95% of everyday Vietnamese merchants**. Always carry a **Visa** and a **Mastercard** as your primary payment cards.

### What should I do if a Vietnamese ATM swallows my debit card?
First, **take a photo of the ATM's serial/terminal ID sticker** and the bank branch address printed on the front of the machine, and call the **24/7 Customer Hotline** printed on the ATM screen immediately. Second, **freeze the card temporarily inside your mobile banking app**. If the ATM is physically attached to an open bank branch (`e.g., Vietcombank or VPBank during 08:00–16:30 Monday–Friday`), walk inside with your **physical passport**—staff can often verify your identity and retrieve the card on the spot or the following morning. If it is a standalone street/airport kiosk on a weekend, retrieving a foreign card takes `5–10 business days`, which is why **every traveler must pack at least two debit cards and two credit cards kept in separate bags**.

### Can I pay directly in US Dollars (`USD`) at shops and restaurants in Vietnam?
**No—under Vietnamese foreign exchange law (`Ordinance on Foreign Exchange Control`), pricing or settling everyday retail transactions in foreign currency is prohibited.** While tour agencies or cruise operators often quote reference prices in USD (`e.g., "$165 USD Halong Cruise"`), actual payment at the counter is converted into **Vietnamese Dong (`VND`)** at the merchant's daily exchange rate (which is usually `2%–4%` worse than a gold shop rate). Always pay in **VND**.
