## Crossing the Open South China Sea from Vung Tau to Con Dao

Covering `185 km` (`100 nautical miles`) of open ocean between mainland Ba Ria–Vung Tau Province and the Con Dao Archipelago requires a vessel engineered for deep-water swells. Flights into Con Dao Airport (`VCS`) remain restricted by short runway limits and ATR-72 turboprop seat shortages. When airfares spike above `3,800,000 VND` (`$152.00 USD`) round-trip or sell out weeks ahead, the high-speed catamaran from **Cau Da Pier** (`Cảng Cầu Đá`) in Vung Tau to **Ben Dam Port** (`Cảng Bến Đầm`) on Con Son Island serves as the primary maritime link from southern Vietnam's industrial coast.

Operated by **Phu Quoc Express** (`Tàu cao tốc hai thân Phú Quốc Express`), this route deploys the **Con Dao Express 36** alongside sister twin-hull catamarans (*Trung Trac* and *Trung Nhi*) depending on seasonal demand. The crossing takes `3 hours 30 minutes` to `3 hours 50 minutes` in calm conditions. Unlike protected bay ferries in Ha Long or the short `45-minute` hops inside Kien Giang, the Vung Tau to Con Dao sea lane pushes straight across exposed continental shelf waters. Sea state dictates everything here. Knowing the vessel layout, seasonal wind thresholds, and early-morning transfer timing from Ho Chi Minh City separates a smooth voyage from a miserable four-hour battle with motion sickness.

## Vessel Specifications: Inside the Con Dao Express 36 Catamaran

The **Con Dao Express 36** is an aluminum-alloy twin-hull catamaran built in Vietnam to European maritime classification standards. Measuring `46.8 meters` in length with a `12.2-meter` beam, the ship carries `598 passengers` across two enclosed air-conditioned decks plus an open aft sun deck. Four **Rolls-Royce MTU** (`16V2000M70`) diesel engines generate `7,404 horsepower`, driving waterjets or twin propellers at a cruising speed of `28 to 30 knots` (`52 to 55 km/h`) and a maximum speed of `35 knots` (`65 km/h`).

Why does hull architecture matter to passengers? A twin-hull catamaran slices through surface chop using two narrow pontoons rather than slamming flat against oncoming waves like a monohull speedboat. An automated hydrofoil ride-control system adjusts trim tabs beneath the waterline, reducing pitch and roll by roughly `30% to 40%` when waves reach `2.0 to 2.5 meters`. Even so, `185 km` of open sea tests human vestibular limits.

### Choosing Your Seat: Lower Main Deck vs Upper Deck vs VIP

Where you sit directly determines how much vertical acceleration your stomach absorbs. Physics rules the boat. The center of gravity sits lowest and furthest aft on the **Main Deck (Lower Deck)**, specifically between rows `10 and 22` near the middle of the hull. Passengers prone to seasickness should book standard **Eco Class** (`790,000–880,000 VND` / `$31.60–$35.20 USD` Monday through Thursday; `950,000 VND` / `$38.00 USD` Friday through Sunday) and request a central lower-deck aisle seat.

The **VIP Cabin** (`1,200,000 VND` / `$48.00 USD`) occupies the forward section of the upper deck. It features `30 wide leather seats` with `130-degree` reclining backs, built-in footrests, individual entertainment screens, and complimentary bottled water plus a cold towel. On flat summer mornings between April and August, VIP is quiet and comfortable. When swells exceed `2.0 meters`, however, the forward upper bow pitches upward and drops several feet with every wave trough. If the forecast calls for `15-knot` winds, skip VIP and stay low near the stern.

## 2026 Ferry Timetable and Seasonal Monsoon Disruptions

Unlike river ferries that run `365 days` a year, the Vung Tau to Con Dao route operates on a strict weather-dependent calendar. Normally, one daily rotation runs in each direction:

* **Vung Tau (Cau Da Pier) to Con Dao (Ben Dam Port):** Departs at `08:00` sharp (occasional peak-summer adjustments shift boarding to `07:30`). Arrival at Ben Dam Port falls between `11:30` and `11:50` (`3 hours 30 minutes` to `3 hours 50 minutes`).
* **Con Dao (Ben Dam Port) to Vung Tau (Cau Da Pier):** Departs at `13:00` (boarding closes at `12:45`). Arrival back in Vung Tau occurs between `16:30` and `16:50`.

### The Calm Window (March to September)

From **March through August** (and into early September), the sea corridor between Vung Tau and Con Dao reaches its calmest phase. Winds drop to **Beaufort Force 2 to 4** (`6 to 15 knots`), and wave heights stay below `1.0 to 1.5 meters`. During these seven months, dispatch reliability exceeds `95%`. The open upper-aft deck opens about `30 minutes` after clearing Vung Tau's Ganh Rai Bay, allowing passengers to stand outside in the sea breeze.

### The Northeast Monsoon Suspension Season (October to February)

Everything changes in mid-October. When the **Northeast Monsoon** (*Gió mùa Đông Bắc*) sweeps down the South China Sea, sustained winds funnel straight across the `185 km` channel at **Beaufort Force 6, 7, or 8** (`22 to 40 knots`), whipping up steep `3.0 to 4.5-meter` swells. The Maritime Administration of Ba Ria–Vung Tau enforces a strict port lockout whenever winds surpass **Beaufort Force 6–7**.

Between **November and February**, the Vung Tau to Con Dao ferry frequently suspends operations for weeks at a time, or pauses completely for scheduled winter dry-dock maintenance. Even when the port authority grants clearance on a marginal **Force 5–6** winter morning, the 3.5-hour ride involves heavy pitching, sealed exterior doors, and widespread seasickness across the cabin. If the Maritime Administration cancels a sailing due to weather, Phu Quoc Express issues a 100% full ticket refund or free date transfer without penalty fees, though hotel deposits on Con Son Island remain your own responsibility unless your resort offers a weather-waiver clause. Travelers visiting between October and February who cannot secure direct flights from Ho Chi Minh City should consider the shorter 90 km (2.5-hour) sea crossing from Soc Trang Province instead, or compare island weather windows in our [Con Dao vs Phu Quoc](/compare/con-dao-vs-phu-quoc/) guide.

## Connecting from Ho Chi Minh City to Cau Da Pier (Vung Tau)

Because the ferry departs Vung Tau at `08:00` and check-in closes at `07:30`, travelers starting in Ho Chi Minh City (`110 km` northwest) must either sleep in Vung Tau the night before or catch a dawn limousine transfer. Refer to our complete [Saigon to Vung Tau Transport](/plan/saigon-to-vung-tau-transport/) breakdown for highway routing details.

### Option 1: The 05:00 Early-Morning Limousine Van (Same-Day Connection)

Major limousine operators—including **Hoa Mai**, **Toan Thang**, **Huy Hoang**, and **Anh Quoc**—run `9-seat` VIP DCar vans departing District 1 (`Nguyen Thai Binh Street`) and Tan Son Nhat Airport starting at `04:30` and `05:00`.

* **Transit Time:** `2 hours` to `2 hours 15 minutes` via the Ho Chi Minh City – Long Thanh – Dau Giay Expressway (`CT01`) and National Highway `QL51`.
* **Ticket Price:** `160,000 to 220,000 VND` (`$6.40 to $8.80 USD`) per person.
* **Drop-Off:** Tell the driver explicitly that you are boarding the Con Dao ferry at **Cau Da Pier** (`09 Ha Long Street, Ward 2, Vung Tau`). Vans drop passengers right at the Front Beach (*Bãi Trước*) port gate between `06:45` and `07:10`, leaving `20 to 35 minutes` for ticket verification and boarding.

### Option 2: Overnight Stay at Front Beach (Zero-Stress Buffer)

If a `04:30` wake-up call sounds punishing, take an evening limousine (`18:00` or `19:00` departure) to Vung Tau the day prior and book a hotel along `Ha Long Street` or `Tran Phu Street` in the Front Beach area. Walking `300 to 800 meters` to Cau Da Pier at `07:15` after a hot breakfast removes any highway traffic risk on `QL51`.

> **Crucial Port Warning:** Vung Tau has two separate passenger piers on Front Beach. The Ho Chi Minh City GreenlinesDP hydrofoil docks at **Ho May / Cap Treo Pier** (`01A Tran Phu Street`), whereas the **Con Dao Express 36** departs from **Cau Da Pier** (`09 Ha Long Street`), located `1.8 km` further south toward Small Mountain (*Núi Nhỏ*). Always confirm `Cảng Cầu Đá` with your taxi driver.

## Boarding Protocol, ID Rules, and Baggage Allowances

Vietnamese maritime security regulations require every passenger's full legal name, year of birth, and passport or national ID (`CCCD`) number to be printed on the ship manifest before departure.

1. **Check-In Cutoff:** Arrive at **Cau Da Pier** at least `45 minutes` before departure (`07:15` for the `08:00` sailing). Gates close strictly on time. Foreign passengers must present their original physical passport; a smartphone photo is often rejected by border guard inspectors at the gangway.
2. **Pier Entrance Fee:** A nominal port infrastructure fee of `15,000 to 20,000 VND` (`$0.60 to $0.80 USD`) is either bundled into your ticket or collected at the terminal turnstile.
3. **Luggage Weight & Oversized Cargo:** Standard tickets include `15 kg` of total cabin and hold luggage per passenger. Crew members stack medium suitcases (`20 to 24 inches`) in metal racks near the bow and stern entrances. If you bring a `28-inch` dive bag, surfboard, or boxed bicycle, expect an excess cargo surcharge of `50,000 to 150,000 VND` (`$2.00 to $6.00 USD`). Motorbikes can also be loaded onto the aft cargo bay for `300,000 to 500,000 VND` (`$12.00 to $20.00 USD`) depending on engine displacement (`110cc` vs `150cc`), though slots must be reserved `48 hours` in advance.
4. **Seasickness Preparation:** Buy **Nautamine** or **Stugeron** (`Cinnarizine 25mg`) at any Vung Tau pharmacy for `10,000 to 20,000 VND` (`$0.40 to $0.80 USD`) per strip. Take one tablet `45 minutes` before boarding (`07:15`) after eating a light dry meal such as plain bread (*bánh mì không*) or crackers. Avoid greasy pork noodle soup or iced milk coffee right before stepping onto the catamaran.

## Arrival at Ben Dam Port and Transfers into Con Son Town

At `11:30`, the catamaran rounds the dramatic volcanic cliffs of **Shark Cape** (`Mũi Cá Mập`) and glides into the sheltered deep-water basin of **Ben Dam Port** (`Cảng Bến Đầm`), surrounded by forested hills and colorful wooden squid boats. Ben Dam sits at the southwestern tip of Con Son Island, `12 km` (`20 minutes` by road) from the main hotels, markets, and historic prison sites in **Con Son Town** (`Thị trấn Côn Đảo`). For neighborhood breakdowns and resort proximity to the port vs the airport, check our guide on [Where to Stay in Con Dao](/destinations/where-to-stay-in-con-dao/).

As you walk down the concrete jetty (`250 meters` long) to the port gate, four transport options wait in the parking lot:

* **Shared Electric Buggy (*Xe điện*):** Open-air `12-seat` green and yellow electric shuttles wait directly outside the port barrier. They charge a flat `40,000 to 50,000 VND` (`$1.60 to $2.00 USD`) per person and drop passengers directly at any hotel inside Con Son Town. If your group of `6 to 8 travelers` charters the entire buggy privately, the total rate runs `250,000 to 300,000 VND` (`$10.00 to $12.00 USD`).
* **Metered & Fixed-Rate Taxi:** **Mai Linh Con Dao** and **Con Son Taxi** sedans (`4-seat`) and SUVs (`7-seat`) line up beside the terminal. A direct ride over the coastal pass into Con Son Town costs `220,000 to 300,000 VND` (`$8.80 to $12.00 USD`). If you are staying further north near **Dam Trau Beach** or **Six Senses Con Dao** (`22 to 25 km` from Ben Dam Port), expect taxi fares of `400,000 to 500,000 VND` (`$16.00 to $20.00 USD`).
* **Port Motorbike Rental:** Several local shops deliver `110cc` to `125cc` automatic scooters (*Honda Vision* or *AirBlade*) directly to Ben Dam Port for `150,000 to 200,000 VND` (`$6.00 to $8.00 USD`) per `24 hours`. Arrange delivery via WhatsApp or Zalo the day before arrival so your helmet and scooter are waiting at the pier gate, saving the `50,000 VND` transfer fee into town. Be aware that the only petrol station on the southern half of the island sits in Con Son Town (`12 km` away), so verify the tank holds at least `1.5 liters` of fuel before riding up the steep coastal pass past **Bai Nhat Beach**.
