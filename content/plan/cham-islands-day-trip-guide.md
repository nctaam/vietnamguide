# Cham Islands (Cu Lao Cham) Day Trip Guide from Hoi An (2026): Cua Dai Speedboat, Snorkeling & Fees

Rising directly out of the East Sea just **`15 to 18 kilometers` (`8 nautical miles`) offshore** from Hoi An's Cua Dai estuary, the **Cham Islands (`Cù Lao Chàm`)** form a granite archipelago of eight rugged islands designated in 2009 as a **UNESCO World Biosphere Reserve (`Khu Dự Trữ Sinh Quyển Thế Giới Cù Lao Chàm`)**. While the mainland beaches of Hoi An (`An Bàng` and `Cửa Đại`) have golden river-silt sand and gentle surf with modest underwater visibility, crossing the open channel to **Hon Lao (`Hòn Lao`, the main inhabited island)** and **Hon Dai (`Hòn Dài`)** puts you over clear, coral-ringed coves with **`10 to 20 meters` of summer water visibility**, white-sand bays (`Bãi Ông`, `Bãi Chồng`, `Bãi Xếp`), and a centuries-old fishing community (`Bãi Làng`).

Because high-speed canoes cross from **Cua Dai Tourist Pier (`Cảng Du Lịch Cửa Đại`)** to Bai Lang Pier in just **`15 to 20 minutes`**, the Cham Islands are the easiest marine day trip in central Vietnam. However, two operational realities govern every visit: **seasonality is absolute** (rough northeast monsoon swells frequently shut down all boat crossings between **October and February**), and how you structure your day determines whether you get stuck in a 40-person life-jacket crowd at `10:30 AM` or enjoy quiet coral reefs and empty beaches. Here is the complete 2026 empirical guide.

## Seasonality Reality Check: When Can You Actually Visit the Cham Islands?

Unlike inland Hoi An Ancient Town or the sheltered rivers of Ninh Binh, the 15-kilometer sea channel between Cua Dai Estuary and the Cham Islands is completely exposed to the open East Sea (`Biển Đông`):

### 1. Peak Calm-Sea & Snorkeling Season (`March to August`)
- **Weather & Water Conditions**: From **late March through August**, central Vietnam enjoys hot, sunny dry-season skies (`28°C–34°C`), gentle south/southeast breezes, and **flat-calm turquoise swells**.
- **Underwater Visibility**: Peaks in **May, June, July, and early August** at **`12 to 20+ meters`**, with sea temperatures around `27°C–29°C`. Speedboats operate 100% on schedule every morning.

### 2. Transition Month (`September`)
- Early September often still delivers warm sunny mornings, but late September marks the onset of autumn tropical depressions (`Áp Thấp Nhiệt Đới`). Always check the marine wind forecast (`<12 knots` is comfortable; `>15 knots` becomes bumpy) 48 hours before booking.

### 3. Rough Winter Monsoon & Port Suspensions (`October to February`)
- Between **October and February**, the Northeast Monsoon (`Gió Mùa Đông Bắc`) drives heavy 2-to-4-meter swells against the Cua Dai sandbar. The **Cua Dai Border Guard Port Authority (`Đồn Biên Phòng Cửa Đại`)** routinely **prohibits all tourist speedboats from departing** for days or weeks at a time during October–January, and underwater visibility drops below `2 to 3 meters`. If you are visiting Hoi An between October and January, skip the Cham Islands and spend your day exploring **My Son Sanctuary (`Thánh Địa Mỹ Sơn`)** or **Marble Mountains (`Ngũ Hành Sơn`)** instead.

## Mandatory 2026 Island Fees, Border Guard Registration & The Zero-Plastic-Bag Law

Every domestic and international passenger stepping onto **Hon Lao (`Bãi Làng`)** must pass through the **Cua Dai Pier Border Guard manifest check** and comply with two strict biosphere regulations:
1. **Official 2026 Cham Islands Entrance & Eco-Conservation Tariffs**:
   - **Biosphere Reserve Sightseeing Ticket (`Vé Tham Quan Cù Lao Chàm`)**: **`70,000 VND` (`~$2.80 USD`)** per person.
   - **Marine Ecological Conservation Fee (`Phí Sinh Thái`)**: **`20,000 to 30,000 VND` (`~$0.80–$1.20 USD`)** per person.
   - *(Note: Combined **`90,000–100,000 VND` (`~$4.00 USD`)** mandatory island fee is automatically bundled into all `600k–850k VND` organized day tours and official `400k VND` speedboat packages; keep your paper ticket stub to show at the Bai Lang gate).*
   - **Passport / ID Requirement**: Because the Cham Islands occupy an offshore maritime border zone, tour operators must submit your **full name, nationality, birth year, and passport number** to the Cua Dai Coast Guard manifest prior to departure. Bring a photo of your passport bio page on your phone.
2. **Strict Ban on Single-Use Plastic Bags (`Nói Không Với Túi Nilon`)**:
   - Since 2009, the Cham Islands have enforced Vietnam's strictest **zero-plastic-bag campaign**. Security marshals at Cua Dai Pier and Bai Lang Dock inspect bags and fine visitors who bring disposable plastic shopping bags (`túi nilon`) onto the island. Pack your sunscreen, towel, and dry clothes inside a reusable cloth tote or waterproof dry bag.

## What You Actually See on the Island: `Bãi Làng`, `Hòn Dài` Reefs & `Bãi Ông / Bãi Chồng`

A classic `08:30–14:30` Cham Islands excursion splits into three distinct chapters across the archipelago:

### 1. Cultural & Heritage Walk in `Bãi Làng` (Main Fishing Village — `09:00–10:15 AM`)
All speedboats dock first at the crescent harbor of **Bai Lang (`Bãi Làng`)** on the western shore of Hon Lao:
- **Marine Protected Area Museum (`Nhà Bảo Tồn Biển`)**: Overview of the archipelago's 311 coral species, giant clams, and swiftlet bird's-nest (`yến sào`) harvesting caves.
- **200-Year-Old Champa Ancient Well (`Giếng Cổ Chăm` / `Giếng Xóm Cấm`)**: A brick-lined freshwater well built by early Cham seafarers just meters from the salty shoreline that has never run dry, even during peak summer droughts.
- **Hai Tang Pagoda (`Chùa Hải Tạng`, Built in `1758`)**: Set against the granite foothills overlooking a small rice valley on the edge of Bai Lang, this 18th-century pagoda was built during the Le Dynasty as a spiritual sanctuary for merchant junks sheltering from storms along the central coast.
- **Tan Hiep Seafood Market (`Chợ Tân Hiệp`)**: Directly beside the pier, local fishermen sell sun-dried squid (`mực một nắng`), forest herbal tea (`lá rừng Cù Lao Chàm`, `15,000–25,000 VND/bundle`), and steamed `bánh ít lá gai` (sticky rice pyramid cakes wrapped in ramie leaves, `5,000–10,000 VND`).

### 2. Coral Snorkeling at `Hòn Dài`, `Bãi Xếp` or `Hòn Tai` (`10:30–11:45 AM`)
From Bai Lang, boats motor `10 minutes` out to the sheltered coral gardens of **Hon Dai (`Hòn Dài`)**, **Bai Xep (`Bãi Xếp`)**, or **Hon Tai (`Hòn Tai`)**, where hard table corals, brain corals, blue sea stars, and clownfish thrive in `3 to 8 meters` of water.

> [!TIP]
> **How to Avoid the "Mass Speedboat Life-Vest Herd" When Snorkeling**: On standard budget speedboat tours (`600,000 VND`), boat captains require all 30 passengers to wear bulky orange foam life jackets in the water, do **not** provide swim fins, and blow a whistle after only `35 minutes` at the reef. If you actually love snorkeling and want **real silicone masks, swim fins, and 2 full hours on quiet outer reefs**, book your day trip through **Cham Island Diving Center** (`88 Nguyễn Thái Học` in Hoi An Old Town; `~950,000–1,250,000 VND` for snorkelers). Their dedicated wooden dive boat visits two uncrowded reefs while the mass speedboats are already eating lunch!

### 3. Seafood Lunch & Beach Relaxation at `Bãi Ông` or `Bãi Chồng` (`12:00–13:45 PM`)
Around noon, boats pull into either **Bai Ong (`Bãi Ông`)** or **Bai Chong (`Bãi Chồng`)**—two palm-shaded white-sand beaches separated by giant rounded granite boulders with freshwater showers (`free with lunch`). Lunch features Cham Islands specialties: **charcoal-grilled sea urchin with scallion oil (`nhum biển nướng mỡ hành`)**, **stir-fried wild mountain greens with garlic (`rau rừng xào tỏi`)**, **steamed horned sea snails (`ốc vú nàng`)**, and grilled snapper, followed by 60 minutes of swimming in crystal-clear water before the `14:00` speedboat back to Cua Dai Pier.

## Overnighting on Hon Lao (`Bãi Làng` & `Bãi Hương` Homestays) vs Day-Tripping

Ninety percent of visitors to the Cham Islands arrive at `09:00 AM` and depart on the `14:00 PM` speedboat. If you have an extra night in your central Vietnam itinerary between May and August, staying **1 night on Hon Lao Island** reveals a completely different side of the archipelago once the day-tripper canoes return to the mainland:
- **Where to Stay (`350,000–650,000 VND` / `$14–$26 USD` per night)**: Family-run homestays in **Bai Lang (`Bãi Làng`, such as Hammock Homestay, Sunbay Homestay, or Monkey Homestay)** and the quieter southern fishing hamlet of **Bai Huong (`Bãi Hương`)** offer clean private bedrooms with air-conditioning, mosquito nets, and harbor balconies. Because the island was connected to the national submarine power grid in 2016, homestays now have 24-hour electricity and reliable Wi-Fi.
- **Renting a Scooter to Ride the Coastal Cliff Road (`150,000–200,000 VND/day`)**: After `14:30 PM`, rent a semi-automatic motorbike from your homestay host (`150,000 VND` including fuel) and ride the paved concrete cliff road winding along the southwestern coast from **`Bãi Làng` past `Bãi Xếp` and `Bãi Chồng` down to `Bãi Hương` (`~7 km`)**, stopping at the dramatic eastern cliff overlook of **Eo Gio (`Eo Gió`)** for sunset and sunrise over the open East Sea.
- **Evening Harbor Dining & Night Squid Fishing**: Without day-tour crowds, you can sit on plastic stools along Bai Lang harbor at dusk eating freshly caught **grilled barracuda, steamed squid (`mực lá hấp gừng`, `180,000–250,000 VND`), and `ốc vú nàng`** at local village prices, or hire a small wooden basket boat (`400,000–600,000 VND`) for a 2-hour evening squid-jigging trip (`câu mực đêm`).

## Step-by-Step HowTo: Planning Your Cham Islands Day Trip from Hoi An or Da Nang

### 1. First Step — Verify the Marine Forecast & Choose Your Boat Style (`Apr–Aug`)
Check that winds are calm (`under 12 knots`), then choose between:
- **Standard All-Inclusive Speedboat Day Tour (`600k–850k VND`)** if you want hotel pickup, cultural sightseeing at `Bãi Làng`, a 40-minute snorkel, and a beach seafood lunch back at your Hoi An hotel by `15:00 PM`.
- **Dedicated Snorkel/Dive Boat (`950k–1.25M VND`)** if your #1 priority is extended coral reef time with proper fins.
- **DIY Speedboat + Scooter (`400k VND` boat + `150k VND` scooter on Hon Lao)** if you want to skip the tour groups and ride the coastal cliff road from `Bãi Làng` to `Bãi Xếp`, `Eo Gió`, and quiet `Bãi Hương` fishing village on your own.

### 2. Second Step — Pack Reef-Safe Sunscreen, a Reusable Bag & Motion Sickness Tablets
Bring a cloth tote bag (**zero plastic bags**), reef-safe sunscreen, a rashguard shirt, **`200,000–400,000 VND` in small cash notes** for cold coconuts (`30,000 VND`) or extra grilled sea urchins (`40,000–60,000 VND each`), and sit in the **back rows (`stern`) of the speedboat** if you are prone to motion sickness, as the bow bounces hard over Cua Dai estuary waves!

### 3. Final Step — Arrive at Cua Dai Pier (`Cảng Cửa Đại`) by `08:15 AM`
If traveling independently from Hoi An (`6 km` / `12 minutes` from Old Town, `85,000–110,000 VND` via GrabCar) or Da Nang (`30 km` / `45 minutes`), reach **Cua Dai Tourist Pier** by **`08:15 AM`** to clear the Border Guard manifest for the `08:30–09:00 AM` speedboat departures.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do you get from Hoi An to the Cham Islands (`Cù Lao Chàm`) and how long does it take?
All boats depart from **Cua Dai Tourist Pier (`Cảng Du Lịch Cửa Đại`)**, located `6 km` (`12 minutes` by Grab or taxi) east of Hoi An Ancient Town. A **high-speed canoe (`ca nô cao tốc`) takes `15 to 20 minutes`** to cross the `15 km` channel to Bai Lang Pier on Hon Lao (`350,000–450,000 VND` round-trip ticket, or `600,000–850,000 VND` as part of an all-inclusive day tour with lunch and hotel transfers).

### What are the best months to visit the Cham Islands for snorkeling and clear water?
The best time to visit the Cham Islands is from **April through August** (with **March and early September** as shoulder months), when seas are flat-calm, skies are sunny, and underwater visibility reaches **`12 to 20 meters`**. Avoid planning a Cham Islands trip between **October and February**, when rough winter monsoon waves frequently force the Coast Guard to suspend all boats.

### How much is the Cham Islands entrance fee in 2026?
In 2026, the mandatory **Cham Islands UNESCO Biosphere Reserve entrance ticket costs `70,000 VND` (`~$2.80 USD`) per person**, plus a **`20,000 to 30,000 VND` (`~$0.80–$1.20 USD`) ecological conservation fee** (`90,000–100,000 VND` / `$4.00 USD` total). If you book an organized day tour or official speedboat combo, this fee is already included in your ticket price.

### Can you stay overnight on the Cham Islands instead of doing a day trip?
Yes! Once the last tourist speedboats depart back to Cua Dai Pier at `14:30 PM`, the islands become wonderfully quiet. You can stay in a clean, air-conditioned family homestay in **Bai Lang (`Bãi Làng`)** or **Bai Huong (`Bãi Hương`)** for **`350,000 to 650,000 VND` (`~$14–$26 USD`) per night**, rent a motorbike (`150,000 VND/day`) to watch sunset at **Eo Gio (`Eo Gió`)**, eat fresh squid with local fishermen on the harbourfront, and take the next afternoon's speedboat back to Hoi An.
