# Phu Quoc Ferry Guide (2026): Fast Boats, Mainland Ports & Schedules

Every overland journey to Phu Quoc Island funnels into a fundamental maritime choice: depart from **Rach Gia Port** (`Cảng Rạch Giá`) in central Kien Giang province or continue 90 km further northwest up the coast to **Ha Tien Port** (`Cảng Hà Tiên`) near the Cambodian border. Both mainland terminals dispatch high-speed passenger catamarans and roll-on/roll-off (Ro-Ro) vehicle ferries into **Bai Vong Port** (`Cảng Bãi Vòng`) on Phu Quoc's eastern shoreline, yet the two routes differ sharply in highway approach time, open-ocean exposure, ticket pricing, and cargo capacity.

Understanding the trade-off between road kilometers and nautical miles prevents missed connections and seasickness. Buses leaving Ho Chi Minh City or Can Tho reach Rach Gia 2 hours sooner than Ha Tien thanks to the CT02 Lo Te–Rach Soi Expressway, making Rach Gia the faster highway hub. Conversely, Ha Tien cuts the Gulf of Thailand sea crossing from 120 km down to 45 km, halving your time on open water from 150 minutes to just 60 to 75 minutes. For full end-to-end overland bus and flight comparisons from Saigon, read our [Ho Chi Minh City to Phu Quoc Transport](/plan/ho-chi-minh-city-to-phu-quoc-transport/) guide.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Golden Rule of Phu Quoc Ferries:** Take the **Rach Gia ferry** (330,000–340,000 VND, 135–150 minutes) if you are riding an express sleeper bus from Ho Chi Minh City or Can Tho. Take the **Ha Tien ferry** (216,000–250,000 VND, 60–75 minutes) if you suffer from motion sickness, are crossing the Xa Xia border from Kampot/Kep in Cambodia, or are bringing a motorbike or car on the Thanh Thoi Ro-Ro barge.

## When to Choose Rach Gia Port (`Cảng Rạch Giá`)

Rach Gia is the provincial capital and high-volume maritime hub of Kien Giang. Located at the mouth of the Cai Lon River at **Nguyen Cong Tru Street**, the port handles 8 to 10 daily high-speed departures to Phu Quoc alongside inter-island catamarans bound for Hon Tre, Lai Son (`Hòn Sơn`), and Nam Du Archipelago.

1. **Direct Expressway Feeder from HCMC & Can Tho:** Sleeper coaches operated by **Futa Bus Lines (`Phương Trang`)** and **Kumho Samco** run down the CT01 Trung Luong–My Thuan Expressway and connect straight onto the CT02 Lo Te–Rach Soi highway, arriving at Rach Gia Bus Station (`Bến xe Rạch Sỏi`, 8 km south of the pier) in 5.5 hours from Saigon or 2.5 hours from Can Tho (see [Can Tho to Phu Quoc Transport](/plan/can-tho-to-phu-quoc-transport/)). Both bus lines provide complimentary 16-seat shuttle vans (`xe trung chuyển`) directly to Nguyen Cong Tru Wharf.
2. **Connecting from Rach Gia Airport (`VKG`):** Travelers flying domestic regional turboprops into Rach Gia Airport (10 km southeast of the pier, 150,000 VND taxi, 20 minutes) can step straight onto a mid-morning catamaran.
3. **2026 Rach Gia to Phu Quoc Timetable:**
   - **Phu Quoc Express:** Departs Rach Gia at 07:00, 08:45, 10:30, 12:45, and 13:45 (135 minutes crossing; 340,000 VND Eco, 540,000 VND VIP). Return boats leave Bai Vong Port at 07:15, 09:00, 11:15, 13:30, and 14:30.
   - **Superdong Fast Ferry (`Superdong III, IV, V, XI, XII`):** Departs Rach Gia at 07:00, 08:10, 10:30, and 13:10 (150 minutes crossing; 330,000 VND adult, 240,000 VND child). Return sailings leave Phu Quoc at 07:30, 10:10, 13:00, and 13:45.
   - **Lightning 68 (`Tàu Cao Tốc Hòa Bình` / `Lightning 68-68`):** Offers supplementary peak-season departures at 07:40 and 13:00 (330,000 VND Eco).

If your sleeper bus arrives in Rach Gia after the final 13:45 ferry departs, book an overnight room near the Vinh Thanh Van harbor district using our [Where to Stay in Rach Gia](/destinations/where-to-stay-in-rach-gia/) guide and board the 07:00 boat the next morning.

## When to Choose Ha Tien Port (`Cảng Tàu Hà Tiên`)

Located 90 km northwest of Rach Gia along National Highway 80 (`Quốc lộ 80`), Ha Tien Passenger Port on **Kim Du Street, To Chau Ward** sits directly across a narrow channel from Phu Quoc's northern cape. Our dedicated [Ha Tien to Phu Quoc Ferry](/plan/ha-tien-to-phu-quoc-ferry/) guide covers pier transfers in full detail, but three scenarios make Ha Tien the clear winner:

1. **Minimizing Seasickness:** Because the 45 km crossing takes only 60 to 75 minutes and stays partially shielded by Kampot's coastline and the Pirate Islands (`Quần đảo Hải Tặc`), passengers sensitive to pitch and roll experience half the wave exposure of the 120 km Rach Gia run.
2. **Crossing Overland from Cambodia (Kampot & Kep):** The **Xa Xia / Prek Chak International Border Gate** lies just 9 km (15 minutes by taxi, 120,000 to 150,000 VND) northwest of Ha Tien Ferry Terminal. Travelers departing Kampot at 07:30 easily make the 12:00 Superdong or 12:30 Phu Quoc Express catamaran.
3. **Bringing a Motorbike or Car on Ro-Ro Ferries:** While Rach Gia also hosts limited Thanh Thoi vehicle sailings (3 to 3.5 hours, 400,000 VND car / 120,000 VND motorbike), Ha Tien is the primary roll-on/roll-off hub. **Thanh Thoi Ferry** (`Phà Thạnh Thới`) and **Binh An Ferry** (`Phà Bình An`) run 6 to 8 daily steel ramp barges from Ha Tien (150 to 165 minutes), charging 185,000 VND per passenger plus 80,000 VND for a 110cc–150cc motorbike or 700,000 to 1,400,000 VND for a 4 to 16-seat automobile.

## Vessel Engineering: Phu Quoc Express vs. Superdong vs. Thanh Thoi

Not all boats handle Gulf of Thailand chop identically.

- **Phu Quoc Express (`Tàu Đỏ Hai Thân`):** Operates 300-passenger twin-hull aluminum catamarans (33 meters length, 9.5 meters beam) powered by twin Roll-Royce MTU diesel engines cruising at 28 to 30 knots. The wide twin-hull stance and active hydrofoil stabilization dampen lateral roll in 1.5-meter quartering seas. Cabin noise stays below 65 dB, and the forward VIP cabin (350,000 VND from Ha Tien, 540,000 VND from Rach Gia) provides leather recliners with dedicated cabin attendants.
- **Superdong Fast Ferry (`Tàu Cao Tốc Một Thân`):** Operates Malaysian-built aluminum mono-hull speedboats (270 to 306 seats) cruising at 25 to 27 knots. Mono-hull boats slice cleanly through calm morning swells (06:00 to 09:30 departures) and feature a breezy aft outdoor standing deck popular with smokers and photographers, though the narrower hull rolls more noticeably than a catamaran during afternoon winds.
- **Thanh Thoi & Binh An Ro-Ro Ferries (`Phà Biển Chở Xe`):** Heavy displacement steel hulls cruising at 12 to 14 knots. Because of their massive beam and low center of gravity (loaded with trucks, cars, and motorbikes on the lower deck), Ro-Ro ferries pitch very slowly even in moderate chop, though the crossing takes 2.5 to 3 hours.

### Cabin Seat Selection Strategy for Motion Sickness
If you are prone to seasickness, request a seat in the **lower deck, mid-ship to rear-third rows** (Rows H through M in Economy). Avoid the upper deck and the forward bow (VIP cabin sits at the bow nose): while VIP seats offer extra legroom, the bow experiences the highest vertical pitch amplitude when cresting head swells. Take 50 mg of dimenhydrinate (Nautamine, sold for 5,000 VND at pier pharmacies) 45 minutes before boarding.

## Southwest Monsoon (`May–October`) Weather Suspension Protocols

Maritime safety across Kien Giang is governed by the Port Authority (`Cảng vụ Hàng hải Kiên Giang`). Every morning at 05:30, port inspectors evaluate live marine wind bulletins from the Southern Regional Hydrometeorological Center:
- **Dry Season (November to April):** Prevailing northeast winds blow 10 to 20 km/h (**Beaufort Force 2 to 4**). Sea state remains flat (0.5 to 1.0-meter waves), and schedule reliability exceeds 98%.
- **Southwest Monsoon (May to October):** Afternoon squalls are routine, and tropical depressions in July, August, and September can push sustained westerly winds to **Beaufort Force 6 or Force 7** (39 to 61 km/h, wave heights 2.0 to 3.0 meters).
- **Force 6 Cut-Off Rule:** By Vietnamese maritime law, passenger speedboats are prohibited from leaving port whenever winds reach **Beaufort Force 6** or higher. If sailings are suspended for 24 to 48 hours, Superdong and Phu Quoc Express provide a 100% cash/card refund or free rebooking onto the next cleared departure. Never schedule an international flight out of Ho Chi Minh City (`SGN`) on the exact same day as a monsoon-season ferry return from Phu Quoc.

## Step-by-Step Arrival at Bai Vong Port (`Cảng Bãi Vòng`) & Island Transit

Every fast boat from both Rach Gia and Ha Tien docks at **Bai Vong Port** (`Xã Hàm Ninh`), situated on Phu Quoc's east coast 15 km east of Duong Dong Town and 10 km northeast of Phu Quoc International Airport (`PQC`).

- **Ferry Shuttle Buses (40,000–50,000 VND / $1.60–$2.00 USD):** Coaster buses wait inside the pier apron for every arriving boat and drop passengers along Nguyen Trung Truc and Tran Hung Dao streets in central Duong Dong (25 to 30 minutes).
- **Taxis & Grab (`Xanh SM`, `Mai Linh`, `Vinasun`):** A metered cab from Bai Vong Port to Duong Dong or central Long Beach (`Bãi Trường`) costs 200,000 to 250,000 VND ($8.00 to $10.00 USD). Transfers south to An Thoi Port and Sunset Town (26 km, 35 minutes) cost 320,000 to 380,000 VND ($12.80 to $15.20 USD), while northern rides to Ganh Dau and Grand World (38 km, 50 minutes) run 480,000 to 550,000 VND ($19.20 to $22.00 USD).
- **Mandatory Boarding ID:** Bring your physical passport (or Vietnamese CCCD ID card) to the pier 30 to 45 minutes before departure; gate inspectors check every passenger's name and passport number against the vessel manifest before opening the boarding gangway.
