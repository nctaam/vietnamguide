# Ly Son vs Cham Islands: Which Central Island to Pick? (2026)

Off the central coast of Vietnam lie two distinct archipelagos that reward coastal explorers with contrasting marine ecosystems: **[Ly Son Island](/destinations/ly-son-travel-guide/)** (*Cù Lao Ré*) in Quang Ngai province, and the **[Cham Islands](/destinations/cham-islands-travel-guide/)** (*Cù Lao Chàm*) off the historic port of [Hoi An](/destinations/hoi-an-ancient-town-guide/) in Quang Nam province. Both archipelagos offer clean ocean water away from the urban corridors of [Da Nang](/destinations/da-nang-travel-guide/) and [Nha Trang](/destinations/nha-trang-travel-guide/). Yet their geological origins, environmental regulations, ferry logistics, and daily travel costs differ sharply.

Ly Son is an exposed volcanic outpost shaped by Cenozoic eruptions, known across Vietnam for its five basalt craters, wave-cut sea cliffs, and terraced fields of single-clove garlic nourished by mineral ash and coral sand. In contrast, the Cham Islands form a steep granite archipelago covered in primary tropical forest, designated as a UNESCO World Biosphere Reserve with strict bans on single-use plastics and shallow coral reefs sheltered inside a 5,000-hectare marine park.

Choosing the right island for your central Vietnam route requires weighing mainland transfer times, ferry schedules, seasonal sea swells, diving conditions, and overnight comfort. This 2026 field comparison breaks down exact speedboat tariffs, conservation fees, port transfers along Expressway CT01 and National Highway QL1A, underwater visibility metrics, and daily budgets in Vietnamese Dong (VND) and US Dollars (USD).

## Geological Character and Terrestrial Landscapes

The physical landscapes of Ly Son and the Cham Islands reflect two completely different geological histories.

Ly Son was forged by submarine and subaerial volcanic eruptions between the late Neogene and Quaternary epochs, with the final eruptive phase occurring roughly 3,000 years ago. The main island, **Dao Lon** (*Đảo Lớn*, or Big Island), spans 10 square kilometers and is crowned by five distinct volcanic craters. The highest summit is **Thoi Loi Mountain** (*Núi Thới Lới*), an extinct basalt cone rising 149 meters above the sea. Its summit caldera holds a freshwater reservoir storing 270,000 cubic meters of water behind a concrete dam, while a 25-meter flagpole overlooks rust-colored stratified cliffs that drop 60 meters straight into the surf at **Hang Cau** (*Hang Câu*).

On the western headland of Ly Son, **Gieng Tien Mountain** (*Núi Giếng Tiên*) rises 106 meters above the coast. Climbers ascend 120 stone steps up the crater rim to reach **Duc Pagoda** (*Chùa Đục*) and a 27-meter white statue of Quan Am facing the open ocean. Five hundred meters north sits **To Vo Arch** (*Cổng Tò Vò*), a natural black basalt rock bridge measuring 20 meters long and 2.5 meters high, carved by wave erosion from solidified lava flows. Across the flat interior plains, farmers layer weathered volcanic ash with white coral sand dredged from shallow lagoons to cultivate Ly Son's prized single-clove garlic (*Tỏi cô đơn*), which sells at island markets for 800,000 VND to 1,200,000 VND ($32.00 to $48.00 USD) per kilogram.

The Cham Islands lie 15 kilometers to 18 kilometers offshore from Cua Dai beach and form an uplifted granite massif that separated from the Truong Son mountain range during Holocene sea-level rise. The main island, **Hon Lao** (*Hòn Lao*), covers 13.8 square kilometers and rises sharply to 517 meters above sea level. Over 70 percent of Hon Lao remains blanketed in dense primary evergreen forest, sheltering macaques, monitor lizards, and coastal caves where swiftlets build edible nests (*yến sào*) harvested on 40-meter bamboo scaffolding.

Because the Cham Islands operate under UNESCO Biosphere Reserve protections, heavy commercial construction is banned. A narrow 7-kilometer concrete coastal road links the main harbor at **Bai Lang** (*Bãi Làng*) with sandy coves at **Bai Ong** (*Bãi Ông*), **Bai Chong** (*Bãi Chồng*), and the southern fishing village of **Bai Huong** (*Bãi Hương*). Travelers move between beaches by walking, hiring a local motorbike taxi (*xe ôm*) for 50,000 VND to 100,000 VND ($2.00 to $4.00 USD) per ride, or chartering a private wooden sampan for 300,000 VND ($12.00 USD) per hour.

> [!NOTE]
> Environmental officers inspect all bags at the Cua Dai ferry terminal before boarding for the Cham Islands. Single-use plastic shopping bags are confiscated at the gate, and littering carries a municipal fine of 500,000 VND ($20.00 USD). Pack a reusable canvas tote and a 1-liter stainless steel water bottle.

## Marine Exploration, Coral Reefs, and Water Clarity

Underwater topography separates the two archipelagos just as clearly as their terrestrial peaks.

The Cham Islands guard one of central Vietnam's richest coral reef ecosystems. Across the 5,000-hectare marine protected area surrounding Hon Lao, Hon Dai, Hon Tai, and Hon Mo, marine biologists have recorded 135 species of hard and soft corals alongside 202 species of reef fish. Between May and August, calm seas bring underwater visibility of 12 meters to 25 meters, with water temperatures holding steady between 28°C and 30°C.

Certified PADI operators in Hoi An, including **Cham Island Diving Center** and **Blue Coral Diving**, run daily dive boats departing Cua Dai at 08:00 and returning by 15:30. A full-day two-tank scuba package for certified divers costs 1,850,000 VND to 2,100,000 VND ($74.00 to $84.00 USD), including all equipment, marine park permits, and lunch at Bai Chong. Beginners can book a Discovery Scuba Dive to 6 meters depth for 2,250,000 VND ($90.00 USD), while non-divers can join a two-site snorkeling boat trip for 850,000 VND ($34.00 USD) or try a 20-minute underwater helmet sea-trek at **Bai Nhon** at 5 meters depth for 950,000 VND ($38.00 USD).

Ly Son lacks permanent PADI dive centers and deep coral walls near its main harbor. Instead, the Big Island is ringed by wide intertidal basalt shelves and fossilized coral platforms. During afternoon low tides, water recedes 150 meters from the seawall, exposing shallow rock pools where locals harvest sea urchins (*nhím biển*), top shells (*ốc cừ*), and green seaweeds.

To experience clear swimming and snorkeling at Ly Son, you must cross 5 kilometers (3 nautical miles) northwest to **Dao Be** (*Đảo Bé*, or An Binh Commune). Composite speed canoes depart Ly Son central port every 20 minutes between 07:30 and 11:20, completing the crossing in 12 minutes to 15 minutes for a regulated round-trip fare of 120,000 VND to 140,000 VND ($4.80 to $5.60 USD). On Dao Be, electric shuttle buggies carry passengers 1.2 kilometers across the island for 30,000 VND ($1.20 USD) to **Bai Tay** (*Bãi Tây*), a cove where white coral sand meets black volcanic cliffs. From Bai Tay, local boatmen row circular coracles (*thuyền thúng*) 200 meters offshore for 60,000 VND to 80,000 VND ($2.40 to $3.20 USD) per person, dropping snorkelers over basalt boulders in 3 meters to 6 meters of water with 15-meter summer visibility.

### 1. Sa Ky Port to Ly Son Island Logistics
All passenger ferries to Ly Son depart from **Sa Ky Port** (*Cảng Sa Kỳ*) on National Highway QL24B in Binh Son district. Travelers arriving from Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City on the Reunification Express railway alight at **Quang Ngai Station** (*Ga Quảng Ngai*), located 20 kilometers southwest of Sa Ky Port; a metered Mai Linh or Sun Taxi across that 20-kilometer stretch takes 30 minutes and costs 280,000 VND to 320,000 VND ($11.20 to $12.80 USD), while local Bus Route 03 runs from Quang Ngai bus station to Sa Ky Port in 50 minutes for 25,000 VND ($1.00 USD).

If you fly into **Chu Lai Airport** (VCL), located 48 kilometers north of Sa Ky Port, a private 4-seat car transfer takes 55 minutes via National Highway QL1A and costs 500,000 VND to 600,000 VND ($20.00 to $24.00 USD). From **Da Nang International Airport** (DAD), located 135 kilometers north, driving the **CT01 Da Nang–Quang Ngai Expressway** takes 2 hours and 15 minutes at 100 km/h; a private car charter costs 1,350,000 VND to 1,500,000 VND ($54.00 to $60.00 USD) one-way, or you can book a shared 16-seat Ford Transit minibus for 250,000 VND ($10.00 USD) per passenger.

At Sa Ky terminal, four high-speed ferry lines operate 6 to 8 daily departures between 07:30 and 15:00:
* **Phu Quoc Express:** Operates a 300-seat twin-hull catamaran completing the 28-kilometer crossing in 35 minutes. Economy seats cost 182,000 VND ($7.28 USD) one-way, and VIP upper-deck recliners cost 360,000 VND ($14.40 USD).
* **Super Bien Dong & An Vinh Express:** Operate 139-seat to 168-seat monohull vessels taking 40 minutes to 45 minutes, priced at 180,000 VND to 185,000 VND ($7.20 to $7.40 USD) one-way.
* **Chinh Nghia Express:** Runs high-capacity aluminum ferries taking 38 minutes for 190,000 VND to 215,000 VND ($7.60 to $8.60 USD).
* **Mandatory Port & Heritage Fees:** Passengers pay a 15,000 VND ($0.60 USD) terminal fee included in the Sa Ky boarding pass plus a 70,000 VND ($2.80 USD) Ly Son heritage conservation fee upon arrival at Ben Dinh pier.

### 2. Cua Dai Port to Cham Islands Logistics
Reaching the Cham Islands requires far less road travel. **Cua Dai Pier** (*Bến Cửa Đại*) sits 5 kilometers east of Hoi An Ancient Town (a 10-minute GrabCar ride costing 75,000 VND to 90,000 VND, or $3.00 to $3.60 USD) and 30 kilometers south of Da Nang Airport (45 minutes by car for 350,000 VND, or $14.00 USD).

High-speed open composite canoes (*ca nô cao tốc*) depart Cua Dai between 08:00 and 09:30 each morning and return from Bai Lang between 13:30 and 14:30. The 15-kilometer crossing takes 18 minutes to 22 minutes. Independent round-trip speedboat tickets cost 350,000 VND to 450,000 VND ($14.00 to $18.00 USD), plus two mandatory municipal tickets collected at the terminal gate: a 70,000 VND ($2.80 USD) Marine Protected Area entrance ticket and a 20,000 VND ($0.80 USD) environmental fee. All-inclusive day tours from Hoi An hotels bundle hotel pickup, speedboat transfers, the 90,000 VND ($3.60 USD) entrance fees, snorkeling gear, and a seafood lunch at Bai Ong or Bai Chong for 650,000 VND to 800,000 VND ($26.00 to $32.00 USD).

Budget travelers staying overnight can board the wooden public supply vessel (**Tàu Chợ Cù Lao Chàm**), which departs Cua Dai Pier daily at 08:00 (and leaves Bai Lang at 11:30). The 75-minute wooden boat ride costs 150,000 VND ($6.00 USD) per foreign passenger (including the 70,000 VND island ticket), and transporting a rented motorbike costs an extra 50,000 VND ($2.00 USD) each way.

### 3. Seasonal Monsoon Cutoffs
Both islands experience strict maritime shutdowns during the northeast monsoon from October through February. Whenever wind speeds exceed Force 6 on the Beaufort scale (39 km/h to 49 km/h) and offshore swells top 2.0 meters, the Quang Ngai and Quang Nam Port Authorities suspend all passenger vessels. Peak calm conditions run from April through August, when sea surface temperatures average 29°C and wave heights stay below 0.5 meters to 0.8 meters.

## Overnight Stays, Dining, and Cultural Rhythms

Your choice between Ly Son and the Cham Islands depends on whether you want a multi-day scooter expedition or a half-day marine escape.

Ly Son rewards a 2-night, 3-day itinerary (48 hours to 72 hours). Supporting 22,000 permanent residents, Big Island offers four-star seafront hotels like **Muong Thanh Holiday Ly Son** (1,150,000 VND to 1,650,000 VND, or $46.00 to $66.00 USD per night including breakfast), cliffside bungalows at **Ly Son Pearl Island Hotel & Resort** (1,300,000 VND to 1,900,000 VND, or $52.00 to $76.00 USD), and clean family guesthouses (*nhà nghỉ*) near Ben Dinh harbor for 300,000 VND to 450,000 VND ($12.00 to $18.00 USD) per night. On Dao Be, rustic beachside homestays and wooden A-frame tents charge 250,000 VND to 400,000 VND ($10.00 to $16.00 USD) per night.

Renting a 110cc Honda Wave or Vision scooter on Ly Son costs 130,000 VND to 150,000 VND ($5.20 to $6.00 USD) for 24 hours including fuel. You can ride the 10-kilometer coastal road to **Hang Pagoda** (*Chùa Hang*, free entry, 24 meters deep), inspect the **An Hai Whale Skeleton Temple** (*Lăng Tân*, housing the 22-meter-long preserved skeleton of a whale named *Đồng Đình Đại Vương*, entry 10,000 VND / $0.40 USD), and visit the **Hoang Sa Flotilla Memorial Museum** (*Nhà Trưng Bày Hải Đội Hoàng Sa Kiêm Quản Bắc Hải*, free admission, open 07:30 to 17:00), which honors the 17th-century sailors who patrolled the Paracel Islands in wooden junks. At dusk, harborfront seafood stalls serve steamed young garlic stalk salad (*gỏi tỏi non*) for 50,000 VND ($2.00 USD), grilled sea urchins with scallion oil for 25,000 VND ($1.00 USD) each, and steamed king crab (*cua huỳnh đế*) for 950,000 VND to 1,200,000 VND ($38.00 to $48.00 USD) per kilogram.

The Cham Islands work best as a 6-hour day trip from Hoi An or a quiet 1-night homestay retreat. Over 90 percent of visitors arrive at 09:00, walk 400 meters through Bai Lang village to see the 1758 **Hai Tang Pagoda** (*Chùa Hải Tạng*) and the 200-year-old **Cham Ancient Well** (*Giếng Cổ Chăm*), snorkel for 90 minutes, eat lunch at Bai Chong, and board the 14:00 speedboat back to the mainland.

Travelers who stay behind after 14:30 experience a peaceful island evening. Family homestays in Bai Lang, Bai Ong, and Bai Huong (such as **Hammock Homestay**, **Sunbay Homestay**, and **Monkey Homestay**) charge 250,000 VND to 450,000 VND ($10.00 to $18.00 USD) per double room. Host families serve set dinners for 120,000 VND to 180,000 VND ($4.80 to $7.20 USD) per person featuring boiled wild mountain greens (*rau rừng luộc*) dipped in fermented anchovy sauce (*mắm cái*), grilled squid (*mực một nắng* at 250,000 VND / $10.00 USD per plate), cone snails (*ốc vú nàng* at 120,000 VND / $4.80 USD), and steamed mugwort leaf rice cakes (*bánh ít lá gai* at 5,000 VND / $0.20 USD each).

## Step-by-Step Protocol: How to Plan Your Island Crossing in 2026

Follow this 4-step operational checklist to secure ferry seats and avoid weather disruptions on either archipelago:

1. **Check the 72-Hour Marine Wind Forecast:** Before paying for non-refundable island hotels, verify offshore wind speeds on Windy.com for Quang Ngai or Hoi An. If wind gusts stay under 15 knots (28 km/h) and wave height remains below 1.0 meter, high-speed ferries operate on schedule.
2. **Book Ferry Tickets 48 Hours in Advance with Passport Details:** For Ly Son, reserve online via `cangsaky.com.vn` or directly with Phu Quoc Express (`182,000 VND` / `$7.28 USD`), entering your exact passport number and birth year required by the Maritime Port Authority. For the Cham Islands, reserve your speed canoe (`350,000 VND` / `$14.00 USD` round-trip) through your Hoi An hotel or diving center by 17:00 the day prior.
3. **Arrive at the Mainland Pier by 07:15 AM:** Morning seas between 07:30 and 09:00 are significantly calmer than afternoon crossings. Present your original passport at Sa Ky Gate 1 or Cua Dai Ticket Counter 2 at least 30 minutes before departure, and pack a 50 mg dimenhydrinate motion-sickness tablet (`5,000 VND` / `$0.20 USD` at any pharmacy) if swell exceeds 0.8 meters.
4. **Carry Sufficient Cash in Vietnamese Dong:** Neither Dao Be (Ly Son) nor Bai Huong (Cham Islands) has reliable international ATMs, and coastal homestays add a 3 percent surcharge on card terminals. Withdraw at least 1,500,000 VND to 2,500,000 VND ($60.00 to $100.00 USD) per person in Da Nang, Hoi An, or Quang Ngai City before boarding the boat.

## Strategic Verdict: Which Island Should You Visit?

Match your available travel days and budget to the strengths of each island:

* **Choose Ly Son Island if:**
  * You want to explore volcanic craters (149 m Thoi Loi peak), seaside cave pagodas, and black basalt arches over a 2-to-3-day itinerary.
  * You enjoy riding a rented 110cc scooter (150,000 VND / $6.00 USD per day) along cliffside roads without tour-bus crowds.
  * You are riding the Reunification Express train between Da Nang and Quy Nhon and can stop at Quang Ngai Station (20 km from Sa Ky Port).
  * You want to swim and free-dive over volcanic reefs at Dao Be (120,000 VND / $4.80 USD tender crossing) and feast on fresh sea urchins and garlic salads at local harbor prices.
* **Choose the Cham Islands if:**
  * You are staying in Hoi An or Da Nang and want an easy half-day or overnight marine trip just 18 minutes by speedboat from Cua Dai Pier.
  * Certified PADI scuba diving (1,850,000 VND / $74.00 USD for two tanks) or guided reef snorkeling in 15-to-25-meter visibility is your top priority.
  * You want to support a strict zero-plastic UNESCO Biosphere Reserve and sleep in a quiet fishing hamlet like Bai Huong (300,000 VND / $12.00 USD per night).
  * Your schedule cannot accommodate the 4.5-hour round-trip highway drive between Da Nang and Sa Ky Port.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you visit Ly Son as a day trip from Da Nang or Hoi An?
While technically possible if you hire a private car leaving Da Nang at 04:45 AM (135 km via CT01 Expressway, 2 hours 15 minutes) to catch the 07:30 AM ferry from Sa Ky Port, doing Ly Son as a single day trip is exhausting. You will spend 4.5 hours on highways and 80 minutes on ferries, leaving barely 4 hours on the island before the final 15:00 return boat. Plan at least 1 or 2 nights (24 to 48 hours) on Ly Son, or choose the Cham Islands (20 minutes from Hoi An) if you only have a single day.

### Is certified scuba diving available on Ly Son Island?
No permanent PADI or SSI dive centers operate on Ly Son Island. Visitors can rent masks and fins for free-diving and surface snorkeling from basket boats at Dao Be (`60,000 VND` / `$2.40 USD` per person in `3 m` to `6 m` depth), where summer water clarity reaches `15 meters`. Travelers seeking tank dives with certified divemasters at `12 meters` to `28 meters` depth should choose the Cham Islands off Hoi An (`1,850,000 VND` / `$74.00 USD` for two dives).

### What are the best months of the year to visit both islands?
May, June, July, and August offer the calmest sea conditions and clearest underwater visibility (`12 meters` to `25 meters`) for both Ly Son and the Cham Islands. During these summer months, daytime temperatures range from `29°C` to `34°C`, rainfall averages under `45 mm` per month, and offshore wave heights remain below `0.6 meters`. Avoid October through January, when northeast monsoon gales shut down ferries for days at a time.

### How do you travel between Big Island (Dao Lon) and Little Island (Dao Be) in Ly Son?
Licensed composite speed canoes depart Ly Son central port (Ben Dinh) for Dao Be (An Binh Island) every `20 minutes` between `07:30` and `11:20` daily, returning between `12:00` and `14:30`. The `5 km` crossing takes `12 minutes` to `15 minutes` and costs `120,000 VND` to `140,000 VND` (`$4.80` to `$5.60 USD`) per passenger round-trip. Once on Dao Be, electric buggies shuttle travelers `1.2 km` from the pier to Bai Tay beach for `30,000 VND` (`$1.20 USD`).

### Are foreigners allowed to visit Both Ly Son and the Cham Islands without a special military permit?
Yes. In 2026, foreign nationals do not need a separate military border permit to visit either Ly Son Island or the Cham Islands. You simply present your original passport and valid Vietnamese visa (or visa-exempt entry stamp) when purchasing your ferry ticket at Sa Ky Port or Cua Dai Pier, and register your passport at your island hotel or homestay upon check-in.
