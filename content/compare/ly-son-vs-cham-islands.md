# Ly Son vs Cham Islands: Which Central Island to Pick? (2026)

Off the rugged coast of central Vietnam lie two distinct archipelagos that command the attention of coastal explorers: **Ly Son Island** (*Cù Lao Ré*) in Quang Ngai province, and the **Cham Islands** (*Cù Lao Chàm*) off the historic harbor of Hoi An in Quang Nam province. While both offer respite from the urban bustle of Da Nang and Nha Trang, their geological foundations, administrative conservation policies, maritime logistics, and cultural atmospheres could hardly be more different.

Ly Son is an exposed, dramatic outpost born of violent Cenozoic volcanism, famed across Vietnam for its ancient basalt craters, sacred sea cliffs, and terraced garlic fields nourished by mineral-rich ash and coral sand. In contrast, the Cham Islands form a lush granite archipelago blanketed in primary rainforest, celebrated as a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve with strict bans on single-use plastics and vibrant coral gardens sheltered within a shallow marine sanctuary.

Deciding which island to incorporate into your central Vietnam itinerary requires evaluating nautical access, seasonal weather corridors, accommodation depth, and your personal travel rhythm. This empirical field assessment dissects ferry schedules, statutory marine fees, diving quality, overnight homestay mechanics, and overland transit routes for 2026 travelers.

## Geological Character and Terrestrial Landscapes

The landscape differences between Ly Son and the Cham Islands stem from vastly divergent geological origins.

Ly Son is a volcanic island created through repeated submarine and subaerial eruptions between the late Neogene and Quaternary periods, with the most recent volcanic event occurring roughly 3,000 years ago. The Big Island (*Đảo Lớn*) covers approximately 10 square kilometers dominated by five prominent volcanic cones. The most striking is **Thoi Loi Mountain** (*Núi Thới Lới*), an extinct basalt crater rising 149 meters above sea level. Its summit cradles a freshwater crater lake that feeds the island's irrigation canals, while sheer, rust-colored cliffs plunge straight into the pounding surf below.

Along the western shore of Ly Son sits the iconic **To Vo Arch** (*Cổng Tò Vò*), a natural basalt rock bridge formed when molten lava cooled upon entering the sea and was gradually excavated by wave erosion. Inland, the volcanic soil is meticulously layered with sea sand dredged from coral lagoons to cultivate Ly Son's renowned single-clove "lone pearl" garlic (*Tỏi cô đơn*), transforming the interior plains into a geometric quilt of white and emerald plots.

The Cham Islands, situated 15 kilometers offshore from Hoi An, represent an exposed granite massif that was once part of the continental mainland before sea levels rose during the Holocene epoch. The main island, Hon Lao, spans 13 square kilometers characterized by steep, densely forested ridges rising to 517 meters. Unbroken canopies of primary tropical evergreen broadleaf forest tumble down to sandy coves, providing a habitat for swiftlets whose edible nests (*yến sào*) have been harvested for centuries.

Because the Cham Islands enjoy strict environmental protection under UNESCO stewardship, there are no sprawling concrete highways or multi-story resorts. Visitors hike or take scooter taxis along narrow coastal tracks connecting fishing hamlets, passing sweet-water mountain streams and sheltered coves like Bai Chong, Bai Ong, and Bai Huong.

> [!NOTE]
> Cham Islands environmental wardens conduct mandatory inspections at Cua Dai pier. Plastic shopping bags are strictly confiscated prior to boarding. Travelers should bring reusable canvas dry-bags and refillable water bottles.

## Marine Exploration, Coral Reefs, and Water Clarity

Aquatic conditions vary substantially depending on how you wish to engage with the marine environment.

The waters surrounding the Cham Islands host one of northern and central Vietnam's healthiest coral reef systems. Protected within a 5,000-hectare marine reserve, the archipelago features roughly 135 species of hard and soft corals, 200 species of reef fish, and extensive seagrass meadows. Visibility typically ranges from 8 to 15 meters between May and August.

Professional dive operations based in Hoi An, such as Cham Island Diving Center, operate daily excursions to dive sites around Hon Dai, Hon Tai, and Bai Xep. Novices can participate in discovery scuba dives or join helmet-guided "sea trekking" walks along submerged seabed platforms in 5 meters of water. Snorkelers taking standard day tours are brought to shallow reefs, though heavy tour boat concentrations around Bai Chong can stir up sediment in the afternoon.

Ly Son's aquatic landscape is defined by extensive intertidal basalt shelves and shallow fringe lagoons rather than deep scuba drop-offs. At low tide, the coral platforms surrounding the Big Island emerge above the waterline, allowing visitors to walk across tide pools inhabited by sea cucumbers, mantis shrimp, and colorful crabs.

To find world-class water clarity on Ly Son, you must take a 10-minute wooden canoe or local tender across to **Dao Be** (An Binh Island), located 5 kilometers northwest. Dao Be boasts powdery white coral beaches at Bai Tay and crystalline turquoise water framed by jagged black basalt sea cliffs. While organized dive shops are rare on Dao Be, free-diving and drift-snorkeling over the nearshore volcanic drop-offs provide an raw, unmanicured underwater experience without tour groups.

## Ferry Crossings, Weather Vulnerability, and Port Logistics

Reaching both archipelagos requires navigating open sea crossings that are highly sensitive to seasonal weather patterns.

```
MAINLAND TO ISLAND FERRY TRANSIT CORRIDORS
├── Sa Ky Port to Ly Son (Big Island)
│   ├── Operator: Phu Quoc Express, An Vinh Express, Super Bien Dong
│   ├── Distance: 28 km (15 nautical miles) open South China Sea
│   ├── Transit Time: 35 to 45 minutes on modern 300-seat catamarans
│   ├── Fare: 180,000 – 215,000 VND (~$7.20–$8.50 USD) one-way
│   └── Port Transfer: 20 km from Quang Ngai train station; 130 km from Da Nang (2.5h drive)
└── Cua Dai Port to Cham Islands (Hon Lao)
    ├── Operator: Licensed speed canoes (Song Hoi, Danang Explorer, private charters)
    ├── Distance: 15–18 km across the mouth of the Thu Bon River
    ├── Transit Time: 18 to 22 minutes on 30-to-40-seat open speedboats
    ├── Fare: 350,000 – 500,000 VND round-trip (includes marine conservation fees)
    └── Port Transfer: 5 km from Hoi An Ancient Town (10 min taxi); 30 km from Da Nang (45 min)
```

The ferry to Ly Son departs from Sa Ky Port in Binh Son district. Travelers reaching Sa Ky from Da Nang must coordinate a 2.5-hour car transfer or take a train to Quang Ngai City followed by a 30-minute taxi ride to the harbor. High-speed catamarans operate between 07:00 AM and 15:30 PM. The crossing enters the open ocean, where oceanic swell can cause motion sickness during breezy conditions.

The transit to the Cham Islands from Hoi An is significantly more convenient. Cua Dai pier sits just a 10-minute taxi ride from the center of Hoi An Ancient Town. High-speed canoes blast across the channel in roughly 20 minutes. For budget travelers or those transporting motorbikes, a traditional wooden public supply boat departs daily from Bach Dang wharf near Hoi An's central market at roughly 08:00 AM, costing 150,000 VND for a relaxed 75-minute crossing.

Both destinations shut down entirely during the Northeast monsoon season. From late September through January, powerful ocean swells, gale-force winds, and tropical depressions prompt port authorities to cancel maritime departures for days or weeks at a time. The optimal travel corridor for both islands spans from March through August, with calm seas and peak solar clarity occurring in June and July.

## Overnight Stays, Dining, and Cultural Rhythms

Your choice between the two destinations should reflect whether you want a full multi-day island journey or a swift coastal excursion.

Ly Son requires at least two full days to appreciate. The Big Island boasts a complete commercial ecosystem supporting 22,000 permanent residents. Accommodations range from seaside boutique hotels like Muong Thanh Holiday Ly Son to family-run guesthouses (*nhà nghỉ*) along the main harbor boulevard. Evenings revolve around the night market near the ferry pier, where diners feast on steamed red grouper, grilled sea urchins (*nhím biển*), king crab (*cua huỳnh đế*), and crispy garlic salads made with fresh young stalks.

Renting a semi-automatic scooter for 150,000 to 200,000 VND per day is essential for circling the 10-kilometer coastline, climbing up to the Hang Pagoda (*Chùa Hang*) carved inside a seaside cave, and honoring Vietnam's historic seafaring legacy at the Hoang Sa Flotilla Memorial Museum (*Nhà trưng bày Hải đội Hoàng Sa*).

The Cham Islands function primarily as a day-trip destination for travelers based in Hoi An. Most organized tour groups arrive at Bai Lang pier around 09:30 AM, visit the 250-year-old Hai Tang Pagoda and the ancient Cham well, proceed to Bai Chong for swimming and a seafood lunch, and depart by 14:00 PM before afternoon breezes generate chop on the return crossing.

However, independent travelers who stay overnight in **Bai Huong**—a tranquil fishing village on the island's southern hook—discover an enchanting, unhurried coastal lifestyle. In Bai Huong, family homestays charge 200,000 to 350,000 VND per night, offering home-cooked dinners of wild forest vegetables (*rau rừng luộc*) dipped in pungent fish sauce, grilled squid, and quiet conversations under lantern light as fishing skiffs motor out to sea.

## Strategic Verdict: Which Island Should You Visit?

Match your travel timetable and expectations to the distinct strengths of each archipelago:

- **Choose Ly Son Island if:**
  - You are fascinated by dramatic volcanic topography, craters, and sea cliffs.
  - You want an immersive 2-to-3-day island road trip on a rented scooter.
  - You are traveling through central Vietnam and can easily stop in Quang Ngai by train.
  - You want to visit Dao Be (Little Island) for striking white sand beaches and black volcanic boulders.
  - You enjoy eating fresh seafood at bustling local night markets without resort markups.
- **Choose the Cham Islands if:**
  - You are based in Hoi An or Da Nang and want a quick, seamless island day excursion (20-minute boat ride).
  - You prioritize scuba diving or guided snorkeling among healthy coral gardens.
  - You support strict eco-tourism and want to experience a plastic-free UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.
  - You want a peaceful overnight homestay experience in an authentic fishing hamlet like Bai Huong.
  - You have limited time and cannot afford a full day of mainland transit to Sa Ky Port.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you visit Ly Son as a day trip from Da Nang?
While technically possible with an early private car departure (04:30 AM), doing Ly Son as a single day trip is not recommended. The one-way journey requires 2.5 hours of driving to Sa Ky Port followed by a 45-minute ferry crossing. Spending over 6 hours in transit leaves minimal time to explore the volcanic sights. Plan for at least one or two overnight stays on the island.

### Is scuba diving available on Ly Son Island?
Commercial scuba diving infrastructure on Ly Son is minimal and intermittent. While local dive guides occasionally organize shore-based tank dives on Dao Be during the calm summer months, Ly Son lacks permanent PADI-certified dive shops. If certified scuba diving is your primary priority, the Cham Islands offer far better established dive centers based out of Hoi An.

### What is the best month to visit both islands?
May, June, and July are the ideal months to visit both Ly Son and the Cham Islands. During this peak summer window, ocean swells are minimal, visibility underwater reaches its annual maximum (10 to 15 meters), and ferry crossings operate on steady, predictable daily schedules.

### How do you get from Big Island to Little Island (Dao Be) in Ly Son?
Wooden canoes and composite tenders make regular transfers between Big Island (Dao Lon) and Little Island (Dao Be / An Binh) from 07:00 AM to 14:00 PM daily. The crossing takes 10 to 15 minutes and costs 50,000 to 80,000 VND per person for a round-trip ticket. Electric open-air buggies on Dao Be shuttle visitors from the jetty to Bai Tay beach for 20,000 to 30,000 VND.

### Are plastic bags truly banned on the Cham Islands?
Yes. Environmental officials inspect luggage at Cua Dai pier and actively confiscate single-use plastic bags. Local shops and homestays on the Cham Islands provide woven bamboo baskets, paper bags, or banana-leaf wrappings. Travelers should pack light reusable dry bags and personal water canteens.
