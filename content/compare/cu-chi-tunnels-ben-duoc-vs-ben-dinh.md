# Ben Duoc vs Ben Dinh: Which Cu Chi Tunnels Site to Visit?

Burrowed beneath the rubber plantations and iron-rich laterite soils of Ho Chi Minh City's northwestern periphery lies one of the most sophisticated subterranean guerrilla redoubts in modern military history: the Cu Chi Tunnels (*Địa đạo Củ Chi*). Spanning more than 250 kilometers of interconnected passages across three subterranean levels, the network functioned as a living fortress, logistics corridor, and operational staging base for the National Liberation Front (NLF) throughout the Second Indochina War.

Today, international travelers planning a day trip from central Saigon face a pivotal decision between two distinct public sites: **Ben Dinh** and **Ben Duoc**. Located roughly 20 kilometers apart along the Saigon River basin, these dual sectors deliver vastly disparate visitor experiences, crowd densities, physical challenges, and historical framing.

This empirical field guide compares driving corridors, tunnel bore dimensions, memorial solemnity, physical crawl requirements, live-fire shooting ranges, and public transport options for 2026 travelers.

```
CU CHI SUBTERRANEAN SECTOR OVERVIEW
├── Ben Dinh (Commercial Gateway)
│   ├── Distance: 50 km northwest of District 1 (1.5 hours by road)
│   ├── Tourist Share: Roughly 80% to 85% of commercial group tour buses
│   ├── Passages: Artificially enlarged by 20% to 30%, widened shafts, interior lighting
│   └── Profile: High foot traffic, souvenir corridors, fast half-day turnaround
└── Ben Duoc (National Memorial & Historical Redoubt)
    ├── Distance: 70 km northwest of District 1 (2.0 to 2.5 hours by road or public bus)
    ├── Tourist Share: Independent travelers, military historians, domestic delegations
    ├── Passages: Authentic original dimensions, three steep tiers descending to 10m
    └── Highlights: Ben Duoc Memorial Temple (44,520 martyrs), quiet forest setting
```

## Historical Context and Site Evolution

Understanding why these two sectors feel so different requires examining their wartime roles and postwar development.

During the war, Ben Dinh served primarily as an operational base for the Cu Chi District Party Committee. Because it lay closer to the Saigon River and adjacent American installations—specifically the sprawling 25th Infantry Division base camp at Dong Du—the area suffered intense chemical defoliation via Agent Orange, aerial bombardment by B-52 Stratofortresses, and aggressive search-and-destroy operations. Following 1975, the Ho Chi Minh City tourism authority identified Ben Dinh as the optimal portal for international tourism due to its relative proximity to downtown hotels.

To accommodate visitors of larger stature, engineers widened selected tunnel sections at Ben Dinh by 20 to 30 percent, reinforced collapsing clay ceilings with cement formwork, and installed low-voltage string lighting along the crawlways. Emergency exits were cut every 20 meters, allowing claustrophobic travelers to climb out into fresh air without impeding others.

Ben Duoc, located 20 kilometers further upstream in An Nhon Tay commune, functioned as the regional headquarters for the Saigon-Gia Dinh Military Zone Command (*Khu ủy Sài Gòn - Chợ Lớn - Gia Định*). This complex constituted a vital tactical sanctuary, housing weapon foundries, underground surgical wards, printing presses, and shelter chambers engineered to withstand 500-pound bomb strikes.

Because Ben Duoc remained further from central Ho Chi Minh City, authorities developed it not as a fast-turnaround tourist attraction, but as a formal national memorial park. The surrounding woodland has regenerated into dense foliage, and the underground tunnels retain their claustrophobic, hand-hewn profiles cut through reddish laterite earth.

## Subterranean Reality: Crawl Sizes, Depth Levels, and Physical Demands

The physical exertion required to traverse the passages represents the single greatest distinction between the two locations.

At Ben Dinh, the standard tunnel demonstration segment measures approximately 100 meters long, segmented by discrete exit points at 20-meter intervals. The entry shafts have been widened and fitted with wooden steps, allowing visitors to walk in a hunched or duck-walk posture rather than crawling flat on the floor. Battery-powered lamps illuminate the shafts, mitigating severe disorientation. Most physically active travelers complete the full 100-meter course in 10 to 15 minutes without extreme distress, though high humidity and elevated temperatures (frequently exceeding 32°C underground) produce intense perspiration.

At Ben Duoc, the underground labyrinth tests spatial endurance. While an introductory 50-meter segment has been modestly expanded for initial orientation, the deeper secondary passages preserve their wartime proportions: barely 60 centimeters wide and 80 centimeters tall. Negotiating these deeper sections requires crawling on hands and knees, sliding along bare clay surfaces, and pulling oneself through narrow horizontal portals.

Furthermore, Ben Duoc allows visitors to descend through all three historical tiers:

- **Tier 1 (3 meters depth):** Upper living quarters, firing firing-slits (*lỗ châu mai*), and connection trenches designed to resist infantry assault and artillery shrapnel.
- **Tier 2 (6 meters depth):** Operational offices, kitchen quarters with underground Hoang Cam smoke-dispersion flues, and field surgical theaters.
- **Tier 3 (9 to 10 meters depth):** Deepest bomb shelters and emergency water-well access shafts engineered to survive direct aerial concussions and bulldozer scraping.

The air in Ben Duoc's third tier is noticeably thicker, oxygen levels drop slightly, and total darkness envelops any segment where you switch off your personal headlamp. Travelers with a history of claustrophobia, cardiovascular ailments, asthma, or knee mobility restrictions should remain on the surface walking paths.

> [!NOTE]
> Laterite soil contains high iron oxide concentrations that stain light-colored clothing permanently upon contact. Wear durable dark cotton clothing and enclosed athletic footwear with gripping rubber soles when exploring Ben Duoc's crawl sections.

## Cultural Memorial Atmosphere vs Commercialized Tourism

The surface environments at Ben Dinh and Ben Duoc present contrasting emotional tones.

Ben Dinh processes thousands of international tourists daily. Mid-morning arrivals encounter hundreds of diesel tour buses filling the paved parking lots. The surface walking paths feature theatrical setups: mannequins dressed in black pajamas and checkered kramas illustrating guerrilla combat, captured M41 Walker Bulldog light tanks, bomb craters, and demonstrations of punji stick traps (tiger traps, folding traps, and clipping traps). The commercial zone contains rows of souvenir stalls selling military surplus lighters, snake wine, and carved shell casings, alongside loud video briefing rooms playing grainy historical documentaries.

Ben Duoc presents a quiet, commemorative atmosphere. Covering 100 hectares of preserved forest, the complex center is the majestic **Ben Duoc Memorial Temple** (*Đền Tưởng niệm Liệt sĩ Bến Dược*), consecrated in 1995. Built in classical Vietnamese tripartite architectural style with multi-tiered glazed tile roofs and carved ironwood columns, the main sanctuary houses memorial steles carved with the names of 44,520 fallen revolutionary fighters, militia members, and civilian casualties who perished in the Saigon-Cholon-Gia Dinh operational theater.

Visiting Ben Duoc involves walking past tranquil lotus ponds, stone stele gardens, and quiet pine groves. Domestic visitors outnumber foreign tourists, and the site serves as a place of quiet reflection and pilgrimage rather than a casual vacation excursion.

## Live-Fire Firing Ranges and Tactical Demonstrations

Both sites operate live-fire shooting ranges supervised by Vietnamese military personnel from the national defense sports federation.

Travelers can purchase brass ammunition cartridges to fire authentic wartime small arms, including Soviet-designed AK-47 rifles, American M16 assault rifles, M60 light machine guns, and M30 carbines.

Key financial and procedural guidelines apply across both ranges:

- **Ammunition Tariffs:** Cartridges are sold in minimum clips of 5 to 10 rounds. Standard 7.62×39mm (AK-47) and 5.56×45mm (M16) rounds cost 60,000 VND (~$2.40 USD) per round. A 10-round magazine requires an outlay of 600,000 VND (~$24.00 USD).
- **Safety Protocol:** Range officers lock rifles onto stationary steel stanchions to eliminate muzzle deviation. Shooters wear ear protection and trigger rounds under direct, hands-on supervision by range marshals.
- **Auditory Impact:** The gunfire reverberates loudly through adjacent forest trails. Travelers seeking quiet historical immersion should note that gunfire is audible across both Ben Dinh and Ben Duoc during peak operational hours.

## Strategic Verdict: Which Site Matches Your Travel Profile?

Select the site that aligns with your available transit time, physical conditioning, and historical interest:

- **Choose Ben Dinh if:**
  - You have only a half-day window (4 to 5 hours total) and need to return to Saigon for afternoon sightseeing.
  - You prefer booking an inexpensive organized group bus tour ($15 to $25 USD per person including transport).
  - You feel nervous in confined spaces and need wider tunnels with electric lighting and frequent exits.
  - You are traveling with young children or seniors who prefer flat, paved walking trails.
- **Choose Ben Duoc if:**
  - You want to experience the authentic claustrophobia and raw physical dimensions of original military tunnels.
  - You wish to descend into deep second- and third-tier subterranean chambers.
  - You want to avoid tour bus crowds and commercial souvenir markets.
  - You value visiting the solemn Ben Duoc Memorial Temple to understand the human cost of the conflict.
  - You are traveling independently by public bus, rented motorbike, or private car hire.

## Practical Logistics: Routes, Timetables, and Transport Options

Choosing your transportation method dictates travel time and comfort when navigating Ho Chi Minh City's northern traffic corridor.

```

├── Route Option A: Organized Group Bus (To Ben Dinh)
│   ├── Origin: Pham Ngu Lao / Bui Vien (District 1)
│   ├── Fare: 350,000 to 550,000 VND (~$14–$22 USD) including guide and entrance
│   └── Travel Time: 90 to 110 minutes each way via Highway 22
├── Route Option B: Public Bus Network (To Ben Duoc)
│   ├── Step 1: Bus 13 from 23/9 Park (Ben Thanh) to Cu Chi Bus Station (10,000 VND, 75 mins)
│   ├── Step 2: Bus 79 from Cu Chi Station directly to Ben Duoc Gates (7,000 VND, 45 mins)
│   └── Total Cost: 17,000 VND (~$0.70 USD) each way; total journey roughly 2.5 hours
├── Route Option C: High-Speed Riverboat (To Ben Dinh or Ben Duoc)
│   ├── Operator: Les Rives or Saigon River Tour departing Bach Dang Wharf
│   ├── Fare: 1,900,000 to 2,400,000 VND (~$75–$95 USD) all-inclusive half-day package
│   └── Travel Time: 75 minutes cruising along the scenic Saigon River (bypasses all road traffic)
└── Route Option D: Private Car / Grab Charter
    ├── Distance: 50 km (Ben Dinh) or 70 km (Ben Duoc)
    └── Fare: 1,200,000 to 1,600,000 VND (~$48–$64 USD) round-trip with driver waiting time
```

Operating hours run daily from 07:00 AM to 17:00 PM at both sectors. For independent travelers heading to Ben Dinh, arriving between 07:30 AM and 08:30 AM avoids the major group tour coaches, which generally pull into the gates between 09:30 AM and 11:00 AM. At Ben Duoc, visitor arrivals remain steady and uncrowded throughout the entire operational day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Cu Chi Tunnels site is less crowded?
Ben Duoc is significantly less crowded than Ben Dinh. Because Ben Duoc is located 20 kilometers further from Ho Chi Minh City, roughly 80% to 85% of standard commercial group tours bypass it entirely and head to Ben Dinh. At Ben Duoc, visitors encounter quiet forest paths, empty tunnel segments, and minimal queueing.

### Are the tunnels safe for people with claustrophobia?
Travelers with severe claustrophobia should not crawl inside the tunnels at either site. However, entering the underground passages is completely voluntary. The aboveground walking tour features surface bomb shelters, booby-trap demonstrations, historical films, and dining huts where boiled cassava root is served with crushed peanuts and salt.

### How do you get to Ben Duoc using public transport?
Board public Bus 13 at the 23/9 Park bus terminal near Ben Thanh Market in District 1. Ride Bus 13 to its final stop at the Cu Chi Bus Station (fare 10,000 VND, approximately 75 minutes). At Cu Chi Station, transfer to local Bus 79, which drops passengers directly at the main entrance gate of Ben Duoc (fare 7,000 VND, approximately 45 minutes). The total one-way transit cost is 17,000 VND.

### How much does it cost to shoot real guns at the shooting range?
Live-fire cartridges cost approximately 60,000 VND (~$2.40 USD) per bullet for standard military ammunition, including AK-47 (7.62mm) and M16 (5.56mm) rounds. Range marshals enforce a minimum purchase of 5 to 10 bullets per magazine, making the minimum shooting activity cost 300,000 to 600,000 VND (~$12 to $24 USD).

### Can you take a boat from Ho Chi Minh City to Cu Chi?
Yes. Luxury high-speed riverboat operators such as Les Rives provide morning and afternoon departures from Bach Dang Wharf in central District 1. The boat trip takes roughly 75 to 80 minutes along the Saigon River, eliminating suburban highway traffic jams. Tour packages cost between 1.9 million and 2.4 million VND ($75 to $95 USD) per person, inclusive of breakfast or lunch, entrance tickets, and an English-speaking guide.
