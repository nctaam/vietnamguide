# Da Nang Travel Guide: Beaches, City Base & Day Trips

Da Nang is the commercial, logistical, and air transport hub of Central Vietnam. Located equidistant between Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City along the curving crescent of Da Nang Bay, this metropolis of 1.2 million residents presents an unusual urban balance in Southeast Asia: thirty kilometers of clean sandy beaches, wide multilane boulevards free of motorcycle gridlock, a navigable tidal river spanned by architectural showpiece bridges, and forested granite peaks framing the skyline.

Historically known during French colonial rule as Tourane and later functioning as a colossal American military staging base during the Vietnam War, modern Da Nang has reinvented itself into a polished coastal gateway. For international travelers navigating the Central Heritage Circuit, Da Nang provides a comfortable, high-infrastructure base from which to explore the imperial monuments of Hue (100 km north), the atmospheric lanes of Hoi An (30 km south), and the inland Hindu sanctuaries of My Son. 

This comprehensive guide details the practical realities of using Da Nang as a travel base—contrasting beachfront living with downtown riverfront neighborhoods, decoding transport across the Hai Van Pass, and clarifying local safety regulations on the Son Tra Peninsula.

## 1. Choosing Your Da Nang Base: Beach Strip vs. Riverfront Downtown

The Han River bisects Da Nang into two fundamentally different residential environments connected by four major bridges. Deciding where to anchor your stay shapes your daily rhythms, noise levels, and dining options.

### The Coastal Beach Strip (My Khe, An Thuong & Non Nuoc)
Stretching south from Son Tra toward Hoi An, the eastern coastal district (Ngu Hanh Son and Son Tra wards) centers around **My Khe Beach**:
- **An Thuong Expat Quarter:** A compact grid of paved streets adjacent to My Khe packed with surf academies, boutique coffee roasters, craft beer bars, and modern serviced apartments. Highly walkable and English-friendly, it appeals to remote workers and travelers prioritizing immediate surf and sand access.
- **Vo Nguyen Giap Boulevard:** The main shoreline avenue lined with luxury high-rise hotels, infinity-pool towers, and open-air seafood establishments where fresh grouper, mantis shrimp, and clams are kept alive in aerated tanks.
- **Non Nuoc Beach:** Extending further south toward the Marble Mountains, this stretch features gated international beachfront resorts (Hyatt Regency, Marriott, Danang Golden Bay) offering private ocean access and golf course complexes away from city density.

### The Downtown Han River District (Hai Chau District)
Positioned on the western bank of the river, downtown Hai Chau represents the authentic civic heart of the city:
- **Bach Dang Promenade:** A scenic riverfront walking avenue shaded by tropical trees, favored by evening strollers, street performers, and riverside patio bars.
- **Local Food Density:** Hai Chau houses Da Nang's iconic culinary institutions along Nguyen Chi Thanh, Hoang Dieu, and Le Duan streets—serving steaming bowls of *Mi Quang* (turmeric rice noodles with pork and shrimp), *Bun cha ca* (fish cake noodle soup), and *Banh xeo ba Duong*.
- **Transit Convenience:** Downtown sits five minutes from Da Nang International Airport (DAD) and ten minutes from the main railway station, making it the strategic choice for short one- or two-night transit stays.

## 2. The Son Tra Peninsula: Nature Reserve, Lady Buddha, and Road Rules

Jutting into the East Sea like a green barrier directly northeast of the city, the **Son Tra Peninsula** (Monkey Mountain) rises to 693 meters above sea level across 4,400 hectares of protected coastal rainforest.

### Linh Ung Pagoda and the 67-Meter Lady Buddha
Positioned on a scenic coastal bluff overlooking the entire bay, **Linh Ung Pagoda** (Chua Linh Ung - Bai But) is the largest Buddhist sanctuary in Da Nang. Its signature landmark is the colossal white statue of Guanyin (the Goddess of Mercy / Lady Buddha), standing **67 meters tall** atop a 35-meter-wide lotus base. 
- Equivalent in height to a 30-story skyscraper, the statue gazes protectively out toward the sea, historically constructed to shelter local fishermen from oceanic typhoons.
- Inside the statue are seventeen interior floors, each housing small altars. 
- Entrance to the pagoda grounds, gardens, and temple courtyard is completely **free of charge**.
- Visitors must dress respectfully, covering shoulders and knees when entering temple shrines.

### Crucial Safety Checkpoints and Motorbike Regulations
Beyond Linh Ung Pagoda, the road ascends steeply toward **Ban Co Peak** (Chess Peak) and the **Thousand-Year Banyan Tree**:
- Following numerous fatal accidents caused by brake overheating on sheer downward gradients, Da Nang municipal authorities and traffic police instituted a **strict prohibition against fully automatic scooters** (such as Honda Vision, Air Blade, Yamaha Janus, or Lead) traversing the upper mountain roads.
- **Physical Manned Checkpoints:** Police and ranger stations inspect vehicles past the Bai Bac intersection. If you arrive on an automatic scooter, you will be turned back.
- **Permitted Vehicles:** Travelers wishing to reach the summit must operate semi-automatic motorbikes with foot gears (e.g., Honda Wave or Future), manual clutch motorcycles, four-wheel-drive cars, or licensed guided tour vans.
- **Wildlife Conservation:** Son Tra is the primary habitat of the critically endangered **red-shanked douc langur** (*Pygathrix nemaeus*), celebrated for its striking five-colored coat. Foraging occurs primarily in the high tree canopy during early morning (06:00–08:30 AM) and late afternoon (16:00–17:30 PM). Never feed wild macaques along the roadside, as habituated monkeys can bite or grab phones and bags.

## 3. Marble Mountains (Ngu Hanh Son): Karsts, Grottoes, and Sanctuaries

Situated nine kilometers south of the city center, the **Marble Mountains** consist of five craggy limestone and marble outcrops named after the five cosmological elements: *Kim* (Metal), *Moc* (Wood), *Thuy* (Water), *Hoa* (Fire), and *Tho* (Earth). 

All tourist access centers on the largest and most complex formation, **Thuy Son (Water Mountain)**:
- **Admission Tariffs:** The standard adult entry ticket to Thuy Son is **40,000 VND** (10,000 VND for students).
- **Glass Elevator (Optional):** Visitors can bypass the initial steep staircase by paying **15,000 VND per person one-way** to ride a cliffside glass elevator to the mid-mountain terrace.
- **Am Phu Cave (Hell Cave):** Located at the base of the mountain near the road, this separate cave features dramatic representations of Buddhist purgatory, complete with winding tunnels depicting karmic punishment (**20,000 VND** entry).

### Highlights Within Thuy Son
- **Huyen Khong Cave:** The crown architectural cavern of the mountain. Shafts of sunlight filter through natural openings in the ceiling, illuminating an open altar and a stone Buddha carved into the cavern wall. During the Vietnam War, this cavern served as a secret underground field hospital and staging base for local resistance fighters.
- **Van Thong Cave:** A narrow, nearly vertical chimney passage that requires climbing on hands and knees through a smooth stone chute to emerge onto the mountain's upper peak for panoramic vistas of the surrounding coastal plain.

> [!TIP]
> Wear sturdy walking shoes with aggressive rubber grip. Centuries of foot traffic combined with coastal humidity have polished the marble stairs until they are as slick as ice, especially after morning rains. Avoid visiting between 11:00 AM and 13:30 PM when tour buses from Hoi An crowd the narrow cavern passages.

## 4. Bridges of the Han River: The Dragon Bridge Fire and Water Show

Da Nang is widely known across Vietnam as the "City of Bridges." The most renowned is the **Dragon Bridge (Cau Rong)**, a 666-meter-long steel arch bridge constructed in the form of a golden dragon undulating over six traffic lanes of the Han River.

### The Weekend Pyrotechnic Spectacle
Every **Saturday and Sunday at 21:00 PM sharp** (as well as major national holidays like Tet and Reunification Day), municipal authorities close the bridge to motorized traffic for an official pyrotechnic display:
- The dragon's head breathes three massive plumes of real fire, followed by three synchronized bursts of high-pressure water spray across fifteen minutes.
- **Where to Stand:** The best vantage point is on the eastern riverbank along **Tran Hung Dao Street** near the foot of the dragon's head, or aboard river cruise boats moored in the Han River.
- **Wind Direction Alert:** Check the wind before picking your spot. If the breeze blows east toward the riverside promenade, spectators standing directly beneath the dragon's snout will be thoroughly drenched by the water blast.

Other notable bridges along the Han include the **Han River Swing Bridge** (Vietnam's only operational swing bridge, rotating 90 degrees on its axis at 23:00 on weekends to allow maritime cargo ships to pass) and the cable-stayed **Tran Thi Ly Bridge**.

## 5. Strategic Day Trips: Hai Van Pass, Ba Na Hills, and the Heritage Circuit

Da Nang's central geographic location makes it an unmatched launchpad for regional excursions:

### 1. The Hai Van Pass (Deo Hai Van)
Forming the physical and meteorological boundary between Da Nang and Thua Thien Hue province, this 21-kilometer mountain pass winds across the Annamite Range to a crest of 496 meters:
- Immortalized by global road-trippers, the pass offers sweeping vistas of Da Nang Bay to the south and the tranquil Lang Co Lagoon to the north.
- Riding the pass by motorbike requires roughly one hour of skilled cornering. Alternatively, the coastal passenger train (SE trains or the dedicated Heritage Train between Da Nang and Hue) hugs sheer sea cliffs far below the highway, providing one of the most scenic rail journeys in Asia for less than 150,000 VND.

### 2. Sun World Ba Na Hills and the Golden Bridge (Cau Vang)
Perched at an elevation of 1,487 meters on Mount Chua twenty-five kilometers west of Da Nang, Ba Na Hills was originally established as a French colonial hill station in 1919:
- Today, it operates as a massive theme park accessed via a world-record-length single-track cable car system (5,801 meters).
- Its primary draw is the **Golden Bridge** (Cau Vang), a 150-meter curved pedestrian bridge seemingly supported by two giant weathered stone hands emerging from the misty cliffside.
- **Admission Tariff:** Standard adult tickets cost **900,000 VND** (750,000 VND for children 1.0m–1.4m), covering the cable car and theme park attractions. 
- **Weather Caution:** Mountain weather is volatile. Check live webcam feeds at your hotel before departing; thick mountain fog frequently envelops the bridge, completely obscuring visibility for hours.

### 3. My Son Sanctuary
Located forty-five kilometers southwest in a lush forested valley, My Son was the spiritual capital of the Champa Kingdom from the fourth to the fourteenth centuries. Its red brick Hindu tower temples, dedicated to Shiva, remain an essential archaeological study of Southeast Asian Hindu-Buddhist culture (**150,000 VND** entry).

### Airport and Rail Logistics
- **Da Nang International Airport (DAD):** Located just three kilometers west of the city center. A metered taxi or Grab ride to the downtown riverfront costs **60,000 to 90,000 VND** (10 minutes), while reaching the My Khe beach strip runs **120,000 to 150,000 VND** (15 to 20 minutes).
- **Da Nang Railway Station:** Situated on Hai Phong Street downtown, serving daily express trains connecting north to Hue (2.5–3 hours) and south to Quy Nhon (5–6 hours) and Ho Chi Minh City.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Visiting Da Nang

### How many days should I spend in Da Nang?
We recommend dedicating **two to three full days** to Da Nang. This timeline allows you to spend one day exploring My Khe Beach, Son Tra Peninsula, and the Marble Mountains, one day taking a scenic excursion across the Hai Van Pass or to Ba Na Hills, and an evening experiencing the Dragon Bridge fire show and coastal seafood dining.

### Can I base myself in Da Nang and visit Hoi An on day trips?
Yes. Hoi An is only thirty kilometers south along the flat coastal highway (Vo Nguyen Giap / Lac Long Quan). Private cars or Grab taxis take roughly forty minutes and cost **250,000 to 350,000 VND** each way. Many travelers prefer Da Nang's superior hotel standards, modern gyms, and expansive beaches, choosing to take late-afternoon trips into Hoi An for lantern walks and dinner.

### Is My Khe Beach safe for swimming?
My Khe is generally safe, featuring designated swimming zones clearly marked with buoy lines and monitored by professional municipal lifeguards daily between 05:00 AM and 18:30 PM. However, between October and December, strong northeast monsoon winds generate powerful rip currents. Always obey lifeguard flags and avoid swimming outside designated perimeter buoys.

### How do I hire a private car for the Hai Van Pass to Hue?
Private car transfers between Da Nang and Hue via the Hai Van Pass can be booked through any reputable travel desk or hotel. Standard rates for a comfortable modern four-seater sedan range from **1,100,000 to 1,400,000 VND** ($45–$55 USD), including photo stops at the summit bunker, Lang Co Beach, and Lap An Lagoon.
