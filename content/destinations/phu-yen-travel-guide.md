Phu Yen province occupies a pristine coastal expanse of south-central Vietnam, positioned between the granite massif of Cu Mong Pass to the north and the sea cliffs of Deo Ca Pass to the south. Celebrated nationally as the "Land of Yellow Flowers on Green Grass" following Victor Vu’s acclaimed 2015 cinematic adaptation, the province presents a striking juxtaposition of wild volcanic shorelines, vast lagoons, and fertile agricultural deltas nourished by the Da Rang river.

While neighboring Nha Trang developed into an international beach metropolis, Phu Yen remains blissfully unhurried. Its provincial capital, Tuy Hoa, is a clean, tree-lined coastal city where broad sand beaches sit free from high-rise concrete sprawl. The true magnetism of Phu Yen lies in its extraordinary geological anomalies—most notably the world-class polygonal basalt columns of Ganh Da Dia and the towering granite cliffs of Mui Dien, where the first morning rays of dawn strike mainland Vietnam.

The provincial capital, Tuy Hoa, sits beside the mouth of the **Da Rang River**, the largest river drainage basin in south-central Vietnam. The city is laid out on a spacious, orderly grid where wide boulevards like Hung Vuong and Doc Lap parallel a magnificent municipal beachfront. 

Coastal winds blow persistently along this section of the South China Sea. Between **February and August**, calm sea conditions and clear skies prevail, making it the prime season for coastal driving, swimming, and sunrise photography. From **September through December**, the northeast monsoon brings squalls and higher tides that crash dramatically against the coastal basalt cliffs, offering majestic seascapes while restricting open-ocean boat tours.

### Volcanic Genesis & Geological Geometry

The formation originated roughly 100 million years ago through volcanic activity in the Central Highlands plateau. Basaltic lava erupted and flowed toward the sea, where it came into contact with cold ocean water. Rapid thermodynamic contraction caused the molten rock to crack perpendicularly into tens of thousands of interlocking polygonal columns, predominantly hexagonal, pentagonal, and circular prisms.

Over 35,000 dark basalt columns stack closely together over a width of 50 meters and stretch 200 meters into the sea, resembling an enormous, stepped honeycomb or giant stack of black ceramic dinner plates. When white Pacific swells break across the basalt shelves, water pools within the hexagonal cavities, creating natural sea mirrors reflecting the morning sky.

Travelers should wear shoes with textured rubber soles, as sea moss makes the lower sea-level basalt slabs slick when wet. The ticket office charges 40,000 VND ($1.60 USD) per adult.

### The Historic French Beacon & Sunrise Summit

French colonial engineers constructed **Hải Đăng Đại Lãnh** in 1890 atop an isolated granite peak 110 meters above sea level. The gray stone tower rises 26 meters. Its rotating Fresnel lens projects a beam 27 nautical miles across international shipping lanes.

Reaching the lighthouse requires crossing a footbridge over a freshwater lagoon, then ascending one kilometer of stone stairs flanked by wild screwpine trees. At the base of the tower, an iron directional marker marks the coordinates of Vietnam’s easternmost continental edge: 12°53'48" N, 109°27'06" E.

Arriving by 05:00 AM allows travelers to witness the golden sun emerging directly above the unbroken ocean horizon. Below the cliffs lies **Bãi Môn**, a secluded horseshoe-shaped beach with fine sand and calm morning waters where a freshwater mountain brook filters directly across the beach into the sea.

> [!NOTE]
> The ascent to Dai Lanh Lighthouse involves over 800 stone steps with no intermediate rest stations or lighting. Travelers visiting for sunrise must carry a headlamp or phone flashlight, bring drinking water, and wear sturdy walking shoes rather than beach flip-flops.

### Nghinh Phong Wind Tower (Tháp Nghinh Phong)

Dominating the central oceanfront square of Tuy Hoa, Nghinh Phong Tower has rapidly achieved international architectural acclaim. Designed by Huni Architectes, the monument draws poetic inspiration from the legendary Vietnamese founding myth of the Hundred Eggs of Mother Au Co and Father Lac Long Quan, as well as the geometric basalt pillars of Ganh Da Dia.

The structure consists of twin towering stone columns: the taller pillar measures 35 meters in height, representing Father Lac Long Quan, while its 30-meter companion represents Mother Au Co. Between the columns lies a narrow pedestrian wind corridor (*khe gió*), through which coastal sea breezes funnel to create natural acoustic resonant tones. 

At night, high-tech architectural illumination projects dynamic light shows across the faceted stone columns. The expansive stone plaza serves as the city's favorite evening gathering point for families, skaters, and walkers.

### Nhan Cham Tower (Tháp Nhạn)

Erected atop Nhan Mountain on the northern bank of the Da Rang River, this 23.5-meter quadrangular brick sanctuary was built by Cham architects between the late 11th and early 12th centuries. The tower displays the characteristic stepped pyramidal roof tiers and corner pilasters of late Vijaya art.

From the temple esplanade, visitors look down upon the Da Rang river flowing into the sea, crossed by the historic two-kilometer railway and highway bridges. The temple grounds are open daily, with illuminated evening lighting turning the red brick spire into a beacon visible across the Tuy Hoa plain.

### Mang Lang Church: The Cradle of Quoc Ngu

Located 32 kilometers north of Tuy Hoa, Mang Lang Church was erected in 1892 by French missionary Father Joseph Lacassagne in a refined Gothic revival style. The weathered gray stone exterior features twin bell towers and pointed stained-glass arched portals.

The church crypt preserves a treasure of Vietnamese cultural history: the original 1651 catechism *Cathechismus in octo dies* printed in Rome by Jesuit priest Alexandre de Rhodes. This volume represents the earliest printed book in the Latin-script Vietnamese alphabet (*Chữ Quốc Ngữ*), the foundation of modern written Vietnamese.

### O Loan Lagoon (Đầm Ô Loan)

Spanning 1,200 hectares between Tuy An hills and the ocean, O Loan Lagoon is a brackish water ecosystem celebrated for its rich marine biodiversity. The lagoon receives freshwater from surrounding hills while tidal ocean waters filter through the An Hai inlet, creating an ideal habitat for shellfish aquaculture.

Visitors head to waterfront wooden restaurants in An Hai commune, where dining platforms hover over the water. The lagoon's signature culinary star is **sò huyết Ô Loan** (blood cockles), distinguished by their plump crimson meat and sweet, savory brine. Grilled over open charcoal embers with scallion oil and crushed peanuts, cockles cost between 150,000 and 250,000 VND ($6.00–$10.00 USD) per kilogram.

### Bai Xep Beach & Ganh Ong Cliffs

Fourteen kilometers north of Tuy Hoa in An Chan commune lies **Bãi Xép**, a 500-meter sandy cove anchored by the dark headlands of **Gành Ông**. 

Ascending the path to Ganh Ong leads travelers across an open grassy coastal plateau dotted with hundreds of wild prickly pear cacti (*xương rồng*) growing at the edge of sheer red soil cliffs. This panoramic spot served as the backdrop for the iconic childhood scene in the film *Yellow Flowers on Green Grass*. Entry costs 20,000 VND ($0.80 USD).

### Mắt Cá Ngừ Đại Dương: Ocean Tuna Eyes in Herb Broth

Tuy Hoa is the primary industrial port for Vietnam's deep-sea tuna longline fleets. One of its most distinctive culinary creations is *mắt cá ngừ đại dương*.

Chefs place a fist-sized tuna eye inside an individual ceramic clay pot, submerging it in a rich broth infused with traditional medicinal Chinese herbs including goji berries, jujubes, ginger, lemongrass, and spicy green peppercorns. Simmered over glowing charcoal, the gelatinous fats and rich ocular marrow yield an intensely rich, oceanic flavor without fishiness. 

Diners shred crisp perilla leaves and fresh coriander into the bubbling broth before spooning out the tender meat. **Quán Bà Tám** (289 Le Duan street) is the local institution where residents and travelers gather every evening for this delicacy (45,000 to 60,000 VND per pot).

### Cơm Gà Phú Yên: Fragrant Golden Chicken Rice

For lunch, head to **Cơm Gà Tuyết Nhung** (189 Le Thanh Ton). Phu Yen's chicken rice stands apart from Hoi An styles: the long-grain rice is toasted in chicken fat and fragrant garlic before being cooked in rich chicken stock with a dash of fresh turmeric, producing a glistening golden grain. The dish is topped with tender shredded free-range chicken (*gà ta*), pickled red onions, and fresh Vietnamese mint, paired with a specialized sweet-and-sour dipping sauce.

### Air Travel via Dong Tac Terminal

Located just six kilometers south of downtown Tuy Hoa on the coastal flats of Dong Tac, **Tuy Hoa Airport** offers seamless arrivals. Daily flights connect the city directly with Hanoi (Noi Bai HAN) and Ho Chi Minh City (Tan Son Nhat SGN) via Vietnam Airlines and Vietjet. 

Because the airport sits close to the city, metered taxis (Mai Linh, Sun Taxi, or Xanh SM electric vehicles) cost merely 100,000 to 140,000 VND ($4.00–$5.50 USD) for the 15-minute drive into downtown hotels.

### Railway Connections

The North-South railway passes through the center of Tuy Hoa, with **Ga Tuy Hòa** located on Le Trung Kien street. Express trains like SE2, SE4, SE6, SE22, and SE21 provide direct rail links north to Quy Nhon, Da Nang, and Hue, or south to Nha Trang and Saigon.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the 2026 admission fees for Phu Yen's major attractions?
Ganh Da Dia charges 40,000 VND per adult. Mui Dien Cape and Dai Lanh Lighthouse charge 20,000 VND. Bai Xep at Ganh Ong charges 20,000 VND. Nghinh Phong Tower and Nhan Cham Tower are free public attractions.

### How do I get to Mui Dien for the sunrise?
Depart Tuy Hoa by 04:30 AM via coastal road DT657 by motorcycle or private car (35 km, 45 minutes drive). Park at the ranger gate, pay the 20,000 VND entry ticket, and hike the one-kilometer stone staircase for 20 minutes to reach the lighthouse platform before dawn at 05:15 AM.

### When is the ideal season to visit Phu Yen?
The premier window is **February through August**, characterized by calm turquoise seas, sunny skies, and optimal conditions for coastal drives and swimming. Heavy rains occur during the autumn monsoon from October through December.

### Can I visit Ganh Da Dia and Mui Dien on the same day?
Yes, but they lie in opposite directions from Tuy Hoa. Ganh Da Dia sits 35 km north, while Mui Dien sits 35 km south. A classic itinerary dedicates morning hours to Mui Dien and Bai Mon in the south, returns to Tuy Hoa for lunch, and spends the afternoon exploring Ganh Da Dia and O Loan Lagoon in the north.

### What is the most famous local specialty dish to try in Tuy Hoa?
The signature dish is *mắt cá ngừ đại dương* (braised ocean tuna eyes in herbal broth), followed by *sò huyết Đầm Ô Loan* (blood cockles from O Loan Lagoon) and *cơm gà Phú Yên* (fragrant turmeric chicken rice).
