# Cham Islands Travel Guide: Hoi An Day Trip or Overnight?

Rising out of the South China Sea `15 kilometers` (`8 nautical miles`) east of Cua Dai Beach (`Biển Cửa Đại`), the Cham Islands (`Cù Lao Chàm`) form an eight-island granitic archipelago designated as a UNESCO World Biosphere Reserve in 2009. Only the largest island—Hon Lao (`Hòn Lao`, `13.8 km²`, rising `517 meters` above the water)—is inhabited, home to roughly `2,900` residents across fishing hamlets at Bai Lang (`Bãi Làng`) and Bai Huong (`Bãi Hương`).

Because high-speed canoes cross from Hoi An's Cua Dai Pier (`Bến Cửa Đại`) to Bai Lang Pier in just `15 to 20 minutes`, over `90%` of visitors treat the archipelago as a `08:00–14:00` package day trip. Yet the moment the last afternoon speedboats depart back to the mainland at `14:30`, Hon Lao transforms from a busy beach-lunch hub into one of central Vietnam's quietest island communities. This destination guide compares a Hoi An day trip against an overnight homestay, detailing 2026 boat tariffs, mandatory conservation fees, reef snorkeling sites, and seasonal rough-sea port closures.

> [!WARNING]
> **Strict Single-Use Plastic Ban & Autumn Port Closures:** Under Tan Hiep Commune conservation bylaws, bringing disposable plastic grocery bags (`túi nilon`) and single-use plastic water bottles onto the Cham Islands is prohibited and subject to spot checks at Cua Dai Pier. Pack a reusable metal water bottle and cloth tote. Furthermore, between **October and February**, Northeast Monsoon swells (`gió mùa Đông Bắc`) frequently prompt Border Guard authorities (`Bộ đội Biên phòng Cửa Đại`) to suspend all boat departures for days at a time.

## 2. Day Trip vs. Overnight Stay: Which Should You Choose?

### When a Day Trip From Hoi An Is Enough
If your [Hoi An](/destinations/hoi-an-travel-guide/) itinerary is limited to `3 nights` and your primary goal is swimming over shallow coral reefs between **April and August**, a half-day speedboat trip or two-tank diving boat covers the highlights without requiring you to pack an overnight bag. Standard day tours pick you up from your Hoi An hotel around `08:00`, clear Cua Dai Border Post by `08:45`, land at Bai Lang (`Bãi Làng`) at `09:05` for a quick walk past the Marine Protected Area museum, the 200-year-old Champa Ancient Well (`Giếng Cổ Chăm`), and Hai Tang Pagoda (`Chùa Hải Tạng`, built in `1758`), then motor out to **Hon Dai** (`Hòn Dài`) or **Bai Xep** (`Bãi Xếp`) for two `45-minute` snorkeling sessions before a grilled seafood lunch under the coconut palms at **Bai Ong** (`Bãi Ông`) or **Bai Chong** (`Bãi Chồng`).

### Why an Overnight Homestay Changes the Entire Island Experience
Between `09:00` and `13:30`, Bai Lang Pier and Bai Ong Beach can host `1,500 to 3,000` day-trippers simultaneously. Once the last `14:00` speedboats roar back toward Cua Dai estuary, the wooden beach chairs at Bai Ong empty out, children play football along the harbor wall at Bai Lang, and squid boats prepare their halogen lamps for dusk.

Staying one night in a family homestay at **Bai Lang**, **Bai Ong**, or the southern fishing village of **Bai Huong** (`350,000–650,000 VND` per night for a clean air-conditioned room with private bathroom) unlocks three experiences day-trippers never see:
1. **Empty Afternoon Beaches at Bai Xep and Bai Bim:** Rent a local scooter (`150,000–200,000 VND` for `24 hours`) or hire a motorbike taxi (`xe ôm`, `100,000 VND`) at `15:00` to ride the cliffside coastal road south from Bai Lang to **Bai Xep** (`Bãi Xếp`) and **Bai Bim** (`Bãi Bìm`), where you will often have clear turquoise coves and granite boulders completely to yourself until sunset.
2. **Sunset Coastal Drive & Night Squid Fishing:** Watch the sun drop behind the Hoi An mainland skyline from the high coastal bend between Bai Chong and Bai Huong, followed by an optional evening wooden-boat squid excursion (`300,000–400,000 VND` per person) or a harbor dinner of grilled sea urchin (`nhum biển nướng mỡ hành`, `30,000–40,000 VND` each), steamed stone crab (`cua đá hấp bia`—only eat crabs carrying an official eco-tag confirming shell width $\ge 7\text{ cm}$), and forest-leaf herbal tea (`nước lá rừng Cù Lao Chàm`).
3. **Dawn Harbor Market at Bai Lang (`06:00–07:30`):** Two hours before the first tourist speedboats arrive from Hoi An, basket boats (`thuyền thúng`) unload reef fish, squid, and sea snails directly onto Bai Lang quay while villagers eat bowls of *bánh canh cá* (`30,000 VND`).

## 3. Hon Lao Beach & Village Breakdown

- **Bai Lang (`Bãi Làng`):** The administrative harbor and main pier on the northwest coast. Home to the Marine Protected Area exhibition room, Champa Ancient Well, Hai Tang Pagoda, Tan Hiep Market, and the highest concentration of homestays and seafood restaurants—best for evening convenience, though the harbor water itself is for boats rather than swimming.
- **Bai Ong (`Bãi Ông`):** Located `1.2 km` north of Bai Lang Pier (`15 minutes` on foot along the coastal path or `5 minutes` by boat). Features a `500-meter` crescent of fine white sand, tall coconut palms, freshwater shower rooms, and large thatched day-tour restaurants. Busy from `11:30 to 13:45`, then completely tranquil after `14:30`.
- **Bai Xep (`Bãi Xếp`):** A small, undeveloped cove `2 km` south of Bai Lang with no restaurants or tour buffets, backed by coastal boulders and a healthy nearshore coral shelf just `20 meters` from the sand.
- **Bai Chong (`Bãi Chồng`):** Positioned `3 km` south of Bai Lang, known for rounded granitic boulders stacked like giant pebbles, a freshwater mountain stream, and shaded hammocks.
- **Bai Huong (`Bãi Hương`):** A quiet fishing hamlet at the southern end of the coastal road (`5 km` from Bai Lang), ideal for travelers seeking authentic village homestays and wooden sampan coral tours off Hon Tai (`Hòn Tai`).
- **Bai Bac (`Bãi Bắc`):** Located on the northern tip of Hon Lao (`4 km` north of Bai Lang via the forested ridge road), this secluded cove serves as a monitored sea-turtle conservation zone and hosts high-end eco-charter anchorage during calm summer months.

### Snorkeling vs. Certified SCUBA Diving Sites Around the Archipelago
Because the Cham Islands Marine Protected Area (`Khu Bảo tồn Biển Cù Lao Chàm`) encompasses `135` species of hard and soft corals across eight granitic islands, water clarity and reef health vary significantly between standard mass-tour stops and outer island dive walls:
- **Bai Xep (`Bãi Xếp`) & Hon Nhan (`Hòn Nhàn`):** The primary shallow-water snorkeling reefs (`2 to 6 meters` depth) used by morning high-speed canoe tours. Expect large table corals (*Acropora*), blue sea stars, and damselfish schools—best visited before `10:00` or via a private wooden sampan (`350,000–500,000 VND` chartered from Bai Lang or Bai Huong) after `14:30`.
- **Hon Dai (`Hòn Dài`) & Hon Tai (`Hòn Tai`):** Sheltered on their leeward eastern walls (`6 to 18 meters` depth), these outer islands are the main mooring zones for custom wooden boats operated by PADI and SSI dive centers out of Hoi An (`2,200,000–2,650,000 VND` for a 2-tank certified dive trip, or `2,400,000 VND` for a Discover Scuba introductory dive). During peak summer clarity (**May through August**), underwater visibility reaches `12 to 20 meters` over gorgonian sea fans, nudibranchs, moray eels, and schooling barracuda.

### Cultural Heritage Landmarks on Hon Lao Island
Beyond its coral reefs, Hon Lao served for centuries as a freshwater resupply anchorage on the Maritime Silk Road approaching the Thu Bon estuary (`Cửa Đại`). All four heritage stops in Bai Lang and Bai Huong are covered by your `70,000 VND` biosphere admission ticket:
- **Hai Tang Pagoda (`Chùa Hải Tạng`, `1758`):** Built in the 19th year of King Le Hien Tong (`Cảnh Hưng`) at the foot of the western mountain slopes `400 meters` behind Bai Lang Pier, this stone-walled pagoda combines Buddhist, Taoist, and Confucian altars where merchant captains once prayed for calm crossings.
- **Champa Ancient Well (`Giếng Cổ Xóm Cấm`):** Constructed from dry-stacked brick rings over a square stone frame `200 meters` from Bai Lang harbor, this freshwater spring has never run dry or turned brackish despite sitting less than `100 meters` from the tide line.
- **Whale Temple (`Lăng Ông Ngư`) & Bird's Nest Founders Shrine (`Miếu Tổ Nghề Yến`):** Located along the harborfront and at Bai Huong (`celebrated annually on the 9th and 10th days of the 3rd lunar month`), these shrines honor revered whale skeletons (`Cá Ông`) and the island guild that harvests prized edible swiftlet nests (`Yến sào Cù Lao Chàm`) from sheer eastern sea caves.

> [!TIP]
> **How Independent Overnight Canoe Tickets Work:** When booking a round-trip high-speed canoe ticket at Cua Dai Pier (`350,000–450,000 VND`), tell the dispatcher you are returning the **following day** (`về ngày hôm sau`). Operators charge a standard `50,000–100,000 VND` overnight split-manifest fee because they must hold an empty seat for you on the next day's `13:30–14:00` return boat. Keep your dispatcher's Zalo phone number and confirm your pickup pier (`Bãi Làng` or `Bãi Ông`) by `10:00` on departure day.

## 4. Step-by-Step Cham Islands Execution Plan (HowTo)

1. **Confirm the Marine Forecast 24 Hours Ahead:** Between **March and August**, crossings are calm (`95%` operating reliability). In **September** or **March shoulder weeks**, ask your Hoi An hotel or dive center (`Cham Island Diving` on Nguyen Thai Hoc Street) to confirm the Cua Dai Port Authority wind bulletin before paying a deposit.
2. **Bring Original Passports to Cua Dai Pier (`08:00`):** Foreign travelers must register their full name, nationality, passport number, and Vietnam visa expiration date with the Cua Dai Border Guard post prior to boarding either the high-speed canoe (`08:30`) or wooden supply boat (`07:30`).
3. **Pack Cash (`VND`) and Reef-Safe Gear:** There are **no international ATMs** on Hon Lao Island, and most village homestays, scooter rentals, and seafood stalls operate strictly in cash (`VND`) or domestic VietQR bank transfer. Bring `1,500,000–2,000,000 VND` in cash per person for an overnight stay, plus a long-sleeved UV rashguard and reusable water bottle.
4. **Leave Mainland Motorbikes at Cua Dai Parking:** High-speed canoes cannot load motorbikes, and although the `07:30` wooden supply boat can carry bikes (`50,000 VND` freight fee), offloading over wooden planks at Bai Lang is awkward. Park your mainland scooter inside the guarded Cua Dai terminal lot (`10,000–20,000 VND`) and rent an island scooter at Bai Lang (`150,000 VND`) if staying overnight.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I visit the Cham Islands in October, November, December, or January?
We strongly advise against planning a Cham Islands trip between **October and January**. Northeast monsoon winds create `2.0 to 3.5-meter` swells across the Cua Dai channel, underwater visibility drops below `2 meters` from river silt, and port authorities routinely ban all speedboat departures. During winter, spend your Hoi An days exploring [My Son Sanctuary](/destinations/my-son-sanctuary-guide/), cycling Tra Que Village, or taking the [Cham Islands Day Trip Guide](/plan/cham-islands-day-trip-guide/) alternatives on the mainland.

### Is the wooden supply boat (`Tàu gỗ`) better than the high-speed canoe?
The `07:30` wooden supply boat (`150,000–200,000 VND` for foreign passengers, `75 to 90 minutes` crossing) is an atmospheric way to travel alongside local market vendors and cargo sacks, but its slow hull rolls heavily in any cross-swell and its single return departure from Bai Lang leaves early at `11:30`—making it impractical for a day trip. Only take the wooden boat if you are staying overnight on a calm summer morning (**May to August**) and want the slow maritime photography experience; otherwise, take the `18-minute` high-speed canoe.

### Are there ATMs, pharmacies, or reliable Wi-Fi on the Cham Islands?
Hon Lao Island has **no bank branches or ATMs**, though family homestays and seafood restaurants in Bai Lang and Bai Huong provide stable 4G mobile coverage (`Viettel` and `Vinaphone`) and fiber Wi-Fi. A small commune health clinic operates near Bai Lang Pier for basic first aid, while seasickness tablets (`thuốc chống say sóng`), sunscreen, and cash (`1,500,000–2,000,000 VND` per person) should always be purchased in Hoi An before heading to Cua Dai Pier.
