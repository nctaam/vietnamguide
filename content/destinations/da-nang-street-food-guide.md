# Da Nang Street Food Guide: Seafood, Mi Quang & Gems (2026)

Positioned on the central Vietnamese coast where the Hàn River empties into the East Sea, **Da Nang** operates as the culinary hub of the Quảng Nam–Đà Nẵng cultural basin. While northern Hanoi focuses on clear bone broths and southern Ho Chi Minh City favors sweet coconut reductions, Da Nang street cooking is built on coastal seafood salinity, concentrated turmeric and peanut oils, fiery green bird's eye chilies (*ớt xanh*), and fermented anchovy sauce (*mắm nêm*).

Unlike isolated resort enclaves, Da Nang is a working port city of 1.2 million residents where Michelin Bib Gourmand and Michelin Selected kitchens operate inside residential alleyways (*kiệt*) of Hải Châu District. Across the Dragon Bridge (*Cầu Rồng*) on the eastern peninsula, open-air seafood halls along Võ Nguyên Giáp Boulevard display live catch harvested overnight by Sơn Trà fishing boats.

For travelers navigating Da Nang in 2026, neighborhood noodle and rice-paper stalls charge 30,000 VND to 95,000 VND ($1.20 to $3.75 USD) per dish, while 4-seat Grab or Xanh SM electric taxi transfers between the beach strip and downtown Hải Châu markets cost 45,000 VND to 75,000 VND ($1.80 to $2.95 USD) across distances of 3 km to 5.5 km.

## The Provincial Icon: Mì Quảng (Quang-Style Turmeric Noodles)

No dish defines Da Nang and neighboring Quảng Nam province more clearly than **Mì Quảng**. Unlike *phở* or *bún bò Huế*, which submerge noodles in 400 ml of boiling soup, Mì Quảng is constructed as a semi-dry noodle bowl irrigated by only 80 ml to 100 ml of concentrated bone and shrimp stock (*nước nhân*).

Cooks steam wide sheets of stone-ground rice batter coated with peanut oil infused with fried shallots, slicing them into 8 mm flat ribbons. Toppings include braised pork belly (*thịt heo*), whole red river prawns (*tôm*), shredded free-range chicken (*gà ta*), or turmeric-braised frog legs (*ếch*), accompanied by a hard-boiled quail egg, roasted peanuts, and a crisp grilled black-sesame rice cracker (*bánh tráng mè*) that diners shatter directly into the bowl.

Every serving arrives with a basket of nine raw greens (*rau sống*): young mustard sprouts (*cải con*), shredded banana blossom (*bắp chuối*), water mint, basil, lettuce, and green bird's eye chilies.

### Verified Mì Quảng Establishments
- **Mì Quảng 1A (1A Hải Phòng Street, Hải Châu):** Located 2.2 km northwest of Dragon Bridge (6 minutes by Grab for 35,000 VND / $1.40 USD), this busy parlor operates from 06:00 to 21:00. Standard shrimp-and-pork or chicken bowls cost 35,000 to 40,000 VND ($1.40 to $1.55 USD), while large mixed bowls (*tô đặc biệt*) with ribs, prawns, and pork patty cost 55,000 VND ($2.15 USD). Extra sesame rice crackers cost 5,000 VND ($0.20 USD).
- **Mì Quảng Bà Mua (Flagship at 95A Nguyễn Tri Phương; central branch at 19–21 Trần Bình Trọng, 1.5 km from Dragon Bridge):** Operating from 06:00 to 21:30, Bà Mua offers 9 variations including snakehead fish (*mì cá lóc*), eel (*mì lươn*), and frog (*mì ếch*) priced from 40,000 to 65,000 VND ($1.55 to $2.55 USD).

## Bánh Tráng Cuốn Thịt Heo (Two-End Fat Boiled Pork Belly Rolls)

A signature lunchtime meal in Da Nang is **Bánh Tráng Cuốn Thịt Heo**—thinly shaved boiled pork belly featuring translucent layers of fat on both ends (*thịt heo hai đầu da*), wrapped tableside with fresh herbs and dipped into pungent fermented anchovy sauce (*mắm nêm*).

Waitstaff bring four elements to the table: a platter of steamed pork slices kept moist over banana leaves, a stack of soft wet rice noodle sheets (*bánh ướt*) paired with sun-dried rice paper (*bánh tráng Đại Lộc*), a tray of 12 raw vegetables and herbs (sliced green mango, green banana, cucumber, perilla, fish mint, and bean sprouts), and individual bowls of *mắm nêm* spiked with crushed pineapple, garlic, and red chili.

### Top Pork Belly Roll Venues
- **Quán Mậu (35 Đỗ Thúc Tịnh Street, Khuê Trung, Cẩm Lệ):** Situated 6.0 km southwest of Dragon Bridge (15 minutes by Grab car costing 75,000 to 90,000 VND / $2.95 to $3.55 USD) and 3.5 km south of Da Nang International Airport, Quán Mậu holds a Michelin Bib Gourmand distinction. Portions cost 90,000 to 145,000 VND ($3.55 to $5.70 USD) per person and easily feed a full appetite. Open 08:00 to 21:30.
- **Đặc Sản Đại Lộc (97 Trưng Nữ Vương Street, Hải Châu):** Located just 1.1 km southwest of Dragon Bridge (4 minutes by Grab or a 14-minute walk from the Cham Museum), this central restaurant charges 75,000 to 110,000 VND ($2.95 to $4.30 USD) per portion. Open 09:00 to 21:00.

## Bún Chả Cá (Fishcake Noodle Soup) & Bánh Xèo Bà Dưỡng

### Bún Chả Cá (Morning & All-Day Fishcake Soup)
Da Nang's quintessential breakfast soup uses zero meat bones; instead, cooks simmer marine fish spines with chunks of sweet orange pumpkin (*bí đỏ*), white cabbage, ripe tomato, bamboo shoots, and fresh pineapple for 6 hours. The bowl is topped with hand-pounded Spanish mackerel (*cá thu*) and barracuda (*cá nhồng*) fishcakes in two textures: steamed (*chả hấp*) and golden-fried (*chả chiên*).
- **Bún Chả Cá 109 (109 Nguyễn Chí Thanh Street, Hải Châu):** Located 1.8 km north of Dragon Bridge, operating since 1968 from 06:00 to 22:00. Standard bowls cost 35,000 to 45,000 VND ($1.40 to $1.80 USD); special bowls with whole mackerel slices (*bún cá thu*) cost 55,000 VND ($2.15 USD).
- **Bún Chả Cá Bà Lữ (319 Hùng Vương Street, Thanh Khê):** Located 2.1 km northwest of Dragon Bridge (300 meters west of Chợ Cồn), serving bowls from 06:00 to 22:30 for 30,000 to 50,000 VND ($1.20 to $2.00 USD).

### Bánh Xèo & Nem Lụi at Bà Dưỡng (K280/23 Hoàng Diệu)
Located at the end of a 120-meter residential alley off Hoàng Diệu Street (1.3 km southwest of Dragon Bridge), **Bánh Xèo Bà Dưỡng** fries thousands of 15 cm turmeric-rice crepes daily over blazing charcoal rows. Each crepe is packed with fresh river shrimp, pork belly slices, and bean sprouts, then rolled in dry rice paper with **nem lụi** (charcoal-grilled minced pork skewers) and dipped in warm pork-liver and peanut gravy (*nước tương*).
- **Official 2026 Tariffs:** A plate of 4 crispy *bánh xèo* crepes costs 80,000 VND ($3.15 USD); individual *nem lụi* skewers cost 8,000 VND ($0.30 USD) each (or 40,000 VND / $1.55 USD for 5 skewers); corn milk (*sữa bắp*) costs 15,000 VND ($0.60 USD). Open 09:30 to 21:30 daily.

> [!TIP]
> **Alleyway Warning at K280 Hoàng Diệu:** Two smaller imitation stalls sit at the entrance of Alley K280. Walk all the way to the very back of the cul-de-sac (120 meters deep to house number **K280/23**) to reach the genuine, Michelin-recognized **Bánh Xèo Bà Dưỡng** courtyard.

## Chợ Cồn (Con Market) vs Night Markets (Sơn Trà & Helio)

### Chợ Cồn Street Food Arcade (290 Hùng Vương Street)
Situated 1.8 km northwest of Dragon Bridge at the intersection of Hùng Vương and Ông Ích Khiêm streets, **Chợ Cồn** is Da Nang's highest-density local food market. It splits into two distinct eating zones:
1. **Indoor Pavilion (Court 15A, 07:00 – 18:00):** Features two famous **Kem Bơ** (Avocado Ice Cream) stalls—**Kem Bơ Cô Vân** and **Kem Bơ Cô Cúc**—serving whipped butter-avocado puree topped with coconut ice cream and toasted dried coconut flakes for 20,000 to 25,000 VND ($0.80 to $1.00 USD) per glass. Adjacent counters serve *bún mắm nêm* and *bánh bột lọc* for 25,000 to 35,000 VND ($1.00 to $1.40 USD).
2. **Outdoor Afternoon Street Food Alley (15:00 – 18:30):** Under a corrugated metal roof along the market's side lane, 40 vendors sell **Ốc Hút** (spicy lemongrass river snails sucked from their shells, 30,000 to 50,000 VND / $1.20 to $2.00 USD per plate), *ram cuốn cải* (crispy spring rolls wrapped in mustard greens, 25,000 VND / $1.00 USD), and *chè chuối nướng* (grilled banana in coconut cream, 15,000 VND / $0.60 USD).

### Evening Night Markets (17:30 – 23:30)
- **Sơn Trà Night Market (Mai Hắc Đế & Lý Nam Đế, Sơn Trà):** Located 400 meters from the eastern head of Dragon Bridge, this pedestrian market runs from 17:30 to 24:00 daily. It is ideal on Friday, Saturday, and Sunday nights before the 21:00 Dragon Bridge fire-and-water show, serving grilled octopus skewers (30,000 to 50,000 VND / $1.20 to $2.00 USD), half-grilled lobster (150,000 to 220,000 VND / $5.90 to $8.65 USD), and sugarcane juice (15,000 VND / $0.60 USD).
- **Helio Night Market (2 Tháng 9 Street, Hòa Cường Bắc, Hải Châu):** Located 2.5 km south of Dragon Bridge near Asia Park, operating 17:30 to 22:30 with live acoustic bands, craft beer booths, and over 100 food stalls charging 25,000 to 85,000 VND ($1.00 to $3.35 USD).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How can I avoid tourist overcharging at Da Nang beachfront seafood restaurants?
Always verify that the restaurant posts daily tank prices per kilogram (*giá theo kg*) on a whiteboard or digital menu before selecting fish. Stand beside the tank scale when the attendant weighs your mesh basket, ensure excess water is drained before weighing, and keep the printed weight slip to compare against your final bill. Established halls like **Hải Sản Bé Mặn** (Lô 8 Võ Nguyên Giáp) issue itemized computer receipts.

### What should travelers with peanut or shellfish allergies know in Da Nang?
Crushed peanuts (*đậu phộng*), peanut oil (*dầu phụng*), and fermented shrimp/anchovy pastes (*mắm ruốc*, *mắm nêm*) are foundational ingredients in Mì Quảng, Bánh Xèo dipping sauce, and pork rolls. Travelers with severe nut or shellfish allergies must show a written Vietnamese alert card before ordering.

### Is Chợ Cồn or Sơn Trà Night Market better for first-time visitors?
**Chợ Cồn** (especially between 15:00 and 18:00) is far superior for authentic central Vietnamese dishes eaten by Da Nang residents at 20,000 to 45,000 VND ($0.80 to $1.80 USD). **Sơn Trà Night Market** (17:30 to 24:00) is geared toward evening visitors walking to the Dragon Bridge, offering grilled seafood skewers and fruit smoothies in an open pedestrian street.
