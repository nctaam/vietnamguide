# Ba Be Lake Travel Guide (2026): Boat Tours & Homestays

Cradled within the jagged limestone karst towers of Bac Kan province roughly 220 kilometers north of Hanoi (4.5 hours to 5.0 hours by road), **Ba Be Lake** (*Hồ Ba Bể*) is Vietnam's largest natural freshwater mountain lake. Designated as Vietnam's third internationally significant wetland under the UNESCO Ramsar Convention in 2011, this tectonic basin spans 500 hectares (8.0 kilometers in length and 400 meters to 800 meters in width) at an elevation of 145 meters to 150 meters above sea level, encircled by primary evergreen rainforests and limestone peaks rising to 1,554 meters.

Unlike the dense rowboat corridors of Ninh Binh or the steel cruise ship lanes of Ha Long Bay, **Ba Be National Park** (10,048 hectares total protected area) maintains a quiet, pastoral rhythm. Motorized wooden longboats navigate 300 meters subterranean river tunnels, Tay ethnic minority families host travelers in ironwood stilt houses overlooking emerald rice paddies (200,000 VND to 550,000 VND or $8.00 to $22.00 USD per night), and forest trails lead 6.0 km to 15.0 km toward waterfalls and bat-filled karst caverns.

Travelers pairing Ba Be with a 130 km Northeast overland route toward Cao Bang and Ban Gioc Waterfall or planning a 2-night escape from Hanoi will find exact 2026 park entry tariffs, boat charter schedules, village homestay rates, trekking profiles, and transit vectors in this empirical guide.

## Park Geography and Hydrology: The Three-Lake System

The name "Ba Be" translates from the indigenous Tay phrase *Slam Pé* as "Three Lakes." Rather than forming a single circular reservoir, the 8.0 kilometers water corridor is divided into three interconnected limestone basins separated by narrow rocky headlands (constricting to 90 meters at the narrowest channel): **Pe Leng** in the north, **Pe Lu** in the center, and **Pe Lam** in the south.

Two southern mountain tributaries (the Cho Leng and Nam Cuong rivers) feed the lake year-round before water drains northward into the **Nang River** during dry months, or reverses flow during heavy summer monsoons (July to August, when monthly rainfall reaches 280 mm) as nutrient-rich Nang River water floods into the lake basin. Because the lake rests on an ancient Devonian limestone plateau sealed by clay strata at 145 meters altitude, it never dries up during winter.

Water depths average 17 meters to 23 meters across the central channel and plunge to 35 meters at the base of the western cliffs. The park protects 1,268 species of vascular plants (including century-old *Chukrasia tabularis* ironwood trees and 182 orchid species), 81 mammal species including the endangered Francois' langur (*Trachypithecus francoisi*), and 106 freshwater fish species. Between October and April, morning mist blankets the water until 08:30 as temperatures rise from 16°C at dawn to 25°C by midday at 78% humidity.

## Navigating the Waterway: Boat Excursion Circuits

Water excursions depart from **Buoc Lom Wharf** (14 km north of Pac Ngoi along the Nang River, 30 minutes by car) or from the southern lakeside docks at **Bo Lu Wharf** (1.5 km from Pac Ngoi) and **Pac Ngoi Wharf** (700 meters walk from village homestays). Welded steel and timber longboats carry a statutory limit of 10 to 12 passengers with canopy roofs and life jackets.

### 1. The Short Basin Circuit (2.0 to 2.5 Hours | 350,000 VND to 450,000 VND / $14.00 to $18.00 USD)
Designed for late-afternoon arrivals (15:30 to 18:00), this 8.0 kilometers loop covers the calm southern and central basins:
- **An Ma Temple (*Đền An Mạ*):** Rising 30 meters above the Pe Leng basin on Tortoise Islet (*Đảo Rùa*), this 16th-century temple honors Mac Dynasty generals and royalists. Passengers disembark for 30 minutes to climb 120 shaded stone steps through ancient banyan roots to the timber shrine.
- **Widow Island (*Đảo Bà Góa*):** Situated near the center of Pe Lam basin (350 meters offshore from Bo Lu), this 25 meters tall karst rock stack is the centerpiece of the Tay legend of the charitable widow whose dragon-blessed rice husks saved her from the great flood that formed the three lakes. Boats pause for 15 minutes at sunset for swimming in 12 meters deep water.

### 2. The Standard Karst Circuit (4.0 to 4.5 Hours | 750,000 VND to 850,000 VND / $30.00 to $34.00 USD)
The classic half-day route (18 kilometers round-trip) links the three lake basins with the Nang River karst system:
- **Puong Cave (*Động Puông*):** Located 4.5 km upriver from the northern lake mouth, the Nang River bores straight through the base of the 450 meters tall Lung Nham limestone mountain. The resulting 300 meters river tunnel rises 30 meters to 35 meters high and spans 25 meters to 40 meters wide. Boatmen throttle down for 20 minutes so passengers can walk the elevated riverside rock ledges and observe 18 species of bats (numbering over 10,000 individuals) roosting inside ceiling domes.
- **Fairy Pond (*Ao Tiên*):** Docking at the northern edge of Pe Lam basin, visitors walk 100 meters (10 minutes) over a 15 meters high limestone saddle to reach a circular 3,000 square meters karst sinkhole filled with cool, spring-fed emerald water filtered through subterranean rock fissures.

### 3. The Grand Trans-Park Circuit (6.5 to 7.5 Hours | 850,000 VND to 1,100,000 VND / $34.00 to $44.00 USD)
This 28 kilometers full-day expedition covers Puong Cave, all three lake basins, Fairy Pond, An Ma Temple, and the western Nang River rapids down to **Dau Dang Waterfall** (*Thác Đầu Đẳng*) on the Tuyen Quang provincial border. Because rocks block boats at the head of the rapids, travelers moor at Dau Dang Wharf and walk 1.0 kilometer (30 minutes round-trip) along a shaded stone trail to watch the Nang River drop 25 meters across a 1,000 meters corridor of stacked limestone boulders. Riverside bamboo stilt eateries at the trailhead serve freshly caught grilled stream fish (*cá nướng*), bamboo-tube rice (*cơm lam*), and cold Hanoi beer for 120,000 VND to 180,000 VND ($4.80 to $7.20 USD) per person.

> [!NOTE]
> Two-stroke diesel boat motors generate 75 dB to 85 dB of continuous noise over a 6.0 hours tour. Sit in the front three bow rows (4.0 meters ahead of the stern engine) for quiet conversation, or rent a two-person kayak from Bo Lu or Pac Ngoi (250,000 VND to 300,000 VND, or $10.00 to $12.00 USD for 3.0 hours) to paddle 5.0 km across Pe Lam and Pe Lu in complete silence between 06:30 and 09:30.

## Ethnic Homestays: Pac Ngoi, Bo Lu, and Coc Toc Villages

Community-based tourism in Ba Be centers on three lakeside Tay ethnic minority villages along the southern shore:

- **Pac Ngoi Village (*Bản Pác Ngòi*, 80 households, 700 meters from the water):** The liveliest hub, connected by paved road to Cho Ra (16 km east). Traditional ironwood stilt houses and upgraded timber bungalows (such as **Ba Be Lake View Homestay, Mr. Linh's Homestay, Quynh Mai Homestay, and Duy Khanh Homestay**) charge 120,000 VND to 180,000 VND ($4.80 to $7.20 USD) for a curtained dorm mattress with mosquito net, or 300,000 VND to 650,000 VND ($12.00 to $26.00 USD) for a private en-suite room with air conditioning and lake-facing balcony.

- **Bo Lu Village (*Bản Bó Lù*, 2.0 km northwest of Pac Ngoi, 5 minutes by scooter):** Positioned directly on the Pe Lam shoreline beside the main southern boat pier, Bo Lu is quieter than Pac Ngoi and allows guests to step out of their stilt house and launch a kayak within 50 meters.

- **Coc Toc Village (*Bản Cốc Tộc*, 1.5 km west of Pac Ngoi across the Leng River suspension bridge):** An agricultural hamlet of 45 Tay households where residents still fish with hand-woven bamboo traps (*đó*) and wooden dugout canoes (*thuyền độc mộc*).

Evening family dinners (served at 19:00 for 120,000 VND to 180,000 VND, or $4.80 to $7.20 USD per guest) feature local mountain dishes cooked over wood hearths:

- **Charcoal-Grilled Nang River Fish (*Cá mương nướng kẹp tre*):** Finger-sized freshwater carp seasoned with wild ginger and turmeric, clamped in split bamboo sticks, and slow-roasted for 40 minutes (60,000 VND to 90,000 VND or $2.40 to $3.60 USD per plate if ordered à la carte).

- **Hearth-Smoked Pork (*Thịt lợn gác bếp*) & Sour Shrimp (*Tôm chua Ba Bể*):** Cured pork belly stir-fried with wild *mắc khén* pepper, paired with fermented lake shrimp and *rau bò khai* forest greens.

- **Tay *Hát Then* Cultural Performance:** Groups can book a 60 minutes evening performance of UNESCO-recognized *Hát Then* singing and 3-string *Đàn Tính* gourd lute music by village elders for 400,000 VND to 600,000 VND ($16.00 to $24.00 USD) per group.

## Overland Treks, Cycling Loops, and Hua Ma Cave

Beyond the lake, Ba Be's southern limestone valleys and high-altitude minority ridges offer rewarding half-day cycling loops and full-day ranger-led treks.

### 1. The Hua Ma Cave & Silver Waterfall Cycling Loop (16 km Round-Trip)
Rent a mountain bike in Pac Ngoi for 50,000 VND to 80,000 VND ($2.00 to $3.20 USD) per day, or a 110cc Honda Wave scooter for 150,000 VND to 200,000 VND ($6.00 to $8.00 USD) per day. Follow the paved valley road 6.0 kilometers south along the Leng River (25 minutes by bike, 12 minutes by scooter) to **Hua Ma Cave** (*Động Hua Mạ*, open 07:00 to 17:00, entry 45,000 VND or $1.80 USD, parking 10,000 VND or $0.40 USD).

Visitors climb 300 shaded stone steps (15 minutes, 120 meters vertical gain) up Co Don Mountain to enter a colossal dry cavern stretching 700 meters deep with 40 meters to 50 meters ceiling vaults. LED walkways (45 minutes round-trip) illuminate 10 meters tall lotus-shaped stalagmites and terraced rimstone curtains. Continuing 2.0 kilometers further south reaches **Thac Bac** (Silver Waterfall / *Thác Bạc Bản Vàng*, 0 VND entry), where a mountain stream plunges 40 meters into cool rock pools suitable for a 30 minutes swim.

### 2. Full-Day Highland Trekking & Lo Mo Cave (12 km to 18 km)
Through the National Park Visitor Center or licensed homestays, hikers can hire an indigenous Tay or Dzao guide for 500,000 VND to 700,000 VND ($20.00 to $28.00 USD) per day (1 to 4 hikers). The classic 15 kilometers highland circuit (5.5 hours to 6.5 hours, 650 meters cumulative elevation gain) climbs from Pac Ngoi through **Na Nghe** (Red Dzao village at 550 meters altitude) and **Phja Khao** (White Hmong ridge hamlet at 820 meters altitude) before descending via Coc Toc. Adventurous cavers can also book the 6.0 hours **Lo Mo Cave (*Thẳm Phầy*) Expedition** (650,000 VND to 900,000 VND or $26.00 to $36.00 USD per person), wading and swimming 1.5 km through an unlit subterranean river canyon beneath 50 meters limestone domes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best season to visit Ba Be Lake?
The optimal travel windows span **September to November** (autumn) and **March to May** (spring). Autumn delivers dry sunny skies, daytime temperatures of 21°C to 26°C, calm emerald water, and golden rice harvests in Pac Ngoi and Coc Toc. Summer (June to August, 26°C to 32°C) brings lush waterfalls and warm swimming conditions, though heavy overnight rains can turn the Nang River brown and briefly pause Puong Cave boat passage for 24 hours to 48 hours.

### Can you swim in Ba Be Lake?
Yes. Swimming is permitted and popular from April through October when water temperatures sit between 24°C and 27°C. The cleanest, deepest swimming zones are off Widow Island in Pe Lam basin (12 meters depth), near An Ma Temple in Pe Leng basin, and at the natural rock pools of Silver Waterfall (8.0 km south of Pac Ngoi). Boat captains provide life vests free of charge during 15-minute to 30-minute swim stops.

### How much cash should I bring to Ba Be National Park?
Carry at least **1,500,000 VND to 2,500,000 VND ($60.00 to $100.00 USD) in physical cash per person** for a 2-day, 2-night stay. There are zero ATMs inside Ba Be National Park, Pac Ngoi, or Bo Lu; the nearest Agribank and BIDV ATMs are located 16 kilometers east in **Cho Ra Town** (25 minutes each way by scooter). While larger homestays accept VietQR domestic bank transfers, boat tickets, cave counters, and village noodle stalls require cash.

### How long should you spend at Ba Be National Park?
Plan for **2 full days and 2 nights**. Arrive from Hanoi by 11:30 AM on Day 1, take a 3.0 hours afternoon kayak (250,000 VND or $10.00 USD) and cycle 6.0 km to Hua Ma Cave (45,000 VND or $1.80 USD), then dedicate Day 2 to the 7.0 hours Grand Boat Circuit (850,000 VND to 1,100,000 VND or $34.00 to $44.00 USD per boat) covering Puong Cave and Dau Dang Waterfall before departing on Day 3.

### Can Ba Be Lake be combined with Cao Bang and Ban Gioc Waterfall?
Yes. Ba Be sits 130 kilometers southwest of Cao Bang City along Provincial Road DT258 and National Highway QL3 across Giap Khau and Tai Ho Sin passes (3.5 hours to 4.0 hours drive). Daily morning minibuses link Cho Ra Town with Cao Bang Bus Station for 250,000 VND to 300,000 VND ($10.00 to $12.00 USD), or travelers can charter a private 4-seat car from Pac Ngoi straight to Ban Gioc Waterfall (210 km total, 5.5 hours) for 2,200,000 VND to 2,800,000 VND ($88.00 to $112.00 USD).
