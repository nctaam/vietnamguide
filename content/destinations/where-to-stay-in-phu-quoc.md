# Where to Stay in Phu Quoc: Best Beaches & Areas

Stretching 50 km from the Cambodian maritime border in the north to the An Thoi archipelago in the south, Phu Quoc covers 574 km²—roughly the land area of Singapore. Picking the wrong coastline on an island this large costs 45 minutes and 450,000 VND ($18 USD) each way in daily taxi fares, or leaves you staring at red rip-current flags while calm turquoise water sits 20 minutes away on the opposite shore.

Seasonality and wind direction govern every hotel booking here. From **November through April** (the Northeast Monsoon dry season), the entire **West Coast**—running through Central Long Beach, South Long Beach, Ong Lang Beach, and Northern Bai Dai—enjoys glassy seas, 28°C to 31°C sunshine, and direct Gulf of Thailand sunsets. From **May through October** (the Southwest Monsoon wet season), westerly winds whip up 1.5-meter waves and undertows along Long Beach and Ong Lang, while the sheltered **East Coast** at **Bai Khem (Kem Beach)** and **Bai Sao** stays flat and swimmable. Pair this wind calendar with your transport budget before locking in your room.

Staying within 300 meters of a Tran Hung Dao VinBus stop saves 450,000–550,000 VND ($18–$22 USD) per northern trip.

## 1. Central Long Beach & Duong Dong Town (Bai Truong North / Tran Hung Dao Street)

If you want to step outside your hotel gate and walk to thirty seafood grills, specialty coffee shops, pharmacies, ATMs, and beach bars without calling a car, book along **Central Long Beach** (*Bãi Trường* North). This 5 km strip follows **Tran Hung Dao Street** immediately south of **Duong Dong Town**, the island's commercial heart. Landward-side hotels sit 100 meters to 250 meters across the road from the water, while beachfront resorts claim shaded palm lawns directly on the sand.

Location logistics here are effortless. Phu Quoc International Airport (PQC) sits **10–15 minutes (9 km)** south via GrabCar (120,000–160,000 VND / $4.80–$6.40 USD), and free **VinBus electric buses (Routes 17 & 19, 0 VND)** stop every few hundred meters along **Tran Hung Dao Street**. At the northern tip of the bay, **Dinh Cau Temple** perches on wave-carved coastal rocks beside the fishing harbor, 400 meters from the **Phu Quoc Night Market** on **Bach Dang Street** (open 17:00 to 23:30 for grilled sea urchin at 40,000 VND, pepper crab, and *bún quậy* stirred fish-paste noodles at Kiến Xây for 65,000 VND / $2.60 USD).

Nightly rates fall into two distinct tiers. Clean mid-range hotels, hillside bungalows, and apartment towers along Tran Hung Dao Street (such as Cassia Cottage, Tropicana Resort, and Sea Star Resort) charge **850,000–2,200,000 VND ($34–$88 USD)** per night. Established five-star beachfront resorts with private sand frontage—led by **Salinda Resort Phu Quoc Island**, **La Veranda Resort Phu Quoc - MGallery**, and **Dusit Princess Moonrise Beach Resort**—run **3,200,000–6,500,000 VND ($128–$260 USD)** per night including full breakfast buffets. Book 1.5 km to 4 km south of Dinh Cau Harbor for the cleanest swimming water.

## 2. South Long Beach (Bai Truong Integrated Resort Boulevard)

South of the airport highway, Long Beach widens into a master-planned, 10 km coastal boulevard purpose-built for international five-star resorts and private pool villas. Scooter traffic disappears behind four-lane palm avenues, manicured cycling paths, and unbroken golden sand.

Phu Quoc Airport (PQC) sits just **7 minutes (5 km)** from the center of South Long Beach, costing **80,000–120,000 VND ($3.20–$4.80 USD)** by Grab. Flagship luxury properties anchor this shoreline: **Regent Phu Quoc** (all-pool-suite and villa ultra-luxury), **InterContinental Phu Quoc Long Beach Resort**, **Pullman Phu Quoc Beach Resort**, **Novotel Phu Quoc Resort**, and **Best Western Premier Sonasea Phu Quoc**, spanning **2,500,000–14,000,000 VND ($100–$560 USD)** per night. Sunset beach clubs define the evening scene here, led by the 19th-floor **INK 360** rooftop octopus bar at InterContinental (cocktails from 320,000 VND) and beachfront wood-fired dining with 19:45 fire shows at **Sailing Club Phu Quoc**.

Understand the dining trade-off before booking. Outside the Sonasea night walking street and Phu Quoc Marina plaza, independent local restaurants are scarce. You need a taxi (**150,000–220,000 VND / $6.00–$8.80 USD**, 15 to 20 minutes each way) or free **VinBus Route 19 (0 VND)** from its InterContinental terminus to reach Duong Dong Town.

## 3. Ong Lang Beach (Cua Duong — 8 km North of Duong Dong)

Travelers who dislike concrete hotel towers head **8 km north of Duong Dong** into **Cua Duong Commune**, where quiet side lanes branch 1.2 km west off the Cua Can highway toward **Ong Lang Beach**. Instead of a single straight urban strand, Ong Lang splits into three crescent coves of golden sand framed by black volcanic boulders, casuarina pines, and coconut trees. No high-rise towers exist here.

Ong Lang is Phu Quoc's capital for low-rise eco-bungalows. Eco-conscious classics and upscale retreats—led by **Mango Bay Resort** (rammed-earth and timber bungalows cooled by sea breezes across 10 hectares of coastal forest), **Chen Sea Resort & Spa**, **Camia Resort & Spa**, and the hilltop infinity pools of **Lahana Resort**—price luxury eco-villas and sea-view bungalows at **3,500,000–7,000,000 VND ($140–$280 USD)** per night. Along the shaded inner lanes of Dinh Ba and Ong Lang streets, family-run boutique homestays and garden pool hotels charge **750,000–2,400,000 VND ($30–$95 USD)**.

Nights stay relaxed. Within a 600-meter walk along the main Ong Lang lane, expat-owned bistros, wood-fired pizzerias, craft cocktail shacks, and Vietnamese clay-pot kitchens serve dinner for 120,000–350,000 VND ($4.80–$14 USD) per person, though you should carry a phone flashlight after 21:30 for the dimly lit sandy paths down to the coves. A GrabCar from PQC Airport takes 25 minutes (18 km, 240,000–300,000 VND).

## 4. East Coast (Bai Khem / Kem Beach & Bai Sao) & Sunset Town (An Thoi South Tip)

While the West Coast has golden-yellow sand, Phu Quoc's finest white-powder beaches lie 25 km south near **An Thoi** on the **East Coast**: **Bai Khem (Kem Beach)** and **Bai Sao**. Because these east-facing bays sit sheltered behind central hills, their water remains calm, flat, and clear during the **May–October Southwest Monsoon** when West Coast beaches post red rip-current flags.

**Bai Khem** is a clean 1.5 km crescent shared by three upscale properties: Bill Bensley's **JW Marriott Phu Quoc Emerald Bay**, all-villa **New World Phu Quoc Resort**, and family-friendly **Premier Residences Phu Quoc Emerald Bay**, with nightly rates spanning **3,500,000–12,500,000 VND ($140–$500 USD)**. Staying at Bai Khem avoids the midday tour-bus crowds that pack public **Bai Sao** 4 km north between 11:00 and 15:00.

Directly across the 3 km peninsula on the southwestern tip sits **Sunset Town** (*An Thoi*), where pastel hillside shophouse hotels and boutique stays cost **800,000–2,100,000 VND ($32–$84 USD)** per night. Booking here puts you right at the terminal for the **7,899.9m Hon Thom Cable Car** (**850,000 VND** round-trip ticket including Aquatopia Water Park), the **Kiss Bridge** (**50k–100k VND** entry), and **An Thoi harbor** for morning 4-island snorkeling speedboats to May Rut, Gam Ghi, and Mong Tay islets (750,000–950,000 VND per person).

## 5. North Island (Ganh Dau, Vung Bau & Bai Dai / Grand World)

Covering **Ganh Dau**, **Vung Bau**, and **Bai Dai / Grand World**, the North Island borders Phu Quoc National Park **35–45 minutes (32 km)** north of PQC Airport. Families base themselves along Bai Dai Beach (**2,800,000–9,500,000 VND** five-star resorts such as Sheraton, Crowne Plaza Starbay, and Green Bay Vung Bau) or inside the 24/7 **Grand World** canal complex (**900,000–2,500,000 VND** boutique hotels) for immediate access to **Vinpearl Safari** (**750,000 VND** ticket) and **VinWonders** (**950,000 VND** ticket), plus 20-minute transfers east to **Rach Vem Starfish Beach**. Always ride free **VinBus Routes 17 or 19 (0 VND)** from PQC Airport or Duong Dong Town to save **450,000–550,000 VND ($18–$22 USD)** in one-way taxi fares.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which side of Phu Quoc is best during the May to October rainy season?
Book the **East Coast at Bai Khem (Kem Beach)**—such as Premier Residences, New World, or JW Marriott Emerald Bay—during the **May to October** Southwest Monsoon. While West Coast beaches (Long Beach and Ong Lang) face onshore winds and red rip-current flags, Bai Khem and Bai Sao sit sheltered on the leeward eastern shore with calm, clear swimming water.

### Is it better to stay in Central Long Beach (Duong Dong) or Ong Lang Beach?
Choose **Central Long Beach (Tran Hung Dao Street)** for walkable access to 30+ restaurants, Phu Quoc Night Market (Bach Dang), Dinh Cau Temple, free VinBus stops (0 VND), and a 10–15 minute ride from PQC Airport. Pick **Ong Lang Beach** (8 km north) if you prefer low-rise eco-bungalows shaded by casuarina and coconut trees, golden rocky coves, and zero high-rise hotel towers.

### Should I split my stay between North and South Phu Quoc?
On a trip of **5 nights or longer**, split your stay across the 574 km² island: spend **3 nights in Central Long Beach or Ong Lang** for west-coast sunsets, night markets, and Vinpearl Safari (750,000 VND), followed by **2 nights at Bai Khem or Sunset Town** in the south for the 7,899.9m Hon Thom Cable Car (850,000 VND) and An Thoi 4-island speedboats.
