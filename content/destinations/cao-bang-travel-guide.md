# Cao Bang Travel Guide: Ban Gioc Waterfall & Loop Routes

Spanning 3,390 square kilometers across Vietnam's northeastern border with Guangxi (China), **Cao Bang** protects one of Southeast Asia's premier karst landscapes. Designated as the **Non Nuoc Cao Bang UNESCO Global Geopark** in 2018, the province encompasses 500-million-year-old limestone tower karsts (*fenglin*), conical valleys (*fengcong*), subterranean river caves, and alpine peaks reaching 1,931 meters above sea level.

At its eastern frontier stands **Ban Gioc Waterfall** (`Thác Bản Giốc`, located 85 km / 2.2 hours northeast of Cao Bang City via National Highway QL3 and DT206), the fourth-largest transnational cascade on Earth. Here, the Quay Son River plunges 30 meters over three limestone terraces spanning 300 meters in width. Beyond the waterfall, the province rewards riders with the 2,144 meters subterranean gallery of **Nguom Ngao Cave**, the 50 meters natural rock oculus of **Angel Eye Mountain**, the turquoise spring at **Pac Bo** (52 km northwest), and the 14-tier vertical switchbacks of **Khau Coc Cha Pass** (115 km northwest).

For independent riders departing Hanoi (285 km south, 5.5 hours by VIP limousine at 400,000 VND to 450,000 VND / $16.00 to $18.00 USD) or linking east from the Ha Giang Loop via Meo Vac and Bao Lac (210 km, 6.5 hours via QL4B and QL34), this empirical 2026 field guide details statutory entrance tariffs, hydroelectric water-release windows, road gradients, and a 3-day circuit.

## Ban Gioc Waterfall: Hydrology, Rafts, and Border Realities

Straddling the sovereign boundary between Dam Thuy commune (Trung Khanh district, Vietnam) and Detian village (Daxin county, Guangxi, China), **Ban Gioc Waterfall** (`Thác Bản Giốc`) is the widest curtain waterfall in Asia.

Fed by the **Quay Son River** (`Sông Quây Sơn`), which enters Vietnam at Ngoc Khe commune and winds 48 km through cornfields and bamboo waterwheels, the cascade drops 30 meters in vertical height across three travertine limestone shelves totaling 300 meters in width. The eastern main multi-tiered cascade (`Thác Chính`, 150 meters wide) bisects the international border channel near Border Marker 836(2), while the western high cascade (`Thác Phụ`, 150 meters wide) stands 100% inside Vietnamese territory.

### Statutory Admission, Bamboo Rafts & Truc Lam Overlook
At the main Vietnamese ticketing plaza (located 800 meters from the river), adults pay a statutory entrance fee of 45,000 VND ($1.80 USD), children measuring 1.0 meter to 1.3 meters pay 20,000 VND ($0.80 USD), and motorbike parking costs 10,000 VND ($0.40 USD) (cars pay 20,000 VND / $0.80 USD). Open daily from 07:00 to 17:30 (10.5 hours), a 600 meters paved path leads across footbridges to the grassy riverfront.

To feel the spray 15 meters from the plunging curtain, purchase a boarding voucher at the riverside kiosk for 50,000 VND ($2.00 USD) per passenger to ride a motorized blue-canopy bamboo raft (`bè mảng`) on a 15 minutes to 20 minutes loop around the plunge pool (up to 12 passengers per raft, orange life vests mandatory). Chinese rafts (distinguished by yellow-and-red roofs) share the same 80 meters wide river basin, though strict border maritime rules forbid landing on the opposite bank. Before departing, walk 500 meters (8 minutes) south across the highway and climb 150 stone steps (15 minutes ascent, 0 VND / $0.00 USD entry) to **Phat Tich Truc Lam Ban Gioc Pagoda**, built at 380 meters elevation with an unobstructed aerial view over the entire 300 meters cascade and Chinese karst towers behind it.

### Optimal Daily Timing: The Upstream Hydroelectric Release Window
During the rainy summer season (June through September, when monthly rainfall reaches 240 mm to 320 mm and daytime temperatures average 26°C to 32°C), both the main and auxiliary cascades thunder continuously. During the dry autumn and winter months (October through April, 12°C to 24°C, 75% humidity), upstream mini-hydroelectric dams along the Quay Son River hold back water overnight. Dam gates open daily between **10:30 AM and 13:30 PM** (a 180 minutes peak flow window), raising river depth by 0.5 meters and doubling the waterfall's volume within 25 minutes. Time your arrival for 10:45 AM to capture both bright midday sunlight and maximum water discharge.

> [!NOTE]
> Foreign citizens visiting Ban Gioc Waterfall, Nguom Ngao Cave, and Khuoi Ky Village do not need to purchase a separate $10.00 USD border police permit (unlike remote frontier communes in Ha Giang). However, because the waterfall basin sits 30 meters from Chinese territory, you must carry your physical passport (not a phone photo) for routine verification at the hotel registration desk and the waterfall turnstile.

## Subterranean Wonders: Nguom Ngao Cave & Khuoi Ky Stone Village

Located 4.0 km (8 minutes drive along DT206) south of Ban Gioc Waterfall at Gun Village, **Nguom Ngao Cave** (`Động Ngườm Ngao`) is a 300-million-year-old river cave system carved through a limestone massif.

In the local Tay language, *Ngườm Ngao* means "Tiger Cave" (or "Cave of the Roaring Wind," referencing the subterranean stream echoing through its chambers). Surveyed by the British Cave Research Association at 2,144 meters in total length with three natural entrances (`Ngườm Ngao`, `Ngườm Lồm`, and `Bản Thuôn`), the cave stays at a cool 18°C to 21°C year-round. Travelers choose between two official ticket tiers at the entrance booth (open 07:00 to 17:00, parking 10,000 VND / $0.40 USD):
- **Standard Illuminated Walkway (980 meters, 45 minutes to 60 minutes, 45,000 VND / $1.80 USD per adult, 20,000 VND / $0.80 USD per child):** Follows a concrete footpath with safety handrails past the **Inverted Golden Lotus** (`Bông Sen Vàng Ngược`, a 4 meters wide tiered calcite stalagmite), the **Sky Pillar** (`Cột Đỡ Trời`, a 25 meters high limestone column), terraced rimstone pools resembling miniature rice paddies, and the shimmering **Silver Waterfall** (`Thác Bạc`).
- **Ban Thuon Discovery Route (3.0 km total caving loop, 2.0 hours to 2.5 hours, 195,000 VND / $7.80 USD per person):** Includes a trained Geopark guide, caving helmet, 500-lumen headlamp, rubber wading boots, and a 400 meters bamboo raft crossing inside the unlit underground river branch during high water.

Halfway between Ban Gioc and Nguom Ngao (2.0 km from the waterfall) stands **Khuoi Ky Stone Village** (`Làng Đá Khuổi Ky`, entry 20,000 VND / $0.80 USD for day visitors, or 0 VND / $0.00 USD for overnight guests). Established in 1594 during the Mac Dynasty, this 1-hectare settlement comprises 14 traditional Tay stilt houses built entirely of dry-stacked gray limestone walls measuring 40 cm thick on 2.5 meters stone pillars. Overnight homestays inside the stone houses (**Khuổi Ky Eco Homestay** or **Yến Nhi Homestay**) cost 150,000 VND ($6.00 USD) for a dorm mattress or 350,000 VND to 500,000 VND ($14.00 to $20.00 USD) for a private room, with home-cooked Tay family dinners at 120,000 VND ($4.80 USD) per person.

## Geological Marvels: Ma Phuc Pass, Angel Eye Mountain & Thang Hen Lake

Riding 22 km to 35 km (40 minutes to 60 minutes) northeast of Cao Bang City along National Highway QL3 and Provincial Road DT205 unlocks the central karst cluster of the UNESCO Geopark.

### 1. Ma Phuc Pass (`Đèo Mã Phục`, 22 km east on QL3)
Climbing 3.5 km through 7 wide asphalt switchbacks to a summit elevation of 620 meters above sea level, **Ma Phuc Pass** forms the geological boundary between the western river basin and the eastern tower-karst plateau. Named after two 180 meters limestone crags flanking the road like kneeling warhorses, the pass hosts a traditional Tay and Nung highland market at its crest every 5 days (on lunar dates ending in 3 and 8, from 06:00 to 11:00).

### 2. Angel Eye Mountain (`Núi Mắt Thần` / `Núi Thủng`, 32 km northeast)
Turning north onto DT205 just before Ma Phuc Pass and riding 10 km (20 minutes) into Quoc Toan commune (Tra Linh district) leads into the **Nam Tra Valley** (`Thung Lũng Bản Danh`). Rising 200 meters above the flat valley meadow stands **Angel Eye Mountain**, a solitary limestone peak pierced near its summit by a natural circular cave window measuring 50 meters in diameter.

Entry to the valley is 0 VND ($0.00 USD) with a 10,000 VND ($0.40 USD) motorbike parking fee at the trailhead. During the dry season (September through May), the 15-hectare basin is a lush green pasture crossed by a 3 meters wide clear stream where Tay herders graze 200 horses and water buffalo (overnight tent rental costs 150,000 VND to 250,000 VND / $6.00 to $10.00 USD). During peak summer rains (June to August), subterranean karst siphons surcharge the valley floor into a 6 meters deep emerald lake where local boats ferry visitors for 40,000 VND ($1.60 USD).

### 3. Thang Hen Lake (`Hồ Thang Hen`, 35 km northeast)
Just 3.0 km (8 minutes ride) uphill from Angel Eye Mountain sits **Thang Hen Lake** (entry 50,000 VND / $2.00 USD per adult; boat ride 50,000 VND / $2.00 USD). Positioned at 600 meters altitude, this 300 meters wide, 1,500 meters long diamond-shaped lake is the largest of 36 interconnected karst lakes linked by underground limestone caves that rise and fall by up to 10 meters across the year.

## Western Passes & Revolutionary History: Pac Bo & Khau Coc Cha

West of Cao Bang City, the landscape transitions into steep border gorges and 1,900 meters granite massifs.

### 1. Pac Bo Historical Complex (`Khu Di Tích Pác Bó`, 52 km northwest via DT203)
Located 80 minutes northwest of Cao Bang City at the end of paved road DT203 (1.0 km from the Chinese border in Ha Quang district), **Pac Bo** is where Ho Chi Minh crossed Border Marker 108 into Vietnam on January 28, 1941, after 30 years abroad. Visitors pay 45,000 VND ($1.80 USD) entry plus 20,000 VND ($0.80 USD) for the 1.5 km round-trip electric buggy to the streamhead. A 2.0 km paved trail follows the mineral-rich turquoise **Lenin Stream** (`Suối Lê-nin`, 1.5 meters deep with 100% bottom visibility) to the base of **Karl Marx Mountain** (`Núi Các Mác`), the stone worktable (`Bàn Đá`), and **Coc Bo Cave** (`Hang Cốc Bó`, 15 square meters), plus a 1.5 km steep forest hike (45 minutes) up to **Milestone 108** on the Chinese ridge.

### 2. Khau Coc Cha 14-Tier Pass (`Đèo Khau Cốc Chà`, 115 km northwest via QL4A & DT204)
Situated between Xuan Truong commune and Bao Lac town on road DT204, **Khau Coc Cha Pass** is northern Vietnam's most vertically stacked mountain road. Built between 2009 and 2011 to replace a 0.8 m horse track, the 2.5 km paved road climbs 500 vertical meters from 600 meters to 1,100 meters elevation across **14 tight 180-degree hairpin tiers** (totaling 15 curves including the base).

Park your motorbike at the summit refreshment shack (10,000 VND / $0.40 USD parking, cold water 15,000 VND / $0.60 USD) and walk 1.2 km (25 minutes to 35 minutes each way along a dirt cliff trail) out to the **Khau Coc Cha Viewpoint Promontory**, where all 14 asphalt tiers stack directly below your boots like a ribbon staircase.

### 3. Phia Oac - Phia Den National Park (`Vườn Quốc Gia Phia Oắc`, 75 km west via QL34)
Located in Nguyen Binh district, **Phia Oac Peak** rises to **1,931 meters** above sea level (winter temperatures drop to -2°C to 5°C with occasional January frost). Riders can drive all the way to the 1,931 m radio tower summit, explore crumbling 1920s French colonial stone villas in the dwarf moss forest (0 VND / $0.00 USD entry), and tour the **Kolia Organic Tea Plantation** at 1,300 meters (entry 30,000 VND / $1.20 USD; rooms 600,000 VND / $24.00 USD).

## Culinary Heritage: Sour Pho, Steamed Broth Rolls & Roast Duck

Cao Bang's Tay and Nung kitchens serve warming dishes priced between 30,000 VND and 350,000 VND ($1.20 to $14.00 USD):

- **Bánh Cuốn Cao Bằng (Steamed Rice Rolls in Bone Broth, 30,000 VND to 40,000 VND / $1.20 to $1.60 USD):** Unlike Hanoi's cold dipping fish sauce, Cao Bang cooks ladles steaming pork marrow broth into a bowl with 2 poached eggs and pork sausage (*giò*), which you pour over thin rice crepes freshly steamed to order. **Best Stall:** Bánh Cuốn Bà Thiên (Hoang Dinh Giong Street, Cao Bang City, open 06:00 to 10:30, 4.5 hours).
- **Phở Chua Cao Bằng (Crispy Duck & Taro Sour Pho, 40,000 VND to 50,000 VND / $1.60 to $2.00 USD):** Chewy fresh rice noodles tossed dry in a warm, tangy tamarind-and-garlic sauce topped with crispy roast pork belly, shredded roast duck, julienned fried taro sticks (*khoai môn chiên*), pork liver, and roasted peanuts. **Best Stall:** Phở Chua Gia Truyền Quyên (062B Vườn Cam Street, open 06:30 to 14:00, 7.5 hours).
- **Vịt Quay 7 Vị (Seven-Spice Mac Mat Roast Duck, 280,000 VND to 340,000 VND / $11.20 to $13.60 USD per whole 1.6 kg duck, or 45,000 VND / $1.80 USD over rice noodles):** Local stream ducks stuffed with aromatic *mắc mật* leaves, star anise, ginger, garlic, and forest honey before slow-roasting over charcoal for 60 minutes.
- **Hạt Dẻ Trùng Khánh (Trung Khanh Roasted Chestnuts, 90,000 VND to 130,000 VND / $3.60 to $5.20 USD per kilogram):** Harvested in September and October along the Quay Son valley and tumbled in hot black sand with star anise at Kim Dong Night Market (`Phố Đi Bộ Kim Đồng`, open Friday and Saturday 19:00 to 23:00).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do foreigners need a travel permit to visit Ban Gioc Waterfall?
No special border police permit is required in 2026 for foreign visitors touring Ban Gioc Waterfall, Nguom Ngao Cave, Khuoi Ky Stone Village, or Pac Bo. However, because Ban Gioc sits directly on the Chinese border, you must carry your original physical passport with a valid Vietnamese visa or entry stamp for inspection at the 45,000 VND ($1.80 USD) ticket turnstile and your overnight homestay.

### What is the best month to visit Cao Bang?
September and October offer the best overall conditions: daytime temperatures sit at 22°C to 27°C, humidity drops to 74%, the Quay Son River still carries strong post-summer volume over all 300 meters of Ban Gioc Waterfall, and the surrounding Trung Khanh rice paddies turn golden for the autumn harvest. July and August deliver maximum water volume but bring 300 mm of monthly rain, while December to February brings dry, misty 10°C to 18°C days.

### Can you drive a motorbike from Ha Giang (Meo Vac) to Cao Bang?
Yes. The 210 km mountain route linking Meo Vac (Ha Giang Loop) to Cao Bang City via Ly Bon Bridge, Bao Lac (80 km), and National Highway QL34 (130 km) takes 6.0 hours to 7.0 hours of riding. Better yet, take the northern detour from Bao Lac up DT204 over the **14-tier Khau Coc Cha Pass** and Xuan Truong Valley (225 km total). Fill your 3.7-liter tank in Bao Lac and Nguyen Binh (50,000 VND / $2.00 USD per tank), and test both brakes before descending 12% grades.

### Where should I stay near Ban Gioc Waterfall?
Stay 2.0 km from the falls inside **Khuoi Ky Stone Village** (`Làng Đá Khuổi Ky`), where 400-year-old Tay stone stilt homestays (**Khuổi Ky Eco Homestay**, **Yến Nhi**, or **Lan Rừng**) charge 150,000 VND ($6.00 USD) per dorm bed or 350,000 VND to 500,000 VND ($14.00 to $20.00 USD) for a private double room. Travelers seeking luxury can book **Saigon - Ban Gioc Resort** (500 meters from the waterfall, 1,400,000 VND to 1,900,000 VND / $56.00 to $76.00 USD per night).

### How do you get from Hanoi to Cao Bang (and from Cao Bang to Ban Gioc by public bus)?
From Hanoi, 9-seat VIP DCar limousines (**Thanh Ly**, **Vinh Dung**, or **Hiep Giang**, 400,000 VND to 450,000 VND / $16.00 to $18.00 USD one-way) pick up in the Old Quarter at 06:30, 09:30, and 14:30 and reach Cao Bang City in 5.5 hours (285 km via CT07 and QL3), while overnight 24-cabin sleeper buses from My Dinh Station cost 320,000 VND to 350,000 VND ($12.80 to $14.00 USD, 6.5 hours, 21:00 to 04:00). From Cao Bang City, red-and-yellow **Public Bus #07** departs every 30 minutes from 05:30 to 17:30 right to the Ban Gioc Waterfall gate (85 km, 2.5 hours, 70,000 VND to 80,000 VND / $2.80 to $3.20 USD).
