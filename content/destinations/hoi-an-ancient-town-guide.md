# Hoi An Ancient Town Guide: When to Stay, Visit or Skip (2026)

Positioned along the lower estuary of the Thu Bon River in Quang Nam province (**30 km** south of Da Nang International Airport), **Hoi An Ancient Town** stands as Southeast Asia's best-preserved 16th-to-19th-century maritime trading port. Known historically to European and Asian cartographers as *Faifo*, the port flourished for over **300 years** as an international entrepot where Japanese silver ships, Chinese junks, and Dutch merchant vessels traded silk, ceramics, cinnamon, and agarwood. Formally inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in December 1999, the **30-hectare** protected core preserves **1,107 timber-framed buildings** arranged across a riverside grid of narrow lanes.

For travelers planning a Central Vietnam route in 2026, Hoi An requires strategic timing. Between **17:30 and 21:00**, the **800-meter** waterfront along Bach Dang Street and the An Hoi footbridge fills with day-trip tour buses arriving from Da Nang, leather boutiques, and souvenir stalls. Travelers who visit only for **3 hours** during the peak evening rush often find the core congested.

Walk those same lanes at **06:30**, or base yourself **1.5 km to 4.5 km** outside the ticketed perimeter in the rice fields of Cam Chau or along An Bang Beach, and a different town emerges. Seventh-generation merchant families still burn morning incense beneath ironwood roof trusses carved in **1741**, traditional wooden sampans ferry herbs from Tra Que (**3.0 km** north), and master tailors cut bespoke wool suits over **36 hours to 48 hours**. This 2026 guide details the **120,000 VND ($4.80 USD)** heritage ticket rules, pedestrian-only hours, monument highlights, Thu Bon boat rates, custom tailoring benchmarks, regional food institutions, and Da Nang transfer options.

> Hoi An enforces strict **Pedestrian-Only Hours (*Phố đi bộ*)** across the core ancient quarter every day from **09:00 to 11:00 AM** and **15:00 to 21:30 PM** (extended to **22:00 PM** in summer). Gasoline motorbikes, scooters, and cars are barred from entering the heritage perimeter during these **8.5 hours** daily, leaving the streets quiet for walkers and non-motorized bicycles.

## The Heritage Sightseeing Ticket: Rules, Ethics, and Monument Access

Foreign visitors frequently ask how Hoi An's heritage ticket works and where coupons are checked.

### How the Ticket Operates
- **Statutory Tariff:** The official **Hoi An Ancient Town Sightseeing Ticket** costs **120,000 VND ($4.80 USD)** for international visitors and **80,000 VND ($3.20 USD)** for Vietnamese citizens. Children under **16 years old** enter free (**0 VND**).
- **Coupon Structure & Validity:** Each booklet includes **5 detachable entry coupons** plus general street access to the heritage zone and daily traditional street art performances. While the ticket officially covers **24 hours**, keep your paper stub in your wallet throughout your **2-day to 4-day** stay in Hoi An so you can visit remaining monuments without buying a second booklet.
- **Official Kiosk Locations:** Purchase tickets in cash (**VND**) or via QR payment at 11 yellow municipal ticket booths (*Quay ve tham quan*) stationed around the perimeter, including **08 Hoang Dieu** (near Hoi An Market), **78 Le Loi**, **332 Nguyen Duy Hieu**, and the western foot of the **Japanese Covered Bridge** on Nguyen Thi Minh Khai Street. Kiosks operate daily from **07:00 to 21:00**.
- **Where Revenue Goes:** Roughly **75% to 80%** of ticket proceeds fund structural restoration, anti-termite wood preservation, underground fire-hydrant networks, and direct maintenance grants (covering **40% to 75%** of roof repair costs) for private families living inside Class I and Class II heritage houses.

### Essential Sites to Select with Your 5 Coupons
1. **The Japanese Covered Bridge (*Chùa Cầu*, West End of Tran Phu Street):** Built in the **1590s** by Hoi An's Japanese merchant community to link their quarter with the Chinese neighborhood across the stream, this **18-meter** roofed wooden bridge houses a small northern shrine to the Taoist weather god *Bac De Tran Vu*, flanked by carved wooden dog and monkey guardians. Between December 2022 and August 2024, the monument underwent a **20,200,000,000 VND ($808,000 USD)** scientific restoration that reinforced its stone piers and ironwood trusses. Walking across the exterior lane is visible from the street, while stepping inside the bridge gallery clips **1 coupon** (open **07:30 to 21:00**).
2. **Tấn Ký Old House (*Nhà cổ Tấn Ký*, 101 Nguyen Thai Hoc Street):** Built in **1741** (**285 years** ago) and occupied by 7 generations of the Le family, this classic tubular merchant house stretches **35 meters** from its Nguyen Thai Hoc shopfront back to its Bach Dang river loading dock. Built without a single iron nail using mortise-and-tenon ironwood (*go lim*) and jackfruit wood (*go mit*), the interior features Japanese triple-beam roof supports, Chinese mother-of-pearl calligraphy panels composed of flying bird strokes, and stone wall markers recording historical flood peaks, including the November 1964 flood that reached **2.4 meters** above the ground floor.
3. **Phúc Kiến Assembly Hall (*Hội quán Phúc Kiến*, 46 Tran Phu Street):** Founded in **1697** by Hokkien traders from Fujian province, this **120-meter-deep** sanctuary honors the sea goddess *Thien Hau* (Mazu). Visitors pass through a triple-arched pink ceramic gate into courtyards filled with stone dragon fountains, a **1.75-meter** replica of an ancient Chinese trading junk, and a rear altar hung with giant **30-day** conical incense coils (**100,000 VND to 200,000 VND / $4.00 to $8.00 USD** donation to hang a prayer tag).
4. **Phùng Hưng Old House (*Nhà cổ Phùng Hưng*, 04 Nguyen Thi Minh Khai Street):** Constructed in **1780** just **60 meters** west of the Japanese Covered Bridge, this wide two-story timber trading hall sold cinnamon, pepper, porcelain, and silk. Its high wooden balcony and central trapdoor allowed merchants to winch crates of silk **4.0 meters** up to the second floor whenever autumn floods submerged the street below.
5. **Traditional Performing Arts House (*Nhà biểu diễn Nghệ thuật Cổ truyền*, 66 Bach Dang Street) or Museum of Trade Ceramics (80 Tran Phu Street):** Use your fifth coupon for the **30-minute** live folk music, *Bai Choi* opera, and *dan bau* (monochord) recital held daily at **10:15, 15:15, and 16:15** at 66 Bach Dang Street, or inspect **15th-to-18th-century** Hizen Japanese, Chu Dau Vietnamese, and Ming Chinese porcelain shards inside the restored **1858** wooden house at **80 Tran Phu Street**.

## The Evening River Spectacle: Thu Bon Lantern Sampans

Between **17:45 and 21:30**, over 300 wooden sampans illuminated by silk lanterns row across the **150-meter-wide** Hoai River channel separating Bach Dang Street from An Hoi Islet.

```
THU BON RIVER SAMPAN OPERATING PROTOCOLS
========================================================================================
[Step 1] Walk to the official municipal ticket booths on Bach Dang or Nguyen Phuc Chu street
[Step 2] Pay the statutory tariff: 150,000 VND ($6.00, 1–3 pax) or 200,000 VND ($8.00, 4–5 pax)
[Step 3] Buckle the mandatory orange flotation life vest before stepping onto the wooden sampan
[Step 4] Optional: buy biodegradable paper candle lanterns on shore (10,000–20,000 VND each)
[Step 5] Enjoy a regulated 20-minute hand-rowed loop between Cau An Hoi and Cam Nam Bridge
========================================================================================
```

- **Fixed Municipal Pricing:** Never negotiate with freelance touts on the sidewalk quoting **300,000 VND to 500,000 VND ($12.00 to $20.00 USD)**. Walk directly to the blue-uniformed **Hoai River Boat Cooperative Kiosks** along Bach Dang Embankment (opposite 54 Bach Dang) or on the An Hoi side near the bridge. Statutory 2026 tariffs are printed on the booth: **150,000 VND ($6.00 USD)** total per boat for **1 to 3 passengers**, or **200,000 VND ($8.00 USD)** total per boat for **4 to 5 passengers** for a **20-minute** hand-rowed cruise.
- **Floating Candle Lanterns (*Hoa Đăng*):** Elderly vendors along the river steps sell folded paper lotus lanterns containing a small soy-wax candle for **10,000 VND to 20,000 VND ($0.40 to $0.80 USD)** each.
- **Sunset Motor Launch Option:** If you prefer a quiet **60-minute** afternoon cruise (**16:30 to 17:30**) down the wider main Thu Bon River toward the Cua Dai estuary and Thanh Ha pottery docks before the night crowds gather, charter a private wooden canopy boat at the **Bach Dang East Pier** (near Hoi An Market) for **350,000 VND to 500,000 VND ($14.00 to $20.00 USD)** per hour for up to 6 passengers.

## Custom Tailoring & Leatherwork: Mechanics, Timelines, and 2026 Tariffs

Over 400 tailoring ateliers and 120 custom leather workshops operate within a **1.5-kilometer** radius of the ancient quarter. Getting high-grade garments that hold their shape after returning home requires following a multi-fitting schedule.

### The 3-Fitting Protocol (Minimum 36 to 48 Hours)
- **Skip 12-Hour Rush Promises:** While budget stalls advertise "4-hour or 12-hour suits," fusing canvas lapels, cutting lining, and adjusting sleeve pitch properly requires **36 hours to 48 hours** across **2 to 3 fittings**. Walk into your chosen tailor between **08:30 and 10:30** on Day 1 of your stay.
- **Fitting 1 (Consultation & Measurement, 45–60 minutes on Day 1 Morning):** Inspect fabric bolts in person. Perform the **burn or wrinkle test**: clench a corner of wool fabric tightly for **10 seconds**; genuine Australian merino or Italian wool-cashmere springs back smoothly, whereas cheap polyester creases sharply and feels slick. A master cutter records **25 to 32 body measurements** and photographs your posture. A **30% to 50% deposit** is standard.
- **Fitting 2 (Baste Fitting, 20–24 hours later on Day 2 Morning):** Try on the half-stitched garment. Check collar gap against your neck, shoulder seam alignment, jacket waist suppression, and trouser break over your shoes. Mark chalk adjustments with the head cutter.
- **Fitting 3 (Final Pickup, Day 2 Evening or Day 3 Morning):** Try on the finished piece one more time; minor hem or waist tweaks take **90 minutes** and can be delivered free of charge to your hotel reception.

### Verified 2026 Tailoring & Leather Price Benchmarks
- **Two-Piece Men's or Women's Bespoke Suit:** **2,800,000 VND to 4,200,000 VND ($112.00 to $168.00 USD)** for wool-poly blends; **4,800,000 VND to 8,500,000 VND ($192.00 to $340.00 USD)** for high-twist 70%–100% wool or cashmere blends at top houses.
- **Custom Linen or Silk-Cotton Dress:** **950,000 VND to 1,900,000 VND ($38.00 to $76.00 USD)**.
- **Tailored Dress Shirt or Linen Shirt:** **650,000 VND to 1,200,000 VND ($26.00 to $48.00 USD)**.
- **Winter Cashmere-Wool Overcoat:** **3,200,000 VND to 5,500,000 VND ($128.00 to $220.00 USD)**.
- **Custom Full-Grain Leather Chelsea Boots, Loafers, or Weekender Bag:** **1,400,000 VND to 2,800,000 VND ($56.00 to $112.00 USD)** with a **24-hour** fitting turnaround.
- **Reputable Workshops:** **Yaly Couture** (358 Nguyen Duy Hieu & 47 Nguyen Thai Hoc, largest climate-controlled showroom with digital measurement archives), **Bebe Tailor** (05–07 Hoang Dieu & 95 Phan Chau Trinh), **A Dong Silk** (62 Tran Hung Dao & 40 Le Loi), and **Kimmy Custom Tailor** (70 Tran Hung Dao).
- **Avoid Tout Commissions:** Never let a taxi driver, cyclo rider, or street tout walk you into a tailor shop; shops pay drivers a **25% to 35% kickback commission** that gets added directly onto your fabric quote.

## Culinary Architecture: Iconic Dishes & Verified 2026 Prices

Hoi An's centuries of Chinese, Japanese, and Cham maritime trade produced a distinct micro-cuisine found nowhere else in Vietnam:

- **Cao Lầu (Ash-Water Pork & Crouton Noodles):** Thick, chewy rice noodles soaked in lye ash from Cham Island trees and well water from the **Bá Lễ Well** (alley 45/17 Tran Hung Dao), topped with five-spice char siu pork slices, crispy pork-rind squares, bean sprouts, and Tra Que mint in a spoonful of rich broth. Eat a bowl at **Thanh Cao Lầu** (26 Thai Phien, open **07:00 to 19:00**, **40,000 VND / $1.60 USD**), **Cao Lầu Không Gian Xanh** (687 Hai Ba Trung, **50,000 VND / $2.00 USD**), or **Cao Lầu Liên** (16 Thai Phien, **40,000 VND / $1.60 USD**).
- **Hoi An Bánh Mì:** Pointed, ultra-crisp French-style baguettes baked in charcoal ovens and stuffed with pork liver pate, char siu pork, Vietnamese sausage, papaya pickle, fresh herbs, and house chili sauce. **Bánh Mì Phượng** (2B Phan Chu Trinh, open **06:30 to 21:30**) and **Madam Khanh – The Banh Mi Queen** (115 Tran Cao Van, open **07:00 to 19:30**) charge **30,000 VND to 40,000 VND ($1.20 to $1.60 USD)** per sandwich.
- **Cơm Gà Phố Cổ (Turmeric Poached Chicken Rice):** Free-range shredded chicken tossed with Vietnamese coriander (*rau ram*), pickled onions, and green papaya over fragrant rice cooked in chicken broth and turmeric, served with a small bowl of giblet soup. Visit **Cơm Gà Bà Buội** (22 Phan Chu Trinh, operating since 1955, **60,000 VND to 75,000 VND / $2.40 to $3.00 USD**) or **Cơm Gà Bà Nga** (08 Phan Chu Trinh, **65,000 VND / $2.60 USD**).
- **Bánh Bao Bánh Vạc (White Rose Dumplings):** Translucent pleated rice-flour dumplings filled with minced spiced shrimp and pork, steamed like tiny white roses and topped with crispy shallots. Every legitimate restaurant in town buys them from the third-generation family workshop at **White Rose Restaurant** (533 Hai Ba Trung, open **07:30 to 20:30**, **70,000 VND to 90,000 VND / $2.80 to $3.60 USD** per plate, where you can watch artisans fold dumplings at the front table).
- **Mót Hội An Herbal Tea (150 Tran Phu Street):** A chilled cup of lemongrass, monk fruit, chrysanthemum, honey, and lime herbal infusion garnished with a fresh pink lotus petal for **18,000 VND ($0.72 USD)**.

## Timing Your Visit: Peak Crowds vs. Quiet Mornings

```
DAILY RHYTHM IN THE ANCIENT QUARTER
========================================================================================
[06:00 - 08:30 AM] Quiet Morning Hours : Soft golden light, zero tour buses, local market active
[09:00 - 11:00 AM] Pedestrian Morning  : Motorbikes banned, museums open, pleasant cycling
[11:30 - 14:30 PM] Midday Heat Pause   : 33°C–36°C sun, streets quiet, ideal pool/beach window
[15:00 - 17:30 PM] Afternoon Glow      : Pedestrian zone resumes, second-story cafe photography
[18:00 - 21:00 PM] Peak Night Rush     : Heavy Da Nang tour buses, lantern boats, night market
[21:30 - 23:00 PM] Nightfall Calm      : Day-trippers depart, quiet lantern lanes return
========================================================================================
```

- **The Golden Dawn Window (06:00 to 08:30 AM):** Overnighting in Hoi An pays off at sunrise. Between **06:00 and 08:00**, tour bus crowds are **0%**, temperatures sit at a pleasant **25°C**, and morning light reflects off the mustard-yellow lime-washed walls of Tran Phu and Nguyen Thai Hoc streets. Walk down to **Hoi An Central Market** (east end of Tran Phu) at **06:30** to watch fishing boats unload mackerel and squid, then drink a **35,000 VND ($1.40 USD)** iced Vietnamese drip coffee on the third-floor rooftop terrace of **Faifo Coffee** (130 Tran Phu, opens **07:30**) or **The Espresso Station** (28/2 Tran Hung Dao, **45,000 VND to 65,000 VND / $1.80 to $2.60 USD**).
- **Escaping the 18:00 to 21:00 Crush:** When 45-seat coaches from Da Nang unload at **18:00**, avoid the bottleneck at the Japanese Bridge and Nguyen Hoang Night Market (**300 meters** of lantern and street-food stalls on An Hoi Islet). Instead, cross the **Cam Nam Bridge** (**600 meters** east of the market) to eat riverfront grilled clams (*hen xuc banh da*, **50,000 VND / $2.00 USD**), or walk back into the core after **21:15** when day-trippers have departed and the silk lanterns still glow.

## Surrounding Craft Villages & Bicycle Loops (3 km to 5 km)

Because Hoi An sits on a flat coastal delta, a **30,000 VND to 40,000 VND ($1.20 to $1.60 USD)** full-day bicycle rental (free at 90% of hotels) unlocks three historic rural guilds within **15 minutes**:

1. **Trà Quế Organic Vegetable Village (*Làng rau Trà Quế*, 3.0 km North):** Located halfway between the Old Town and An Bang Beach (**10 minutes** by bicycle). Chemical fertilizers are banned across the commune. Over 200 farming households cultivate 40 varieties of basil, mint, perilla, and coriander, enriching the sandy soil with brackish river seaweed harvested from the De Vong lagoon and watering beds with double shoulder cans. Village entry costs **35,000 VND ($1.40 USD)**, and **3-hour** farm-to-table cooking classes including market tour, buffalo-riding/gardening, and a 4-dish lunch cost **650,000 VND to 950,000 VND ($26.00 to $38.00 USD)**.
2. **Thanh Hà Pottery Village (*Làng gốm Thanh Hà*, 3.0 km West on Duy Tan Street):** Founded in the **16th century** along the Thu Bon River (**12 minutes** by bicycle or **60,000 VND / $2.40 USD** GrabCar). Every vessel is shaped by hand. The **35,000 VND ($1.40 USD)** village ticket includes watching artisans throw red river clay on foot-kicked wheels, a complimentary miniature clay zodiac whistle, and entry to the **Terracotta Park Museum** (**50,000 VND / $2.00 USD** separate museum ticket, open **08:00 to 17:30**).
3. **Cẩm Thanh Bay Mau Coconut Forest (*Rừng dừa Bảy Mẫu*, 4.5 km Southeast):** A **100-hectare** tidal nipa-palm estuary where local fishermen paddle round bamboo basket boats (*thung chai*). Official village gate entry costs **30,000 VND ($1.20 USD)** per person, and a **45-minute** basket boat ride for 2 adults costs **150,000 VND to 200,000 VND ($6.00 to $8.00 USD)** per boat. Ask your rower to take the quiet interior canal creeks rather than the loud karaoke-speaker clearing near the main pier.

## Transportation: Da Nang to Hoi An Connections

Hoi An has no railway station or airport. Every visitor connects through **Da Nang International Airport (DAD)** or **Da Nang Railway Station** (**30 km** north):

### 1. Private Car or GrabCar (Fastest Door-to-Door)
- **Distance & Duration:** **28 km to 32 km** south via Vo Nguyen Giap and Lac Long Quan coastal boulevard; **40 minutes to 50 minutes**.
- **2026 Tariff:** Pre-booked hotel sedans or airport GrabCar rides cost **300,000 VND to 380,000 VND ($12.00 to $15.20 USD)** for a 4-seat car, or **400,000 VND to 480,000 VND ($16.00 to $19.20 USD)** for a 7-seat SUV. Adding a **90-minute** sightseeing stop at Marble Mountains en route costs **500,000 VND to 600,000 VND ($20.00 to $24.00 USD)** total.

### 2. Shared Airport Shuttle Vans & Barter Bus
- **Service:** Operators including **Hoi An Express** and **Barri Ann Travel** run scheduled 16-seat Ford Transit shuttles between Da Nang Airport/My Khe Beach and Hoi An hotels every **60 minutes** from **07:00 to 21:00**.
- **2026 Tariff:** **130,000 VND to 150,000 VND ($5.20 to $6.00 USD)** per seat.

### 3. Intra-Town Electric Buggy (*Xe Điện*) & Cyclo
- **Green Electric Shuttles:** Licensed green 8-to-14-seat electric buggies (*Xe Dien Hoi An*) run from shuttle hubs around the Ancient Town perimeter to An Bang Beach (**4.5 km**) and Cua Dai for **100,000 VND to 150,000 VND ($4.00 to $6.00 USD)** per charter trip (**20,000 VND / $0.80 USD** minimum for short **2 km** hops).
- **Heritage Cyclos (*Xích Lô*):** Blue-uniformed union cyclo drivers stationed near Phan Chu Trinh and Tran Phu offer **30-minute** pedicab loops around the old quarter for a regulated rate of **150,000 VND ($6.00 USD)** per cyclo.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I really need to buy the 120,000 VND ticket just to walk down the street or eat at a restaurant?
If you walk into the Ancient Town after **21:00** for drinks, or enter side streets solely to visit a specific tailor or restaurant, checkpoint attendants rarely stop independent walkers. However, between **07:30 and 21:00**, attendants verify the **120,000 VND ($4.80 USD)** ticket booklet at all **21 historic monuments** (including the Japanese Covered Bridge interior, Tan Ky Old House, and Phuc Kien Assembly Hall) and at the main entry gates on Tran Phu and Nguyen Thi Minh Khai streets. Buying the **$4.80 USD** booklet is well worth the cost and directly funds preservation of the town's **1,107** wooden structures.

### When is the Hoi An Full Moon Lantern Festival held in 2026?
The **Hoi An Lantern Festival (*Đêm Rằm Phố Cổ*)** takes place once every lunar month on the **14th day of the lunar calendar** (the night before the full moon) from **18:00 to 21:30**. During those **3.5 hours**, fluorescent shop signs and streetlamps across the core heritage zone are switched off, leaving the streets lit entirely by silk lanterns and river candles while traditional *Bai Choi* folk games play along the riverbank. Because domestic visitor numbers double on the 14th lunar night, book your hotel at least **2 to 3 weeks** in advance.

### Should I visit Hoi An as a day trip from Da Nang or stay overnight for 2 to 3 nights?
Stay overnight for **at least 2 to 3 nights**. Day-trippers who commute **30 km** from Da Nang arrive during the **16:00 to 20:30** peak window when pedestrian lanes are most crowded and miss the tranquil **06:00 to 08:30** morning hours, the **36-to-48-hour** three-fitting tailoring cycle, and morning bicycle rides through Tra Que and Cam Kim Island. Only do a half-day visit (**15:30 to 21:00**) if your entire Central Vietnam schedule is limited to **2 nights** total.

### How do seasonal autumn floods affect travel in Hoi An?
Between **October 1 and November 20**, tropical depressions dropping **300 mm to 500 mm** of rain into the Quang Nam mountains combine with high tides and upstream hydropower dam releases to push the Thu Bon River over its banks 1 to 3 times per season. Water covers **Bach Dang Street** and **Nguyen Thai Hoc Street** by **0.4 meters to 1.2 meters** for **24 hours to 72 hours**. Streets just **300 meters** north (such as **Ly Thuong Kiet**, **Hai Ba Trung**, and **Tran Hung Dao**) and coastal resorts at **An Bang Beach** (**4.5 km** away) remain high and dry, so book accommodation along those higher streets if visiting in October or November.
