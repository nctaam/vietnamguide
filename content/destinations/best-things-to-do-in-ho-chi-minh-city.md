# Best Things to Do in Ho Chi Minh City (2026 Guide)

Ho Chi Minh City—still affectionately and colloquially known as Saigon—is the commercial powerhouse, cultural laboratory, and economic engine of modern Vietnam. Home to ten million residents and more than eight million motorbikes, this sprawling southern metropolis hums with relentless entrepreneurial vitality. Here, French colonial brick edifices sit under the glass shadows of modern skyscrapers, ancient Taoist smoke drifts through bustling alley markets, and a burgeoning craft-beer and specialty coffee culture thrives in repurposed apartment blocks.

Because Saigon lacks the compact pedestrian scale of Hanoi's Old Quarter or Hoi An, planning your days around geographic clusters and traffic patterns is paramount. Haphazard itineraries waste hours trapped in rush-hour traffic jams along Cong Hoa or Nam Ky Khoi Nghia streets.

This guide provides an uncompromising, highly curated filter of the premier experiences in Ho Chi Minh City. We evaluate monumental war history museums, historic Chinatown alleys, elevated skyline viewing decks, and river transit routes, complete with concrete admission tariffs and scheduling strategies for 2026.

## 1. Confront Modern History at the War Remnants Museum

Located in District 3 on Vo Van Tan Street, the **War Remnants Museum (Bao tang Chung tich Chien tranh)** is Vietnam's most visited and emotionally demanding historical institution:
- **Admission Tariff:** **60,000 VND per adult**; 30,000 VND for seniors (60+); children under six enter free. Open daily 07:30 AM to 17:30 PM.
- **Outdoor Heavy Armour:** The front courtyard displays captured American military hardware: an M48 Patton tank, an A-37 Dragonfly attack jet, a UH-1 "Huey" helicopter, and an unexploded 15,000-pound BLU-82 "Daisy Cutter" bomb.
- **The Requiem Exhibition:** Curated by veteran war photographer Tim Page, this gallery displays the work of 134 photographers from eleven nations who died covering combat across Indochina. Moving collections from Robert Capa, Larry Burrows, and Henri Huet capture the raw human tragedy of the conflict without political propaganda.
- **Agent Orange Legacy:** Upper-floor galleries document the ecological and intergenerational physiological devastation caused by seventy-five million liters of defoliant chemicals sprayed across the Vietnamese countryside between 1961 and 1971.
- **The "Tiger Cages":** A recreated outdoor prison wing showcases the confinement cells and torture devices utilized on political prisoners at Con Dao Island by French colonial and South Vietnamese regimes.

> [!NOTE]
> The museum offers an unvarnished, intensely graphic presentation of wartime suffering. Give yourself at least two to two and a half hours, and consider scheduling a quiet coffee break afterward to process the heavy emotional weight of the exhibits.

## 2. Walk Through Political History at Independence Palace (Reunification Palace)

Occupying twelve hectares of manicured parkland in District 1, the **Independence Palace (Dinh Doc Lap)** stands as a monumental masterpiece of Vietnamese modernist architecture designed by French-trained architect Ngo Viet Thu:
- **Historical Significance:** This served as the official working residence of South Vietnamese President Nguyen Van Thieu during the Vietnam War. On the morning of April 30, 1975, North Vietnamese Army tank #390 crashed through the main iron gates, ending thirty years of civil conflict and bringing about national reunification.
- **Architectural Preservation:** The interior halls remain preserved exactly as they appeared on April 30, 1975. Highlights include the Grand Banquet Hall, the National Security Council Chamber, and the private presidential living quarters with interior courtyards.
- **Underground War Command Bunker:** Descending beneath the palace brings you into a reinforced subterranean bunker capable of withstanding heavy artillery bombardment. Concrete corridors connect top-secret communications rooms, map tables, radio transmitters, and President Thieu's emergency bedroom.
- **Admission Tariffs:** **40,000 VND per adult** for general palace entry; an expanded combo ticket for **80,000 VND** includes entry to the historical Norodom Palace exhibition on the grounds.

## 3. Explore French Colonial Heritage in Central District 1

During the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, French colonial planners shaped central Saigon into the "Paris of the East." An easy two-hour walking loop connects four iconic landmarks within a four-block radius:

### Saigon Central Post Office (Buu Dien Trung Tam Sai Gon)
Completed in 1891 under chief architect Alfred Foulhoux with structural ironwork designed under Gustave Eiffel's engineering office:
- The interior features a majestic barrel-vaulted ceiling supported by wrought-iron pillars.
- Hand-painted historical cartography maps on the walls depict telegraph lines across Southern Vietnam and Cambodia in 1892.
- The post office remains fully functional; you can buy commemorative postage stamps, write postcards at antique wooden writing desks, and mail them overseas from the central counters. Entrance is completely **free of charge**.

### Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon (Nha Tho Duc Ba)
Constructed between 1863 and 1880 using red bricks shipped directly from Marseille:
- Two 58-meter-tall square bell towers anchor Paris Square directly facing the Post Office.
- *Restoration Update:* The cathedral is currently undergoing a comprehensive structural renovation extending through 2027. While interior access is closed to tourists, the majestic exterior brick facade and surrounding plaza remain an essential stop.

### Saigon Opera House & The Municipal Theatre
Built in 1898 in the French Third Republic architectural style on Dong Khoi Boulevard:
- Today, it hosts the acclaimed **A O Show** by Lune Production—a spectacular contemporary bamboo circus performance depicting Vietnamese rural-to-urban transition through acrobats, bamboo props, and live folk music (tickets from 800,000 to 1,400,000 VND).

## 4. Uncover Heritage and Medicine Streets in Cho Lon (District 5 Chinatown)

Established in the late eighteenth century by Ming loyalist Chinese merchants fleeing dynastic upheavals, **Cho Lon** ("Big Market") is Vietnam's largest Chinatown:

### Ba Thien Hau Pagoda (Chua Ba Thien Hau)
Erected in 1760 by the Cantonese merchant congregation on Nguyen Trai Street:
- Dedicated to Mazu (Thien Hau), the Taoist goddess of the sea who protects sailors, navigators, and fishermen crossing the East Sea.
- The open-air central courtyard is illuminated by giant coiled incense spirals suspended from the timber roof beams, burning slowly for weeks carrying personal prayers.
- Elaborate porcelain dioramas depicting Chinese operatic legends and mythical dragons line the roof ridges. Entrance is **free of charge**.

### Binh Tay Market & Medicinal Herb Alleys
- **Binh Tay Market (Chợ Bình Tây):** A colossal two-story wholesale market rebuilt in 1928 with funding from Chinese merchant Quach Dam. Its yellow-roofed courtyard features hundreds of wholesale stalls selling textiles, dried seafood, spices, and copper cookware.
- **Hai Thuong Lan Ong Street:** The sensory heart of traditional Chinese medicine (*Thuoc bac*). The street is perfumed with aromas of drying star anise, cinnamon bark, ginseng roots, and lotus seeds, where master herbalists measure ancient remedies on brass balance scales.

## 5. Sunset Navigation on the Saigon Waterbus

For an economical and highly scenic perspective of the skyline, skip expensive commercial dinner party boats and board the municipal **Saigon Waterbus**:
- **Route:** The public river ferry departs from **Bach Dang Water Terminal** on Ton Duc Thang Street in central District 1, cruising north along the winding Saigon River to **Binh An Pier** in Thu Duc City (and onward to Linh Dong).
- **Admission Tariff:** Exactly **15,000 VND per person each way** (approximately $0.60 USD).
- **The Experience:** Boarding a 17:00 or 17:30 PM departure allows you to watch the golden sunset reflect off the glass facades of Bitexco Financial Tower and Landmark 81. Step off at Binh An Pier, enjoy a cold coconut or local iced coffee at a riverfront cafe overlooking the water, and catch a return ferry back to District 1 after dusk as the city illuminates.

## 7. Craft Beer and Sidewalk Alleys: Saigon's Night Rhythms

Ho Chi Minh City is the craft-beer brewing capital of Southeast Asia:
- **Pioneering Ateliers:** Visit the taproom of **Pasteur Street Brewing Company** (144/7 Pasteur Street), which pioneered integrating Vietnamese ingredients like jasmine flower, Phu Quoc black pepper, and Marou chocolate into American-style IPAs.
- **Heart of Darkness Brewery:** Located on Dong Du Street, featuring thirty rotating taps of bold stouts and hazy IPAs.
- **Bui Vien Walking Street Reality:** The famed backpacker street in Pham Ngu Lao becomes a sensory blitz of ear-splitting techno music, flashing neon signs, and street performers after 19:00 PM. While entertaining for a chaotic beer on a low plastic stool, travelers seeking sophisticated dining should explore the hidden cafe apartments at **42 Nguyen Hue** or the local craft-beer alleys in District 3.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Activities in Ho Chi Minh City

### How many days should I budget for Ho Chi Minh City?
We recommend dedicating **two to three full days**. Two days allow you to comfortably cover the essential historical and cultural core (War Remnants Museum, Independence Palace, Post Office, and Cho Lon), while a third day permits a half-day excursion to the Cu Chi Tunnels or an afternoon boat tour into the nearby mangrove biosphere of Can Gio.

### What is the best way to get around the city between attractions?
Use the **Grab** smartphone application for transparent, metered transport without language barriers. GrabCar provides air-conditioned sedans, while GrabBike offers quick, low-cost transport for solo travelers wishing to navigate urban traffic. Traditional metered taxis are reliable if you stick exclusively to **Vinasun** (white cars with red/green stripes) or **Mai Linh** (bright green cars); ensure the driver turns on the digital meter upon departure.

### Are the Cu Chi Tunnels worth the half-day trip?
Yes, but choose your site deliberately. The **Ben Duoc tunnels** (seventy kilometers northwest) offer an authentic historical exploration with fewer tourists and original tunnel corridors, whereas **Ben Dinh** (fifty kilometers) is heavily commercialized and crowded with tour bus convoys. Private tours or speedboat transfers along the Saigon River provide a far superior experience compared to cramped budget tour buses.

### Is street food in Saigon safe for international visitors?
Street food in Saigon is exceptionally vibrant and generally very safe if you patronize bustling stalls with high local turnover. Look for vendors cooking on open gas burners or charcoal grills where raw ingredients are replenished continuously. Stick to hot, freshly prepared dishes like *Com tam* (broken rice with grilled pork chop) and *Banh mi*, and avoid raw leafy salads or unpeeled fruits from low-turnover street carts.
