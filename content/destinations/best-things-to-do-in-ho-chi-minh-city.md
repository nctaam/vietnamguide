# Best Things to Do in Ho Chi Minh City (2026 Guide)

Ho Chi Minh City—still colloquially known across Vietnam as Saigon—functions as the commercial engine and southern gateway of the country. Home to more than 9.5 million residents and roughly 8.5 million registered motorbikes spread across 2,095 square kilometers, this dense delta hub demands deliberate geographic clustering. Unlike compact walking towns such as Hoi An, Saigon stretches across interconnected urban districts where unscheduled cross-town taxi rides during the 07:30 to 09:00 or 16:30 to 19:00 rush hours can easily consume 60 minutes over just 5 km of road along Đường Nam Kỳ Khởi Nghĩa or Đường Cộng Hòa.

Planning your days around tight neighborhood zones and the newly operational Metro Line 1 saves hours of gridlock. This guide evaluates the highest-value museums, heritage quarters, river ferries, skyline viewpoints, and half-day excursions in Ho Chi Minh City, complete with verified 2026 admission tariffs in VND, exact operating windows, and transit durations.

## Quick Verdict: Prioritizing Your Time in Saigon

If you have 48 hours to 72 hours in the city, anchor your schedule around three distinct operational blocks rather than crisscrossing the urban core at random:

- **Best Half-Day Historical Pair (3.5 hours to 4 hours):** Start at the **War Remnants Museum** (**60,000 VND**) at 07:30 before tour buses arrive, then walk 850 meters (10 minutes) east along Đường Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai to **Independence Palace** (**40,000 VND** to **80,000 VND**).
- **Best Cultural & Market Walk (3 hours):** Spend a morning in **Chợ Lớn (District 5)** exploring **Bà Thiên Hậu Pagoda** (**0 VND**), the herbalist shophouses on **Đường Hải Thượng Lãn Ông**, and **Bình Tây Market** (15 minutes or 5.5 km southwest of District 1 by GrabCar for **65,000 VND** to **85,000 VND**).
- **Best Golden-Hour Transit Experience (1.5 hours):** Skip overpriced **600,000 VND** tourist dinner boats and board the public **Saigon Waterbus** at **Bến Bạch Đằng** (**15,000 VND** one-way, 15 minutes to Bến Bình An), or ride **Metro Line 1** underground from **Ga Bến Thành** out to the elevated Saigon River crossing at **Ga Tân Cảng** (**12,000 VND**, 12 minutes).
- **Best Half-Day Excursion Outside the Core (5 hours to 6 hours):** Drive 70 km northwest along National Route QL22 (90 minutes to 110 minutes each way) to the **Bến Dược sector of the Củ Chi Tunnels** (**135,000 VND** foreign entry including site guide), avoiding the crowded bus traps at Bến Đình (50 km).

> [!NOTE]
> Central District 1 and District 3 heat peaks between 12:00 and 15:00, when asphalt temperatures regularly reach 34°C to 37°C (93°F to 99°F). Schedule outdoor colonial walks, open-air rooftop terraces, and Chợ Lớn market alleys before 10:30 or after 16:00, reserving air-conditioned museum galleries and specialty coffee apartments for midday.

## 1. Confront Modern History at the War Remnants Museum & Independence Palace

### War Remnants Museum (Bảo tàng Chứng tích Chiến tranh)
Located at 28 Đường Võ Văn Tần in Ward Võ Thị Sáu, District 3, just 1.2 km (15 minutes on foot) northwest of Bến Thành Market, the War Remnants Museum is Vietnam's most heavily visited historical institution:
- **2026 Tariff & Hours:** Standard adult admission is **60,000 VND** ($2.40) following the municipal tariff update effective October 1, 2026. Seniors aged 60 and above, disabled visitors, and students qualify for a 50% concession rate of **30,000 VND** ($1.20), while children under 6 years old enter free. Audio guides rent for **120,000 VND** ($4.80). Doors open daily from 07:30 to 17:30 (ticket booth closes at 17:00).
- **Courtyard Armor Collection:** The walled forecourt displays captured U.S. military hardware from the pre-1975 conflict, including an M48A3 Patton tank, an A-37B Dragonfly light attack jet, a UH-1H "Huey" utility helicopter, a Chinook CH-47, and an unexploded 6,800-kilogram (15,000-pound) BLU-82 "Daisy Cutter" concussive bomb.
- **Interior Photography Galleries:** Climb to the top floor first and work downward. The *Requiem* exhibition, co-curated by combat photojournalist Tim Page and Horst Faas, presents 250 photographs taken by 134 journalists from 11 countries killed between 1945 and 1975 across Indochina, including works by Robert Capa, Larry Burrows, and Henri Huet. Adjacent galleries document the long-term ecological and human impact of 75 million liters of dioxin-laced herbicides (Agent Orange) sprayed across southern Vietnam between 1961 and 1971.
- **Con Dao "Tiger Cages" Replica:** An outdoor ground-floor annex reconstructs the stone-walled solitary confinement cells and overhead iron grating used at Côn Đảo Island prison. Allow 120 minutes to 150 minutes for a thorough visit.

### Independence Palace (Dinh Độc Lập / Reunification Palace)
Set within a 12-hectare (120,000-square-meter) wooded park at 135 Đường Nam Kỳ Khởi Nghĩa, District 1, just 850 meters (10 minutes walking) from the War Remnants Museum:
- **2026 Tariff & Hours:** Entry to the main palace building costs **40,000 VND** ($1.60) per adult, or **80,000 VND** ($3.20) for the comprehensive combo ticket that includes the separate two-story historical exhibition on colonial Norodom Palace (1868–1962). Seniors (60+) and university students pay **40,000 VND** for the combo, and children aged 6 to 17 pay **20,000 VND**. An optional internal electric buggy shuttle across the lawn costs **25,000 VND** per passenger. Open daily from 08:00 to 15:30.
- **Architecture & Command Bunker:** Designed by Prix de Rome laureate Ngô Viết Thụ and completed in 1966, the modernist structure preserves the exact cabinet rooms, diplomatic reception halls, and rooftop helipad where North Vietnamese tank #390 breached the wrought-iron gates at 10:45 on April 30, 1975. Descend 2.5 meters below ground to inspect the reinforced steel-and-concrete war command bunker housing vintage rotary field telephones, tactical cartography rooms, and radio transmitters.

## 2. French Colonial Core, Cafe Apartments & Chợ Lớn Heritage

### Central District 1 Colonial Loop (1.5 km / 90 minutes)
From the front gate of Independence Palace, walk 400 meters (5 minutes) northeast through the shaded mahogany trees of 30/4 Park to reach Công xã Paris (Paris Commune Square):
- **Saigon Central Post Office (2 Công xã Paris):** Built between 1886 and 1891 under French architect Alfred Foulhoux, the ochre-yellow hall features a vaulted green iron framework, two hand-painted 1892 telegraph maps of Saigon and Southern Vietnam, and wooden telephone booths converted into ATM alcoves. Entry is **0 VND**, and mailing an international postcard costs **35,000 VND** to **45,000 VND** in stamps. Open 07:30 to 18:00 Monday–Saturday (08:00 to 17:00 Sunday).
- **Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica (1 Công xã Paris):** Erected between 1863 and 1880 with Marseille red bricks and twin 58-meter bell towers. Comprehensive scaffolding and roof restoration continue through 2027; interior entry remains closed, but exterior viewing from the square takes 15 minutes.
- **Đường sách Nguyễn Văn Bình (Book Street) & Opera House:** Walk 100 meters along the pedestrian-only book lane next to the Post Office, then continue 600 meters (8 minutes) south down Đường Đồng Khởi to the 1898 **Saigon Opera House (7 Công Trường Lam Sơn)**. Evening performances of the 60-minute **A O Show** bamboo acrobatics production run **800,000 VND** (aah! zone), **1,250,000 VND** (ooh! zone), and **1,750,000 VND** (wow! zone).
- **The Cafe Apartments (42 Đường Nguyễn Huệ):** Walk 300 meters (4 minutes) south from the Opera House onto the 670-meter-long, 64-meter-wide granite pedestrian plaza of Nguyễn Huệ Boulevard. At #42, a nine-story 1960s residential block houses over 30 independent coffee roasters, matcha bars, and boutiques. Taking the tiny central elevator costs **3,000 VND** (refunded by most cafes upon ordering a **55,000 VND** to **95,000 VND** pour-over or iced coconut coffee), or you can climb the concrete spiral stairwell for free.

### Chợ Lớn (District 5 & District 6 Chinatown)
Located 5.5 km (15 minutes to 20 minutes by GrabCar for **65,000 VND** to **85,000 VND**) southwest of District 1, Chợ Lớn was founded in 1778 by Teochew, Cantonese, and Hokkien merchants:
- **Bà Thiên Hậu Pagoda (710 Đường Nguyễn Trãi, District 5):** Built around 1760 by the Cantonese congregation (Hội quán Tuệ Thành) to honor Mazu, goddess of seafarers. Admission is **0 VND** (06:00 to 17:30), while purchasing a large conical incense coil to hang from the courtyard rafters costs **20,000 VND** to **50,000 VND**. Examine the 19th-century Shiwan ceramic friezes lining the roof eaves.
- **Đường Hải Thượng Lãn Ông & Bình Tây Market (57A Đường Tháp Mười, District 6):** Walk 1.1 km (14 minutes) west from the pagoda along Đường Hải Thượng Lãn Ông, where five blocks of traditional Chinese medicine (Thuốc Bắc) wholesalers weigh dried ginseng, star anise, and cinnamon bark on brass scales. Continue to **Chợ Bình Tây**, a 1928 wholesale market built by merchant Quách Đàm featuring a central clock tower and lotus courtyard (06:00 to 18:00, free entry).

## 3. Skyline Views, River Transit & Cu Chi Tunnels

### Saigon Waterbus vs. Metro Line 1
- **Saigon Waterbus (Bến Bạch Đằng, 10B Đường Tôn Đức Thắng, District 1):** Public yellow river ferries depart from central District 1 across the Saigon River to **Bến Bình An** (4.5 km, 15 minutes), **Bến Thanh Đa** (25 minutes), and **Bến Linh Đông** (10.8 km, 52 minutes). Fares are fixed at **15,000 VND** ($0.60) per trip regardless of distance. Book the 16:30 or 17:00 departure to Bình An (**30,000 VND** round-trip) at the pier ticket counter 45 minutes in advance to watch sunset illuminate the Bitexco and Landmark 81 towers.
- **HCMC Metro Line 1 (Ga Bến Thành to Ga Suối Tiên):** The 19.7 km urban rail line links 3 underground stations in District 1 (Ga Bến Thành, Ga Nhà hát Thành phố / Opera House, and Ga Ba Son) with 11 elevated stations running through Bình Thạnh and Thủ Đức City in 30 minutes. Single-journey fares cost **7,000 VND** to **20,000 VND** with cash (**6,000 VND** to **19,000 VND** contactless EMV card), while unlimited **1-Day Passes cost 40,000 VND** ($1.60) and **3-Day Passes cost 90,000 VND** ($3.60). Trains run every 8 minutes to 12 minutes from 05:00 to 22:00.

### Elevated Observation Decks vs. Craft Beer Taprooms
- **Saigon Skydeck at Bitexco Financial Tower (36 Đường Hồ Tùng Mậu, District 1):** Positioned on Floor 49 at 178 meters above street level inside the 262-meter lotus-inspired tower (09:30 to 21:30). Tickets cost **240,000 VND** ($9.60) per adult and **160,000 VND** for children (4–12) or seniors (65+). One floor above on Floor 50 (182 meters), EON51 Café serves drinks from **130,000 VND** to **250,000 VND** with the same 360-degree view and zero elevator fee.
- **Landmark 81 SkyView (720A Đường Điện Biên Phủ, Bình Thạnh District, reachable via Metro Line 1 Ga Tân Cảng plus a 600-meter walk):** Spanning Floors 79 to 81 (382 meters to 461.2 meters total spire height), tickets cost **420,000 VND** ($16.80) Monday–Friday and **500,000 VND** ($20.00) Saturday–Sunday (09:30 to 22:00).
- **Artisanal Craft Breweries:** Unwind in the evening at **Pasteur Street Brewing Company** (144/7 Đường Pasteur, District 1) or **Heart of Darkness** (31D Đường Lý Tự Trọng, District 1), where 330 ml to 400 ml pours of passionfruit wheat ale or Phu Quoc black pepper IPA run **95,000 VND** to **165,000 VND** ($3.80 to $6.60).

## On-the-Ground Pro-Tips for Ho Chi Minh City

> [!TIP]
> **Avoid the Coconut Seller & Shoe-Shine Scams Around Công xã Paris:** Around Turtle Lake (Hồ Con Rùa), Independence Palace, and Notre-Dame Square, roving vendors carrying shoulder poles may place them on your shoulders for a photo and immediately demand **200,000 VND** to **500,000 VND** ($8.00 to $20.00) for a single coconut. Similarly, decline aggressive street shoe-shiners who squirt glue onto your sneakers and demand **300,000 VND**. Keep moving and say a firm *"Không, cảm ơn"* (No, thank you).

- **Choose Bến Dược Over Bến Đình at Củ Chi:** The Củ Chi Tunnels operate two separate gates 18 km apart. **Bến Đình** (50 km from District 1) receives 85% of mass-market bus tours, resulting in 30-minute queues to crawl through widened concrete tubes alongside noisy AK-47 shooting ranges (**60,000 VND** per bullet, minimum 10 rounds = **600,000 VND**). **Bến Dược** (70 km from District 1, **135,000 VND** entry including the **35,000 VND** ticket and **100,000 VND** mandatory site guide fee) preserves original narrower Viet Cong command bunkers in quiet forest shade. Compare both sites in our [Bến Dược vs Bến Đình Củ Chi guide](/compare/cu-chi-tunnels-ben-duoc-vs-ben-dinh).
- **Beat Saigon Waterbus Sellouts:** Because municipal ferries seat only 66 passengers at **15,000 VND**, sunset departures between 16:30 and 17:30 sell out rapidly on weekends. Book online via saigonwaterbus.com a day ahead or buy both outbound and return tickets simultaneously at the Bến Bạch Đằng booth before 15:30.
- **Cross Streets with Steady Pace:** When crossing multi-lane boulevards like Đường Lê Lợi or Đường Cách Mạng Tháng Tám, step off the curb when cars have passed and walk at a slow, predictable 3 km/h pace. Never sprint or step backward abruptly; oncoming motorbike riders calculate their trajectory around your steady forward motion.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many days do you need in Ho Chi Minh City?
Dedicate **2 full days to 3 full days**. Two full days (48 hours) cover the District 1 and District 3 historical core, Chợ Lớn's pagodas, the Saigon Waterbus, and evening street food alleys without rushing. Adding a third day unlocks either a 6-hour morning excursion to the Bến Dược Củ Chi Tunnels (70 km) or a 9-hour day trip south to the coconut canals of Bến Tre in the Mekong Delta (85 km via CT01 Expressway).

### Is Metro Line 1 useful for tourists in 2026?
Yes. Metro Line 1 runs 19.7 km from **Ga Bến Thành** in central District 1 through **Ga Nhà hát Thành phố** (Opera House) and **Ga Ba Son**, then rises onto an elevated viaduct crossing the Saigon River to **Ga Tân Cảng** (for Landmark 81) and **Ga Thảo Điền** (for District 2 expat dining and specialty cafes) in just 12 minutes to 15 minutes for **8,000 VND** to **12,000 VND**, bypassing surface bridge bottlenecks completely.

### Where are the best evening street food alleys in Saigon?
Skip the inflated tourist stalls immediately outside Bến Thành Market after 19:00 and head 3.2 km (10 minutes by GrabCar for **45,000 VND**) west to **Hồ Thị Kỷ Food Street** in District 10 (17:00 to 23:00), or cross the Calmette Bridge (1.5 km) into **Đường Vĩnh Khánh** in District 4 for charcoal-grilled sea snails (Ốc) priced at **50,000 VND** to **90,000 VND** per plate.
