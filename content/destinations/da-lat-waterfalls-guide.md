# Da Lat Waterfalls Guide: Top 5 Falls, Routes & Map (2026)

Perched on the Lang Biang Plateau at an altitude of 1,500 meters above sea level in Vietnam's Central Highlands, the provincial capital of **Da Lat** is framed by deep pine valleys, volcanic basalt plateaus, and dramatic geological fault lines. As mountain runoff drains toward the lowlands of Dong Nai and Binh Thuan, rivers plummet over sheer rock ledges, creating Southeast Asia's highest concentration of accessible, world-class waterfalls.

However, Da Lat's cascades vary drastically in both geography and commercial character. While some—such as Datanla—have been engineered into commercial adventure hubs equipped with 2,400-meter alpine bobsled coasters and guided canyoning drops, others—like the seven-tiered terraced amphitheater of Pongour or the thundering abyss of Elephant Falls—remain wild, raw natural spectacles situated far out in surrounding agricultural districts.

Choosing which cascades to prioritize, coordinating motorcycle routes, and understanding seasonal flow dynamics can determine whether your highland excursion feels like a tourist conveyor belt or an epic mountain adventure. ## 1. Datanla Waterfall: High-Thrill Alpine Coasters & Canyoning

Situated just 5 kilometers south of central Da Lat along National Highway QL20, **Datanla Waterfall** is the most visited, accessible, and commercially developed cascade in the Central Highlands.

Hidden within a steep pine canyon, the falls drop over a series of granite ledges into deep, cool pools. While walking paths wind down the canyon floor, the vast majority of visitors descend via the **Datanla Alpine Coaster 3**. Spanning an audited length of 2,400 meters, this gravity-driven, tracked bobsled system winds through dense pine forest down to the base of the waterfall, reaching speeds of up to 40 kilometers per hour with manual handbrakes allowing riders to control their velocity. A round-trip coaster ticket costs 250,000 VND (~$10.00 USD) for adults.

### Professional Canyoning & Abseiling
Datanla is also Southeast Asia's premier hub for technical canyoning expeditions. Guided exclusively by certified adventure operators (such as Viet Challenge and Highland Sport), full-day canyoning programs cost between 1,800,000 and 2,200,000 VND ($75 to $90 USD) per person, inclusive of professional European-standard harnesses, wetsuits, helmets, and English-speaking guides.

The standard canyoning course features:
- A 25-meter dry cliff abseil for initial technical orientation.
- Natural water slides where participants slide backward down water-smoothed granite chutes into deep rock pools.
- A technical 14-meter vertical descent straight down the face of a thundering waterfall, negotiating torrents of water before releasing into the plunge pool.
- A heart-pounding 7-to-11-meter cliff jump into a deep canyon basin.

> [!NOTE]
> Canyoning at Datanla involves genuine physical risk. Always book through established, licensed operators who provide certified international safety equipment (UIAA/CE-rated harnesses and carabiners). Never participate in unguided or makeshift canyoning activities organized by unlicensed freelance touts.

## 2. Pongour Waterfall: The Seven-Tiered Basalt Amphitheater

Located 50 kilometers south of Da Lat in Tan Thanh commune, Duc Trong district, **Pongour Waterfall** (*Thác Pongour*) is widely acclaimed as one of Vietnam's most photogenic geological wonders.

Referred to in local lore as the "Seven-Tiered Waterfall" (*Thác Bảy Tầng*), Pongour is fed by the Da Nhim River basin. The geological formation spans roughly 100 meters in width, cascading 40 meters downward across seven distinct, stepped basalt rock terraces formed by prehistoric volcanic flows. The horizontal stone ledges break the falling water into dozens of braided white ribbons, creating a natural amphitheater of mist and stone.

### Access and Walking Descent
From the main ticket pavilion (admission 40,000 VND), visitors navigate a shaded 15-minute downhill walk along stone pathways lined with jackfruit and mango trees to reach the riverbed. For travelers with limited mobility, open-air electric carts shuttle passengers up and down the paved road for 20,000 VND each way.

At the base of the falls, massive polished shale and basalt boulders allow visitors to walk close to the cascades for panoramic photography. While swimming directly beneath the heavy central torrent is prohibited due to underwater currents and submerged rocks, wading along the shallow peripheral rock pools is a popular way to cool off during warm midday hours.

Seasonal fluctuations alter the site dramatically. Between July and November, monsoon runoff fuels a deafening torrent across the entire 100-meter volcanic ledge. Conversely, the dry months from January through April reveal intricate geometric basalt formations flanking serene jade channels.

## 3. Elephant Falls: Roaring Power & The Wind Cave

Positioned roughly 30 kilometers southwest of Da Lat near the bustling agricultural town of Nam Ban in Lam Ha district, **Elephant Falls** (*Thác Voi*, known in the indigenous K'Ho language as *Liêng Rơwoa*) delivers an uncompromising display of untamed hydrological force.

Here, the Cam Ly stream drops 30 meters over a sheer granite precipice spanning over 15 meters wide. Indigenous legends associate the site's name with an array of massive, rounded basalt boulders resting at the foot of the gorge that resemble the broad backs of bathing wild elephants.

### Navigating the Steep Descent & Wind Cave (*Hang Gió*)
Admission costs a modest 30,000 VND per person. Reaching the base requires descending a crude, steep staircase carved directly into natural rock fissures, wet with perpetual spray and bordered by rusty iron handrails. Sturdy athletic footwear with reliable rubber grip is mandatory; navigating these slippery steps in flip-flops or smooth-soled shoes invites injury.

Halfway down the canyon trail, an unmarked opening leads into the **Wind Cave** (*Hang Gió*). Travelers squeeze through a narrow, dark rock crevice where roaring subterranean drafts and icy mist blast outward from behind the falling wall of water. The sensory impact inside the cave—deafening noise, swirling spray, and vibrating bedrock—delivers an unforgettable encounter with the sheer power of the mountain river.

Directly adjacent to the waterfall entrance sits **Linh Ẩn Pagoda** (*Chùa Linh Ẩn*), home to a monumental 71-meter outdoor statue of the Bodhisattva of Compassion (Lady Buddha), the tallest in the Central Highlands, offering commanding views across the surrounding coffee plantations.

## 4. Prenn Waterfall: Family Strolls Behind the Curtain

Located 10 kilometers south of Da Lat right at the base of Prenn Pass, **Prenn Waterfall** represents a gentler, heritage-oriented attraction that has welcomed travelers since French colonial times.

The waterfall drops roughly 10 meters over a wide, curved sandstone overhang, forming a continuous curtain of white water that empties into a quiet mountain pool surrounded by manicured flower gardens and pine woodland. The defining highlight of Prenn is a concrete footbridge built directly into the cavernous recess behind the falling water sheet, allowing visitors to walk behind the falls without getting drenched.

Admission is 50,000 VND per adult. The surrounding botanical park features an overhead cable car line crossing the canyon (50,000 VND), rowboat rentals, and traditional arch bridges. Because of its gentle walking paths, Prenn is exceptionally well-suited for families with young children or elderly travelers who cannot navigate the steep, slippery rocks of Elephant or Pongour falls.

## 5. Dambri Waterfall: The 60-Meter Giant of Bao Loc

For travelers journeying between Da Lat and Ho Chi Minh City along National Highway QL20, **Dambri Waterfall** (*Thác Dambri*) justifies a dedicated detour. Located 130 kilometers southwest of Da Lat in Bao Loc city, Dambri is the highest, most voluminous vertical waterfall in Lam Dong province.

The cascade plunges an unbroken 60 meters straight down a near-vertical cliff into an ancient volcanic gorge surrounded by 300 hectares of primary subtropical rainforest. The sound of the falling water can be heard more than a kilometer away through the jungle canopy.

Admission is 200,000 VND per adult, which functions as an all-inclusive pass covering access to a modern glass elevator descending the cliff face, panoramic suspension footbridges spanning the gorge, and a thrilling 1,750-meter alpine coaster that swoops down through the rainforest toward the lower Dasara cascade.

Motorcyclists should exercise acute caution when traversing mountain passes like **Tà Nung Pass** and **Prenn Pass**. The asphalt can become slick following afternoon highland drizzles, and large interprovincial sleeper buses frequently overtake aggressively around blind hairpin bends. Ensure your rented scooter has responsive front and rear brakes, sound tires with deep tread, and a working horn.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best waterfall to visit in Da Lat if you only have time for one?
If you seek high-adrenaline adventure and family-friendly attractions close to town, choose **Datanla Waterfall** for its 2,400-meter alpine coaster and world-class canyoning drops. If you want raw, cinematic natural beauty and sweeping scale, prioritize **Pongour Waterfall**, widely regarded as the most majestic seven-tiered terraced cascade in the Central Highlands despite its 50-kilometer distance from the city.

### Is canyoning at Datanla Waterfall safe for beginners?
Yes, provided you book with a reputable, licensed adventure company with certified guides. Operators provide comprehensive ground training on a 25-meter dry cliff where participants learn harness control, rope feeding, and braking techniques before descending active waterfalls. You do not need prior mountaineering experience, but you must be in good physical health and comfortable swimming in deep water.

### Can you swim at Pongour and Elephant Falls?
Swimming directly beneath the main plunge pools is strictly prohibited and extremely hazardous at both Elephant Falls and Pongour Falls due to powerful undertows, high water impact force, and submerged jagged boulders. However, during the dry season, visitors can wade safely in shallow peripheral rock pools and streams along the riverbed at Pongour.

### What is the best season to photograph Da Lat's waterfalls?
The best window for photography is from **September through November** (the transition from rainy to dry season). During these months, river discharge remains high, resulting in thunderous, wide cascades, while rainfall tapers off, providing clear blue skies and optimal sunlight without the danger of flash floods.

### How much does it cost to hire a private taxi for a full-day waterfall tour?
Hiring a private 4-seat or 7-seat taxi (such as Lado Taxi or Mai Linh) for an 8-hour private charter covering Datanla, Elephant Falls, and Pongour Waterfall typically costs between 900,000 and 1,300,000 VND ($36 to $52 USD), inclusive of driver waiting time and highway tolls. Splitting this cost among 2 to 4 travelers makes it an exceptionally comfortable and cost-effective alternative to renting motorbikes.
