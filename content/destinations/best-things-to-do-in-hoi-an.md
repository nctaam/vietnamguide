# Best Things to Do in Hoi An: What Is Actually Worth It

Between the fifteenth and nineteenth centuries, Hoi An flourished as Faifo, one of Southeast Asia's busiest commercial trading ports. Merchant vessels from Japan, China, Portugal, the Netherlands, and India dropped anchor along the estuary of the Thu Bon River, exchanging spices, silk, fine porcelain, and medicinal herbs. Because river siltation shifted maritime shipping north to Da Nang in the late nineteenth century, Hoi An escaped modern urban redevelopment, preserving an exceptionally intact architectural street grid of more than eight hundred historic timber buildings.

Today, this UNESCO-listed town attracts millions of annual visitors. The sheer volume of street stalls, lantern shops, tailor counters, and organized tours can easily overwhelm newcomers. Some experiences offer deep cultural and sensory satisfaction, while others border on commercial contrivance. This guide rigorously evaluates the top activities in and around Hoi An—providing concrete logistics, ticket regulations, fair price benchmarks, and timing advice to ensure your itinerary delivers genuine substance.

## 1. Explore Ancient Town Architecture via the Unified Heritage Ticket

Walking through the car-free alleys of the Ancient Town requires an official **Hoi An Heritage Sightseeing Ticket**. 

### Ticketing Mechanics and Site Selection
Sold at eleven municipal ticket kiosks situated around the perimeter of the pedestrian zone, the ticket costs **120,000 VND** per international visitor (children under sixteen enter free). Each ticket booklet contains five tear-off coupons valid for five individual heritage sites chosen from four distinct categories:

| Heritage Category | Recommended Historic Sites to Prioritize | Architectural Details & Highlights |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Merchant Residences** | Tan Ky House or Phung Hung Old House | Two-century-old timber residences with carved roof brackets and flood markers |
| **Chinese Assembly Halls** | Fujian (Phuc Kien) or Cantonese Assembly Hall | Ornate ceramic dragons, stone courtyards, and giant coiled incense spirals |
| **Communal Monuments** | Japanese Covered Bridge (Chua Cau) | Sixteenth-century arched wooden bridge temple, fully restored in August 2024 |
| **Traditional Arts & Crafts** | Traditional Performing Arts Theatre | Live folk music, Dan Bau monocord demonstrations, and Hat Boi opera (10:15 & 15:15) |
| **Museums** | Museum of Trade Ceramics (80 Tran Phu) | Shipwreck ceramics from Vietnam, China, and the Middle East (130 Tran Phu) |

> [!NOTE]
> The Japanese Covered Bridge (*Chua Cau*) completed a meticulous two-year restoration project unveiled in August 2024. Teams reinforced subterranean timber pillars, re-grouted ancient mortar joints, and preserved weathered roof tiles while restoring the small Taoist shrine dedicated to Tran Vu (protector against floods). Crossing the bridge requires one ticket coupon during peak hours.

To experience the Old Town peacefully, plan your walk during the official **motorized vehicle ban hours** (09:00–11:00 AM and 15:00–21:30 PM daily). Motorbikes, delivery scooters, and cars are prohibited from entering historic streets, creating a calm atmosphere for admiring wooden facades, flowering bougainvillea vines, and yellow ochre walls.

## 2. Cycle Through Cam Chau Rice Fields to An Bang Beach

One of Hoi An's greatest charms is how quickly dense timber streetscapes dissolve into emerald countryside. Renting a city cruiser bicycle (30,000 to 50,000 VND per day, frequently provided complimentary by boutique homestays) opens up a flat, scenic five-kilometer route to the ocean.

### The Countryside Path
Instead of taking the bustling main corridor of Cua Dai Road, navigate northeast along small paved paths through **Cam Chau ward**:
- Weave between irrigated rice paddies bordered by water hyacinth canals.
- Pass grazing water buffaloes wading in shallow wetlands and white egrets hunting in freshly tilled soil.
- Stop at rustic open-air cafes (such as Roving Chillhouse or Xom Chieu Coffee) perched directly on bamboo boardwalks beside rice fields for iced coconut coffee (*Ca phe cot dua*).
- After four kilometers of tranquil cycling, the road terminates at **An Bang Beach**. Unlike the eroded seawalls of Cua Dai, An Bang maintains a wide expanse of pale sand and clean swimming waters from March through September. Park your bicycle at designated lots near the beachfront (typically 10,000 VND) and relax under thatched bamboo cabanas at beachside lounges like Soul Kitchen or Shore Club.

## 3. Work Alongside Organic Farmers at Tra Que Vegetable Village

Situated three kilometers north of the town center along the De Vong River, **Tra Que Herb Village** (Lang rau Tra Que) has cultivated traditional culinary greens for more than three centuries. The village supplies virtually all the fresh herbs—sweet mint, holy basil, sawtooth coriander, lemon verbena, and perilla—that give local dishes like *Cao lau* and *Mi Quang* their distinctive aromatics.

### The 35,000 VND Farming Experience
Visitors pay a modest **35,000 VND** community heritage ticket at the village entrance:
- The village eschews chemical fertilizers, utilizing decomposing water weeds (*Rong*) harvested daily from the nearby Tra Que lagoon to enrich the sandy alluvial soil.
- Local farmers demonstrate traditional agricultural techniques: tilling raised soil beds with long-handled wooden hoes, raking organic mulch, and carrying dual copper watering cans balanced across bamboo shoulder poles (*Don ganh*).
- Visitors can roll up their trousers, dig into the rich earth, practice rhythmic watering, and finish with a soothing warm foot bath infused with lemongrass and ginger. Several family farmsteads operate half-day cooking academies teaching how to assemble crispy *Banh xeo* rice pancakes stuffed with shrimp and pork.

## 4. Navigate Nipa Palm Canals on Cam Thanh Basket Boats

Located five kilometers southeast of Hoi An where the river meets the sea, **Cam Thanh Coconut Village** preserves eighty-four hectares of nipa saltwater palm forests (Rung dua Bay Mau). During the Vietnam War, this dense swamp served as a revolutionary guerrilla base shielded by maze-like waterways.

### Basket Boat (Thuyen Thung) Logistics
Traditional round bamboo basket boats (*Thuyen thung*) are woven from split bamboo strips waterproofed with tree resin and tar. 
- Entry to the ecological zone is ticketed at **30,000 VND** per adult.
- A private 45-minute basket boat excursion costs **150,000 VND per boat** (each boat seats two adult passengers plus the local rower).
- The rower navigates narrow channels arched over by wild nipa fronds, pointing out purple fiddler crabs on muddy banks and showing passengers how to fold palm fronds into origami grasshoppers and flower rings.
- Toward the central river channel, local boatmen perform high-speed "basket boat spinning" demonstrations, swinging their paddles to rhythmic techno music. If you prefer a quiet, nature-focused experience, politely inform your rower in advance: *"Chi di dao em dem"* (please keep it calm and quiet).

## 5. Attend the Hoi An Memories Outdoor Spectacle (Ky Uc Hoi An)

Staged on **Memories Island** (Con Hen) in the center of the Thu Bon River, **Hoi An Memories** is Vietnam's largest outdoor theatrical performance. The 60-minute production involves five hundred professional dancers and actors performing on an immense 25,000-square-meter open-water stage featuring real island topography and scaled historic architecture.

| Seating Class | Adult Ticket Price (VND) | Child Ticket (1.0m–1.4m) | Seating Location & View |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Eco Class** | 600,000 VND | 300,000 VND | Lower front grandstand rows; close to water effects |
| **High Class** | 750,000 VND | 375,000 VND | Central elevated rows; ideal panoramic perspective |
| **VIP Class** | 1,200,000 VND | 600,000 VND | Top covered pavilion with leather armchairs & refreshments |

*Children under one meter tall enter free when sharing a seat with parents. Showtimes run from 20:00 to 21:00 PM nightly except Tuesdays. Tickets include complimentary afternoon entry to the Impression Theme Park from 17:00 PM onwards.*

The show traces four centuries of maritime trade across five acts: weaving silk, construction of ancient houses, the royal wedding of Princess Huyen Tran to the King of Champa, the bustling international trading port era, and a stunning finale featuring one hundred performers walking across water dressed in glowing traditional *Ao dai* tunics.

## 6. Commission Custom Garments at Master Tailor Ateliers

Hoi An is internationally renowned for bespoke tailoring, housing over four hundred active tailoring shops. Master seamstresses can replicate existing garments, fit tailored Italian wool suits, or cut flowing silk summer dresses within twenty-four to forty-eight hours.

### How to Navigate Tailoring Successfully
To avoid disappointment with fabric durability or uneven stitching, follow these field rules:
1. **Choose Reputable Established Houses:** Premium ateliers like **Yaly Couture** (47 Nguyen Thai Hoc), **Bebe Tailor** (11 Hoang Dieu), and **A Dong Silk** (62 Tran Hung Dao) employ dedicated in-house pattern-makers and multiple master tailors rather than outsourcing to random sweatshops.
2. **Insist on Multiple Fittings:** Never commission garments on your final morning expecting to pick them up an hour before departure. High quality requires an initial measurement, a first fitting twelve hours later to adjust posture, sleeve lengths, and trouser break, and a final fitting to ensure seams lay flat.
3. **Inspect the Fabric Content:** Natural mulberry silk, high-thread-count Egyptian cotton, and linen blends will cost more than synthetic polyester but maintain comfort and breathability in warm climates. A tailored two-piece wool-blend suit typically ranges from $160 to $350 USD, while custom linen shirts run $25 to $45 USD.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Visiting Hoi An

### Is the 120,000 VND Old Town ticket mandatory just to walk the streets?
Strictly speaking, the heritage ticket is designed to fund the structural conservation of historic monuments. Uniformed municipal ticket wardens staff main entry points into the pedestrian zone. While tourists walking directly to a specific restaurant without entering heritage halls are occasionally waved through, purchasing the 120,000 VND ticket is required by city law, supports UNESCO conservation, and grants access to five historic sites. Always carry your ticket booklet while strolling in town.

### What is the fair price for a lantern boat ride on the Thu Bon River?
Wooden sampan rides on the river are regulated by the municipal tourist board with fixed published pricing posted at piers along Bach Dang Street: **150,000 VND** for a boat seating one to three passengers, and **200,000 VND** for a boat seating four to five passengers, for a standardized twenty-minute cruise. Cardboard paper lantern floats with tea candles cost 10,000 to 20,000 VND from street vendors.

### When is the best time of year to visit Hoi An?
The dry, sunny period runs from February through August. February to April provides mild temperatures between 22°C and 28°C (72°F–82°F) with minimal rainfall. May through August brings intense dry heat often reaching 36°C (97°F). From October through December, central Vietnam experiences the heavy northeast monsoon; high tides combined with torrential rains can cause the lower streets of Hoi An along Bach Dang to flood knee-deep for several days.

### Can I do a day trip to My Son Sanctuary from Hoi An?
Yes. My Son Sanctuary, the ancient spiritual capital of the Champa Kingdom, sits forty-five kilometers southwest of Hoi An. Private cars take roughly one hour each way (600,000 to 800,000 VND round trip), while admission costs 150,000 VND per person. Depart early at 06:30 AM to explore the Hindu brick tower temples before tour buses arrive and midday temperatures peak.
