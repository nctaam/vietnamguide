# Da Lat Coffee Farms: Arabica Tour & Cupping Guide (2026)

Vietnam ranks as the world's second-largest coffee producer, yet global markets overwhelmingly associate the country with commercial-grade Robusta grown across the red clay plains of Dak Lak and Gia Lai. The temperate highlands of Lam Dong province disrupt this narrative. Perched on a volcanic plateau between 1,450 and 1,650 meters above sea level, **Da Lat** commands a microclimate uniquely qualified to cultivate high-density, specialty-grade **Arabica**.

Frequent mountain mists, daytime temperatures hovering between 15°C and 24°C, and mineral-dense basaltic soils combine to prolong cherry maturation. This slower ripening cycle concentrates natural sugars and complex organic acids within the bean, producing cup profiles marked by bright malic acidity, stone fruit notes, and delicate jasmine aromatics.

Over the past decade, Da Lat transformed from a regional agricultural hub into Southeast Asia's premier specialty coffee destination. Indigenous farming collectives, third-generation plantation owners, and visionary roasters have established farm-to-cup circuits where visitors can walk through agroforestry orchards, inspect raised drying beds, and evaluate micro-lots in calibrated cupping laboratories.

This empirical guide examines the region's primary coffee corridors, certified specialty producers, tour tariffs, sensory evaluation sessions, and harvest logistics for the 2026 travel season.

## Cau Dat Historical Plantation: The 1927 French Legacy

Situated 25 kilometers southeast of Da Lat's city center along National Route 20, the **Cau Dat Plateau** represents the historical epicenter of commercial coffee cultivation in the Central Highlands. French agronomists first recognized the agricultural potential of Xuan Truong's undulating granite and basalt hills in the early 1900s, formally establishing the estate in 1927.

At an elevation of 1,650 meters, Cau Dat endures dense morning fog banks and sharp diurnal temperature swings, providing textbook conditions for high-density bean formation. The estate operates on an industrial scale, supplying green beans to domestic exporters and international specialty buyers.

Visitors walking the grounds encounter vast contoured fields of dark-green Catimor and heirloom Bourbon trees intercropped alongside rows of century-old tea bushes. The original French-built processing sheds have been partially modernized, allowing travelers to observe large-scale wet-milling equipment, mechanical pulpers, and extensive covered drying patios.

### Practical Logistics:
- **Location:** Xuan Truong Commune, 25 km southeast of Da Lat (40–45 minutes by motorbike or taxi).
- **Tariffs:** Basic estate access costs 90,000 VND (~$3.60 USD). Specialized farm and factory tours led by resident agronomists cost 150,000 VND (~$6.00 USD), including a single-origin pour-over.
- **Operating Hours:** Open daily from 07:00 to 17:00. Arriving before 08:30 AM allows visitors to witness morning cloud inversions rolling through the tea and coffee valleys.

## K'Ho Coffee: Indigenous Agroforestry & Heirloom Cultivars

For a deeply personal, ecologically conscious coffee encounter, journey 12 kilometers north of Da Lat to the Lac Duong district at the foot of Lang Biang Mountain. Here, **K'Ho Coffee** functions as a community enterprise founded by Rolan Co Lieng, an indigenous K'Ho woman, and her American agronomist husband, Joshua Guikema.

The K'Ho people have inhabited the Lang Biang plateau for generations. Unlike monoculture plantations that clear forest canopies, K'Ho Coffee practices traditional agroforestry. Arabica shrubs grow beneath the filtered shade of native mountain pines, wild persimmon trees, and broadleaf banana plants. This canopy shields delicate coffee leaves from scorching noon sun, while falling organic matter enriches the soil without synthetic fertilizers.

The cooperative cultivates heirloom **Bourbon** and **Typica** varieties brought to the region during the colonial era, alongside resilient Catimor strains. Cherries are hand-picked exclusively at peak ripeness (measured by Brix refractometers to ensure sugar content exceeds 20%), pulped using hand-cranked machinery, and slowly dried on raised mesh screens for 14 to 21 days.

### Cupping & Educational Experience:
- **Location:** Bon Dong Village, Lac Duong (25 minutes north of Da Lat).
- **Tariffs:** Walk-in visitors can sample pour-over coffees and cold brews in the rustic courtyard garden for 45,000 to 65,000 VND. Comprehensive 2.5-hour farm tours with structured cupping sessions cost 300,000 to 500,000 VND per person and require advance reservation via their community portal.
- **Atmosphere:** An intimate, respectful encounter emphasizing indigenous land stewardship, traditional backstrap weaving, and meticulous bean selection.

> [!TIP]
> Purchase whole-bean roasted packages directly from the K'Ho workshop (180,000 to 260,000 VND per 250g bag). Retail sales provide direct, unmediated income to 50+ local indigenous families participating in the agricultural collective.

## Boutique Terroirs: Ta Nung Pass & Son Pacamara Farm

Descending southwest toward the Nam Ban valley through the winding curves of **Ta Nung Pass**, the terrain transitions into a warmer sub-tropical pocket situated at 1,450 meters. This micro-region has become an experimental playground for boutique growers focusing on rare, demanding cultivars.

The foremost pioneer along this corridor is **Son Pacamara Specialty Coffee Farm**. Operated by Nguyen Van Son, a dedicated third-generation highland grower, this boutique estate focuses almost exclusively on **Pacamara**—a natural hybrid of the Pacas and Maragogipe varietals known for producing unusually massive bean sizes and intense sensory complexity.

Son also cultivates rare micro-lots of **Geisha** and **Yellow Bourbon**. At the farm's cupping station, visitors can evaluate how experimental post-harvest fermentation affects the finished cup. Son implements carbonic maceration (fermenting whole intact cherries inside sealed carbon dioxide tanks) and yeast-inoculated natural processing.

### Practical Logistics:
- **Location:** DT725 Highway, Ta Nung Pass, 18 km southwest of Da Lat.
- **Tasting Costs:** Guided farm walks and sensory flights featuring three distinct processing methods cost 250,000 to 450,000 VND per participant.
- **Transit:** Accessible via a scenic 30-minute motorcycle or taxi ride down the Ta Nung mountain pass. Combine this excursion with a stop at Elephant Waterfall further down the valley.

## Urban Tasting Laboratories: From Cupping Room to Extraction

Travelers unable to allocate a half-day for rural farm journeys can experience Da Lat's specialty coffee movement within the city's sophisticated urban roasteries and tasting rooms.

### The Married Beans Workspace
Founded by Duy Ho, a central figure in Vietnam's third-wave coffee evolution, **The Married Beans** bridges the gap between rural smallholders and urban coffee connoisseurs. Their flagship workspace operates out of a restored colonial-era villa at 44 Hung Vuong Street, surrounded by fragrant pine groves.

The interior houses a professional sensory training lab equipped with calibrated espresso machines, precision grinders, and cupping tables. Guests can order curated **Tasting Flights** (65,000 to 120,000 VND) displaying how identical Catimor cherries taste when processed using washed, honey, and natural techniques.

### La Viet Coffee Warehouse
Located at 200 Nguyen Cong Tru in Da Lat's Ward 8, **La Viet** resembles an industrial warehouse converted into an artisanal production workshop. The open-plan facility allows patrons to drink coffee while watching green bean sorting machines, sample roasters, and commercial packing lines operate through glass partitions.

La Viet offers complimentary 15-minute walk-throughs of their roasting and laboratory facilities upon request. Their brewing bar features siphon vacuum pots, Kalita wave drippers, cold brew draft taps, and traditional Vietnamese phin filters re-engineered for light-roast specialty Arabica. Tasting sets range from 60,000 to 95,000 VND.

## Harvest Timing, Post-Harvest Protocols, and Logistics

Timing an agricultural visit drastically shapes the sensory experience in Da Lat.

```
DA LAT COFFEE ANNUAL CALENDAR
========================================================================================
[Jan - Mar] Dry Winter Season  : Sun-drying on raised beds, hulling, green grading, cupping
[Apr - Sep] Rainy Summer Season: Shrub flowering, cherry development, foliage pruning
[Oct - Dec] Peak Harvest Window: Red cherry picking, wet pulping, active fermentation
========================================================================================
```

### Post-Harvest Processing Protocols:
1. **Washed (Wet) Processing:** Cherries are pulped immediately after picking, fermented in water tanks for 24 to 36 hours to loosen mucilage, then washed thoroughly and dried. This yields the cleanest cup profile, highlighting crisp citric acidity and delicate floral notes.
2. **Honey (Pulped Natural) Processing:** The outer skin is removed, but sticky fruit mucilage is left clinging to the parchment during drying. Depending on remaining pulp levels (Yellow, Red, or Black Honey), this yields stone fruit sweetness and a syrupy mouthfeel.
3. **Natural (Dry) Processing:** Whole intact cherries dry slowly on raised mesh beds under direct sunlight for up to 3 weeks. Natural fermentation inside the fruit imparts wine-like complexity, wild berry notes, and heavy body.

### Transport & Navigation:
- **Motorcycle Rental:** Exploring Cau Dat and Ta Nung independently by semi-automatic scooter costs 120,000 to 150,000 VND ($5.00 to $6.00 USD) per day. Roads are paved but require caution due to mountain curves and morning fog.
- **Private Car Hire:** Chartering a private taxi or car for a full 8-hour day covering Cau Dat or Lac Duong costs roughly 700,000 to 950,000 VND ($28.00 to $38.00 USD) including driver and fuel.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When is the absolute best time of year to visit coffee farms in Da Lat?
The optimal window runs from **November through January**. During these winter months, coffee branches are laden with crimson ripe cherries, picking teams are active in the groves, and outdoor raised beds are covered with drying beans. The weather is dry, crisp, and sunny, with cool daytime temperatures averaging 18°C.

### How does Da Lat Arabica compare to Vietnamese Robusta from Buon Ma Thuot?
Robusta from Buon Ma Thuot grows at low elevations (400–800m), containing roughly double the caffeine, higher bitterness, and heavy earthy notes suitable for sweetened condensed milk coffee (*cà phê sữa đá*). Da Lat Arabica grows above 1,500m, featuring lower caffeine, refined sweetness, crisp acidity, and delicate fruit or floral aromatics intended for pour-over, Aeropress, or specialty espresso extraction.

### Do I need to book farm tours in advance?
For large commercial estates like Cau Dat, advance reservations are unnecessary; walk-in tickets are purchased at the entrance gate. For boutique operations such as K'Ho Coffee and Son Pacamara, advance booking (at least 24 to 48 hours prior) is mandatory for guided tours and sensory cupping workshops, as owners personally manage agricultural operations.

### Can international travelers purchase unroasted green beans to bring home?
Most farms sell vacuum-sealed 250g or 500g bags of roasted whole beans, but specialty estates like K'Ho Coffee and The Married Beans can accommodate requests for small quantities of vacuum-sealed green coffee beans for home roasters. Check your home country's agricultural quarantine regulations before transporting raw seeds internationally.

### Are children and non-coffee drinkers welcome at these estates?
Yes. Both Cau Dat and K'Ho Coffee feature scenic outdoor walking trails, tea gardens, and lush mountain panoramas. On-site cafes provide fresh herbal teas, hot chocolate, fresh fruit smoothies, and locally sourced snacks for non-coffee drinkers.

### What should I wear when visiting rural highland farms?
Wear sturdy closed-toe walking shoes or light hiking boots suitable for navigating steep clay slopes and uneven dirt rows. Dress in layers, including a light fleece or windbreaker jacket, as morning temperatures on the plateau frequently dip below 14°C before warming up under direct midday sunshine.
