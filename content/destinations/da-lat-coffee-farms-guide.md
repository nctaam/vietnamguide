# Da Lat Coffee Farms: Arabica Tour & Cupping Guide (2026)

Vietnam ranks as the world's second-largest coffee producer, yet global markets overwhelmingly associate the country with commercial-grade Robusta grown at 400 meters to 800 meters across the red clay plains of Dak Lak and Gia Lai. The temperate highlands of Lam Dong province disrupt this narrative. Perched on a volcanic plateau between 1,450 meters and 1,650 meters above sea level, **Da Lat** commands a mountain microclimate ideally suited to cultivate high-density, specialty-grade **Arabica**.

Frequent mountain mists, daytime temperatures hovering between 16°C and 24°C, average relative humidity around 82%, and mineral-dense basaltic soils combine to prolong cherry maturation across an 8-month to 9-month cycle. This slower ripening concentrates natural sucrose and complex organic acids within the seed, producing cup profiles marked by bright malic acidity, stone fruit notes, and delicate jasmine aromatics.

Over the past decade, Da Lat transformed from a regional agricultural supply zone into Southeast Asia's premier specialty coffee hub. Indigenous farming collectives located 12 km north of the city center, third-generation plantation owners situated 18 km to 25 km along mountain passes, and technical roasters operating within a 3 km radius of Xuan Huong Lake have established farm-to-cup circuits. Visitors walk through shaded agroforestry orchards for 90 minutes, inspect raised drying beds elevated 1 meter above the ground, and evaluate micro-lots during 60-minute sessions in calibrated cupping laboratories.

This empirical guide examines the region's primary coffee corridors, certified specialty producers, tour tariffs in VND and USD, sensory evaluation protocols, and harvest logistics for the 2026 travel season.

## Cau Dat Historical Plantation: The 1927 French Legacy

Situated 25 kilometers southeast of Da Lat's central market along National Route QL20B, the **Cau Dat Plateau** represents the historical epicenter of commercial coffee and tea cultivation in the Central Highlands. French agronomists first surveyed the agricultural potential of Xuan Truong's undulating granite and red basalt hills in 1915, formally establishing the 220-hectare estate in 1927.

At an elevation of 1,650 meters, Cau Dat endures dense morning fog banks, 82% to 88% relative humidity, and sharp diurnal temperature swings from 12°C at dawn to 22°C at midday. These atmospheric conditions foster slow cellular development and high bean density. The estate operates on an industrial scale, supplying washed and honey-processed green beans to domestic roasters and international buyers.

Visitors walking the 3.5-kilometer perimeter trail encounter contoured fields of dark-green Catimor and heirloom Yellow Bourbon shrubs planted at 2-meter intervals alongside rows of 100-year-old tea bushes. The original French-built processing sheds have been modernized with optical color sorters, mechanical siphon pulpers capable of processing 2,000 kg of cherries per hour, and 1,500 square meters of covered solar drying patios.

## K'Ho Coffee: Indigenous Agroforestry & Heirloom Cultivars

For an ecologically grounded coffee encounter, travel 12 kilometers north of Da Lat along Provincial Road DT726 to the Lac Duong district at the base of the 2,167-meter Lang Biang Mountain peak. Here, **K'Ho Coffee** operates as a community social enterprise founded by Rolan Co Lieng, an indigenous K'Ho woman, and her American agronomist husband, Joshua Guikema.

The K'Ho people have inhabited the Lang Biang plateau at elevations between 1,480 meters and 1,700 meters for centuries. Unlike commercial monoculture estates that clear native trees, K'Ho Coffee practices multi-strata agroforestry. Arabica shrubs grow beneath a 15-meter canopy of native three-needle mountain pines (*Pinus kesiya*), wild persimmon trees, and broadleaf banana plants. This shade canopy lowers leaf surface temperatures by 3°C to 4°C during midday sunshine, while decomposing pine needles regulate soil pH and suppress weeds without synthetic herbicides.

The collective cultivates heirloom **Bourbon** and **Typica** trees planted during the 1930s and 1940s, alongside disease-resistant Catimor lines. Pickers harvest cherries exclusively at crimson peak ripeness, verified in the field using optical Brix refractometers to confirm sugar concentrations between 20% and 24% Brix. Workers pulp the harvest within 6 hours using hand-calibrated disc pulpers, ferment the parchment for 24 hours to 36 hours in mountain spring water, and dry the lots on elevated mesh screens 1 meter off the ground for 14 days to 21 days until moisture stabilizes at 10.5% to 11.5%.

### Cupping & Educational Experience:
- **Location and Transit:** Bon Dong 1 Village, Lac Duong Town, 12 km north of Da Lat Market (25 minutes by car or motorbike via Xo Viet Nghe Tinh and DT726; GrabCar fare averages 160,000 VND / $6.40 USD to 210,000 VND / $8.40 USD one way).
- **Walk-In Cafe vs Guided Tours:** Walk-in guests can sample Chemex, V60 pour-over, French press, and 18-hour cold brew coffees in the timber courtyard from 08:00 to 16:30 for 45,000 VND ($1.80 USD) to 65,000 VND ($2.60 USD) per cup. Comprehensive 2.5-hour (150-minute) farm walks with SCA-protocol cupping sessions cost 300,000 VND ($12.00 USD) to 500,000 VND ($20.00 USD) per person and require booking 24 hours to 48 hours in advance.
- **Cultural Context:** The 150-minute visit pairs agronomic instruction with visits to traditional K'Ho backstrap loom weavers producing hand-dyed cotton textiles priced from 250,000 VND ($10.00 USD) to 850,000 VND ($34.00 USD).

> [!TIP]
> Purchase whole-bean roasted packages directly from the K'Ho roastery shed at 180,000 VND ($7.20 USD) to 260,000 VND ($10.40 USD) per 250g bag, or 680,000 VND ($27.20 USD) to 950,000 VND ($38.00 USD) per 1 kg bag. Retail purchases deliver direct income to 55 indigenous K'Ho smallholder families participating in the Lac Duong cooperative.

## Boutique Terroirs: Ta Nung Pass & Son Pacamara Farm

Descending 18 kilometers southwest from central Da Lat toward the Nam Ban valley along the switchbacks of **Ta Nung Pass (Provincial Road DT725)**, the elevation drops from 1,550 meters down to a sheltered mountain pocket at 1,450 meters. This corridor receives warm afternoon updrafts from the lower 1,000-meter valley while retaining cool 15°C night air, creating an ideal micro-terroir for experimental varietals.

The leading producer along this corridor is **Son Pacamara Specialty Coffee Farm**. Managed by Nguyen Van Son, a third-generation highland grower, this 6-hectare hillside estate specializes in **Pacamara**, a large-seeded cross between the Salvadoran Pacas and giant Maragogipe cultivars. The trees yield screen-18 to screen-20 beans (measuring 7.1 mm to 8.0 mm across) with pronounced stone-fruit sweetness.

Son also tends high-altitude plots of **Panamanian Geisha** and **Yellow Bourbon** spaced 2.5 meters apart on 30-degree slopes. At the farm's open-air wooden cupping deck, visitors evaluate how controlled post-harvest microbiology alters flavor chemistry. Son executes 72-hour to 120-hour anaerobic carbonic maceration (fermenting intact cherries inside sealed 200-liter food-grade tanks flushed with carbon dioxide) and yeast-inoculated honey processing.

### Practical Logistics for Ta Nung Pass:
- **Location and Route:** Provincial Road DT725, Ta Nung Commune, 18 km southwest of Da Lat Market (30 minutes to 35 minutes by motorbike or private car via Hoang Van Thu Street and Cam Ly Pass).
- **Tasting and Workshop Tariffs:** Guided 2-hour (120-minute) orchard walks followed by a 4-cup comparative cupping flight (Washed, Red Honey, Natural, and 96-hour Anaerobic Pacamara) cost 250,000 VND ($10.00 USD) to 450,000 VND ($18.00 USD) per participant. Whole-bean 200g retail pouches of Pacamara or Geisha sell for 220,000 VND ($8.80 USD) to 480,000 VND ($19.20 USD).
- **Corridor Combination:** Pair a 08:30 morning session at Son Pacamara with a 12 km drive further down DT725 (20 minutes) to inspect Elephant Waterfall (*Thác Voi*, entrance 30,000 VND / $1.20 USD) and Linh An Pagoda's 71-meter Guanyin statue before returning 30 km (50 minutes) to Da Lat by 14:30.

## Step-by-Step Protocol: How to Evaluate Da Lat Arabica at a Cupping Table

When attending a 60-minute to 90-minute sensory session at K'Ho Coffee, Son Pacamara, or The Married Beans, roasters follow the standardized Specialty Coffee Association (SCA) cupping protocol. Knowing the 6 stages below helps visitors extract maximum value from their 250,000 VND ($10.00 USD) to 500,000 VND ($20.00 USD) workshop fee:

1. **Dry Fragrance Assessment (Minutes 0 to 5):** Coarsely ground coffee (12g of beans per 200ml ceramic cupping bowl) is weighed to a 1:18.18 brew ratio. Lift the lid and inhale deeply for 3 seconds without touching the bowl to identify dry volatile aromatics such as jasmine, toasted almond, or cocoa nibs.
2. **Hot Water Infusion & Wet Aroma (Minutes 5 to 9):** Baristas pour 93°C (200°F) filtered water (total dissolved solids calibrated at 120 ppm to 150 ppm) directly onto the grounds to form a floating crust. Lean within 10 cm of the rim to evaluate the rising steam aroma for 4 minutes.
3. **Breaking the Crust (Minute 4 after pour):** Using a silver-plated cupping spoon, push the floating grounds backward 3 times at a depth of 2 cm while holding your nose 5 cm above the surface. This sudden mechanical release liberates trapped caramel, stone-fruit, and citrus esters.
4. **Skimming Foam & Cooling to 70°C (Minutes 5 to 12):** Two spoons are drawn across the surface to skim off floating oils and chaff. Wait 8 minutes to 10 minutes for the liquid temperature to drop from 93°C down to 70°C (158°F) so you do not scald your tongue.
5. **High-Velocity Slurping (Minutes 12 to 25):** Dip the cupping spoon, fill it with 6ml of liquid, and aspirate sharply across your entire palate. Aerating the coffee atomizes volatile compounds toward the retronasal olfactory bulb, revealing malic green-apple acidity, peach sweetness, and tactile body. Rinse your spoon in a 250ml cup of 80°C water between every sample bowl.
6. **Thermal Decay Evaluation at 40°C (Minutes 25 to 40):** Re-taste each bowl as the liquid cools from 55°C down to 38°C (room temperature). High-scoring 85+ point Cau Dat Bourbon and Lac Duong Typica lots become sweeter and more floral as they cool, whereas defective low-elevation beans turn astringent or papery.

## Harvest Timing, Post-Harvest Protocols, and Transport Logistics

Timing an agricultural visit shapes what you observe in the groves and on the drying patios across Lam Dong province.

```
DA LAT COFFEE ANNUAL AGRICULTURAL & CLIMATIC CALENDAR
========================================================================================
[Nov - Jan] Peak Harvest Window : 14°C-22°C, low rain (40 mm/mo); cherry picking, wet pulping
[Feb - Mar] Late Dry & Milling  : 16°C-25°C, dry sun; raised-bed drying, hulling, flowering
[Apr - Oct] Monsoon Growth      : 18°C-24°C, afternoon rain (220 mm/mo); berry expansion
========================================================================================
```

### Three Core Post-Harvest Processing Protocols:
1. **Washed (Wet) Processing (36-Hour Fermentation + 12-Day Drying):** Cherries are pulped within 6 hours of picking, fermented in tiled water tanks for 24 hours to 36 hours to dissolve pectin mucilage, washed in clean channels, and sun-dried for 10 days to 14 days. This method produces the cleanest cup profile, highlighting crisp citric acidity and jasmine notes.
2. **Honey (Pulped Natural) Processing (0-Hour Water Soak + 18-Day Drying):** The outer skin is removed by mechanical pulpers, leaving 40% to 80% of sticky fruit mucilage clinging to the parchment during a 15-day to 20-day drying period. Depending on turning frequency (Yellow Honey turned every 2 hours, Red Honey every 6 hours, Black Honey once per 24 hours), this yields yellow peach sweetness and a syrupy mouthfeel.
3. **Natural & Anaerobic Processing (72-Hour Tank + 21-Day Drying):** Whole intact cherries ferment inside sealed tanks for 48 hours to 120 hours before drying slowly on raised mesh beds at 1,500 meters for 18 days to 25 days. Intracellular fermentation imparts red-wine complexity, ripe strawberry notes, and heavy body.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When is the best time of year to visit coffee farms in Da Lat?
The primary harvest window runs from **November through January**. During these 3 winter months, coffee branches at 1,500 meters to 1,650 meters carry ripe crimson cherries, picking teams harvest 80 kg to 120 kg per worker daily, and outdoor raised beds hold tons of drying parchment. Weather stays dry and sunny with daytime temperatures averaging 18°C to 22°C. Visiting in **February or March** rewards travelers with snow-white coffee blossoms perfuming the valleys for 48 hours to 72 hours after each spring rain.

### How does Da Lat Arabica compare to Vietnamese Robusta from Buon Ma Thuot?
Robusta from Buon Ma Thuot grows at lower elevations between 400 meters and 800 meters, containing 2.2% to 2.7% caffeine by dry weight, low sugar (3% to 7%), and heavy pyrazine notes suited for sweetened condensed milk coffee (*cà phê sữa đá*, 25,000 VND / $1.00 USD to 35,000 VND / $1.40 USD). Da Lat Arabica grows between 1,450 meters and 1,650 meters, containing 1.2% to 1.5% caffeine, 6% to 9% natural sucrose, and delicate stone-fruit aromatics designed for V60 pour-over, AeroPress, or light-roast espresso extraction at 60,000 VND ($2.40 USD) to 95,000 VND ($3.80 USD) per cup.

### Do I need to book Da Lat coffee farm tours in advance?
For large estates like Cau Dat Plantation (25 km southeast), advance reservations are unnecessary for the 90,000 VND ($3.60 USD) general grounds pass, which is sold at the entrance booth from 07:00 to 16:30. For boutique family estates like K'Ho Coffee (300,000 VND / $12.00 USD to 500,000 VND / $20.00 USD) and Son Pacamara (250,000 VND / $10.00 USD to 450,000 VND / $18.00 USD), booking 24 hours to 48 hours ahead via WhatsApp or email is mandatory so roasters can prepare sample roasts 24 hours before your cupping session.

### Can international travelers purchase unroasted green beans to bring home?
Most estates sell nitrogen-flushed 200g, 250g, or 500g valve bags of roasted whole beans priced between 180,000 VND ($7.20 USD) and 480,000 VND ($19.20 USD). Specialty producers including K'Ho Coffee, Son Pacamara, and La Viet also sell 500g to 1 kg vacuum-sealed bags of unroasted green Arabica (screen 16 to screen 18, 11% moisture) for 220,000 VND ($8.80 USD) to 380,000 VND ($15.20 USD) per kilogram for home roasters. Check your destination country's customs biosecurity rules before packing raw unroasted agricultural seeds.

### Are children and non-coffee drinkers welcome at these highland estates?
Yes. Both Cau Dat Plantation and K'Ho Coffee feature 2 km to 3.5 km outdoor walking paths, shaded pine groves, and mountain viewpoints. On-site cafes serve Cau Dat Oolong and green teas (45,000 VND / $1.80 USD to 65,000 VND / $2.60 USD), hot Lam Dong cacao (55,000 VND / $2.20 USD), fresh mulberry or avocado smoothies (50,000 VND / $2.00 USD), and wind-dried Da Lat persimmons (*hồng treo gió*, 120,000 VND / $4.80 USD per 250g pack).

### What should I wear when visiting rural highland coffee farms?
Wear closed-toe trail runners or hiking shoes with lugged rubber soles to maintain grip on 25-degree red clay slopes and damp morning grass. Dress in removable layers: bring a windproof jacket or fleece for 07:00 departures when temperatures at 1,650 meters dip to 12°C to 14°C, paired with a breathable cotton shirt and SPF 50 sunscreen once the mountain mist clears by 10:00 and temperatures climb to 22°C to 24°C.
