# Quy Nhon Travel Guide: Quiet Beaches, Ky Co & Transfers

Quy Nhon occupies a sheltered coastal amphitheater in Binh Dinh province, framed by the emerald headlands of Phuong Mai peninsula to the east and the rounded granite hills of the South-Central coast to the west. While Da Nang and Nha Trang transformed rapidly into high-density international resort strips, Quy Nhon preserved the unhurried cadence of a working maritime city of roughly 480,000 residents, where timber fishing smacks anchor along broad municipal beaches and open-air seafood halls outnumber high-rise chain hotels.

Beyond its relaxed urban crescent, the surrounding coastline harbors some of Vietnam's most dramatic seaside geography. Windswept cliffside trails at Eo Gio drop sheer into turquoise coves, hidden sandy bays like Ky Co rival offshore island waters in clarity, and solitary brick towers erected by the Champa civilization eight centuries ago command panoramic vistas over rolling agricultural plains.

The city beach is remarkably uncommercialized compared to other central hubs. Municipal regulations prohibit jet skis and motorized watercraft inside the central urban bay, ensuring quiet conditions for ocean swimmers. Morning exercise groups gather at dawn along the beachfront promenade, and fishermen haul small beach seines directly onto the sand within sight of downtown office buildings.

Climate patterns divide the year into two distinct phases. From **March through September**, the offshore waters remain glassy, sunlit, and warm, with daily temperatures ranging between 28°C and 34°C (82°F–93°F). From October through January, the northeast monsoon brings overcast skies, ocean swell, and intermittent rainfall, making beach lounging less predictable while keeping inland temperatures pleasantly cool.

### Eo Gio: The Windy Strait

Located on the rugged coastline of Nhon Ly commune, **Eo Gio** (*The Strait of Wind*) is a natural rock amphitheater carved by millennia of wave erosion. A painted concrete-and-timber boardwalk snakes along the contour of sheer coastal bluffs, descending toward pebble-strewn tide pools where waves crash dramatically against volcanic basalt outcrops.

Admission to the boardwalk is 25,000 VND ($1.00 USD). Morning hours between 06:30 and 08:30 AM provide the best conditions, with gentle morning sunlight illuminating the cliffs and light breezes coming off the sea before midday heat builds across the exposed rocks.

### Ky Co Beach: The Hidden Crescent

Hidden behind a jagged ridge of coastal hills, **Ky Co** is widely considered one of the cleanest mainland beaches in central Vietnam. The bay features pale golden sand bordered by steep rocky headlands that block strong ocean currents, creating a calm natural lagoon with turquoise water.

Visitors have two primary routes to access Ky Co:

- **Scenic Speedboat Transfer (*Cano*)**: Boarding at Nhon Ly fishing harbor, twin-engine speedboats skim along the rocky coast for fifteen minutes to dock directly at Ky Co's floating wooden jetty. Standard packages cost 350,000 to 450,000 VND ($14–$18 USD) per person, bundling round-trip boat transit, beach entry, life jackets, snorkeling equipment at Hon Seo or Bai Dua coral reef, and a seafood lunch at a local village homestay.
- **Overland Mountain Pass & Electric Buggy**: Travelers driving motorbikes or private cars can approach via the mountain toll gate. The road winds across scenic ridge crests before terminating at an upper parking plateau. Visitors pay 100,000 VND for beach entry plus 90,000 VND for the specialized electric shuttle buggy (*xe điện*) that navigates the steep, curving mountain descent down to the shoreline.

> [!NOTE]
> During winter months (November to January), high ocean swell frequently forces the closure of the Nhon Ly speedboat docks. When sea conditions are rough, overland buggy transit remains the only safe way to access Ky Co beach.

## Millennium Monuments: The Hilltop & Urban Cham Towers

Binh Dinh province served as the political and ceremonial capital of the ancient Kingdom of Champa (known as Vijaya) from the 10th to the 15th centuries. Fourteen surviving brick towers remain scattered across the province, displaying a muscular, monumental architectural style distinct from the more ornate sanctuaries of My Son.

### Banh It Towers (Tháp Bánh Ít)

Rising from an isolated 100-meter-high hill near the confluence of the Con and Tan An rivers, Banh It comprises four distinct brick structures. Local folklore derives the temple's popular name from its visual similarity to *bánh ít*, a pyramid-shaped sticky rice dumpling native to Binh Dinh.

Rising 29 meters, the main sanctuary (*Kalan*) features fiery red brickwork decorated with carved pilasters and miniature roof pavilions. Cham bricklayers joined precision-carved clay bricks with plant-derived resin mortar before kiln-firing the finished structure, enabling it to withstand centuries of monsoons without structural disintegration. Ascending the stone stairways to the hilltop temple rewards travelers with quiet breezes and sweeping panoramas over emerald rice paddies and distant blue mountain ridges.

### The Twin Towers (Tháp Đôi)

Set inside a tranquil public park on Tran Hung Dao street just three kilometers from central Quy Nhon, the Twin Towers present an unusual dual-sanctuary layout. The northern tower measures 20 meters in height, while its southern companion stands at 18 meters. 

Constructed in the late 12th century, the towers reflect strong Khmer artistic influences from the Angkor Empire, visible in the carved sandstone Garuda birds of prey that brace the upper roof tiers with outstretched wings. Entry costs 20,000 VND ($0.80 USD), making it an effortless cultural stop easily reached by bicycle or short taxi ride.

### Ghenh Rang Tien Sa & Queen's Beach

Located at the southern foot of Xuan Van mountain, Ghenh Rang Tien Sa is a landscaped hillside park overlooking the entire curve of Quy Nhon Bay. 

The site features **Queen's Beach** (*Bãi Tắm Hoàng Hậu* / *Bãi Trứng*), so named because Queen Nam Phuong, the consort of Emperor Bao Dai, selected this cove as her private seaside retreat in 1927. The shoreline here consists entirely of thousands of smooth, giant rounded granite boulders resembling dinosaur eggs. Higher up the hillside sits the memorial tomb of beloved Vietnamese modernist poet Han Mac Tu (1912–1940), who spent his final months writing verse in the seaside leprosarium of nearby Quy Hoa.

### Bai Xep: The Slow-Travel Fishing Cove

Twelve kilometers south along Highway 1D lies **Bai Xep**, a traditional fishing hamlet situated between twin rocky promontories. Here, motor vehicles cannot enter the narrow village alleys, where colorful round coracle boats (*thuyền thúng*) line the sandy lanes beside drying fish nets.

Over the past decade, Bai Xep developed an organic network of laid-back traveler guesthouses and boutique retreats, led by pioneers like **Life’s A Beach** and **Mira Quy Nhon**. Travelers spend days swinging in hammocks, learning to paddle round basket boats through breaking waves, and eating grilled mackerel with ginger fish sauce at open-air village eateries.

### Bánh Xèo Tôm Nhảy: The Jumping Shrimp Crepe

The definitive culinary emblem of Quy Nhon is *bánh xèo tôm nhảy*. Small cast-iron pans are heated over open flame with lard. Cooks drop a handful of live, translucent freshwater shrimp (*tôm đất*) directly onto the hot metal, where they sizzle and leap in the pan. A thin ladle of rice batter is poured over the shrimp, followed by a handful of bean sprouts and scallions, cooking until the base turns golden and shatteringly crisp.

Diners wrap pieces of the hot crepe inside soft rice paper along with bitter green herbs, sliced cucumber, and green mango, dipping the roll into a pungent chili-garlic fish sauce. Legendary local shops like **Gia Vỹ** (14 Dien Bien Phu) and **Rau Mầm** (91 Dong Da) serve crepes from late afternoon until nightfall.

### Bún Chả Cá: Coastal Fishcake Broth

For breakfast, residents flock to **Bún Cá Ngọc Liên** (379 Nguyen Hue street). Quy Nhon's fish noodle soup stands out for its savory broth, simmered for hours using mackerel and sailfish bones, sweet pumpkin slices, fresh pineapple, and ripe tomatoes. Bowls are loaded with two styles of fishcakes: steamed white fishloaf and golden fried patties crafted from hand-pounded barracuda meat (*cá thu thuẫn*), served with fresh shredded lettuce and lime.

### Aviation Gateways and Airport Connections

Located 35 kilometers northwest of Quy Nhon on the flat plains of Phu Cat district, the commercial airport handles domestic jet services arriving daily from Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City on Vietnam Airlines, Vietjet, and Bamboo Airways.

The most economical transit link is the **Official Airport Shuttle Bus**, which waits outside the arrivals terminal for all commercial flight arrivals. Tickets cost 50,000 VND ($2.00 USD), purchased directly from the conductor aboard the bus. The shuttle follows Highway 19B and drops passengers off at **01 Nguyen Tat Thanh** in downtown Quy Nhon, from where travelers can walk or take a 25,000 VND taxi to beachfront hotels. Metered taxis and electric Xanh SM vehicles charge between 250,000 and 350,000 VND for direct hotel delivery.

### Dieu Tri Railway Station Transfers

Mainline Reunification Express trains halt at **Dieu Tri Station** (*Ga Diêu Trì*), 11 kilometers west of downtown. Taxis wait outside the terminal gate, charging 150,000 to 200,000 VND ($6.00–$8.00 USD). Budget travelers can board **Bus T22** on the main road outside the station for 20,000 VND per person to reach the central bus station.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the 2026 admission fees for Quy Nhon's top sights?
Admission rates remain modest across the province: the scenic Eo Gio cliffside walkway costs 25,000 VND per visitor, while Ky Co beach charges 100,000 VND entry along with a 90,000 VND fee for the return electric shuttle buggy down the mountain. Banh It Cham Towers and the downtown Twin Towers each charge 20,000 VND, whereas Queen's Beach at Ghenh Rang Tien Sa charges no entry fee at all.

### How do I get from Phu Cat Airport to downtown Quy Nhon?
Catch the official airport shuttle bus waiting directly outside the passenger exit for 50,000 VND per person. The trip takes approximately 45 to 50 minutes to reach 01 Nguyen Tat Thanh in the city center, though electric Xanh SM or metered Mai Linh taxis offer point-to-point hotel drop-offs for 250,000 to 350,000 VND.

### When is the best time of year to visit Quy Nhon?
Plan your visit between **March and September** for sunny skies, calm sea conditions, and ideal underwater visibility for snorkeling at Ky Co and Eo Gio. Conversely, the winter monsoon from October through December triggers frequent cloud cover, rough surf breaks, and coastal rains.

### Can I travel to Ky Co beach by road instead of speedboat?
Yes, travelers can ride a scooter or take a car over the mountain road to the Ky Co entry gate, pay the standard 100,000 VND ticket, and take the official electric buggy down the steep pass directly to the sand.

### Is Quy Nhon suitable for family travelers?
Families will appreciate the quiet city beach with its ban on noisy jet skis, manageable roads with light traffic, and accessible coastal sights like Ky Co beach and the Twin Towers.
