# Where to Stay in Ly Son (2026): Big vs Small Island

Choosing where to sleep on **Ly Son (Đảo Lý Sơn)**—a 10 km² volcanic archipelago known across Vietnam as the "Kingdom of Garlic" (Vương Quốc Tỏi), sitting 15 nautical miles (30 km) off the Quang Ngai coast—comes down to one core split: staying on paved, bustling **Big Island (Đảo Lớn — An Vinh & An Hai)** or spending a night on car-free **Little Island (Đảo Bé — An Binh)**. Five extinct craters formed these islands. Black basalt sea arches, layered tuff cliffs, and white-sand garlic plots (Tỏi cô đơn Lý Sơn) now define the coastline.

Every trip starts on the mainland at **Sa Ky Port (Cảng Sa Kỳ)**. The terminal sits 45 km (50 minutes by taxi) south of **Chu Lai Airport (VCL)**, 135 km (2.5 hours) south of **Da Nang International Airport (DAD)**, and 20 km northeast of Quang Ngai Railway Station. High-speed ferries (Super Biển Đông, Chín Nghĩa, and An Vĩnh Express) cross from Sa Ky to Ly Son's **Ben Dinh Passenger Port (Cầu Cảng Mới Bến Đình)** in 30–35 minutes (200,000–240,000 VND / $8.00–$9.60 USD one-way).

> **Crucial Island Power, Water & Typhoon Rule:** Before booking autumn or winter stays (October through January), check **Windy.com** for northeast monsoon gusts. Whenever winds reach **Beaufort Level 6 (>= 22 knots / 39 km/h)**, the Quang Ngai Port Authority halts all Sa Ky–Ly Son ferries and Little Island canoes for 1–3 days at a stretch. Always build a mandatory 1-day mainland buffer before your flight home, and remember that on **Little Island (Đảo Bé)**, life shifts to quiet evening routines and conserved rainwater/desalinated supply once the last day-tripper canoe departs at 14:30.

## 1. An Vinh Port & Southwest Coast (Cảng Lý Sơn / Cầu Cảng Mới Bến Đình)

Step off the Sa Ky high-speed ferry and you land directly along the southwest coast of **Big Island (Đảo Lớn)** in the former An Vinh ward. For 80% of visitors, sleeping within 1.5 km of the **Ly Son Port (Cảng Lý Sơn)** and the newer **Ben Dinh Pier (Cầu Cảng Mới Bến Đình)** is the smartest logistical move. Roll your bag straight into the lobby. You can rent a semi-automatic scooter on the spot (120,000–150,000 VND/day including a full tank of petrol) and access the island's primary **Agribank ATM**, pharmacy strip, and minimarts.

Staying by the harbor also simplifies the early-morning dash to **Little Island (Đảo Bé)**. Passenger speed canoes depart from Ben Dinh between 07:30 AM and 08:30 AM (120,000–140,000 VND round-trip, 8–10 minutes crossing time) and return between 11:30 AM and 14:30 PM. At dusk, the harbor seawall transforms into an open-air seafood market. Plastic tables fill with plates of steamed **King Crab (Cua Huỳnh Đế, 750,000–1,100,000 VND/kg)**, grilled **Moon Snail (Ốc Cừ, 150,000–220,000 VND)**, raw sea urchin with scallion oil (Nhum biển, 25,000–35,000 VND each), and crunchy **Ly Son Garlic Stem Salad (Gỏi tỏi Lý Sơn, 60,000 VND)**.

### Top Stays Around An Vinh Port & South Coast

* **Ly Son Pearl Island Hotel & Resort:** Anchoring the southern volcanic reef 2 km east of the pier, this standout 4-star seafront property (1,400,000–2,600,000 VND / $56–$104 USD) features a huge saltwater infinity pool built right above black basalt tide pools, spacious ocean-balcony rooms, and a reliable buffet breakfast.
* **Muong Thanh Holiday Ly Son:** Rising seven stories above the An Vinh waterfront (1,300,000–2,400,000 VND / $52–$95 USD), Muong Thanh delivers full-power central air conditioning, an outdoor swimming pool, elevator access, and panoramic views across the garlic fields and harbor.
* **Central Ly Son Hotel & Harbor Guesthouses (Hoa Biển, Đại Hằng, Phát Đạt):** Right opposite the ferry gates, **Central Ly Son Hotel** charges 650,000–1,200,000 VND ($26–$48 USD), while clean family-run harbor guesthouses (Nhà nghỉ) line the waterfront road for 350,000–650,000 VND ($14–$26 USD) per night.

## 2. To Vo Gate, Duc Pagoda & Gieng Tien Volcano Rim (Tây Bắc Đảo Lớn)

Follow the coastal road 2 km northwest of the old port and the shoreline rises into jagged black lava flows formed by the extinct **Gieng Tien Volcano (Núi lửa Giếng Tiền)**. Here stands **To Vo Gate (Cổng Tò Vò, 0 VND entry)**, a natural 2.5m-high volcanic basalt arch sculpted by wave erosion. Just 400 meters further along the cliff sits **Duc Pagoda (Chùa Đục)**, where a 27m white Quan Am statue gazes out over the East Sea and 139 stone steps climb inside the grassy volcanic crater bowl.

Small ocean-breeze homestays and mini-hotels (450,000–950,000 VND / $18–$38 USD) cluster along the road leading to To Vo Gate and the nearby **Am Linh Temple (Đình làng An Hải)** and **Hoang Sa Flotilla Exhibition House (Nhà trưng bày Hải đội Hoàng Sa)**. Book here if you want to walk two minutes with your tripod at 17:15 to capture the sun sinking directly behind the basalt arch. Crowds thin out fast by 18:15. Afterward, you can sit at a quiet cliffside cafe enjoying grilled squid, steamed sea snails, and cold beer while fishing boat lamps flicker along the northwestern horizon.

## 3. Hang Cau Cliffs, Thoi Loi Peak & Mu Cu Lighthouse (Đông An Hải)

The eastern half of Big Island (Đông An Hải, 4–6 km from the port) feels dramatically wilder. Vast checkerboards of white-sand garlic plots stretch toward the foot of **Thoi Loi Volcano (Đỉnh Thới Lới)**, the island's highest peak at 169m above sea level, which holds a freshwater reservoir inside its summit caldera that irrigates An Hai's onion and garlic fields. Along the northern base of the volcano, **Hang Pagoda (Chùa Hang)** hides inside a deep sea cave carved into 25m cliffs, while the southeastern flank drops sheer into the ocean at **Hang Cau (Hang Câu)**, where wind and surf have hollowed out fluted, multi-layered volcanic tuff walls above a shallow reef flat.

At the extreme eastern tip, a concrete causeway leads out to **Mu Cu Islet Lighthouse (Hải đăng Mù Cu)**—the undisputed #1 sunrise photography spot on Ly Son at 05:15 AM. Dawn light hits the black basalt wave-breaker boulders first. Staying on this eastern coast (Ly Son Bungalow, Bếp's House, Angel Ly Son, 500,000–1,100,000 VND / $20–$44 USD) trades port bustle for peaceful garden bungalows, 10-minute sunrise walks to Mu Cu Lighthouse, and afternoon kayaking or stand-up paddleboarding (100,000–150,000 VND/hour) along the Hang Cau reef. Most hosts here also prepare home-cooked An Hai lunches featuring sweet garlic-fed reef fish, fried garlic-bulb pancakes, and chilled watermelon for 120,000–180,000 VND per person.

## 4. Little Island / An Binh (Đảo Bé / Xã đảo An Bình)

Three nautical miles north of Big Island lies **Little Island (Đảo Bé / Xã đảo An Bình)**, a tiny 0.69 km² basalt islet home to roughly 100 fishing and garlic-farming households. No cars exist here. Locals move visitors around the 3 km coastal perimeter road by electric buggy (30,000–50,000 VND/person) or scooter.

While Big Island's coast is mostly dark volcanic rock, Little Island features **Bai Ngang (Bãi Ngang)**—a crescent cove of powdery white coral sand framed by coconut palms, black lava boulders, and clear turquoise water ideal for snorkeling over shallow coral gardens (60,000–100,000 VND wooden basket-boat thuyền thúng + mask rental). Between 08:30 AM and 14:00 PM, hundreds of day-trippers pack Bai Ngang. By 14:30 PM, the last scheduled canoe returns to Big Island, leaving overnight guests (Xa La Homestay, Gió Biển, Be Ecolodge, charging 750,000–1,300,000 VND / $30–$52 USD for A/C wooden beach bungalows or 400,000–700,000 VND for simpler fan/garden huts) with the entire island to themselves.

**What to Expect Overnight on Little Island:** Although Little Island now connects to submarine power and solar-diesel microgrids alongside a seawater desalination plant, fresh water remains precious and high-draw appliances (like room air conditioners) may cycle during off-peak midday hours at smaller family homestays. Bring cash, insect repellent, and a flashlight for stargazing on the basalt cliff boardwalk, and pre-order your evening seafood BBQ (200,000–350,000 VND/person) with your host by noon.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I sleep on Big Island (Đảo Lớn) or Little Island (Đảo Bé)?
If you have **1 or 2 nights**, sleep on **Big Island (Đảo Lớn)**—either near **An Vinh Port** for 4-star pool comfort (Ly Son Pearl, Muong Thanh) or near **Hang Cau / Mu Cu** for sunrise bungalows—and visit **Little Island (Đảo Bé)** on a half-day morning canoe trip (07:30 AM–13:30 PM, 120,000–140,000 VND round-trip). If you have **3 days / 2 nights** between April and August, spend 1 night on Big Island and 1 night on Little Island to experience Bai Ngang beach in complete serenity after 14:30.

### When is the best season to book Ly Son hotels, and when do ferries get cancelled?
The prime dry, calm-sea window runs from **April to August**, with **February–March** bringing green coastal moss on the volcanic reefs and the **autumn garlic-planting season (September–October)** followed by the **garlic harvest (February–March)**. Avoid booking tight flight connections between **October and January**, when northeast monsoon winds (Beaufort Level >= 6 on Windy.com) regularly suspend all Sa Ky–Ly Son ferries for 1–3 days.

### How do I get around Ly Son Island from my hotel?
On **Big Island (Đảo Lớn)**, virtually every hotel and homestay rents motorbikes on the spot for 120,000–150,000 VND/day (fuel included, no deposit required for overnight guests), and electric buggies or 4–7 seat taxis (150,000–300,000 VND per trip) wait at Ben Dinh Port. On **Little Island (Đảo Bé)**, you can walk across the 0.69 km² island in 20 minutes or hop on a shared electric buggy (30,000–50,000 VND/person).
