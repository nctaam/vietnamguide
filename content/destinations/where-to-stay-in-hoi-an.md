# Where to Stay in Hoi An: Old Town vs Beach & Fields (2026)

Picking where to stay in Hoi An comes down to a simple three-way geographical split across a compact 5 km radius: the lantern-lit pedestrian streets around the UNESCO Ancient Town, the open emerald rice paddies of Cam Chau and Cam Thanh in the middle, or the sandy shoreline of An Bang and Cua Dai beaches to the northeast. Unlike Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City, Hoi An has no commercial airport or railway station inside town limits. Every visitor arrives via a 30 km, 45-minute road transfer south from Da Nang International Airport (DAD) or Da Nang Railway Station, costing 300,000 to 380,000 VND ($12 to $15 USD) by private 4-seat sedan.

Because UNESCO zoning prohibits modern high-rise hotel towers and swimming pools inside the historic timber shophouses of Minh An Ward, most properties sit along the immediate perimeter ring, across river bridges on An Hoi and Cam Nam islands, or along the 4.5 km corridor linking town to the East Sea. Below is the 2026 neighborhood breakdown by nightly VND and USD rates, seasonal rice and beach calendars, bicycle commute distances, and autumn flood-zone boundaries.

> **The Seasonal Split Strategy:** Visit between March and August and you can swim in calm, 28°C ocean water at An Bang Beach every morning before riding a Grab into the Ancient Town at 17:00. Visit between October and January, however, and northeast monsoon swells erode the beachfront while bringing cool 21°C rain showers. During autumn and winter months, skip beachfront homestays and book a heated-pool boutique hotel along the Cam Pho or Hai Ba Trung edge of the Ancient Town.

## 1. Ancient Town Edge, Cam Pho & An Hoi Islet: Best for Walkability & Short Stays

Strict heritage rules forbid modern hotels inside the core pedestrian blocks of Tran Phu, Nguyen Thai Hoc, and Bach Dang, where a 120,000 VND ($4.80 USD) sightseeing ticket grants entry to historic merchant houses, Chinese assembly halls, and the 1593 Japanese Covered Bridge. Instead, the smartest walkable hotels cluster 300 to 700 meters outside the ticket booths along Hai Ba Trung, Phan Chu Trinh, Ly Thuong Kiet, and Hung Vuong in Cam Pho Ward, or just across the footbridge on An Hoi Islet.

From these perimeter streets, you walk 5 to 8 minutes into the car-free zone during the daily motor-vehicle curfews (09:00 to 11:00 and 15:00 to 21:30). Nightly rates for four-star boutique hotels with courtyard pools, elevators, and buffet breakfasts run 950,000 to 2,400,000 VND ($38 to $95 USD) in 2026. If you plan to commission custom linen suits, silk dresses, or leather boots at long-standing workshops along Le Loi, Tran Hung Dao, and Phan Chu Trinh, staying within 500 meters of the Ancient Town saves hours of transit time. Custom tailoring requires two to three in-person fittings spaced 12 to 24 hours apart, and walking 4 minutes back to your hotel room between morning fabric selection and evening hemline adjustments beats waiting for taxis in 33°C afternoon heat.

Pay close attention to micro-geography on An Hoi Islet (Nguyen Phuc Chu Street). The eastern half of An Hoi around the Night Market and Ba Le Well bridge hosts loud late-night backpacker bars and karaoke speakers until 01:00. Book 400 meters further west along quiet West An Hoi or across in Cam Pho Ward for silent sleep.

## 2. Cam Chau & Cam Thanh Rice Paddies: Best Value Villa Base Between Town & Sea

Roughly 2 km to 3 km east of the Ancient Town along Cua Dai Road, Nguyen Duy Hieu, and Tran Nhan Tong, urban streets open into 300 hectares of irrigated rice fields and nipa palm waterways. This zone splits the difference between town and sea. Cam Chau and Cam Thanh sit 2.5 km east of the Japanese Covered Bridge and 2.5 km west of An Bang or Cua Dai Beach.

Land plots are generous here. Boutique villas and eco-lodges offer 35-square-meter balconies, 20-meter saltwater pools, and complimentary single-speed bicycles for 800,000 to 2,200,000 VND ($32 to $88 USD) per night. Cycling along flat concrete paddy paths to Ba Le Market or Old Town coffee roasters takes 10 minutes. Prefer air-conditioned transit? A metered GrabCar costs 40,000 to 60,000 VND ($1.60 to $2.40 USD) each way. In southern Cam Thanh near the Bay Mau coconut forest (3.5 km southeast of town), bamboo basket-boat docks attract large tour bus groups between 09:30 and 16:00. Verify that your Cam Thanh villa sits on a quiet cul-de-sac lane off Tran Nhan Tong at least 400 meters away from the main basket-boat parking lots, where amplified karaoke speakers echo across the water during daytime excursion hours.

Timing matters for paddy views. Farmers cultivate two annual rice crops: the winter-spring cycle grows from late January to late April, while the summer-autumn cycle runs from June through August. Fields lie fallow, plowed, or flooded with shallow mirror water during October, November, and May.

## 3. An Bang Beach & Tan Thanh Fishing Village: Best for Coastal Cafes & Summer Swims

Located 4.5 km northeast of the Ancient Town at the northern end of Hai Ba Trung Street, An Bang Beach and the adjacent Tan Thanh neighborhood serve as Hoi An's primary swimming coast. A pedestrian-friendly back lane—Nguyen Phan Vinh Street—runs parallel to the dunes for 1.8 km, lined with French-Vietnamese bistros, live jazz bars, seafood grills, and yoga studios.

Low-rise beachfront homestays and garden bungalows along Nguyen Phan Vinh charge 750,000 to 1,800,000 VND ($30 to $72 USD) per night, placing your room 80 meters from the tide line. Upscale boutique resorts with private sun loungers in Tan Thanh and southern Ha My Beach cost 2,500,000 to 5,500,000 VND ($100 to $220 USD). Most properties run two free daily shuttle vans into the Ancient Town at 10:00 and 17:00, or you can hail a 10-minute GrabCar for 75,000 to 95,000 VND ($3 to $3.80 USD). An Bang shines brightest from March through August when waves stay flat. Between October and January, winter storm surges narrow the dry sand strip behind protective geotextile sandbags.

## 4. Cua Dai Beach, Cam Nam Island & Thu Bon Riverfront: Best for Quiet Resorts & Island Boats

South of An Bang along Au Co and Cua Dai streets, the Thu Bon River meets the East Sea near Cua Dai Pier. Large full-service resorts with expansive palm gardens and river-facing infinity pools line this 3 km estuary peninsula, charging 1,600,000 to 6,000,000 VND ($64 to $240 USD) per night. Closer to town, Cam Nam Island (connected to the eastern end of the Ancient Town via Cam Nam Bridge along Nguyen Tri Phuong Street) offers quiet riverfront homestays for 650,000 to 1,300,000 VND ($26 to $52 USD) per night, famous for local hen xao (stir-fried baby river clams) and crispy corn dishes costing 50,000 VND ($2 USD).

Staying near Cua Dai provides a direct logistical advantage for marine excursions. High-speed canoes to the Cham Islands UNESCO Biosphere Reserve depart Cua Dai Pier (`1.5 km` south of the resort strip) every morning between 08:00 and 08:30 during the March-to-September calm sea window, reaching Bai Lang Port in 20 minutes for 650,000 to 850,000 VND ($26 to $34 USD) including snorkeling gear and seafood lunch.

> [!WARNING]
> **October & November Flood-Zone Protocol:** During peak central Vietnam monsoon weeks between mid-October and late November, upstream dam releases and high tides cause the Thu Bon River to overflow onto **Bach Dang Street, Nguyen Phuc Chu (An Hoi Islet), and low-lying sections of Cam Nam Island** by 0.5 to 1.2 meters for 48 to 72 hours at a time. When traveling in October or November, book properties on elevated ground north of **Hai Ba Trung / Ly Thuong Kiet**, in **Cam Chau**, or at **An Bang Beach**, and request a room on the 2nd floor or higher.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I stay in Hoi An Ancient Town or at An Bang Beach?
If your trip lasts 2 nights or takes place between October and February, stay on the edge of the Ancient Town (Cam Pho or Hai Ba Trung) so you can walk to tailors, lantern streets, and restaurants in 5 minutes without relying on taxis in the rain. If you stay 3 to 5 nights between March and August, book An Bang Beach or a Cam Chau rice field pool villa and take a 10-minute, 60,000 to 85,000 VND ($2.40 to $3.40 USD) Grab into the Ancient Town after 16:30 once afternoon heat drops.

### How much is a taxi or Grab from Da Nang Airport to Hoi An hotels in 2026?
A pre-booked private 4-seat car arranged through your Hoi An hotel or Klook costs 300,000 to 350,000 VND ($12 to $14 USD) flat rate for the 30 km, 45-minute drive from Da Nang International Airport (DAD) directly to any hotel in Minh An, Cam Chau, or An Bang Beach. Hailing a GrabCar on arrival at the airport pickup lane costs 330,000 to 400,000 VND ($13.20 to $16 USD) including the 10,000 VND airport toll gate fee.

### When are the rice fields in Hoi An green?
Hoi An farmers cultivate two annual rice crops in Cam Chau and Cam Thanh. You will see lush emerald green paddies in February through March and again in June through July, transitioning to golden harvest stalks in April and August. During May and from mid-September through December, the paddies are either harvested stubble or flooded water basins.

### Can cars drive to hotels inside Hoi An Ancient Town?
No motor vehicles are permitted inside the core UNESCO pedestrian streets (Tran Phu, Nguyen Thai Hoc, Bach Dang, Nguyen Thi Minh Khai) from 09:00 to 11:00 and 15:00 to 21:30 daily. However, virtually all hotels sit just outside these barriers along Hai Ba Trung, Phan Chu Trinh, Hung Vuong, or Nguyen Phuc Chu, where airport cars and Grab taxis can drop you directly at the hotel lobby 24 hours a day.
