# Phu Quoc Travel Guide: Beaches, Where to Stay & Costs (2026)

Resting in the warm, sheltered waters of the Gulf of Thailand off the southwestern coast of Vietnam, **Phu Quoc** is the nation's largest island, encompassing 574 square kilometers of mountainous terrain, tropical evergreen rainforests, and powdery silica beaches. Known historically for its prized artisanal fish sauce and black pepper cultivation, this tear-shaped island has rapidly matured into Southeast Asia's foremost luxury beach destination.

What sets Phu Quoc apart from mainland Vietnamese destinations is its unique immigration status. Under statutory Vietnamese border decrees, international travelers flying directly into Phu Quoc International Airport benefit from a special **30-day visa exemption**, enabling foreign passport holders to explore the island without pre-arranged consular visas.

However, the island presents marked contrasts across its geography. The western shoreline buzzes with expansive beachfront resorts, sunset cocktail venues, and lively night markets. In contrast, the southern An Thoi archipelago offers exceptional coral reef snorkeling, while the dense northern interior remains protected under Phu Quoc National Park.

This comprehensive field guide details statutory 30-day visa exemption regulations, verified beach profiles, An Thoi island-hopping logistics, seafood dining institutions, and realistic 2026 travel budgets.

> [!NOTE]

Passports must be valid for at least six months with proof of onward departure within 30 days.

Phu Quoc features over 150 kilometers of coastline. Understanding seasonal wind patterns is critical when choosing your beach base.

### 1. Long Beach (Bãi Trường)
Extending for nearly 20 kilometers along the southwestern coast from Duong Dong town down toward the airport, Long Beach serves as the island's primary tourism corridor.
- **The Scene:** Broad golden sand shaded by leaning coconut palms. The shoreline is lined with beachfront resorts, casual beach bars, massage palapas, and sunset seafood lounges.
- **Best Season:** November through April, when the ocean is glass-calm and sunset views across the Gulf of Thailand are exceptional.
- **Suitability:** Ideal for travelers who prioritize walking access to diverse dining, evening nightlife, and reliable convenience amenities.

### 2. Sao Beach (Bãi Sao)
Tucked into the southeastern coastline 25 kilometers south of Duong Dong, **Bãi Sao** is celebrated for its distinctive white silica sand that feels like fine flour underfoot.
- **The Scene:** A dramatic crescent bay protected from prevailing southwestern summer swells, featuring shallow turquoise waters that remain calm even during the summer monsoon months.
- **Practical Information:** Public beach access is free. Independent vendors rent sun loungers and beach umbrellas for **50,000 to 100,000 VND (~$2.00 to $4.00 USD)** per day. Water sports operators offer jet skis (600,000 VND for 15 minutes) and kayak rentals (150,000 VND per hour).

### 3. Khem Beach (Bãi Khem)
Located immediately south of Bai Sao, **Bãi Khem** was previously a restricted military zone, preserving its pristine shoreline.
- **The Scene:** Exceptional powdery white sand framing emerald waters, now home to upscale master-planned resort complexes including JW Marriott Phu Quoc Emerald Bay and Premier Residences.
- **Suitability:** Perfect for travelers seeking private resort luxury, manicured beachfronts, and fine international dining away from street traffic.

### 4. Rạch Vẹm (Starfish Beach)
Located 25 kilometers north of Duong Dong on the tranquil northern coastline, **Rạch Vẹm** is an authentic stilted fishing village famous for colonies of native red starfish (*Protoreaster nodosus*) that congregate in the warm, clear shallows.
- **Traveler Etiquette:** Observe starfish underwater without lifting them out of the sea. Exposing starfish to direct air and tropical sun induces fatal thermal shock within seconds.
- **Access:** The final 5-kilometer access road passes through unpaved red clay forest trails; exercise caution on rented motorbikes during wet periods.

## Landmark Highlights: An Thoi Archipelago & The Cable Car

Beyond lounging on coastal sands, southern Phu Quoc delivers world-class marine adventures.

### Sun World Hòn Thơm Cable Car
Connecting the southern port of An Thoi with **Hòn Thơm Island**, this Austrian-engineered aerial ropeway holds the Guinness World Record as the longest three-rope sea-crossing cable car on Earth at **7,899 meters**.

The 15-minute flight glides 160 meters above turquoise open seas, providing panoramic bird's-eye views of vibrant fishing flotillas, emerald islets, and coastal coral reefs.
- **Tariffs:** Standard return tickets cost **650,000 to 700,000 VND (~$26.00 to $28.00 USD)** for adults, including admission to Aquatopia Water Park and Exotica theme park on Hon Thom.
- **Operating Hours:** Operates daily with morning departures (09:00 to 11:30) and afternoon runs (13:30 to 17:00).

### An Thoi Archipelago Speedboat Excursions
A cluster of 15 offshore islets stretches south of the main island. Modern four-island speedboat and wooden boat tours depart daily from An Thoi port.
- **Hòn Móng Tay (Fingernail Island):** Shallow sandy lagoon perfect for swimming.
- **Hòn Gầm Ghì:** A marine sanctuary featuring dense shallow cabbage and staghorn coral reefs ideal for snorkeling.
- **Hòn Mây Rút:** Soft sand beach with open-air beach bars and fresh coconut concessions.
- **Tour Tariffs:** Shared small-group speedboat day tours range from **450,000 to 750,000 VND ($18 to $30 USD)** per person, covering hotel transfers, snorkeling gear, life vests, and lunch.

### The Culinary Phenomenon: Bún Quậy Kiến Xây
No culinary exploration of Phu Quoc is complete without tasting **Bún Quậy** (literally "Stirred Noodles"). Invented by family-owned **Kiến Xây** over two decades ago, the dining process is entirely interactive:
1. Fresh rice flour paste is pressed directly through a perforated cylinder into boiling water right before your eyes.
2. Fresh raw shrimp paste and squid paste are smeared thinly across the bottom of your ceramic bowl.
3. Boiling noodle water is ladled into the bowl, flash-poaching the seafood paste into tender, savory dumplings.
4. Diners head to the condiment counter to hand-whisk their custom dipping sauce using fresh calamansi lime juice, sugar, coarse salt, MSG, and crushed red bird's eye chilies.

A steaming bowl costs between **40,000 and 65,000 VND ($1.60 to $2.60 USD)**.

### Traditional Fish Sauce Barrel Houses (*Nhà Thùng*)
Phu Quoc produces Vietnam's most revered fish sauce (*nước mắm*), granted Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) status by the European Union. Small black anchovies (*cá cơm than*) caught in surrounding Gulf waters are salted immediately on fishing boats at a precise 3:1 fish-to-salt ratio, then fermented in massive wooden vats crafted from local ironwood (*bời lời*) for 12 to 15 months.

Visitors can tour established barrel facilities such as **Khải Hoàn** or **Phụng Hưng** for free to view dozens of towering 15-ton wooden vats and sample pure, unblended 40° to 43°N first-press extract (*nước mắm cốt*).

> [!TIP]
> Commercial airlines strictly prohibit carrying bottles of fish sauce inside both carry-on and checked luggage due to odor damage risks in the event of breakage. Reputable producers like Khai Hoan provide secure international postal dispatch services directly from the factory counter.

## Transportation & Practical Island Navigation

Moving around Phu Quoc requires selecting the transport option matching your confidence level.

### 1. Motorcycle Rental
Renting an automatic scooter (120,000 to 160,000 VND per day) offers maximum freedom to reach remote northern beaches and national park dirt trails. Main north-south highways (DT45 and DT47) are smooth and well-paved, but rural tracks in the north feature red clay, loose sand, and potholes. Always inspect tires and brakes before departing.

### 2. VinBus Electric Network
Phu Quoc features an extensive, modern electric bus network operated by VinBus. **Route 17** and **Route 19** connect Phu Quoc International Airport with Duong Dong town and Grand World in the north, operating continuously with tickets costing **10,000 to 40,000 VND** (free on designated resort loops). Buses are air-conditioned, clean, and offer digital stop tracking.

### 3. Taxis and Ride-Hailing
Metered taxis (Mai Linh and Taxi Phu Quoc) and app-based **GrabCar** operate extensively across Duong Dong and the southern resort zones. A taxi ride from the airport into downtown Duong Dong costs **140,000 to 180,000 VND ($5.60 to $7.20 USD)**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the 30-day visa exemption work for Phu Quoc?
Foreign travelers of any nationality are exempt from entry visa requirements for stays up to 30 days on Phu Quoc, provided they arrive **directly** at Phu Quoc International Airport or sea port without entering mainland Vietnam first. You must present a passport valid for at least 6 months, roundtrip air tickets proving departure from Phu Quoc within 30 days, and stay strictly on Phu Quoc Island. If you intend to travel onward to mainland Vietnam (e.g., Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi), you must hold a valid Vietnam e-visa.

### When is the absolute best time of year to visit Phu Quoc?
The prime window spans from **November through April**. During this dry winter season, the sea on the western coast is calm and turquoise, rainfall is minimal, skies are clear, and daytime temperatures hover comfortably between 28°C and 31°C.

### Is tap water safe to drink on Phu Quoc?
No. Tap water on Phu Quoc comes from local municipal reservoirs and groundwater sources that are not treated to international potable standards. Drink only bottled water or filtered water provided by hotels. Ice served in established restaurants, cafes, and cocktail lounges is commercially produced and safe.

### Which is better: Phu Quoc or Da Nang?
Phu Quoc is an island getaway dedicated to tropical beaches, luxury coastal resorts, water sports, and tranquil seafood villages. Da Nang is a major modern metropolitan city with extensive urban beaches, bridges, sophisticated dining, and unmatched proximity to three UNESCO World Heritage Sites (Hoi An, My Son, and Hue). Choose Phu Quoc for beach relaxation; choose Da Nang for cultural exploration and urban beach diversity.

### How do I get to Phu Quoc from Ho Chi Minh City?
The fastest, most convenient method is flying from **Tan Son Nhat Airport (SGN)** directly to **Phu Quoc Airport (PQC)**. Flight duration is approximately 50 to 55 minutes, with over 15 daily departures operated by Vietnam Airlines, Vietjet Air, and Bamboo Airways (one-way fares range from 700,000 to 1,400,000 VND). Overland alternatives require a 6-hour bus to Rach Gia or Ha Tien, followed by a 1.2- to 2.5-hour high-speed ferry ride.
