# Phong Nha Caves Guide: Cavern Systems, Trekking, and Route Fit

Designated a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2003 and expanded in 2015, **Phong Nha-Ke Bang National Park** (*Vườn quốc gia Phong Nha - Kẻ Bàng*) in Quang Binh province protects the oldest major karst massif in Asia, dating back roughly 400 million years to the Paleozoic era. Spanning 123,326 hectares along the Annamite Range (*Dãy Trường Sơn*) adjacent to Laos, this subterranean landscape contains over 300 surveyed caves and grottoes carved out by the subterranean river systems of the Son and Chay rivers.

The park encompasses a distinct spectrum of underground exploration. Independent day-trippers can tour massive show caves along wooden boardwalks and river dragon boats, while adventure travelers can embark on multi-day speleological expeditions into Hang En (the world's third-largest cave) or Son Doong (the world's largest known cavern).

This operational guide provides verifiable tariffs, transport logistics, physical difficulty ratings, seasonal flooding windows, and route-planning protocols for exploring the Phong Nha cave network in 2026.

### 1. Paradise Cave (Dong Thien Duong)

Discovered in 2005 by local woodcutter Ho Khanh and surveyed by the British Cave Research Association (BCRA), Paradise Cave extends for 31.4 km inside a limestone mountain 25 km west of Phong Nha town. The initial 1.0 km section is open to general visitors via an elevated wooden walkway:

- **Entrance Ticket**: 250,000 VND ($10.00 USD) per adult; 125,000 VND ($5.00 USD) for children measuring 1.1 to 1.3 meters; free for children under 1.1 meters.
- **Electric Buggy Transit**: From the ticket barrier to the base of the mountain is a 1.6 km paved road. Electric buggies carry passengers for 60,000 VND one-way or 100,000 VND round-trip per person (or 150,000–250,000 VND for private 4 to 8-seat buggies). Visitors seeking exercise can walk the path on foot in 20 minutes without charge.
- **The Cave Ascent**: From the buggy drop-off point, visitors must ascend either 524 concrete switchback stairs or a zig-zagging 1.6 km concrete ramp to reach the narrow cave mouth.
- **Interior Experience**: Descending into the cavern reveals massive vaulted ceilings reaching heights of 72 meters and widths up to 150 meters. Strategically positioned warm LED lights illuminate towering stalagmites shaped like pagodas, petrified waterfalls, and towering stone columns without disrupting natural mineral colors.
- **Operating Hours**: 07:00 to 16:30 daily.

### 2. Phong Nha Cave and Tien Son Cave

Situated 5 km upstream along the Son River, Phong Nha Cave is a classic river cavern entered exclusively by water:
- **Entrance Ticket**: 150,000 VND ($6.00 USD) per adult; free for children under 1.3 meters.
- **Boat Charter Fee**: 550,000 VND ($22.00 USD) per boat round-trip. Each traditional wooden dragon boat holds a maximum of 12 passengers. Solo travelers and couples can easily assemble informal groups at the central ticket dock (*Bến thuyền Du lịch Phong Nha*) along the main town strip to divide the boat charter cost, reducing individual transport fees to 46,000–55,000 VND.
- **Navigational Protocol**: Motorboats transport visitors 30 minutes up the Son River past pastoral rural riverbanks. Upon reaching the cave mouth, the boat engine is switched off, and the boatman paddles silently by hand for 1.2 km through illuminated subterranean chambers filled with dripping stalactites. Passengers disembark near the exit to walk 300 meters across dry sandbars before boarding for the return trip.
- **Tien Son Cave Option**: Perched 200 vertical meters directly above the water entrance of Phong Nha Cave sits **Tien Son Cave** (*Động Tiên Sơn*), a dry fossil cave known for resonant limestone columns that sound like gongs when tapped. Entry is 80,000 VND ($3.20 USD) per person, with an additional 50,000 VND boat waiting supplement. Reaching Tien Son requires climbing 330 steep stone steps.

### 3. Dark Cave (Hang Toi) and Chay River

Located along the West Branch of the Ho Chi Minh Highway, Dark Cave offers high-energy adventure recreation:
- **Full Adventure Package**: 450,000 VND ($18.00 USD) per adult. The ticket includes a 400-meter two-cable zipline ride crossing the jade-colored Chay River, safety helmet with LED headlamp, life vest, guided unlit cave exploration, entry into the deep subterranean mud bath chamber, a kayak paddle back across the river, and aerial obstacle courses.
- **Sightseeing Only Package**: 270,000 VND ($11.00 USD), covering kayaking and river games without the cave mud bath.
- **Physical Requirements**: Participants swim 20 meters across an open pool to access the cave mouth, then navigate 800 meters through total darkness. The interior floor transitions into thick, buoyant, natural clay mud where visitors float effortlessly. Dark swimwear is mandatory; two-piece bikinis or tight swim trunks are recommended, as clothing will absorb mud.

### The Hang En 2-Day Expedition

Hang En ranks as the world's third-largest cavern and serves as the gateway camp along the approach route to Son Doong. Managed by Oxalis Adventure, the 2-day, 1-night trek is one of Vietnam's most iconic outdoor journeys:
- **The Route**: Trekkers descend a steep jungle ridge with an elevation drop of 400 meters, cross dozens of shallow river braids in Rao Thuong Valley, and visit the remote **Ban Doong** village inhabited by the Bru-Van Kieu ethnic minority.
- **The Cavern**: The main chamber of Hang En stretches over 100 meters wide and rises 120 meters high, illuminated by natural daylight pouring through a colossal mountain archway.
- **Camp Setup**: Participants camp on a pristine subterranean white sand beach beside an emerald subterranean lake. The package covers porters, safety guides, hot cooked camp dinners, certified dry bags, and caving helmets.

### Son Doong: Strict Quotas and Booking Reality

Measuring over 38.5 million cubic meters, Hang Son Doong contains underground chambers large enough to fit a Boeing 747 aircraft or a 40-story city skyscraper.
- **Environmental Limits**: Quang Binh provincial authorities cap visitation at 1,000 guests annually to safeguard delicate cave pearls and pristine calcite formations.
- **Physical Clearance**: Applicants must complete a detailed medical questionnaire verifying endurance for continuous jungle hiking, river wading, and ascending the 90-meter-tall calcite formation known as the "Great Wall of Vietnam" using mechanical climbing ascenders.
- **Booking Window**: Oxalis opens annual permits 12 months in advance, and allocations typically sell out within weeks of release.

> [!NOTE]
> All backcountry expeditions supply caving boots with drainage holes, protective gloves, life vests, and filtered water systems. Independent hikers are legally prohibited from entering primary park zones without licensed rangers and certified guides.

## Seasonal Weather, River Levels, and Flooding Windows

Quang Binh experiences a sharp tropical monsoon climate with distinct weather phases that dictate which caves remain accessible.

### Navigating the Flood Season (September to November)

During late September, October, and early November, typhoons crossing the East Sea dump immense volumes of rainfall over the Annamite mountains:
- **Water Gate Closure**: As the Son River swells, water levels rise to seal the arched mouth of Phong Nha Cave completely, forcing boat tours to suspend operations for days or weeks.
- **Dry Cave Viability**: Paradise Cave and Tien Son Cave sit hundreds of meters above valley floor floodplains and remain operational throughout most heavy rains.
- **Expedition Moratorium**: Multi-day expedition treks into Hang En, Tu Lan, and Son Doong cease completely from mid-September through mid-November due to flash flood hazards inside river tunnels.

## Step-by-Step 3-Day Exploration Protocol

This structured itinerary balances self-guided show caves with rural valley cycling and technical adventure sports.

### Day 1: Monumental Show Caves and Son River Cruising

- **08:00 – 11:30**: Rent a semi-automatic scooter (120,000–150,000 VND) or hire an open-air electric buggy to travel 25 km west along the Ho Chi Minh Highway to Paradise Cave. Walk the 1.0 km boardwalk, taking photographs of the illuminated cathedral chambers before midday heat sets in.
- **12:00 – 13:30**: Enjoy a lunch of charcoal-grilled local pork and morning glory vegetables at a riverside restaurant in Son Trach village (80,000–120,000 VND per person).
- **14:00 – 16:30**: Head to the Phong Nha Tourism Center boat pier. Share a 550,000 VND dragon boat charter with fellow travelers, gliding silently inside Phong Nha Cave to inspect illuminated flowstone formations and the underground sandbar.
- **17:00 – 19:00**: Cycle through the rural rice paddies of Bong Lai Valley or enjoy craft beer at a local garden lounge along the Son River.

### Day 2: High-Energy Adrenaline at Dark Cave and Chay River

- **08:30 – 12:30**: Drive 15 km northwest to Dark Cave. Gear up with harnesses and headlamps, launch across the 400-meter Chay River zipline, and swim into the cavern to soak in the thick mineral mud bath. Kayak back across the river to test your balance on the floating ropes course.
- **13:00 – 14:30**: Refuel with a traditional feast of chicken grilled on bamboo slats (*gà nướng muối ớt*) served with sticky rice and wild pepper salt (250,000–350,000 VND for a whole chicken serving 2–3 people).
- **15:00 – 17:30**: Relax at the **Nuoc Mooc Eco Trail** (*Suối Nước Moọc*), located 3 km past Dark Cave along the river. Walk along elevated bamboo bridges suspended above turquoise freshwater springs. Admission is 80,000 VND ($3.20 USD) for general entry or 180,000 VND ($7.20 USD) including kayak rentals and water slide access.

### Day 3: The Rural Valleys of Bong Lai or 1-Day Jungle Trek

- **Option A (Rural Leisure)**: Explore the **Bong Lai Valley**, riding through rolling dirt lanes past cassava fields, duck farms, and rural hamlets. Stop at the iconic "Duck Stop" to feed domesticated flocks and ride water buffaloes (100,000–150,000 VND including banh xeo pancakes).
- **Option B (Introductory Caving Trek)**: Embark on a scheduled 1-day guided trek into the **Tu Lan Cave System** or **Elephant Cave / Ma Da Valley** with Oxalis or Jungle Boss (1,800,000–2,200,000 VND). Trek through primary forest and swim through a 200-meter river cave wearing headlamps and flotation vests.
- **18:30 – 21:00**: Farewell dinner in Son Trach, sampling local freshwater fish hotpot (*lẩu cá sông*) and local banana wine (*rượu chuối hột*).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which cave is better: Paradise Cave or Phong Nha Cave?
Both caverns offer complementary geological perspectives. Paradise Cave is an enormous, dry cavern viewed from an elevated wooden boardwalk, showcasing towering cathedral chambers and delicate stalagmites with excellent ambient lighting. Phong Nha Cave is a water-filled river cavern where visitors sit inside a wooden boat drifting along an underground river. If time permits, visit both; if limited to one and seeking effortless walking, choose Phong Nha Cave; if prioritizing monumental subterranean scale, choose Paradise Cave.

### How do I get to Phong Nha from Dong Hoi?
Phong Nha sits 45 km northwest of Dong Hoi city. Travelers can take local public bus B4 (running hourly between 05:30 and 17:00, costing 35,000–45,000 VND with a 75-minute travel time), hire a metered taxi or GrabCar (400,000–500,000 VND, 45 minutes), or arrange a shared hotel shuttle minivan (100,000–150,000 VND per seat).

### Can children and seniors visit the caves comfortably?
Phong Nha Cave is exceptionally accessible for travelers of all ages because the entire interior journey is completed seated in a stable boat, requiring only a brief 300-meter flat walk on sand. Paradise Cave requires moderate stamina to climb the 524 stone steps or 1.6 km ramp leading up to the cave mouth, but the interior boardwalk features handrails and rest benches. Dark Cave is unsuitable for young children or travelers with limited mobility due to the required swimming, mud wading, and ziplining.

### What clothing and footwear should I pack?
For show caves like Paradise and Phong Nha, wear comfortable walking shoes with grippy rubber soles (steps can be wet and slippery), breathable cotton clothing, and carry a light jacket, as cave interiors stay at an ambient 18°C–22°C. For Dark Cave and technical river treks, pack dark swimwear, quick-drying athletic shorts, a dry bag for electronics, and synthetic socks that dry rapidly.

### Are credit cards widely accepted in Phong Nha?
Major tour operators (Oxalis, Jungle Boss) and international-style hotels accept Visa and Mastercard, frequently with a 3 percent processing fee. However, park ticket counters, local dragon boat operators, family-run restaurants, and rural homestays deal almost exclusively in cash. Multiple Agribank and Vietcombank ATMs operate along the main road in Son Trach town to withdraw Vietnamese Dong.
